# Glossary

Terms taught in weeks 1–4, listed by subject. Each entry links to the lesson that teaches it, where the Frayer card (definition, origin when it is known, example, near-miss, and a connection) is given. Use this page to look a word up; use the week-end vocabulary reviews to retrieve it from memory.

Pronunciations are on the lesson page (Hear, in English). Spanish and Latin *words* still have their own Hear-word buttons in those lessons.

## Mathematics

- **[AM–GM inequality](days/d07.html#term-am-gm)** — Day 7, Mathematics · Algebra III: inequalities and the mean. For a, b ≥ 0, (a + b)/2 ≥ √(ab), with equality only when a = b.
- **[angle bisector](days/d12.html#term-angle-bisector)** — Day 12, Mathematics · Geometry VI: the angle bisector and a parallel through a point. The ray that divides an angle into two equal angles.
- **[arithmetic progression](days/d20.html#term-arithmetic-progression)** — Day 20, Mathematics · Two-week problem set: constructions to tangents, and a first look at progressions. A sequence in which each term after the first is obtained by adding the same fixed number, the common difference d.
- **[axiom](days/d02.html#term-axiom)** — Day 2, Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs. A starting statement accepted without proof, so that theorems can be proved from it.
- **[bar model](days/d01.html#term-bar-model)** — Day 1, Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation. A drawing of quantities as bars of equal units, so the relationship is visible before any equation is written.
- **[chord](days/d18.html#term-chord)** — Day 18, Mathematics · Geometry IX: the circle, chords. A segment joining two points of a circle.
- **[completing the square](days/d05.html#term-completing-the-square)** — Day 5, Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations. Rewriting x² + px as a square plus or minus a constant, so a quadratic can be solved or a parabola’s vertex read off.
- **[congruence](days/d04.html#term-congruence)** — Day 4, Mathematics · Geometry II: congruent triangles and the isosceles triangle. A matching of two figures so that corresponding sides and corresponding angles are equal; one can be placed exactly on the other.
- **[construction](days/d11.html#term-construction)** — Day 11, Mathematics · Geometry V: constructions with compass and straightedge. A drawing made with only an unmarked straightedge and a compass, justified by axioms and earlier theorems, not by measuring with a ruler.
- **[diagonal](days/d14.html#term-diagonal)** — Day 14, Mathematics · Geometry VII: the parallelogram. A segment joining two vertices of a polygon that are not already joined by a side.
- **[discriminant](days/d05.html#term-discriminant)** — Day 5, Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations. The quantity that decides how many real roots a quadratic has: two if D > 0, one if D = 0, none if D < 0.
- **[distance from the center](days/d18.html#term-central-distance)** — Day 18, Mathematics · Geometry IX: the circle, chords. The perpendicular distance from the center of a circle to a chord.
- **[exponent](days/d16.html#term-exponent)** — Day 16, Mathematics · Algebra V: the laws of exponents. The small number that says how many times a base is used as a factor: in aⁿ, a is the base and n is the exponent.
- **[exterior angle](days/d06.html#term-exterior-angle)** — Day 6, Mathematics · Geometry III: parallel lines and the angle sum. An angle formed by one side of a triangle and the extension of an adjacent side.
- **[factor](days/d03.html#term-factor)** — Day 3, Mathematics · Algebra I: identities and factoring. To write an expression as a product; or one of those pieces.
- **[function](days/d09.html#term-function)** — Day 9, Mathematics · Functions and their graphs. A rule that assigns to each allowed input exactly one output.
- **[geometric progression](days/d20.html#term-geometric-progression)** — Day 20, Mathematics · Two-week problem set: constructions to tangents, and a first look at progressions. A sequence in which each term after the first is obtained by multiplying by the same fixed number, the common ratio r.
- **[heuristic](days/d10.html#term-heuristic)** — Day 10, Mathematics · Two-week problem set. A deliberate method of attack, not a guess: for example, draw a bar, try a smaller case, or write a short proof plan before computing.
- **[identity](days/d03.html#term-identity)** — Day 3, Mathematics · Algebra I: identities and factoring. An equality that is true for every allowed value of its letters, and that must be proved by expanding or by using a known identity.
- **[inequality](days/d07.html#term-inequality)** — Day 7, Mathematics · Algebra III: inequalities and the mean. A statement that one quantity is greater or less than another.
- **[isosceles](days/d04.html#term-isosceles)** — Day 4, Mathematics · Geometry II: congruent triangles and the isosceles triangle. A triangle with two sides equal.
- **[locus](days/d08.html#term-locus)** — Day 8, Mathematics · Geometry IV: inequalities in triangles. The set of all points that satisfy a given condition.
- **[parallel through a point](days/d12.html#term-parallel-construction)** — Day 12, Mathematics · Geometry VI: the angle bisector and a parallel through a point. The unique line through a given point P, not on a line ℓ, that is parallel to ℓ.
- **[parallelogram](days/d14.html#term-parallelogram)** — Day 14, Mathematics · Geometry VII: the parallelogram. A quadrilateral whose opposite sides are parallel (both pairs).
- **[perpendicular bisector](days/d11.html#term-perpendicular-bisector)** — Day 11, Mathematics · Geometry V: constructions with compass and straightedge. The line that cuts a segment in half and meets it at a right angle.
- **[power](days/d16.html#term-power)** — Day 16, Mathematics · Algebra V: the laws of exponents. The result of raising a base to an exponent, or the expression aⁿ itself.
- **[radical](days/d17.html#term-radical)** — Day 17, Mathematics · Algebra VI: radicals. An expression that uses a root symbol, such as √a or ³√a.
- **[ratio](days/d01.html#term-ratio)** — Day 1, Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation. A comparison of two quantities by units of the same size, written a : b.
- **[rectangle](days/d15.html#term-rectangle)** — Day 15, Mathematics · Geometry VIII: rectangle, rhombus and square. A parallelogram with a right angle.
- **[remainder](days/d01.html#term-remainder)** — Day 1, Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation. What is left of a quantity after a part of it has been taken.
- **[rhombus](days/d15.html#term-rhombus)** — Day 15, Mathematics · Geometry VIII: rectangle, rhombus and square. A parallelogram with all four sides equal.
- **[square root](days/d17.html#term-square-root)** — Day 17, Mathematics · Algebra VI: radicals. For a ≥ 0, the square root √a is the unique number ≥ 0 whose square is a.
- **[substitution](days/d13.html#term-substitution)** — Day 13, Mathematics · Algebra IV: systems of linear equations. A method for solving a system: use one equation to express one unknown in terms of the other, and put that expression into the second equation.
- **[system of equations](days/d13.html#term-system-of-equations)** — Day 13, Mathematics · Algebra IV: systems of linear equations. Two or more equations that must be true at the same time, about the same unknowns.
- **[tangent to a circle](days/d19.html#term-tangent-circle)** — Day 19, Mathematics · Geometry X: tangents to a circle. A line that meets a circle at exactly one point, called the point of contact.
- **[theorem](days/d02.html#term-theorem)** — Day 2, Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs. A statement that is proved from definitions, axioms, and earlier theorems.
- **[transversal](days/d06.html#term-transversal)** — Day 6, Mathematics · Geometry III: parallel lines and the angle sum. A line that crosses two or more other lines, forming corresponding, alternate interior, and same-side interior angles.
- **[triangle inequality](days/d08.html#term-triangle-inequality)** — Day 8, Mathematics · Geometry IV: inequalities in triangles. In a triangle, each side is less than the sum of the other two (and greater than their difference).
- **[two tangents from a point](days/d19.html#term-two-tangents)** — Day 19, Mathematics · Geometry X: tangents to a circle. From a point outside a circle, the two tangent segments to the points of contact are equal.
- **[unit (in a bar or ratio)](days/d10.html#term-unit-bar)** — Day 10, Mathematics · Two-week problem set. One equal part in a bar model or a ratio.
- **[vertex](days/d09.html#term-vertex)** — Day 9, Mathematics · Functions and their graphs. The turning point of a parabola.
- **[vertical angles](days/d02.html#term-vertical-angles)** — Day 2, Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs. A pair of angles whose sides are the extensions of each other’s sides: the opposite angles formed when two lines meet.
- **[Vieta's formulas](days/d05.html#term-vietas-formulas)** — Day 5, Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations. For the roots r and s of x² + px + q = 0, the sum of the roots is −p and the product is q.

## Physics

- **[acceleration](days/d03.html#term-acceleration)** — Day 3, Physics · Acceleration and velocity–time graphs. The rate of change of velocity: how quickly velocity increases, decreases, or changes direction.
- **[action–reaction pair](days/d10.html#term-action-reaction-pair)** — Day 10, Physics · Newton's third law and the two-week check. The two forces named by the third law: they are equal, opposite, simultaneous, of the same type, and they act on two different bodies.
- **[centripetal acceleration](days/d16.html#term-centripetal-acceleration)** — Day 16, Physics · Uniform circular motion and centripetal force. The inward, center-seeking acceleration of a body that moves at constant speed on a circle, of size a = v²/r (or a = 4π²r/T², where T is the period).
- **[centripetal force](days/d16.html#term-centripetal-force)** — Day 16, Physics · Uniform circular motion and centripetal force. The net force toward the center that produces the centripetal acceleration: F_net, inward = m v²/r.
- **[component](days/d13.html#term-component)** — Day 13, Physics · Vectors and components. The projection of a vector along a chosen axis.
- **[conservation of momentum](days/d18.html#term-conservation-of-momentum)** — Day 18, Physics · Momentum. If two (or more) bodies interact and the net external force on the system is zero (or is negligible for a short collision), the total momentum of the system does not change.
- **[displacement](days/d01.html#term-displacement)** — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. The change in position: final minus initial.
- **[free fall](days/d05.html#term-free-fall)** — Day 5, Physics · Free fall. Motion under gravity alone, with air resistance neglected.
- **[friction](days/d11.html#term-friction)** — Day 11, Physics · Friction and the normal force. A force between surfaces in contact that resists their relative sliding.
- **[gravity](days/d05.html#term-gravity)** — Day 5, Physics · Free fall. The attraction that gives a free-falling object its downward acceleration g near Earth.
- **[impulse](days/d20.html#term-impulse)** — Day 20, Physics · Two-week check: friction to momentum. The product of a net force and the time it acts, equal to the change in momentum: J = F_net Δt = Δp, when the force is constant (or using an average force).
- **[independence of components](days/d20.html#term-independence-of-components)** — Day 20, Physics · Two-week check: friction to momentum. The rule, used for projectiles, that the horizontal motion (constant v_x, if air is neglected) and the vertical motion (acceleration g) can be solved separately, and that a change in one component does not by itself change the other.
- **[inertia](days/d06.html#term-inertia)** — Day 6, Physics · Newton's first law: inertia. The tendency of a body to keep its velocity — to stay at rest, or to keep moving in a straight line at constant speed — unless a net force acts on it.
- **[mass](days/d08.html#term-mass)** — Day 8, Physics · Newton's second law. A measure of how much a body resists acceleration (inertial mass).
- **[momentum](days/d18.html#term-momentum)** — Day 18, Physics · Momentum. The product of mass and velocity, p = m v, a vector in the same direction as the velocity.
- **[net force](days/d06.html#term-net-force)** — Day 6, Physics · Newton's first law: inertia. The vector sum of all forces on a body.
- **[Newton's second law](days/d08.html#term-newtons-second-law)** — Day 8, Physics · Newton's second law. The net force on a body equals its mass times its acceleration.
- **[Newton's third law](days/d10.html#term-newtons-third-law)** — Day 10, Physics · Newton's third law and the two-week check. If body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts a force on A that is equal in size, opposite in direction, and of the same type.
- **[normal force](days/d11.html#term-normal-force)** — Day 11, Physics · Friction and the normal force. The force a surface exerts on an object, perpendicular to the surface.
- **[position](days/d01.html#term-position)** — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. Where something is, measured from a chosen origin along a line, with a sign for direction.
- **[projectile](days/d15.html#term-projectile)** — Day 15, Physics · Projectile motion. A body in free flight under gravity alone (air resistance neglected in this course), with a constant horizontal velocity and a vertical acceleration of g downward.
- **[range (of a projectile)](days/d15.html#term-range)** — Day 15, Physics · Projectile motion. The horizontal distance traveled from the launch point to the landing point, equal to vx times the time of flight, when the landing is at the same vertical level or at a stated lower level.
- **[vector](days/d13.html#term-vector)** — Day 13, Physics · Vectors and components. A quantity that has both a size (magnitude) and a direction, such as displacement, velocity, acceleration, or force.
- **[velocity](days/d01.html#term-velocity)** — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. Displacement divided by the time taken (average velocity).
- **[velocity–time graph](days/d03.html#term-velocity-time-graph)** — Day 3, Physics · Acceleration and velocity–time graphs. A graph with time on the horizontal axis and velocity on the vertical axis.

## Science

- **[autoxidation](days/d14.html#term-autoxidation)** — Day 14, Science · Autoxidation and the oil film. A reaction in which a substance combines with oxygen in the air, without being set on fire.
- **[binder](days/d17.html#term-binder)** — Day 17, Science · Pigment and binder. The substance that holds the pigment particles in a film and attaches the film to the support.
- **[combustion](days/d04.html#term-combustion)** — Day 4, Science · Combustion and gases: the quantitative model. The chemical burning of a fuel with oxygen, producing new gases (and often water) and releasing heat.
- **[drying oil](days/d12.html#term-drying-oil)** — Day 12, Science · Phenomenon: why oil paint dries. A plant oil, such as linseed oil (from flax seed), that can form a solid film by reacting with oxygen in the air, rather than by the evaporation of water.
- **[evaporation](days/d12.html#term-evaporation)** — Day 12, Science · Phenomenon: why oil paint dries. The change in which a liquid becomes a gas at its surface.
- **[frequency](days/d09.html#term-frequency)** — Day 9, Science · Light: polarization and the quantitative model. The number of wave cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz).
- **[mass check (of a film)](days/d19.html#term-mass-check)** — Day 19, Science · The oil-paint module: a quantitative check and a short synthesis. A measurement that can distinguish evaporation from autoxidation: a film that is mainly losing a liquid to the air should lose mass; a drying-oil film can gain mass as it takes up oxygen (before any later, slower losses).
- **[model (scientific)](days/d02.html#term-scientific-model)** — Day 2, Science · Phenomenon: the candle under the jar. A reasoned picture of what is happening that makes a testable prediction.
- **[model of oil-paint drying](days/d19.html#term-oil-paint-model)** — Day 19, Science · The oil-paint module: a quantitative check and a short synthesis. The short account this module has built: a drying-oil binder, open to the air, undergoes autoxidation and then polymerization, forming a solid film that holds the pigment; a solvent, if used, may evaporate, but that is not what makes the oil itself into a film; watercolor is a…
- **[phenomenon](days/d02.html#term-phenomenon)** — Day 2, Science · Phenomenon: the candle under the jar. A real event or appearance that you can observe, and that you then try to explain with a model and a test.
- **[pigment](days/d17.html#term-pigment)** — Day 17, Science · Pigment and binder. A finely divided solid that gives a paint its color (and often its opacity) and that remains as particles in the film.
- **[polarization](days/d09.html#term-polarization)** — Day 9, Science · Light: polarization and the quantitative model. The preferring of one direction of vibration in a light wave.
- **[polymerization](days/d14.html#term-polymerization)** — Day 14, Science · Autoxidation and the oil film. A process in which small molecules join into much larger ones (polymers).
- **[quantitative](days/d04.html#term-quantitative)** — Day 4, Science · Combustion and gases: the quantitative model. Described with measured numbers and a relation, not only with words such as ‘more’ or ‘less’.
- **[Rayleigh scattering](days/d07.html#term-rayleigh-scattering)** — Day 7, Science · Phenomenon: why is the sky blue and the sunset red?. The scattering of light by particles much smaller than the wavelength.
- **[wavelength](days/d07.html#term-wavelength)** — Day 7, Science · Phenomenon: why is the sky blue and the sunset red?. The distance from one crest of a wave to the next.

## History

- **[Alexander of Macedon](days/d16.html#term-alexander)** — Day 16, History · Alexander and the end of the Achaemenid empire. The Macedonian king who, from 336 BCE (the death of his father, Philip II) until his own death at Babylon in 323 BCE, conquered the Achaemenid Persian empire.
- **[apology](days/d08.html#term-apology)** — Day 8, History · The trial of Socrates. A defense speech.
- **[Arrian](days/d16.html#term-arrian)** — Day 16, History · Alexander and the end of the Achaemenid empire. A Greek historian (2nd century CE) whose Anabasis of Alexander is a main surviving narrative of the campaign.
- **[citizenship](days/d06.html#term-citizenship)** — Day 6, History · Athens: Pericles' Funeral Oration. Membership in the political community, with its rights and duties.
- **[corroboration](days/d01.html#term-corroboration)** — Day 1, History · How historians work. Checking whether an independent source agrees, in part or in whole, with the claim you are testing.
- **[democracy](days/d06.html#term-democracy)** — Day 6, History · Athens: Pericles' Funeral Oration. In Pericles’ usage (as Thucydides reports it), a constitution whose administration ‘favors the many instead of the few’.
- **[diplomatic copy](days/d15.html#term-diplomatic-copy)** — Day 15, History · The Egyptian–Hittite treaty of year 21 of Ramesses II. A version of an agreement written for one court, often in a lingua franca (here Akkadian on the tablet from Ḫattuša) or in the court’s own script (Egyptian at Karnak), after the terms have been negotiated.
- **[gadfly](days/d08.html#term-gadfly)** — Day 8, History · The trial of Socrates. A biting fly; in Socrates’ metaphor, the stinging critic who keeps a large, sluggish horse — the city — awake.
- **[Hellenistic](days/d18.html#term-hellenistic)** — Day 18, History · The Hellenistic world and Ptolemaic Egypt. The period, after Alexander’s death in 323 BCE, in which his empire was divided among successor kingdoms (the Diadochi and their heirs) whose courts used Greek as a language of rule, while older cultures continued.
- **[historiē](days/d05.html#term-historie)** — Day 5, History · Greece: Herodotus and the idea of "inquiry". The Greek word (ἱστορίη) meaning inquiry, investigation.
- **[inquiry](days/d05.html#term-inquiry)** — Day 5, History · Greece: Herodotus and the idea of "inquiry". A systematic asking of questions in order to find out and to explain, not only to list events.
- **[judgment](days/d10.html#term-judgment)** — Day 10, History · Synthesis: law, citizenship and judgment. The act of deciding a case, or the standard by which people are judged.
- **[kingship (Egyptian, this period)](days/d13.html#term-kingship)** — Day 13, History · Kingship and the afterlife: the Pyramid Texts of Unas. The office of the Egyptian king (often called pharaoh in later usage) as both a political ruler and a figure in religion: the king’s tomb, titles, and afterlife texts present him as the one who maintains order and who will join the gods.
- **[lex talionis](days/d03.html#term-lex-talionis)** — Day 3, History · Babylon: the laws of Hammurabi. The rule of retaliation in kind: the penalty matches the injury, ‘an eye for an eye’.
- **[Old Kingdom](days/d11.html#term-old-kingdom)** — Day 11, History · Egypt: the Old Kingdom and the pyramids. The period of Egyptian history in which the great pyramids of Giza were built, conventionally the Third to Sixth Dynasties.
- **[periodization](days/d20.html#term-periodization)** — Day 20, History · Two-week synthesis: sources for kingship, from Khufu to the Ptolemies. The decision to cut a long past into named periods (Old Kingdom, New Kingdom, Hellenistic) so that change can be talked about.
- **[primary source](days/d01.html#term-primary-source)** — Day 1, History · How historians work. A source produced at the time by someone involved or present: a law, a letter, a coin, an inscription, a painting, a speech written down in the period.
- **[Ptolemaic Egypt](days/d18.html#term-ptolemaic-egypt)** — Day 18, History · The Hellenistic world and Ptolemaic Egypt. The Hellenistic kingdom in Egypt ruled by the dynasty founded by Ptolemy, a companion of Alexander (the same Ptolemy Arrian later preferred as a source), from the late 4th century BCE until 30 BCE.
- **[pyramid (Egyptian)](days/d11.html#term-pyramid)** — Day 11, History · Egypt: the Old Kingdom and the pyramids. A royal tomb of this period, with a square base and four sloping faces, built in stone, of which the Great Pyramid of Khufu at Giza is the largest.
- **[Pyramid Texts](days/d13.html#term-pyramid-texts)** — Day 13, History · Kingship and the afterlife: the Pyramid Texts of Unas. Religious texts, in the Egyptian language, carved on the walls of royal pyramids beginning with the pyramid of Unas (Wenis), last king of the 5th Dynasty, at Saqqara.
- **[secondary source](days/d01.html#term-secondary-source)** — Day 1, History · How historians work. A source that interprets the past afterwards: a textbook, a modern biography, a documentary, a history written long after the events.
- **[stele](days/d03.html#term-stele)** — Day 3, History · Babylon: the laws of Hammurabi. An upright stone slab with a relief or an inscription, set up to be seen in public.
- **[synthesis](days/d10.html#term-synthesis)** — Day 10, History · Synthesis: law, citizenship and judgment. A reasoned putting-together of several sources around an idea, not a tour of one document after another.
- **[synthesis (this week)](days/d20.html#term-synthesis-essay)** — Day 20, History · Two-week synthesis: sources for kingship, from Khufu to the Ptolemies. A short argued piece that puts more than one source, from more than one period, under a single thesis, with sourcing sentences and a limit.
- **[treaty of year 21](days/d15.html#term-treaty-year-21)** — Day 15, History · The Egyptian–Hittite treaty of year 21 of Ramesses II. A peace treaty between Ramesses II of Egypt and Ḫattušili III of Ḫatti, dated on the Egyptian side to year 21 of Ramesses II.

## Literature

- **[aidōs](days/d07.html#term-aidos)** — Day 7, Literature · Iliad, Books 4–6. Shame before others; a sense of honor that answers to the community’s gaze.
- **[analytical essay](days/d09.html#term-analytical-essay)** — Day 9, Literature · Writing about the Iliad: the analytical essay. A short argued piece in which every paragraph begins with a claim, every quotation is introduced and explained, and the other side is acknowledged.
- **[aristeia](days/d07.html#term-aristeia)** — Day 7, Literature · Iliad, Books 4–6. A hero’s ‘best moment’: a stretch of the poem in which one warrior dominates the battlefield.
- **[catalogue](days/d04.html#term-catalogue)** — Day 4, Literature · Iliad, Books 2–3. A formal list built into a poem.
- **[ekphrasis](days/d17.html#term-ekphrasis)** — Day 17, Literature · Iliad, Books 17–22: the shield, the return, the death of Hector. A set-piece description of a work of art inside a poem.
- **[embassy (to Achilles)](days/d12.html#term-embassy)** — Day 12, Literature · Iliad, Books 7–9: the embassy to Achilles. The episode in Iliad Book 9 in which Odysseus, Ajax, and Phoenix go to Achilles’ camp with Agamemnon’s offers, and Achilles refuses to return.
- **[epithet](days/d02.html#term-epithet)** — Day 2, Literature · Homer, Iliad, Book 1. A fixed descriptive phrase attached to a name, used and reused in oral poetry (‘swift-footed Achilles’, ‘rosy-fingered Dawn’).
- **[in medias res](days/d02.html#term-in-medias-res)** — Day 2, Literature · Homer, Iliad, Book 1. ‘Into the middle of things’: the story begins not at the first event, but in the midst of an already ongoing action.
- **[invocation](days/d02.html#term-invocation)** — Day 2, Literature · Homer, Iliad, Book 1. The opening move of an epic in which the poet asks the Muse to sing, and names the subject of the poem.
- **[paradeigma (the Meleager story)](days/d12.html#term-meleager-paradeigma)** — Day 12, Literature · Iliad, Books 7–9: the embassy to Achilles. A story told inside another story as an example meant to persuade: Phoenix tells Achilles the tale of Meleager, who also withdrew in anger and was later begged to save his city, as a warning and a model.
- **[Patroclus in Achilles’ armor](days/d14.html#term-patroclus-in-armor)** — Day 14, Literature · Iliad, Books 11–16: Patroclus. The decision, in Book 16, that Patroclus will put on Achilles’ armor, lead the Myrmidons, and drive the Trojans from the ships, but go no further — a command Achilles gives and Patroclus does not keep.
- **[ransom of Hector](days/d19.html#term-ransom-of-hector)** — Day 19, Literature · Iliad, Books 23–24: the funeral, the ransom, a last written response. The return of Hector’s body to Priam, for burial, after the supplication and the giving of gifts, with a pause in the fighting agreed for the funeral.
- **[simile](days/d04.html#term-simile)** — Day 4, Literature · Iliad, Books 2–3. A comparison that names itself with ‘like’ or ‘as’, often extended over several lines in Homer, usually from nature or ordinary work.
- **[supplication (Priam)](days/d19.html#term-supplication)** — Day 19, Literature · Iliad, Books 23–24: the funeral, the ransom, a last written response. The scene in Book 24 in which Priam comes by night to Achilles’ shelter, kisses his hands, and asks for Hector’s body, appealing to Achilles’ own father, Peleus.
- **[the death of Hector](days/d17.html#term-hector-death)** — Day 17, Literature · Iliad, Books 17–22: the shield, the return, the death of Hector. The climax of Book 22: Achilles, now back in the war, chases Hector around the walls of Troy and kills him, then mistreats the body.
- **[thesis](days/d09.html#term-thesis)** — Day 9, Literature · Writing about the Iliad: the analytical essay. One sentence that can be argued against, stating the judgment the essay will defend.

## Writing & Rhetoric

- **[although-sentence](days/d09.html#term-although-sentence)** — Day 9, Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin's method, part 3: verse into prose. A sentence that concedes a real point on the other side, then states the judgment it can still defend.
- **[appositive](days/d03.html#term-appositive)** — Day 3, Writing & Rhetoric · Appositives and subordinating conjunctions. A noun phrase set beside another noun to rename it: ‘Leonardo, the draughtsman, …’.
- **[attribution](days/d18.html#term-attribution)** — Day 18, Writing & Rhetoric · Quotations and attribution. The naming of who said or wrote the words, and in what work or on what occasion, so that a reader can find the source.
- **[claim](days/d08.html#term-claim)** — Day 8, Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium II, logic: claim, evidence, warrant. A sentence that can be argued with, which the rest of the paragraph or essay must support.
- **[clause](days/d05.html#term-clause)** — Day 5, Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium I, grammar: the anatomy of a sentence. A group of words with a subject and a verb.
- **[comparison step](days/d17.html#term-comparison-step)** — Day 17, Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin's method: reconstruction from the hints. The last step of a reconstruction: laying your version beside the original and naming, in writing, two or three specific choices the author made that you did not — a verb, a delay, an apposition, a transition — not a vague judgment that the original is ‘better’.

- **[cast (sculpture)](days/d07.html#term-cast-sculpture)** — Day 7, Atelier · Cast drawing 1: set-up and block-in. A plaster (or similar) copy of a sculpture, used as a still, pale subject for training the eye in shape and value.
- **[cast shadow](days/d06.html#term-cast-shadow)** — Day 6, Atelier · Light on form: the egg. The shadow a form throws onto another surface when it blocks the light.
- **[contour](days/d03.html#term-contour)** — Day 3, Atelier · Bargue plate: refining the contour and mapping shadow. The edge of a shape as you see it from the viewing point: its angles, tilts, and changes of direction, drawn after the block-in is right.
- **[control of value](days/d14.html#term-value-control)** — Day 14, Atelier · Cross-hatching and control of value. The skill of making a patch as light or as dark as you intend, and of keeping a family even where the form is even.
- **[core shadow](days/d06.html#term-core-shadow)** — Day 6, Atelier · Light on form: the egg. The band of darker value on a form, where the surface turns away from the light and before the reflected light on the shadow side begins.
- **[critique](days/d10.html#term-critique)** — Day 10, Atelier · Critique and plan. A structured look at a finished or nearly finished drawing, naming what is true (shape, value, family) and what must be corrected, with a reason for each point.
- **[cross-hatching](days/d14.html#term-cross-hatching)** — Day 14, Atelier · Cross-hatching and control of value. A second (or later) layer of hatch laid across the first, at a new angle, to deepen value or to tighten form.
- **[edge quality](days/d18.html#term-edge-quality)** — Day 18, Atelier · Lost-and-found edges. How sudden or gradual a change of value is at a boundary: razor-sharp, firm, soft, or lost.
- **[ellipse](days/d12.html#term-ellipse)** — Day 12, Atelier · The cylinder: the ellipse. The shape a circle appears to take when it is foreshortened — the end of a cylinder, or a wheel seen at an angle.
- **[feature study](days/d15.html#term-feature-study)** — Day 15, Atelier · A second Bargue plate: the feature. A drawing of a single facial feature from a plate (here, a Bargue lithograph), treated as a constructed form with planes, not as a symbol of an eye or a mouth.
- **[foreshortening](days/d11.html#term-foreshortening)** — Day 11, Atelier · The cube: planes and foreshortening. The apparent shortening of a length or a shape that recedes from you.
- **[form](days/d05.html#term-form)** — Day 5, Atelier · Bargue plate: modeling and completion. The three-dimensional shape of the subject, as it is turned by light and as it must read on a flat page.
- **[halftone](days/d09.html#term-halftone)** — Day 9, Atelier · Cast drawing 3: halftones. The middle values on the lit side of a form, between the lights and the terminator, that show the surface turning toward shadow.
- **[hatching](days/d13.html#term-hatching)** — Day 13, Atelier · The sphere, and hatching that follows the form. A way of building value with rows of strokes, rather than with a smudge alone.
- **[highlight](days/d09.html#term-highlight)** — Day 9, Atelier · Cast drawing 3: halftones. The smallest, brightest spot where the light is most directly reflected to the eye; it sits inside the lights, not in the shadows.
- **[light and shadow families](days/d08.html#term-light-shadow-families)** — Day 8, Atelier · Cast drawing 2: separating light and shadow. The rule that values on the lit side stay in the light range, and values on the shadow side stay in the shadow range, so the two sides do not get mixed.
- **[lost-and-found edge](days/d18.html#term-lost-and-found-edge)** — Day 18, Atelier · Lost-and-found edges. A contour or an inner edge that is sharp in one passage (found) and soft or gone in another (lost), according to the light and the turn of the form.
- **[modeling](days/d05.html#term-modeling)** — Day 5, Atelier · Bargue plate: modeling and completion. Using value to turn a shape so that it reads as a form in light, not as a flat cut-out.
- **[nine-step scale](days/d04.html#term-nine-step-scale)** — Day 4, Atelier · Value: the nine-step scale. A row of nine even steps of gray from white to black, used so that values in a drawing can be chosen and compared.
- **[overlap](days/d16.html#term-overlap)** — Day 16, Atelier · Combining solids: a small still life. One form partly hiding another, which both creates a depth cue and forces you to measure where a contour stops and another begins.
- **[plan (for the next work)](days/d10.html#term-next-work-plan)** — Day 10, Atelier · Critique and plan. A short, practical list of what to keep and what to change in the next set-up or the next drawing, written after the critique.
- **[plan for the next sittings](days/d20.html#term-next-sittings-plan)** — Day 20, Atelier · Critique and a two-week plan. A short, written plan that names one or two skills to practice, the set-up that will make that practice possible, and how you will know the sittings have done their work.
- **[plane (of a form)](days/d11.html#term-plane-of-form)** — Day 11, Atelier · The cube: planes and foreshortening. A flat face of a form, or a flattened patch used to understand a curve.
- **[plaster feature](days/d17.html#term-plaster-feature)** — Day 17, Atelier · A plaster feature: from plane to a more particular form. A small white or off-white cast of a nose, ear, mouth, or eye, used so that color does not distract and the light can be read as value.
- **[plumb line](days/d01.html#term-plumb-line)** — Day 1, Atelier · Setting up, sight-size and line control. A weighted string, or a pencil held up as a vertical, used to check which points in the subject line up one above the other.
- **[point of focus](days/d19.html#term-focus)** — Day 19, Atelier · Accents and a point of focus. The place in the drawing you want the eye to arrive at first: usually the most particular passage of form, with a slightly stronger contrast or a slightly sharper found edge, while the surroundings are relatively quieter.
- **[proportion](days/d02.html#term-proportion)** — Day 2, Atelier · Bargue plate: the block-in. The relation of one length or shape to another, and to the whole, as seen from the viewing point.
- **[relative value](days/d16.html#term-relative-value)** — Day 16, Atelier · Combining solids: a small still life. How light or dark a passage is compared with its neighbors, not only compared with a scale in the margin.
- **[set-up](days/d07.html#term-set-up)** — Day 7, Atelier · Cast drawing 1: set-up and block-in. The fixed arrangement of viewing point, easel, subject, and light, which must not wander from session to session.
- **[sight-size](days/d01.html#term-sight-size)** — Day 1, Atelier · Setting up, sight-size and line control. A way of setting up so that, from a fixed viewing point, the drawing and the subject appear the same size, and lengths can be compared with a straight arm and a pencil.
- **[symbol versus form](days/d15.html#term-symbol-vs-form)** — Day 15, Atelier · A second Bargue plate: the feature. A symbol is the remembered sign for a thing (an eye as an almond, a mouth as a line).
- **[terminator](days/d08.html#term-terminator)** — Day 8, Atelier · Cast drawing 2: separating light and shadow. The line (often soft) where light on a form gives way to shadow: the boundary along which the surface turns away from the light.
- **[the sphere as a form](days/d13.html#term-sphere-as-form)** — Day 13, Atelier · The sphere, and hatching that follows the form. A solid whose surface is everywhere curved, with no plane edges.
- **[transition of planes](days/d17.html#term-transition-of-planes)** — Day 17, Atelier · A plaster feature: from plane to a more particular form. The way one plane of a form gives way to the next: as a sharp fold, as a soft turn, or as a series of small planes that together read as a curve.
- **[two-week critique](days/d20.html#term-two-week-critique)** — Day 20, Atelier · Critique and a two-week plan. A structured look at the fortnight’s work (cube, cylinder, sphere, Bargue feature, still life, plaster feature), naming what is constructed, what is still a symbol or a wire, and what the next two or three sittings should fix.
- **[value](days/d04.html#term-value)** — Day 4, Atelier · Value: the nine-step scale. How light or dark a surface is, considered separately from its color.

## Seminar (logic, music, art history, economics, electives)

- **[Aegean Bronze Age](days/d13.html#term-aegean-bronze-age)** — Day 13, Art history · The Aegean: Cycladic, Minoan, Mycenaean. The Bronze Age cultures of the Aegean Sea and its islands and shores, treated here in three parts: Cycladic (the Cyclades islands), Minoan (Crete), and Mycenaean (the mainland palatial centers, such as Mycenae).
- **[caesura and enjambment](days/d20.html#term-caesura-enjambment)** — Day 20, Elective · Poetry 2: caesura, enjambment, and the volta. A caesura is a pause inside a line of verse, often (but not only) in the middle, and often marked by punctuation.
- **[clavicle](days/d05.html#term-clavicle)** — Day 5, Elective · Artistic anatomy 1: the skeleton under the skin. The collarbone: a subcutaneous landmark running from the sternum to the point of the shoulder (near the acromion).
- **[clef](days/d02.html#term-clef)** — Day 2, Music theory · Pitch, staff and clefs. The sign at the start of a staff that fixes which line is a named pitch: the treble (G) clef or the bass (F) clef in this lesson.
- **[composite view](days/d08.html#term-composite-view)** — Day 8, Art history · Egypt: the art of permanence. The Egyptian convention of showing each part of a figure in its most characteristic aspect: head in profile, eye as if from the front, shoulders from the front, legs and feet in profile.
- **[compound interest](days/d09.html#term-compound-interest)** — Day 9, Economics & personal finance · Compound interest. Interest paid on the principal and on the interest already earned.
- **[conditional](days/d01.html#term-conditional)** — Day 1, Logic · Propositions and truth tables. The ‘if p then q’ connective.
- **[contrapositive](days/d01.html#term-contrapositive)** — Day 1, Logic · Propositions and truth tables. The statement formed by swapping and negating both sides of a conditional.
- **[contrapposto](days/d18.html#term-contrapposto)** — Day 18, Art history · The kouros and contrapposto. A standing pose in which the weight rests mainly on one leg, the other is relaxed, and the hips and shoulders tilt in opposite directions, so the spine forms a slight S-curve.
- **[De Morgan’s laws](days/d11.html#term-de-morgan)** — Day 11, Logic · De Morgan’s laws. Two equivalences that move a negation through ‘and’ and ‘or’, and switch the connective: not (P and Q) is equivalent to (not P) or (not Q); not (P or Q) is equivalent to (not P) and (not Q).
- **[demand and supply](days/d19.html#term-demand-supply)** — Day 19, Economics & personal finance · Supply and demand. Demand is the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity buyers are willing and able to buy, other things equal.
- **[equilibrium, shortage, and surplus](days/d19.html#term-equilibrium-price)** — Day 19, Economics & personal finance · Supply and demand. The equilibrium price is the price at which the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied.
- **[fallacy](days/d06.html#term-fallacy)** — Day 6, Logic · Validity, soundness and two classic fallacies. A tempting but invalid form of argument.
- **[foot (verse)](days/d10.html#term-verse-foot)** — Day 10, Elective · Poetry: meter and the iambic pentameter. The repeating unit of meter: iamb (da-DUM), trochee (DUM-da), anapest (da-da-DUM), dactyl (DUM-da-da), spondee (DUM-DUM).
- **[formal analysis](days/d03.html#term-formal-analysis)** — Day 3, Art history · How to look; the first images. A description of what is visibly there in a work — line, shape, mass, value, color, texture, space, composition — before any interpretation.
- **[hierarchical scale](days/d08.html#term-hierarchical-scale)** — Day 8, Art history · Egypt: the art of permanence. The convention of making the most important figure (a pharaoh or a god) larger than the others, not because they stand closer, but because of rank.
- **[iambic pentameter](days/d10.html#term-iambic-pentameter)** — Day 10, Elective · Poetry: meter and the iambic pentameter. A line of five iambs (five da-DUM feet), the most common meter of English verse from Chaucer onward, and the line of Shakespeare’s sonnets and blank verse.
- **[interpretation](days/d03.html#term-interpretation)** — Day 3, Art history · How to look; the first images. A claim about meaning or purpose, clearly marked as such, and paired with the evidence that would test it.
- **[interval](days/d12.html#term-interval)** — Day 12, Music theory · Intervals. The distance between two pitches, named by counting letter-names inclusively, then qualified as perfect, major, or minor (and, later, augmented or diminished).
- **[key signature](days/d07.html#term-key-signature)** — Day 7, Music theory · The major scale and key signatures. The sharps or flats placed at the start of the staff to keep a scale’s pattern, added in a fixed order (sharps: F C G D A E B; flats: B E A D G C F).
- **[kouros](days/d18.html#term-kouros)** — Day 18, Art history · The kouros and contrapposto. An Archaic Greek standing youth, typically in marble, usually nude, with the left foot forward, the weight still even on both legs, the arms at the sides, and often an ‘Archaic smile’.
- **[logical equivalence](days/d11.html#term-logical-equivalence)** — Day 11, Logic · De Morgan’s laws. Two statements that have the same truth value in every possible case.
- **[major and minor triad](days/d17.html#term-major-minor-triad)** — Day 17, Music theory · The triad. A major triad has a major third and a perfect fifth above the root (the upper interval, from the third to the fifth, is then a minor third).
- **[major scale](days/d07.html#term-major-scale)** — Day 7, Music theory · The major scale and key signatures. A scale whose steps follow the pattern whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half (W W H W W W H).
- **[market](days/d14.html#term-market)** — Day 14, Economics & personal finance · Markets and prices. A meeting of buyers and sellers of a particular good or service, in which they can trade.
- **[meter (verse)](days/d10.html#term-verse-meter)** — Day 10, Elective · Poetry: meter and the iambic pentameter. The pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in a line of verse.
- **[necessary condition](days/d16.html#term-necessary-condition)** — Day 16, Logic · Necessary and sufficient conditions. A condition that must hold if the result holds: if P is necessary for Q, then Q → P (equivalently, ¬P → ¬Q).
- **[opportunity cost](days/d04.html#term-opportunity-cost)** — Day 4, Economics & personal finance · Scarcity and opportunity cost. The value of the single best alternative given up when a choice is made, not the sum of every alternative.
- **[origin and insertion](days/d15.html#term-origin-insertion)** — Day 15, Elective · Artistic anatomy 2: muscles and tendons. The two attachments of a muscle.
- **[perfect, major, and minor](days/d12.html#term-perfect-and-major)** — Day 12, Music theory · Intervals. Qualities of intervals.
- **[pitch](days/d02.html#term-pitch)** — Day 2, Music theory · Pitch, staff and clefs. How high or low a note sounds: the perceived frequency of the sound.
- **[price (as a signal)](days/d14.html#term-price-signal)** — Day 14, Economics & personal finance · Markets and prices. The amount of money (or other payment) asked or agreed for a good or service, which also serves as a signal: a high price discourages some buying and encourages some selling and production; a low price does the reverse, other things equal.
- **[principal](days/d09.html#term-principal)** — Day 9, Economics & personal finance · Compound interest. The original amount of money borrowed or invested, before interest.
- **[proposition](days/d01.html#term-proposition)** — Day 1, Logic · Propositions and truth tables. A sentence that is either true or false (not a command, not a question).
- **[rule of 72](days/d09.html#term-rule-of-72)** — Day 9, Economics & personal finance · Compound interest. A short estimate: money at r percent a year doubles in about 72/r years, a good approximation for rates between about 2% and 15%.
- **[scarcity](days/d04.html#term-scarcity)** — Day 4, Economics & personal finance · Scarcity and opportunity cost. The fact that resources (time, money, materials, attention) are limited relative to what we want, so every choice gives something up.
- **[soundness](days/d06.html#term-soundness)** — Day 6, Logic · Validity, soundness and two classic fallacies. The property of an argument that is valid and whose premises are actually true.
- **[staff](days/d02.html#term-staff)** — Day 2, Music theory · Pitch, staff and clefs. The five lines and four spaces on which notes are written.
- **[subcutaneous landmark](days/d05.html#term-subcutaneous-landmark)** — Day 5, Elective · Artistic anatomy 1: the skeleton under the skin. A place where bone lies just under the skin and does not change shape with fat or muscle, so it is a reliable measuring point in figure drawing.
- **[sufficient condition](days/d16.html#term-sufficient-condition)** — Day 16, Logic · Necessary and sufficient conditions. A condition that is enough to guarantee the result: if P is sufficient for Q, then P → Q.
- **[sunk cost](days/d04.html#term-sunk-cost)** — Day 4, Economics & personal finance · Scarcity and opportunity cost. A cost already paid that cannot be recovered, and that should not affect a forward-looking decision.
- **[tendon](days/d15.html#term-tendon)** — Day 15, Elective · Artistic anatomy 2: muscles and tendons. The tough, cord-like or flattened connective tissue that often joins a muscle to a bone.
- **[the name ‘Minoan’](days/d13.html#term-minoan-name)** — Day 13, Art history · The Aegean: Cycladic, Minoan, Mycenaean. A modern name, coined by Arthur Evans in the early twentieth century from Minos, the mythical king of Crete, for the Bronze Age culture he was excavating at Knossos.
- **[triad](days/d17.html#term-triad)** — Day 17, Music theory · The triad. A three-note chord built from a root, a third above the root, and a fifth above the root.
- **[validity](days/d06.html#term-validity)** — Day 6, Logic · Validity, soundness and two classic fallacies. A property of an argument’s form: the conclusion must be true whenever the premises are true.
- **[volta](days/d20.html#term-volta)** — Day 20, Elective · Poetry 2: caesura, enjambment, and the volta. The turn in a sonnet: the place where the argument or the figure shifts.
