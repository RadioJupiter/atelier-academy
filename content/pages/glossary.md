# Glossary

Terms taught in weeks 1–2, listed by subject. Each entry links to the lesson that teaches it, where the Frayer card (definition, origin when it is known, example, near-miss, and a connection) is given. Use this page to look a word up; use the week-end vocabulary reviews to retrieve it from memory.

Pronunciations are on the lesson page (Hear, in English). Spanish and Latin *words* still have their own Hear-word buttons in those lessons.

## Mathematics

- **[AM–GM inequality](days/d07.html#term-am-gm)** — Day 7, Mathematics · Algebra III: inequalities and the mean. For a, b ≥ 0, (a + b)/2 ≥ √(ab), with equality only when a = b. It is a tool for maxima and minima.
- **[axiom](days/d02.html#term-axiom)** — Day 2, Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs. A starting statement accepted without proof, so that theorems can be proved from it.
- **[bar model](days/d01.html#term-bar-model)** — Day 1, Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation. A drawing of quantities as bars of equal units, so the relationship is visible before any equation is written.
- **[completing the square](days/d05.html#term-completing-the-square)** — Day 5, Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations. Rewriting x² + px as a square plus or minus a constant, so a quadratic can be solved or a parabola’s vertex read off.
- **[congruence](days/d04.html#term-congruence)** — Day 4, Mathematics · Geometry II: congruent triangles and the isosceles triangle. A matching of two figures so that corresponding sides and corresponding angles are equal; one can be placed exactly on the other.
- **[discriminant](days/d05.html#term-discriminant)** — Day 5, Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations. The quantity that decides how many real roots a quadratic has: two if D > 0, one if D = 0, none if D < 0.
- **[exterior angle](days/d06.html#term-exterior-angle)** — Day 6, Mathematics · Geometry III: parallel lines and the angle sum. An angle formed by one side of a triangle and the extension of an adjacent side. It equals the sum of the two remote interior angles.
- **[factor](days/d03.html#term-factor)** — Day 3, Mathematics · Algebra I: identities and factoring. To write an expression as a product; or one of those pieces. Identities used backwards are the usual method.
- **[function](days/d09.html#term-function)** — Day 9, Mathematics · Functions and their graphs. A rule that assigns to each allowed input exactly one output. The graph is the set of points (x, f(x)).
- **[heuristic](days/d10.html#term-heuristic)** — Day 10, Mathematics · Two-week problem set. A deliberate method of attack, not a guess: for example, draw a bar, try a smaller case, or write a short proof plan before computing.
- **[identity](days/d03.html#term-identity)** — Day 3, Mathematics · Algebra I: identities and factoring. An equality that is true for every allowed value of its letters, and that must be proved by expanding or by using a known identity.
- **[inequality](days/d07.html#term-inequality)** — Day 7, Mathematics · Algebra III: inequalities and the mean. A statement that one quantity is greater or less than another. Adding the same number, or multiplying by a positive number, preserves the inequality; multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the sign.
- **[isosceles](days/d04.html#term-isosceles)** — Day 4, Mathematics · Geometry II: congruent triangles and the isosceles triangle. A triangle with two sides equal. The angles at the base (opposite those sides) are equal.
- **[locus](days/d08.html#term-locus)** — Day 8, Mathematics · Geometry IV: inequalities in triangles. The set of all points that satisfy a given condition. A locus proof has two directions: every point on the figure has the property, and every point with the property lies on the figure.
- **[ratio](days/d01.html#term-ratio)** — Day 1, Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation. A comparison of two quantities by units of the same size, written a : b. One quantity in a ratio can stay unchanged while the other changes.
- **[remainder](days/d01.html#term-remainder)** — Day 1, Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation. What is left of a quantity after a part of it has been taken. In a fraction-of-a-remainder problem, the next fraction is of this leftover amount, not of the original whole.
- **[theorem](days/d02.html#term-theorem)** — Day 2, Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs. A statement that is proved from definitions, axioms, and earlier theorems.
- **[transversal](days/d06.html#term-transversal)** — Day 6, Mathematics · Geometry III: parallel lines and the angle sum. A line that crosses two or more other lines, forming corresponding, alternate interior, and same-side interior angles.
- **[triangle inequality](days/d08.html#term-triangle-inequality)** — Day 8, Mathematics · Geometry IV: inequalities in triangles. In a triangle, each side is less than the sum of the other two (and greater than their difference). If this fails, the three lengths do not form a triangle.
- **[unit (in a bar or ratio)](days/d10.html#term-unit-bar)** — Day 10, Mathematics · Two-week problem set. One equal part in a bar model or a ratio. A stated change (an amount spent, a number added) is often a whole number of units, which is how the unit is found.
- **[vertex](days/d09.html#term-vertex)** — Day 9, Mathematics · Functions and their graphs. The turning point of a parabola. For y = (x − h)² + k, the vertex is (h, k).
- **[vertical angles](days/d02.html#term-vertical-angles)** — Day 2, Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs. A pair of angles whose sides are the extensions of each other’s sides: the opposite angles formed when two lines meet.
- **[Vieta's formulas](days/d05.html#term-vietas-formulas)** — Day 5, Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations. For the roots r and s of x² + px + q = 0, the sum of the roots is −p and the product is q. (Named after François Viète.)

## Physics

- **[acceleration](days/d03.html#term-acceleration)** — Day 3, Physics · Acceleration and velocity–time graphs. The rate of change of velocity: how quickly velocity increases, decreases, or changes direction.
- **[action–reaction pair](days/d10.html#term-action-reaction-pair)** — Day 10, Physics · Newton's third law and the two-week check. The two forces named by the third law: they are equal, opposite, simultaneous, of the same type, and they act on two different bodies.
- **[displacement](days/d01.html#term-displacement)** — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. The change in position: final minus initial. It has a size and a direction, and may be positive, negative, or zero.
- **[free fall](days/d05.html#term-free-fall)** — Day 5, Physics · Free fall. Motion under gravity alone, with air resistance neglected. Near Earth, the acceleration has size g and is directed downward, the same for all objects.
- **[gravity](days/d05.html#term-gravity)** — Day 5, Physics · Free fall. The attraction that gives a free-falling object its downward acceleration g near Earth. In these lessons g is taken as about 9.8 m/s², downward.
- **[inertia](days/d06.html#term-inertia)** — Day 6, Physics · Newton's first law: inertia. The tendency of a body to keep its velocity — to stay at rest, or to keep moving in a straight line at constant speed — unless a net force acts on it. This is Newton’s first law.
- **[mass](days/d08.html#term-mass)** — Day 8, Physics · Newton's second law. A measure of how much a body resists acceleration (inertial mass). In SI it is measured in kilograms.
- **[net force](days/d06.html#term-net-force)** — Day 6, Physics · Newton's first law: inertia. The vector sum of all forces on a body. Newton’s first law says that if the net force is zero, the velocity does not change.
- **[Newton's second law](days/d08.html#term-newtons-second-law)** — Day 8, Physics · Newton's second law. The net force on a body equals its mass times its acceleration. Acceleration is in the direction of the net force.
- **[Newton's third law](days/d10.html#term-newtons-third-law)** — Day 10, Physics · Newton's third law and the two-week check. If body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts a force on A that is equal in size, opposite in direction, and of the same type. The two forces act on different bodies.
- **[position](days/d01.html#term-position)** — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. Where something is, measured from a chosen origin along a line, with a sign for direction.
- **[velocity](days/d01.html#term-velocity)** — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. Displacement divided by the time taken (average velocity). It has a direction. Instantaneous velocity is the slope of a position–time graph at a point.
- **[velocity–time graph](days/d03.html#term-velocity-time-graph)** — Day 3, Physics · Acceleration and velocity–time graphs. A graph with time on the horizontal axis and velocity on the vertical axis. The slope is acceleration; the area between the graph and the time axis is displacement.

## Science

- **[combustion](days/d04.html#term-combustion)** — Day 4, Science · Combustion and gases: the quantitative model. The chemical burning of a fuel with oxygen, producing new gases (and often water) and releasing heat.
- **[frequency](days/d09.html#term-frequency)** — Day 9, Science · Light: polarization and the quantitative model. The number of wave cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz). For light in vacuum, frequency times wavelength equals the speed of light c.
- **[model (scientific)](days/d02.html#term-scientific-model)** — Day 2, Science · Phenomenon: the candle under the jar. A reasoned picture of what is happening that makes a testable prediction. If the evidence disagrees, the model must change.
- **[phenomenon](days/d02.html#term-phenomenon)** — Day 2, Science · Phenomenon: the candle under the jar. A real event or appearance that you can observe, and that you then try to explain with a model and a test.
- **[polarization](days/d09.html#term-polarization)** — Day 9, Science · Light: polarization and the quantitative model. The preferring of one direction of vibration in a light wave. Scattered sunlight is partly polarized, most strongly about 90° from the Sun.
- **[quantitative](days/d04.html#term-quantitative)** — Day 4, Science · Combustion and gases: the quantitative model. Described with measured numbers and a relation, not only with words such as ‘more’ or ‘less’.
- **[Rayleigh scattering](days/d07.html#term-rayleigh-scattering)** — Day 7, Science · Phenomenon: why is the sky blue and the sunset red?. The scattering of light by particles much smaller than the wavelength. Short wavelengths are scattered much more strongly than long ones (the 1/λ⁴ law). Named after John William Strutt, Lord Rayleigh.
- **[wavelength](days/d07.html#term-wavelength)** — Day 7, Science · Phenomenon: why is the sky blue and the sunset red?. The distance from one crest of a wave to the next. Visible light runs from about 400 nm (violet) to about 700 nm (red).

## History

- **[apology](days/d08.html#term-apology)** — Day 8, History · The trial of Socrates. A defense speech. Plato’s Apology is not an admission of fault; the Greek apologia means a defense.
- **[citizenship](days/d06.html#term-citizenship)** — Day 6, History · Athens: Pericles' Funeral Oration. Membership in the political community, with its rights and duties. In classical Athens it was a restricted legal status, not a synonym for ‘everyone who lived in the city’.
- **[corroboration](days/d01.html#term-corroboration)** — Day 1, History · How historians work. Checking whether an independent source agrees, in part or in whole, with the claim you are testing.
- **[democracy](days/d06.html#term-democracy)** — Day 6, History · Athens: Pericles' Funeral Oration. In Pericles’ usage (as Thucydides reports it), a constitution whose administration ‘favors the many instead of the few’. In Athens this meant many citizens, not all adults.
- **[gadfly](days/d08.html#term-gadfly)** — Day 8, History · The trial of Socrates. A biting fly; in Socrates’ metaphor, the stinging critic who keeps a large, sluggish horse — the city — awake.
- **[historiē](days/d05.html#term-historie)** — Day 5, History · Greece: Herodotus and the idea of "inquiry". The Greek word (ἱστορίη) meaning inquiry, investigation. It is the source of the English word history.
- **[inquiry](days/d05.html#term-inquiry)** — Day 5, History · Greece: Herodotus and the idea of "inquiry". A systematic asking of questions in order to find out and to explain, not only to list events. Herodotus’s word historiē means inquiry.
- **[judgment](days/d10.html#term-judgment)** — Day 10, History · Synthesis: law, citizenship and judgment. The act of deciding a case, or the standard by which people are judged. In this course the word also means your own reasoned decision about a source.
- **[lex talionis](days/d03.html#term-lex-talionis)** — Day 3, History · Babylon: the laws of Hammurabi. The rule of retaliation in kind: the penalty matches the injury, ‘an eye for an eye’. In Hammurabi’s code it applies fully only to a victim of full status.
- **[primary source](days/d01.html#term-primary-source)** — Day 1, History · How historians work. A source produced at the time by someone involved or present: a law, a letter, a coin, an inscription, a painting, a speech written down in the period.
- **[secondary source](days/d01.html#term-secondary-source)** — Day 1, History · How historians work. A source that interprets the past afterwards: a textbook, a modern biography, a documentary, a history written long after the events.
- **[stele](days/d03.html#term-stele)** — Day 3, History · Babylon: the laws of Hammurabi. An upright stone slab with a relief or an inscription, set up to be seen in public.
- **[synthesis](days/d10.html#term-synthesis)** — Day 10, History · Synthesis: law, citizenship and judgment. A reasoned putting-together of several sources around an idea, not a tour of one document after another.

## Literature

- **[aidōs](days/d07.html#term-aidos)** — Day 7, Literature · Iliad, Books 4–6. Shame before others; a sense of honor that answers to the community’s gaze. Hector names it when he refuses to stay on the wall.
- **[analytical essay](days/d09.html#term-analytical-essay)** — Day 9, Literature · Writing about the Iliad: the analytical essay. A short argued piece in which every paragraph begins with a claim, every quotation is introduced and explained, and the other side is acknowledged.
- **[aristeia](days/d07.html#term-aristeia)** — Day 7, Literature · Iliad, Books 4–6. A hero’s ‘best moment’: a stretch of the poem in which one warrior dominates the battlefield.
- **[catalogue](days/d04.html#term-catalogue)** — Day 4, Literature · Iliad, Books 2–3. A formal list built into a poem. The Catalogue of Ships in Iliad Book 2 is a roll-call of the Greek contingents.
- **[epithet](days/d02.html#term-epithet)** — Day 2, Literature · Homer, Iliad, Book 1. A fixed descriptive phrase attached to a name, used and reused in oral poetry (‘swift-footed Achilles’, ‘rosy-fingered Dawn’).
- **[in medias res](days/d02.html#term-in-medias-res)** — Day 2, Literature · Homer, Iliad, Book 1. ‘Into the middle of things’: the story begins not at the first event, but in the midst of an already ongoing action.
- **[invocation](days/d02.html#term-invocation)** — Day 2, Literature · Homer, Iliad, Book 1. The opening move of an epic in which the poet asks the Muse to sing, and names the subject of the poem.
- **[simile](days/d04.html#term-simile)** — Day 4, Literature · Iliad, Books 2–3. A comparison that names itself with ‘like’ or ‘as’, often extended over several lines in Homer, usually from nature or ordinary work.
- **[thesis](days/d09.html#term-thesis)** — Day 9, Literature · Writing about the Iliad: the analytical essay. One sentence that can be argued against, stating the judgment the essay will defend.

## Writing & Rhetoric

- **[although-sentence](days/d09.html#term-although-sentence)** — Day 9, Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin's method, part 3: verse into prose. A sentence that concedes a real point on the other side, then states the judgment it can still defend.
- **[appositive](days/d03.html#term-appositive)** — Day 3, Writing & Rhetoric · Appositives and subordinating conjunctions. A noun phrase set beside another noun to rename it: ‘Leonardo, the draughtsman, …’.
- **[claim](days/d08.html#term-claim)** — Day 8, Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium II, logic: claim, evidence, warrant. A sentence that can be argued with, which the rest of the paragraph or essay must support.
- **[clause](days/d05.html#term-clause)** — Day 5, Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium I, grammar: the anatomy of a sentence. A group of words with a subject and a verb. A main clause can stand as a sentence; a subordinate clause cannot.
- **[copia](days/d02.html#term-copia)** — Day 2, Writing & Rhetoric · Imitatio: copywork and copia. Plenty of expression: many accurate ways of saying the same idea, produced on purpose.
- **[diction](days/d07.html#term-diction)** — Day 7, Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin's method, part 2: reconstruction. The choice of words: precise, ordinary, or borrowed from the model, each chosen on purpose.
- **[ethos](days/d10.html#term-ethos)** — Day 10, Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium III, rhetoric: the three appeals. The character the speaker or writer seems to have: credible, careful, fair. One of the three appeals in classical rhetoric.
- **[evidence](days/d08.html#term-evidence)** — Day 8, Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium II, logic: claim, evidence, warrant. The material that is supposed to support a claim: a quotation, a fact, a detail from a source, given accurately.
- **[expansion](days/d01.html#term-expansion)** — Day 1, Writing & Rhetoric · The sentence: kernels and expansion. The deliberate adding of information to a kernel by answering a short list of questions (when, where, why, how), without losing the original verb.
- **[Franklin's method](days/d04.html#term-franklins-method)** — Day 4, Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin's method, part 1: making hints. A practice from Benjamin Franklin’s Autobiography: take a passage, jot brief hints of the ideas in order, put the passage away, and rewrite from the hints; then compare with the original.
- **[grammar](days/d05.html#term-grammar)** — Day 5, Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium I, grammar: the anatomy of a sentence. In the trivium, the craft of accurate sentences: parts of speech, agreement, and punctuation that make the structure visible.
- **[hint (in Franklin's method)](days/d04.html#term-franklin-hint)** — Day 4, Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin's method, part 1: making hints. A brief cue for an idea, written in order, just strong enough to recall the thought and not the original wording.
- **[imitatio](days/d02.html#term-imitatio)** — Day 2, Writing & Rhetoric · Imitatio: copywork and copia. The Renaissance school practice of first copying an excellent passage exactly, noticing how it is built, then writing a new version that keeps the structure and changes the content.
- **[kernel](days/d01.html#term-kernel)** — Day 1, Writing & Rhetoric · The sentence: kernels and expansion. A short, complete sentence that can later be expanded: a subject and a verb, with nothing added yet.
- **[logos](days/d10.html#term-logos)** — Day 10, Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium III, rhetoric: the three appeals. The appeal to reason: the structure of claim, evidence, and warrant, and the consistency of the argument.
- **[pathos](days/d10.html#term-pathos)** — Day 10, Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium III, rhetoric: the three appeals. The appeal to feeling: the reader’s pity, anger, or loyalty, used on purpose and still tied to a claim.
- **[revision](days/d07.html#term-revision)** — Day 7, Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin's method, part 2: reconstruction. A second (or third) pass that changes structure, order, or wording after a draft exists, using the original as a standard of comparison.
- **[single-paragraph outline](days/d06.html#term-single-paragraph-outline)** — Day 6, Writing & Rhetoric · The single-paragraph outline. A plan for one paragraph: a topic sentence (a claim), then a short list of the points or evidence in order, written before the draft.
- **[subordinate clause](days/d03.html#term-subordinate-clause)** — Day 3, Writing & Rhetoric · Appositives and subordinating conjunctions. A clause that cannot stand alone as a sentence; it starts with a word such as because, although, when, who, or that, and it depends on a main clause.
- **[topic sentence](days/d06.html#term-topic-sentence)** — Day 6, Writing & Rhetoric · The single-paragraph outline. The claim that opens a paragraph and that the rest of the paragraph must support.
- **[variety](days/d09.html#term-variety)** — Day 9, Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin's method, part 3: verse into prose. Deliberate change in sentence length and opening, so that a paragraph is not a string of the same pattern.
- **[warrant](days/d08.html#term-warrant)** — Day 8, Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium II, logic: claim, evidence, warrant. The reason that the evidence counts as support for the claim: the general rule or principle that joins the two.

## Latin

- **[accusative](days/d06.html#term-accusative)** — Day 6, Latin · Capitulum III, part 1: the accusative. The case that often marks the direct object: the person or thing the verb acts on.
- **[agreement](days/d02.html#term-agreement)** — Day 2, Latin · Capitulum I, part 2. The rule that an adjective takes the same gender and number (and, later, the same case) as the noun it describes.
- **[case](days/d08.html#term-case)** — Day 8, Latin · Capitulum III, part 3 and review. The role a noun or adjective is playing in the sentence, shown by its ending: subject, possessor, object, and others still to come.
- **[conjugation](days/d10.html#term-conjugation)** — Day 10, Latin · Capitulum IV, part 2 and two-week check. The pattern of endings a verb follows as the person, number, and (later) tense change.
- **[cuius](days/d05.html#term-cuius)** — Day 5, Latin · Capitulum II, part 2: cuius? and the genitive in use. The question word ‘whose?’, asking for a possessor. The adjective that answers (meus, mea, meum) agrees with the thing owned, not with the owner.
- **[declension](days/d10.html#term-declension)** — Day 10, Latin · Capitulum IV, part 2 and two-week check. The pattern of endings a noun (and its adjectives) follows as the case and number change.
- **[direct object](days/d06.html#term-direct-object)** — Day 6, Latin · Capitulum III, part 1: the accusative. The person or thing that receives the action of the verb. In Latin it is typically in the accusative.
- **[est and sunt](days/d01.html#term-est-and-sunt)** — Day 1, Latin · Capitulum I, part 1. The Latin verbs meaning ‘is’ (est) and ‘are’ (sunt). They must agree with the number of the subject.
- **[gender (Latin)](days/d02.html#term-latin-gender)** — Day 2, Latin · Capitulum I, part 2. The class of a Latin noun — masculine, feminine, or neuter — which adjectives and some pronouns must follow. You cannot always guess it from the meaning.
- **[genitive](days/d04.html#term-genitive)** — Day 4, Latin · Capitulum II, part 1: gender and the genitive. The case that often marks a possessor or a ‘of’ relation: whose thing it is.
- **[imperative](days/d09.html#term-imperative)** — Day 9, Latin · Capitulum IV, part 1: calling and commanding. The verb form used to give a command: ‘do this’ or ‘do not do this’.
- **[natural method](days/d01.html#term-natural-method)** — Day 1, Latin · Capitulum I, part 1. Learning Latin by reading Latin that is made understandable through pictures, context, and a few notes, rather than by starting from an English grammar list alone.
- **[neuter plural](days/d03.html#term-neuter-plural)** — Day 3, Latin · Capitulum I, part 3 and review. The plural of a neuter noun (often ending in -a, like oppida). It takes a neuter plural adjective and the verb sunt.
- **[nominative](days/d08.html#term-nominative)** — Day 8, Latin · Capitulum III, part 3 and review. The case of the subject: the form of the noun (and of its adjective) that you have used from the first chapter for who or what the sentence is about.
- **[possessive adjective](days/d05.html#term-possessive-adjective)** — Day 5, Latin · Capitulum II, part 2: cuius? and the genitive in use. An adjective such as meus, mea, meum (‘my’) that shows ownership and agrees with the noun it describes.
- **[pronoun](days/d07.html#term-pronoun)** — Day 7, Latin · Capitulum III, part 2: verbs and pronouns. A short word that stands in for a noun (I, you, he, she, it, we, they), often already marked by the verb ending in Latin.
- **[quis and quid](days/d04.html#term-quis-and-quid)** — Day 4, Latin · Capitulum II, part 1: gender and the genitive. Question words: quis, ‘who?’, for a person; quid, ‘what?’, for a thing.
- **[review (of a chapter)](days/d03.html#term-chapter-review)** — Day 3, Latin · Capitulum I, part 3 and review. A check that you can still read a sentence, hear est and sunt, and make an adjective agree, before the next chapter adds case.
- **[verb ending](days/d07.html#term-verb-ending)** — Day 7, Latin · Capitulum III, part 2: verbs and pronouns. The last part of a Latin verb, which shows who is acting (I, you, he or she, we, you plural, they) and, later, tense.
- **[vocative](days/d09.html#term-vocative)** — Day 9, Latin · Capitulum IV, part 1: calling and commanding. The case used for calling or addressing someone: the form you use when you speak to a person, not about them.

## Spanish

- **[-ar verb](days/d05.html#term-ar-verb)** — Day 5, Modern language · Spanish: present tense of -ar verbs. A Spanish verb whose infinitive ends in -ar. In the present tense it follows a regular pattern of endings once the -ar is removed.
- **[-er and -ir verbs](days/d08.html#term-er-ir-verbs)** — Day 8, Modern language · Spanish: -er and -ir verbs. The two other regular infinitive classes. In the present they share many endings; a few forms differ (nosotros and vosotros).
- **[article](days/d02.html#term-article)** — Day 2, Modern language · Spanish: nouns, gender and articles. The small word placed before a noun (el, la; later un, una) that must agree with the noun’s gender and number.
- **[composition (Spanish)](days/d10.html#term-spanish-composition)** — Day 10, Modern language · Spanish: review and composition. A short piece of connected Spanish, written from words and patterns already taught, not from a translator.
- **[contrast of ser and estar](days/d10.html#term-ser-estar-contrast)** — Day 10, Modern language · Spanish: review and composition. The choice, in every ‘to be’ sentence, between what something is (ser) and where or how it is for now (estar).
- **[estar](days/d04.html#term-estar)** — Day 4, Modern language · Spanish: numbers 0–30 and estar. The Spanish verb used for place and for a temporary condition, not for identity or a lasting characteristic.
- **[gender (Spanish)](days/d02.html#term-spanish-gender)** — Day 2, Modern language · Spanish: nouns, gender and articles. The class of a Spanish noun, shown by the article: el with many masculine nouns, la with many feminine nouns. The article must match.
- **[greeting](days/d01.html#term-greeting)** — Day 1, Modern language · Spanish: sounds and greetings. A set phrase used to open a meeting or to be polite, learned here as a complete chunk (hola, buenos días, gracias, por favor).
- **[infinitive](days/d05.html#term-infinitive)** — Day 5, Modern language · Spanish: present tense of -ar verbs. The dictionary form of the verb (in Spanish, the form ending in -ar, -er, or -ir), naming the action without a subject.
- **[ir](days/d09.html#term-ir)** — Day 9, Modern language · Spanish: ir and the near future. The Spanish verb ‘to go’, irregular in the present: voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van.
- **[irregular present](days/d07.html#term-irregular-present)** — Day 7, Modern language · Spanish: tener. A present-tense pattern that does not simply drop -ar, -er, or -ir and add the regular endings; the stem or the ending (or both) must be learned as they are.
- **[near future](days/d09.html#term-near-future)** — Day 9, Modern language · Spanish: ir and the near future. A present-tense way of talking about a planned or soon-to-happen action: ir + a + infinitive.
- **[number words (0–30)](days/d04.html#term-spanish-numbers)** — Day 4, Modern language · Spanish: numbers 0–30 and estar. The Spanish words for the numbers 0 to 30, learned as language (how the word is spelled and said), not as a mathematics drill.
- **[present tense of ser](days/d03.html#term-ser-present)** — Day 3, Modern language · Spanish: ser and description. The forms of ser used for now: soy, eres, es, somos, sois, son. Each form belongs to a subject.
- **[question (Spanish)](days/d06.html#term-spanish-question)** — Day 6, Modern language · Spanish: questions. A sentence that asks, marked in writing by ¿ at the start and ? at the end, and often begun with a question word such as qué, dónde, or cómo.
- **[question word](days/d06.html#term-question-word)** — Day 6, Modern language · Spanish: questions. A word such as qué (what), dónde (where), or cómo (how) that opens many questions and is written with an accent in these forms.
- **[ser](days/d03.html#term-ser)** — Day 3, Modern language · Spanish: ser and description. The Spanish verb used for what something is: identity, origin, characteristics, and time. (It will be contrasted with estar later.)
- **[stem](days/d08.html#term-stem)** — Day 8, Modern language · Spanish: -er and -ir verbs. The part of the verb that remains when the infinitive ending -ar, -er, or -ir is removed, to which the present endings are added.
- **[tener](days/d07.html#term-tener)** — Day 7, Modern language · Spanish: tener. The Spanish verb ‘to have’, irregular in the present: tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tenéis, tienen.
- **[vowel (Spanish)](days/d01.html#term-spanish-vowel)** — Day 1, Modern language · Spanish: sounds and greetings. Spanish has five pure vowel sounds — a, e, i, o, u — that do not glide the way many English vowels do.

## Atelier

- **[angle (in a drawing)](days/d03.html#term-drawing-angle)** — Day 3, Atelier · Bargue plate: refining the contour and mapping shadow. The tilt of a line or an edge, compared with a horizontal, a vertical, or a neighboring edge.
- **[block-in](days/d02.html#term-block-in)** — Day 2, Atelier · Bargue plate: the block-in. The first stage of a Bargue copy: the largest shapes, the big angles, and the main proportions, all in light line, with no features yet.
- **[cast (sculpture)](days/d07.html#term-cast-sculpture)** — Day 7, Atelier · Cast drawing 1: set-up and block-in. A plaster (or similar) copy of a sculpture, used as a still, pale subject for training the eye in shape and value.
- **[cast shadow](days/d06.html#term-cast-shadow)** — Day 6, Atelier · Light on form: the egg. The shadow a form throws onto another surface when it blocks the light.
- **[contour](days/d03.html#term-contour)** — Day 3, Atelier · Bargue plate: refining the contour and mapping shadow. The edge of a shape as you see it from the viewing point: its angles, tilts, and changes of direction, drawn after the block-in is right.
- **[core shadow](days/d06.html#term-core-shadow)** — Day 6, Atelier · Light on form: the egg. The band of darker value on a form, where the surface turns away from the light and before the reflected light on the shadow side begins.
- **[critique](days/d10.html#term-critique)** — Day 10, Atelier · Critique and plan. A structured look at a finished or nearly finished drawing, naming what is true (shape, value, family) and what must be corrected, with a reason for each point.
- **[form](days/d05.html#term-form)** — Day 5, Atelier · Bargue plate: modeling and completion. The three-dimensional shape of the subject, as it is turned by light and as it must read on a flat page.
- **[halftone](days/d09.html#term-halftone)** — Day 9, Atelier · Cast drawing 3: halftones. The middle values on the lit side of a form, between the lights and the terminator, that show the surface turning toward shadow.
- **[highlight](days/d09.html#term-highlight)** — Day 9, Atelier · Cast drawing 3: halftones. The smallest, brightest spot where the light is most directly reflected to the eye; it sits inside the lights, not in the shadows.
- **[light and shadow families](days/d08.html#term-light-shadow-families)** — Day 8, Atelier · Cast drawing 2: separating light and shadow. The rule that values on the lit side stay in the light range, and values on the shadow side stay in the shadow range, so the two sides do not get mixed.
- **[modeling](days/d05.html#term-modeling)** — Day 5, Atelier · Bargue plate: modeling and completion. Using value to turn a shape so that it reads as a form in light, not as a flat cut-out.
- **[nine-step scale](days/d04.html#term-nine-step-scale)** — Day 4, Atelier · Value: the nine-step scale. A row of nine even steps of gray from white to black, used so that values in a drawing can be chosen and compared.
- **[plan (for the next work)](days/d10.html#term-next-work-plan)** — Day 10, Atelier · Critique and plan. A short, practical list of what to keep and what to change in the next set-up or the next drawing, written after the critique.
- **[plumb line](days/d01.html#term-plumb-line)** — Day 1, Atelier · Setting up, sight-size and line control. A weighted string, or a pencil held up as a vertical, used to check which points in the subject line up one above the other.
- **[proportion](days/d02.html#term-proportion)** — Day 2, Atelier · Bargue plate: the block-in. The relation of one length or shape to another, and to the whole, as seen from the viewing point.
- **[set-up](days/d07.html#term-set-up)** — Day 7, Atelier · Cast drawing 1: set-up and block-in. The fixed arrangement of viewing point, easel, subject, and light, which must not wander from session to session.
- **[sight-size](days/d01.html#term-sight-size)** — Day 1, Atelier · Setting up, sight-size and line control. A way of setting up so that, from a fixed viewing point, the drawing and the subject appear the same size, and lengths can be compared with a straight arm and a pencil.
- **[terminator](days/d08.html#term-terminator)** — Day 8, Atelier · Cast drawing 2: separating light and shadow. The line (often soft) where light on a form gives way to shadow: the boundary along which the surface turns away from the light.
- **[value](days/d04.html#term-value)** — Day 4, Atelier · Value: the nine-step scale. How light or dark a surface is, considered separately from its color.

## Seminar (logic, music, art history, economics, electives)

- **[clavicle](days/d05.html#term-clavicle)** — Day 5, Elective · Artistic anatomy 1: the skeleton under the skin. The collarbone: a subcutaneous landmark running from the sternum to the point of the shoulder (near the acromion).
- **[clef](days/d02.html#term-clef)** — Day 2, Music theory · Pitch, staff and clefs. The sign at the start of a staff that fixes which line is a named pitch: the treble (G) clef or the bass (F) clef in this lesson.
- **[composite view](days/d08.html#term-composite-view)** — Day 8, Art history · Egypt: the art of permanence. The Egyptian convention of showing each part of a figure in its most characteristic aspect: head in profile, eye as if from the front, shoulders from the front, legs and feet in profile.
- **[compound interest](days/d09.html#term-compound-interest)** — Day 9, Economics & personal finance · Compound interest. Interest paid on the principal and on the interest already earned. With principal P, annual rate r, and n years, compounded yearly: A = P(1 + r)^n.
- **[conditional](days/d01.html#term-conditional)** — Day 1, Logic · Propositions and truth tables. The ‘if p then q’ connective. It is false only when p is true and q is false. It is true on a sunny day if the claim was ‘if it rains, the street is wet’.
- **[contrapositive](days/d01.html#term-contrapositive)** — Day 1, Logic · Propositions and truth tables. The statement formed by swapping and negating both sides of a conditional. It always has the same truth value as the original.
- **[fallacy](days/d06.html#term-fallacy)** — Day 6, Logic · Validity, soundness and two classic fallacies. A tempting but invalid form of argument. Two classics: affirming the consequent, and denying the antecedent.
- **[foot (verse)](days/d10.html#term-verse-foot)** — Day 10, Elective · Poetry: meter and the iambic pentameter. The repeating unit of meter: iamb (da-DUM), trochee (DUM-da), anapest (da-da-DUM), dactyl (DUM-da-da), spondee (DUM-DUM).
- **[formal analysis](days/d03.html#term-formal-analysis)** — Day 3, Art history · How to look; the first images. A description of what is visibly there in a work — line, shape, mass, value, color, texture, space, composition — before any interpretation.
- **[hierarchical scale](days/d08.html#term-hierarchical-scale)** — Day 8, Art history · Egypt: the art of permanence. The convention of making the most important figure (a pharaoh or a god) larger than the others, not because they stand closer, but because of rank.
- **[iambic pentameter](days/d10.html#term-iambic-pentameter)** — Day 10, Elective · Poetry: meter and the iambic pentameter. A line of five iambs (five da-DUM feet), the most common meter of English verse from Chaucer onward, and the line of Shakespeare’s sonnets and blank verse.
- **[interpretation](days/d03.html#term-interpretation)** — Day 3, Art history · How to look; the first images. A claim about meaning or purpose, clearly marked as such, and paired with the evidence that would test it.
- **[key signature](days/d07.html#term-key-signature)** — Day 7, Music theory · The major scale and key signatures. The sharps or flats placed at the start of the staff to keep a scale’s pattern, added in a fixed order (sharps: F C G D A E B; flats: B E A D G C F).
- **[major scale](days/d07.html#term-major-scale)** — Day 7, Music theory · The major scale and key signatures. A scale whose steps follow the pattern whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half (W W H W W W H).
- **[meter (verse)](days/d10.html#term-verse-meter)** — Day 10, Elective · Poetry: meter and the iambic pentameter. The pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in a line of verse. (US spelling: meter.)
- **[opportunity cost](days/d04.html#term-opportunity-cost)** — Day 4, Economics & personal finance · Scarcity and opportunity cost. The value of the single best alternative given up when a choice is made, not the sum of every alternative.
- **[pitch](days/d02.html#term-pitch)** — Day 2, Music theory · Pitch, staff and clefs. How high or low a note sounds: the perceived frequency of the sound.
- **[principal](days/d09.html#term-principal)** — Day 9, Economics & personal finance · Compound interest. The original amount of money borrowed or invested, before interest.
- **[proposition](days/d01.html#term-proposition)** — Day 1, Logic · Propositions and truth tables. A sentence that is either true or false (not a command, not a question).
- **[rule of 72](days/d09.html#term-rule-of-72)** — Day 9, Economics & personal finance · Compound interest. A short estimate: money at r percent a year doubles in about 72/r years, a good approximation for rates between about 2% and 15%.
- **[scarcity](days/d04.html#term-scarcity)** — Day 4, Economics & personal finance · Scarcity and opportunity cost. The fact that resources (time, money, materials, attention) are limited relative to what we want, so every choice gives something up.
- **[soundness](days/d06.html#term-soundness)** — Day 6, Logic · Validity, soundness and two classic fallacies. The property of an argument that is valid and whose premises are actually true. Only then must the conclusion be true in the world.
- **[staff](days/d02.html#term-staff)** — Day 2, Music theory · Pitch, staff and clefs. The five lines and four spaces on which notes are written.
- **[subcutaneous landmark](days/d05.html#term-subcutaneous-landmark)** — Day 5, Elective · Artistic anatomy 1: the skeleton under the skin. A place where bone lies just under the skin and does not change shape with fat or muscle, so it is a reliable measuring point in figure drawing.
- **[sunk cost](days/d04.html#term-sunk-cost)** — Day 4, Economics & personal finance · Scarcity and opportunity cost. A cost already paid that cannot be recovered, and that should not affect a forward-looking decision.
- **[validity](days/d06.html#term-validity)** — Day 6, Logic · Validity, soundness and two classic fallacies. A property of an argument’s form: the conclusion must be true whenever the premises are true. Validity does not require the premises to be actually true.
