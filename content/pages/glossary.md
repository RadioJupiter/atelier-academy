Terms taught in weeks 1–13, listed by subject. Each entry links to the lesson that teaches it, where the Frayer card (definition, origin when it is known, example, near-miss, and a connection) is given. Use this page to look a word up; use the week-end vocabulary reviews to retrieve it from memory.

Pronunciations are on the lesson page (Hear, in English). Spanish and Latin *words* still have their own Hear-word buttons in those lessons.

## Mathematics

- **[a downward-opening quadratic inequality](days/d53.html#term-downward-opening-quadratic-inequality)** — Day 53, Mathematics · Algebra XVIII: a downward-opening parabola, and a square. When the x² coefficient is negative, the already-taught parabola opens downward, so the regions swap: y > 0 between the roots, and y < 0 outside them.
- **[a line and a parabola, as a system](days/d48.html#term-line-and-parabola-as-a-system)** — Day 48, Mathematics · Algebra XVI: a line and a parabola, as a system. Two equations together, one the already-taught parabola y = ax² + bx + c (often y = x² in a first look) and one a line, solved by substitution.
- **[a perfect-square inequality](days/d53.html#term-perfect-square-inequality)** — Day 53, Mathematics · Algebra XVIII: a downward-opening parabola, and a square. A quadratic that is a square, such as (x − 3)², is never negative.
- **[a short mix of this week’s algebra and geometry](days/d54.html#term-short-mix-algebra-geometry-w11)** — Day 54, Mathematics · Geometry XXIV: a short mix — a tangent-secant, and an inequality. A sitting that uses Monday’s and Wednesday’s quadratic inequalities and Tuesday’s tangent-secant theorem, each in its own picture, without mixing the theorems into one invented hybrid.
- **[AA similarity](days/d62.html#term-aa-similarity)** — Day 62, Mathematics · Geometry XXVI: AA similarity. If two angles of one triangle are equal to two angles of another triangle, the third angles are equal as well (because the angle sum is 180°), and the triangles are similar.
- **[alternate segment theorem (III.32)](days/d36.html#term-alternate-segment-iii32)** — Day 36, Mathematics · Geometry XVII: the tangent-chord angle (Euclid III.32). The angle between a tangent and a chord, at the point of contact, equals the inscribed angle in the alternate segment of the circle (the segment on the other side of the chord).
- **[AM–GM inequality](days/d07.html#term-am-gm)** — Day 7, Mathematics · Algebra III: inequalities and the mean. For a, b ≥ 0, (a + b)/2 ≥ √(ab), with equality only when a = b.
- **[angle bisector](days/d12.html#term-angle-bisector)** — Day 12, Mathematics · Geometry VI: the angle bisector and a parallel through a point. The ray that divides an angle into two equal angles.
- **[angle in a semicircle](days/d22.html#term-angle-in-a-semicircle)** — Day 22, Mathematics · Geometry XI: the angle in a semicircle, and angles in the same segment. An angle inscribed in a semicircle — the angle at the point C on the circle when AB is a diameter — is a right angle.
- **[angles in the same segment](days/d22.html#term-angles-same-segment)** — Day 22, Mathematics · Geometry XI: the angle in a semicircle, and angles in the same segment. Inscribed angles that stand on the same arc (and so lie in the same segment of the circle) are equal.
- **[arithmetic progression](days/d20.html#term-arithmetic-progression)** — Day 20, Mathematics · Two-week problem set: constructions to tangents, and a first look at progressions. A sequence in which each term after the first is obtained by adding the same fixed number, the common difference d.
- **[axiom](days/d02.html#term-axiom)** — Day 2, Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs. A starting statement accepted without proof, so that theorems can be proved from it.
- **[bar model](days/d01.html#term-bar-model)** — Day 1, Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation. A drawing of quantities as bars of equal units, so the relationship is visible before any equation is written.
- **[central angle](days/d21.html#term-central-angle)** — Day 21, Mathematics · Geometry X: central and inscribed angles. An angle whose vertex is the center of a circle, and whose sides pass through two points of the circle.
- **[chord](days/d18.html#term-chord)** — Day 18, Mathematics · Geometry IX: the circle, chords. A segment joining two points of a circle.
- **[circle through three points (review)](days/d29.html#term-circle-through-three-points)** — Day 29, Mathematics · Geometry XV: the circumcircle, revisited with this fortnight’s theorems. A unique circle passes through three points that are not on a single line (the circumcircle of the triangle they form).
- **[completing the square](days/d05.html#term-completing-the-square)** — Day 5, Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations. Rewriting x² + px as a square plus or minus a constant, so a quadratic can be solved or a parabola’s vertex read off.
- **[composition of functions](days/d41.html#term-composition-of-functions)** — Day 41, Mathematics · Algebra XIII: composition of functions. The function (f ∘ g) that sends x to f(g(x)): first apply g, then apply f to that result.
- **[congruence △ABC ≅ △DEF](days/d04.html#term-congruence)** — Day 4, Mathematics · Geometry II: congruent triangles and the isosceles triangle. A matching of two figures so that corresponding sides and corresponding angles are equal; one can be placed exactly on the other.
- **[construction](days/d11.html#term-construction)** — Day 11, Mathematics · Geometry V: constructions with compass and straightedge. A drawing made with only an unmarked straightedge and a compass, justified by axioms and earlier theorems, not by measuring with a ruler.
- **[cyclic quadrilateral](days/d23.html#term-cyclic-quadrilateral)** — Day 23, Mathematics · Geometry XII: cyclic quadrilaterals. A quadrilateral whose four vertices all lie on a single circle.
- **[diagonal](days/d14.html#term-diagonal)** — Day 14, Mathematics · Geometry VII: the parallelogram. A segment joining two vertices of a polygon that are not already joined by a side.
- **[discriminant D = b² − 4ac](days/d05.html#term-discriminant)** — Day 5, Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations. The quantity that decides how many real roots a quadratic has: two if D > 0, one if D = 0, none if D < 0.
- **[distance from the center](days/d18.html#term-central-distance)** — Day 18, Mathematics · Geometry IX: the circle, chords. The perpendicular distance from the center of a circle to a chord.
- **[equation of a circle](days/d42.html#term-equation-of-a-circle)** — Day 42, Mathematics · Geometry XIX: the equation of a circle. The coordinate form of the locus of points at a fixed distance r from a center (h, k): (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r².
- **[exponent an](days/d16.html#term-exponent)** — Day 16, Mathematics · Algebra V: the laws of exponents. The small number that says how many times a base is used as a factor: in aⁿ, a is the base and n is the exponent.
- **[exterior angle](days/d06.html#term-exterior-angle)** — Day 6, Mathematics · Geometry III: parallel lines and the angle sum. An angle formed by one side of a triangle and the extension of an adjacent side.
- **[f(x)](days/d31.html#term-f-of-x)** — Day 31, Mathematics · Algebra VIII: functions and function notation. Function notation: the output of f when the input is x.
- **[factor](days/d03.html#term-factor)** — Day 3, Mathematics · Algebra I: identities and factoring. To write an expression as a product; or one of those pieces.
- **[Friday mix (composition and a circle)](days/d45.html#term-friday-mix-composition-circle)** — Day 45, Mathematics · Friday mix: composition, inverses, and a circle. A Friday mix of this week’s mathematics: composition of functions, composition with an inverse, the equation of a circle, and a line-and-circle system.
- **[Friday mix: functions and loci](days/d35.html#term-functions-loci-mix)** — Day 35, Mathematics · Friday mix: functions, graphs, and a locus. A Friday mix, not a new topic: evaluate f(x), read a linear or quadratic graph, and name one of the two loci taught yesterday.
- **[function y = f(x)](days/d09.html#term-function)** — Day 9, Mathematics · Functions and their graphs. A rule that assigns to each allowed input exactly one output.
- **[geometric progression](days/d20.html#term-geometric-progression)** — Day 20, Mathematics · Two-week problem set: constructions to tangents, and a first look at progressions. A sequence in which each term after the first is obtained by multiplying by the same fixed number, the common ratio r.
- **[heuristic](days/d10.html#term-heuristic)** — Day 10, Mathematics · Two-week problem set. A deliberate method of attack, not a guess: for example, draw a bar, try a smaller case, or write a short proof plan before computing.
- **[identity](days/d03.html#term-identity)** — Day 3, Mathematics · Algebra I: identities and factoring. An equality that is true for every allowed value of its letters, and that must be proved by expanding or by using a known identity.
- **[identity function](days/d43.html#term-identity-function)** — Day 43, Mathematics · Algebra XIV: composition and the inverse. The function that sends every allowed x to x itself, often written id(x) = x.
- **[incenter](days/d49.html#term-incenter)** — Day 49, Mathematics · Geometry XXII: the incenter, and A = r s. The center of a triangle’s incircle: the point where the angle bisectors meet, equally far from all three sides.
- **[incircle](days/d47.html#term-incircle)** — Day 47, Mathematics · Geometry XXI: the incircle, from equal tangents. The unique circle that sits inside a triangle and is tangent to all three sides.
- **[inequality](days/d07.html#term-inequality)** — Day 7, Mathematics · Algebra III: inequalities and the mean. A statement that one quantity is greater or less than another.
- **[inradius](days/d49.html#term-inradius)** — Day 49, Mathematics · Geometry XXII: the incenter, and A = r s. The radius of the incircle: the perpendicular distance from the incenter to any side.
- **[inscribed angle](days/d21.html#term-inscribed-angle)** — Day 21, Mathematics · Geometry X: central and inscribed angles. An angle whose vertex lies on the circle, and whose sides are chords of the circle.
- **[intersecting chords](days/d24.html#term-intersecting-chords)** — Day 24, Mathematics · Geometry XIII: intersecting chords. If two chords of a circle meet at a point E inside the circle, then the products of the segments of each chord are equal: AE · EC = BE · ED.
- **[intersection of a line and a circle](days/d44.html#term-intersection-line-circle)** — Day 44, Mathematics · Geometry XX: a line and a circle, as a system. The point or points that lie on both a line and a circle.
- **[inverse of a linear function](days/d39.html#term-inverse-linear-function)** — Day 39, Mathematics · Algebra XII: the inverse of a linear function. The function that undoes a given linear function.
- **[isosceles](days/d04.html#term-isosceles)** — Day 4, Mathematics · Geometry II: congruent triangles and the isosceles triangle. A triangle with two sides equal.
- **[iteration of a function](days/d46.html#term-iteration-of-a-function)** — Day 46, Mathematics · Algebra XV: iterating a function. Applying the same function more than once: f(f(x)) is f applied to the result of f(x).
- **[linear function](days/d32.html#term-linear-function)** — Day 32, Mathematics · Algebra IX: graphs of linear functions. A function whose graph is a straight line.
- **[locus](days/d08.html#term-locus)** — Day 8, Mathematics · Geometry IV: inequalities in triangles. The set of all points that satisfy a given condition.
- **[mixed circle problems](days/d28.html#term-mixed-circle-problems)** — Day 28, Mathematics · Geometry XIV: mixed circle problems. Problems that use more than one circle fact from this fortnight — inscribed and central angles, the angle in a semicircle, cyclic quadrilaterals, or intersecting chords — in a single figure, with the reason named at each step.
- **[mixed progressions](days/d27.html#term-mixed-progressions)** — Day 27, Mathematics · Algebra IX: choosing an AP or a GP, and a first applied sum. Problems that ask you to decide whether a sequence is arithmetic, geometric, or neither, and then to use the matching formula for a term or a sum — not to apply one formula to every list of numbers.
- **[opposite angles supplementary](days/d23.html#term-opposite-angles-supplementary)** — Day 23, Mathematics · Geometry XII: cyclic quadrilaterals. In a cyclic quadrilateral, each pair of opposite angles sums to 180°.
- **[order of composition](days/d41.html#term-order-of-composition)** — Day 41, Mathematics · Algebra XIII: composition of functions. Which function is applied first.
- **[parabola](days/d33.html#term-parabola)** — Day 33, Mathematics · Algebra X: graphs of quadratic functions. The graph of a quadratic function, y = ax² + bx + c with a ≠ 0.
- **[parallel through a point](days/d12.html#term-parallel-construction)** — Day 12, Mathematics · Geometry VI: the angle bisector and a parallel through a point. The unique line through a given point P, not on a line ℓ, that is parallel to ℓ.
- **[parallelogram](days/d14.html#term-parallelogram)** — Day 14, Mathematics · Geometry VII: the parallelogram. A quadrilateral whose opposite sides are parallel (both pairs).
- **[perpendicular bisector](days/d11.html#term-perpendicular-bisector)** — Day 11, Mathematics · Geometry V: constructions with compass and straightedge. The line that cuts a segment in half and meets it at a right angle.
- **[perpendicular bisector as a locus](days/d34.html#term-perp-bisector-locus)** — Day 34, Mathematics · Geometry XVI: loci — a circle, and the perpendicular bisector. The locus of points equidistant from two fixed points A and B is the perpendicular bisector of the segment AB.
- **[power](days/d16.html#term-power)** — Day 16, Mathematics · Algebra V: the laws of exponents. The result of raising a base to an exponent, or the expression aⁿ itself.
- **[quadratic inequality](days/d51.html#term-quadratic-inequality)** — Day 51, Mathematics · Algebra XVII: a quadratic inequality, from the graph. An inequality whose sides, once arranged, give a quadratic on one side, such as (x − 2)(x − 6) < 0.
- **[radical](days/d17.html#term-radical)** — Day 17, Mathematics · Algebra VI: radicals. An expression that uses a root symbol, such as √a or ³√a.
- **[ratio](days/d01.html#term-ratio)** — Day 1, Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation. A comparison of two quantities by units of the same size, written a : b.
- **[rectangle](days/d15.html#term-rectangle)** — Day 15, Mathematics · Geometry VIII: rectangle, rhombus and square. A parallelogram with a right angle.
- **[regular polygon](days/d37.html#term-regular-polygon)** — Day 37, Mathematics · Geometry XVIII: regular polygons, and the interior angle. A polygon with all sides equal and all interior angles equal.
- **[remainder](days/d01.html#term-remainder)** — Day 1, Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation. What is left of a quantity after a part of it has been taken.
- **[rhombus](days/d15.html#term-rhombus)** — Day 15, Mathematics · Geometry VIII: rectangle, rhombus and square. A parallelogram with all four sides equal.
- **[scale factor (similarity)](days/d63.html#term-scale-factor-similarity)** — Day 63, Mathematics · Geometry XXVII: a missing side, from a scale factor. The constant ratio of corresponding sides of two similar triangles.
- **[secant](days/d52.html#term-secant)** — Day 52, Mathematics · Geometry XXIII: a tangent and a secant (Euclid III.36). A line that cuts a circle at two points.
- **[similar triangles](days/d61.html#term-similar-triangles)** — Day 61, Mathematics · Geometry XXV: similar triangles. Two triangles are similar when their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional — they are the same shape, not necessarily the same size.
- **[slope](days/d32.html#term-slope)** — Day 32, Mathematics · Algebra IX: graphs of linear functions. The constant rate of change of a linear function: rise over run, (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁), between any two points on the line.
- **[square root √a](days/d17.html#term-square-root)** — Day 17, Mathematics · Algebra VI: radicals. For a ≥ 0, the square root √a is the unique number ≥ 0 whose square is a.
- **[substitution](days/d13.html#term-substitution)** — Day 13, Mathematics · Algebra IV: systems of linear equations. A method for solving a system: use one equation to express one unknown in terms of the other, and put that expression into the second equation.
- **[sum of a finite geometric progression](days/d26.html#term-sum-of-a-finite-gp)** — Day 26, Mathematics · Algebra VIII: the sum of a finite geometric progression. The sum of the first n terms of a geometric progression with first term a and common ratio r is Sn = a(rn − 1)/(r − 1) when r ≠ 1.
- **[sum of an arithmetic progression](days/d25.html#term-sum-of-an-arithmetic-progression)** — Day 25, Mathematics · Algebra VII: the sum of an arithmetic progression. The sum of the first n terms of an arithmetic progression with first term a and last term ℓ is Sn = n/2 · (a + ℓ).
- **[system of equations](days/d13.html#term-system-of-equations)** — Day 13, Mathematics · Algebra IV: systems of linear equations. Two or more equations that must be true at the same time, about the same unknowns.
- **[tangent (origin recalled)](days/d36.html#term-tangent-origin)** — Day 36, Mathematics · Geometry XVII: the tangent-chord angle (Euclid III.32). A line that meets a circle at exactly one point.
- **[tangent to a circle](days/d19.html#term-tangent-circle)** — Day 19, Mathematics · Geometry X: tangents to a circle. A line that meets a circle at exactly one point, called the point of contact.
- **[the tangent-secant theorem](days/d52.html#term-tangent-secant-theorem)** — Day 52, Mathematics · Geometry XXIII: a tangent and a secant (Euclid III.36). If from a point P outside a circle a tangent touches the circle at T and a secant cuts the circle at A (nearer) and B (farther), then PT² = PA × PB.
- **[theorem](days/d02.html#term-theorem)** — Day 2, Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs. A statement that is proved from definitions, axioms, and earlier theorems.
- **[transversal](days/d06.html#term-transversal)** — Day 6, Mathematics · Geometry III: parallel lines and the angle sum. A line that crosses two or more other lines, forming corresponding, alternate interior, and same-side interior angles.
- **[triangle inequality](days/d08.html#term-triangle-inequality)** — Day 8, Mathematics · Geometry IV: inequalities in triangles. In a triangle, each side is less than the sum of the other two (and greater than their difference).
- **[two tangents from a point](days/d19.html#term-two-tangents)** — Day 19, Mathematics · Geometry X: tangents to a circle. From a point outside a circle, the two tangent segments to the points of contact are equal.
- **[two-week mathematics check](days/d30.html#term-two-week-math-check)** — Day 30, Mathematics · Two-week problem set: circles and progressions. A mixed problem set on this fortnight’s skills: central and inscribed angles, the angle in a semicircle, cyclic quadrilaterals, intersecting chords, and the sums of arithmetic and geometric progressions, with the reason named each time.
- **[two-week mix](days/d40.html#term-two-week-math-mix)** — Day 40, Mathematics · Friday mix: two weeks of functions, graphs, and circle geometry. A Friday mix, not a new chapter: functions and f(x); linear and quadratic graphs; two loci; the tangent-chord theorem (III.32); a regular polygon’s interior angle; Vieta’s formulas; the inverse of a linear function.
- **[unit (in a bar or ratio)](days/d10.html#term-unit-bar)** — Day 10, Mathematics · Two-week problem set. One equal part in a bar model or a ratio.
- **[vertex](days/d09.html#term-vertex)** — Day 9, Mathematics · Functions and their graphs. The turning point of a parabola.
- **[vertex of a parabola](days/d33.html#term-vertex-parabola)** — Day 33, Mathematics · Algebra X: graphs of quadratic functions. The turning point of the graph of a quadratic.
- **[vertical angles ∠AOB and ∠COD](days/d02.html#term-vertical-angles)** — Day 2, Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs. A pair of angles whose sides are the extensions of each other’s sides: the opposite angles formed when two lines meet.
- **[Vieta's formulas r + s = −p, rs = q](days/d05.html#term-vietas-formulas)** — Day 5, Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations. For the roots r and s of x² + px + q = 0, the sum of the roots is −p and the product is q.
- **[Vieta’s formulas (quadratic)](days/d38.html#term-vieta-quadratic)** — Day 38, Mathematics · Algebra XI: Vieta’s formulas for a quadratic. For a quadratic equation, the sum and the product of the roots can be read from the coefficients.
- **[week’s mathematics mix](days/d50.html#term-weeks-mathematics-mix-w10)** — Day 50, Mathematics · Friday mix: iteration, an incircle, and a line with a parabola. A Friday mix of this week’s skills only: iteration f(f(x)); the incircle from equal tangents; a line and a parabola as a system; and the incenter with A = r s.
- **[week’s mathematics mix](days/d55.html#term-weeks-mathematics-mix-w11)** — Day 55, Mathematics · Friday mix: quadratic inequalities, and a tangent with a secant. A Friday mix of this week’s quadratic inequalities (upward, downward, and a square) and the tangent-secant theorem (Euclid III.36).
## Physics

- **[acceleration aavg = Δv / Δt](days/d03.html#term-acceleration)** — Day 3, Physics · Acceleration and velocity–time graphs. The rate of change of velocity: how quickly velocity increases, decreases, or changes direction.
- **[action–reaction pair](days/d10.html#term-action-reaction-pair)** — Day 10, Physics · Newton's third law and the two-week check. The two forces named by the third law: they are equal, opposite, simultaneous, of the same type, and they act on two different bodies.
- **[centripetal](days/d41.html#term-centripetal)** — Day 41, Physics · A first look at circular motion, and a center-seeking force. Center-seeking: a force (or an acceleration) directed toward the center of the circle, needed if the motion is to stay on that circle.
- **[centripetal acceleration a = v²/r](days/d16.html#term-centripetal-acceleration)** — Day 16, Physics · Uniform circular motion and centripetal force. The inward, center-seeking acceleration of a body that moves at constant speed on a circle, of size a = v²/r (or a = 4π²r/T², where T is the period).
- **[centripetal force F = mv²/r](days/d16.html#term-centripetal-force)** — Day 16, Physics · Uniform circular motion and centripetal force. The net force toward the center that produces the centripetal acceleration: F_net, inward = m v²/r.
- **[circular motion](days/d41.html#term-circular-motion)** — Day 41, Physics · A first look at circular motion, and a center-seeking force. Motion along a circular path.
- **[component vx, vy](days/d13.html#term-component)** — Day 13, Physics · Vectors and components. The projection of a vector along a chosen axis.
- **[conservation (mechanical, first model)](days/d28.html#term-conservation-mechanical)** — Day 28, Physics · Mechanical energy, conserved when friction does no work. The statement that KE + PE is constant when only gravity (in the PE = m g h model) and forces that do no work (such as a normal force perpendicular to the motion) act.
- **[conservation of momentum](days/d18.html#term-conservation-of-momentum)** — Day 18, Physics · Momentum. If two (or more) bodies interact and the net external force on the system is zero (or is negligible for a short collision), the total momentum of the system does not change.
- **[displacement Δx = xfinal − xinitial](days/d01.html#term-displacement)** — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. The change in position: final minus initial.
- **[free fall g ≈ 9.8 m/s², downward](days/d05.html#term-free-fall)** — Day 5, Physics · Free fall. Motion under gravity alone, with air resistance neglected.
- **[friction f = μN](days/d11.html#term-friction)** — Day 11, Physics · Friction and the normal force. A force between surfaces in contact that resists their relative sliding.
- **[Friday mix (circular motion)](days/d45.html#term-friday-mix-circular-motion)** — Day 45, Physics · Friday mix: circular motion, and a center-seeking force. A Friday mix of this week’s first look: circular motion, tangent velocity, a centripetal (center-seeking) force, and what happens if that force stops — still with no invented speed.
- **[Friday mix: power and torque](days/d35.html#term-power-torque-mix)** — Day 35, Physics · Friday mix: power and a first look at torque. A Friday mix, not a new topic: compute a power in watts, and a torque in newton-meters, keeping the units distinct.
- **[fulcrum](days/d63.html#term-fulcrum)** — Day 63, Physics · The lever, as a case of rotational equilibrium. The support about which a lever turns.
- **[g from G M / R²](days/d46.html#term-g-from-gm-over-r-squared)** — Day 46, Physics · g from G M / R², with sourced Earth figures. Near a sphere, the gravitational field g is G M / R², because F = G M m / R² and F = m g, so the m cancels.
- **[g on Mars, from the same formula](days/d51.html#term-g-on-mars-from-the-same-formula)** — Day 51, Physics · g on Mars, from the same formula and sourced figures. The already-taught formula g = G M / R², applied to NASA fact-sheet figures for Mars, giving about 3.7 m/s², which matches the fact sheet’s own listed gravity 3.7 m/s².
- **[g on the Moon, a first sourced look](days/d48.html#term-g-on-the-moon)** — Day 48, Physics · g on the Moon, from the same formula and sourced figures. The same formula g = G M / R², now used with sourced Moon figures, compared with a NASA table value and with a NASA Science qualitative comparison.
- **[gravitational potential energy (near Earth)](days/d26.html#term-gravitational-pe)** — Day 26, Physics · Gravitational potential energy near the Earth. In this course’s first model, the gravitational potential energy of an object near the Earth, treated as a particle, is PE = m g h, where h is the height above a chosen reference level, g is 9.8 N/kg (or 9.8 m/s²), and PE is in joules.
- **[gravity g](days/d05.html#term-gravity)** — Day 5, Physics · Free fall. The attraction that gives a free-falling object its downward acceleration g near Earth.
- **[impulse J = Δp](days/d20.html#term-impulse)** — Day 20, Physics · Two-week check: friction to momentum. The product of a net force and the time it acts, equal to the change in momentum: J = F_net Δt = Δp, when the force is constant (or using an average force).
- **[independence of components](days/d20.html#term-independence-of-components)** — Day 20, Physics · Two-week check: friction to momentum. The rule, used for projectiles, that the horizontal motion (constant v_x, if air is neglected) and the vertical motion (acceleration g) can be solved separately, and that a change in one component does not by itself change the other.
- **[inertia](days/d06.html#term-inertia)** — Day 6, Physics · Newton's first law: inertia. The tendency of a body to keep its velocity — to stay at rest, or to keep moving in a straight line at constant speed — unless a net force acts on it.
- **[inverse-square (gravitation)](days/d36.html#term-inverse-square-gravitation)** — Day 36, Physics · A first look at gravitation: an inverse-square force. A pattern in which a quantity is proportional to 1/r²: if r is multiplied by 2, the quantity is multiplied by 1/4; if r is multiplied by 3, the quantity is multiplied by 1/9.
- **[joule](days/d21.html#term-joule)** — Day 21, Physics · Work done by a constant force. The SI unit of work (and of energy): one joule (J) is one newton-meter, the work done by a one-newton force along one meter of displacement in its own direction.
- **[kinetic energy](days/d23.html#term-kinetic-energy)** — Day 23, Physics · Kinetic energy, and the work–energy theorem. The energy an object has because it is moving.
- **[mass (mechanics)](days/d38.html#term-mass-mechanics)** — Day 38, Physics · Mass and weight. A measure of the amount of matter, and of how strongly the body resists a change of motion (inertia), in this first look.
- **[mass m](days/d08.html#term-mass)** — Day 8, Physics · Newton's second law. A measure of how much a body resists acceleration (inertial mass).
- **[mechanical energy (no friction)](days/d28.html#term-mechanical-energy)** — Day 28, Physics · Mechanical energy, conserved when friction does no work. In this course’s first conservation model, mechanical energy is the sum KE + PE, with PE = m g h near the Earth.
- **[momentum p = mv](days/d18.html#term-momentum)** — Day 18, Physics · Momentum. The product of mass and velocity, p = m v, a vector in the same direction as the velocity.

- **[cross-hatching](days/d14.html#term-cross-hatching)** — Day 14, Atelier · Cross-hatching and control of value. A second (or later) layer of hatch laid across the first, at a new angle, to deepen value or to tighten form.

A study of an eye (or a pair of eyes) as it sits in the already-blocked head: the brow plane, the socket, the lids, and the value family of the head, not a floating almond drawn first and made “alive” with a highlight.
- **[feature study](days/d15.html#term-feature-study)** — Day 15, Atelier · A second Bargue plate: the feature. A drawing of a single facial feature from a plate (here, a Bargue lithograph), treated as a constructed form with planes, not as a symbol of an eye or a mouth.

The group of the two eyes (or the brows) and the base of the nose and mouth, placed as one shape on the front plane of the face, rather than as three ornaments.
- **[first move from a plan](days/d36.html#term-first-move-from-plan)** — Day 36, Atelier · The first move from last week’s plan. The single, named correction you wrote at the end of last week — a placement, a width, a tilt, or a value family — now made on the study, and then checked against the plate or plaster.

- **[form](days/d05.html#term-form)** — Day 5, Atelier · Bargue plate: modeling and completion. The three-dimensional shape of the subject, as it is turned by light and as it must read on a flat page.
- **[hair as mass](days/d32.html#term-hair-as-mass)** — Day 32, Atelier · Hair as masses, not as strands first. Hair is first drawn or painted as a few large shapes of light and dark that sit on the skull, not as a collection of single hairs.
- **[halftone](days/d09.html#term-halftone)** — Day 9, Atelier · Cast drawing 3: halftones. The middle values on the lit side of a form, between the lights and the terminator, that show the surface turning toward shadow.
- **[hatching](days/d13.html#term-hatching)** — Day 13, Atelier · The sphere, and hatching that follows the form. A way of building value with rows of strokes, rather than with a smudge alone.
- **[head on the neck](days/d33.html#term-head-on-neck)** — Day 33, Atelier · The neck, and how the head sits on it. The head does not float: it sits on the neck, which has width, a slight forward or backward lean, and a connection into the shoulders.
- **[highlight](days/d09.html#term-highlight)** — Day 9, Atelier · Cast drawing 3: halftones. The smallest, brightest spot where the light is most directly reflected to the eye; it sits inside the lights, not in the shadows.
- **[light and shadow families](days/d08.html#term-light-shadow-families)** — Day 8, Atelier · Cast drawing 2: separating light and shadow. The rule that values on the lit side stay in the light range, and values on the shadow side stay in the shadow range, so the two sides do not get mixed.
- **[light on a simple form](days/d41.html#term-light-on-a-simple-form)** — Day 41, Atelier · Light on a sphere: highlight, midtone, core shadow. A first study of how a single, steady light falls on a simple solid (a sphere is today’s form): a highlight, a midtone, a core shadow, and, if the light and the table allow, a cast shadow and a little reflected light.
- **[likeness check in context](days/d37.html#term-likeness-check-context)** — Day 37, Atelier · A likeness check of the features in context. A slow look at whether the features, already placed, still sit in the right heights, distances, and tilts on this head, judged as a set against the plate or plaster.
- **[likeness to the plate](days/d29.html#term-likeness-to-the-plate)** — Day 29, Atelier · Accents, and a likeness check against the plate or plaster. The match of the drawing to this Bargue head or plaster: tilt, silhouette, feature triangle, and light.
- **[lost-and-found at the hair and ear](days/d38.html#term-lost-found-hair-ear)** — Day 38, Atelier · Hair edges and the ear: lost-and-found, again. A second, slower look at which edges of the hair masses, and of the ear they may overlap, should stay soft (lost) and which may be a little clearer (found), as the plate or plaster shows.
- **[lost-and-found edge](days/d18.html#term-lost-and-found-edge)** — Day 18, Atelier · Lost-and-found edges. A contour or an inner edge that is sharp in one passage (found) and soft or gone in another (lost), according to the light and the turn of the form.
- **[lost-and-found on the head](days/d28.html#term-lost-and-found-head)** — Day 28, Atelier · Lost-and-found edges on the head. The week-4 skill of losing an edge where values meet softly or nearly match, and finding an edge where a plane cuts clearly, now applied to the head: hair against a temple, jaw into neck, a lid into a socket, the far cheek into shadow.
- **[modeling](days/d05.html#term-modeling)** — Day 5, Atelier · Bargue plate: modeling and completion. Using value to turn a shape so that it reads as a form in light, not as a flat cut-out.
- **[nine-step scale](days/d04.html#term-nine-step-scale)** — Day 4, Atelier · Value: the nine-step scale. A row of nine even steps of gray from white to black, used so that values in a drawing can be chosen and compared.
- **[nose and mouth in context](days/d27.html#term-nose-mouth-context)** — Day 27, Atelier · The nose and mouth, in the context of the head. The nose and mouth placed as part of the feature triangle on the existing head, with the under-plane of the nose, the center line through the mouth, and their value families, rather than a separately perfected nostril or lip on a blank mid-face.
- **[one light, held](days/d42.html#term-one-light-held)** — Day 42, Atelier · The same sphere, the same lamp: a value check. The same single lamp, in the same place, so that yesterday’s highlight and core shadow can be checked and slow value can be added, rather than a new arrangement invented mid-study.
- **[overlap](days/d16.html#term-overlap)** — Day 16, Atelier · Combining solids: a small still life. One form partly hiding another, which both creates a depth cue and forces you to measure where a contour stops and another begins.
- **[plan (for the next work)](days/d10.html#term-next-work-plan)** — Day 10, Atelier · Critique and plan. A short, practical list of what to keep and what to change in the next set-up or the next drawing, written after the critique.
- **[plan for the next sittings](days/d20.html#term-next-sittings-plan)** — Day 20, Atelier · Critique and a two-week plan. A short, written plan that names one or two skills to practice, the set-up that will make that practice possible, and how you will know the sittings have done their work.
- **[plane (of a form)](days/d11.html#term-plane-of-form)** — Day 11, Atelier · The cube: planes and foreshortening. A flat face of a form, or a flattened patch used to understand a curve.
- **[planes of a cube, under one lamp](days/d46.html#term-planes-of-a-cube-under-one-lamp)** — Day 46, Atelier · A cube under one lamp: three planes. The visible faces of a cube, seen as flat planes of different value under a single lamp: typically a light plane (most turned toward the lamp), a mid plane, and a dark plane.
- **[planes of the head](days/d23.html#term-planes-of-the-head)** — Day 23, Atelier · Planes of the head, and one large terminator. A way of simplifying the head into a few large, relatively flat surfaces that catch or turn from the light — forehead, front plane of the face, side plane, jaw, and the underside of the chin — so the light can be read before small forms are carved in.
- **[plaster feature](days/d17.html#term-plaster-feature)** — Day 17, Atelier · A plaster feature: from plane to a more particular form. A small white or off-white cast of a nose, ear, mouth, or eye, used so that color does not distract and the light can be read as value.
- **[plumb line](days/d01.html#term-plumb-line)** — Day 1, Atelier · Setting up, sight-size and line control. A weighted string, or a pencil held up as a vertical, used to check which points in the subject line up one above the other.
- **[point of focus](days/d19.html#term-focus)** — Day 19, Atelier · Accents and a point of focus. The place in the drawing you want the eye to arrive at first: usually the most particular passage of form, with a slightly stronger contrast or a slightly sharper found edge, while the surroundings are relatively quieter.

- **[sight-size](days/d01.html#term-sight-size)** — Day 1, Atelier · Setting up, sight-size and line control. A way of setting up so that, from a fixed viewing point, the drawing and the subject appear the same size, and lengths can be compared with a straight arm and a pencil.

- **[terminator of the head](days/d23.html#term-terminator-head)** — Day 23, Atelier · Planes of the head, and one large terminator. - **[the sphere as a form](days/d13.html#term-sphere-as-form)** — Day 13, Atelier · The sphere, and hatching that follows the form. A solid whose surface is everywhere curved, with no plane edges.
- **[transition of planes](days/d17.html#term-transition-of-planes)** — Day 17, Atelier · A plaster feature: from plane to a more particular form. The way one plane of a form gives way to the next: as a sharp fold, as a soft turn, or as a series of small planes that together read as a curve.

- **[two cast shadows, one lamp](days/d53.html#term-two-cast-shadows-one-lamp)** — Day 53, Atelier · Two cast shadows, on the same ground. Each form throws a cast shadow on the ground.

A structured look at the fortnight’s work (cube, cylinder, sphere, Bargue feature, still life, plaster feature), naming what is constructed, what is still a symbol or a wire, and what the next two or three sittings should fix.

A short, honest review of the study against the plate or plaster after two weeks of work on the same head: what is right, what still fails, and what the first move of a later sitting will be.

- **[value pass (head)](days/d25.html#term-value-pass-head)** — Day 25, Atelier · Friday: a value pass on the head. A sitting devoted to organizing light and shadow on the head (or plaster) in a few values — usually a clear light, a clear large shadow, and one or two middles — rather than to adding new outline.

- **[iambic pentameter](days/d10.html#term-iambic-pentameter)** — Day 10, Elective · Poetry: meter and the iambic pentameter. A line of five iambs (five da-DUM feet), the most common meter of English verse from Chaucer onward, and the line of Shakespeare’s sonnets and blank verse.
- **[imagery](days/d30.html#term-imagery)** — Day 30, Elective · Poetry 3: imagery and metaphor, with a public-domain poem. Language that appeals to the senses — seeing, hearing, touch, and the rest — so a reader can picture or otherwise sense a scene or an object.

- **[inclusive or, in a first look](days/d51.html#term-inclusive-or-in-a-first-look)** — Day 51, Logic · Disjunctive syllogism, and an inclusive “or”. An “or” that is true when at least one side is true, including the case where both are true.

- **[interval](days/d12.html#term-interval)** — Day 12, Music theory · Intervals. The distance between two pitches, named by counting letter-names inclusively, then qualified as perfect, major, or minor (and, later, augmented or diminished).
- **[interval inversion](days/d37.html#term-interval-inversion)** — Day 37, Music theory · Interval inversion, with the octave as the frame. The partner of an interval when one of the two notes is moved by an octave, so that the old top note is now below, or the old bottom note is now above.
- **[inverse](days/d21.html#term-inverse-conditional)** — Day 21, Logic · Converse, inverse, and contrapositive. The statement obtained by negating both sides of a conditional, without swapping them.
- **[inward eye](days/d55.html#term-inward-eye)** — Day 55, Elective · Poetry 6: Wordsworth, “I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud” (first published 1807). Wordsworth’s figure, in the last stanza of “I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud,” for the later memory of the scene: the daffodils “flash upon that inward eye / Which is the bliss of solitude.” It is a figure in a poem, not a claim in a lesson on the anatomy of the eye.
- **[Keats, On First Looking into Chapman's Homer](days/d50.html#term-keats-on-first-looking-into-chapmans-homer)** — Day 50, Elective · Poetry 5: Keats, “On First Looking into Chapman's Homer” (1817). A sonnet by John Keats, published in *Poems* (1817), in the public domain, about first encountering George Chapman's English Homer.
- **[key signature](days/d07.html#term-key-signature)** — Day 7, Music theory · The major scale and key signatures. The sharps or flats placed at the start of the staff to keep a scale’s pattern, added in a fixed order (sharps: F C G D A E B; flats: B E A D G C F).
- **[kouros](days/d18.html#term-kouros)** — Day 18, Art history · The kouros and contrapposto. An Archaic Greek standing youth, typically in marble, usually nude, with the left foot forward, the weight still even on both legs, the arms at the sides, and often an ‘Archaic smile’.
- **[Laocoön (date caution)](days/d28.html#term-laocoon-caution)** — Day 28, Art history · Hellenistic sculpture: the Nike of Samothrace, and the Laocoön with a date caution. The Vatican Laocoön group, a marble of Laocoön and his sons attacked by serpents, famous since its Renaissance rediscovery.

- **[major and minor triad](days/d17.html#term-major-minor-triad)** — Day 17, Music theory · The triad. A major triad has a major third and a perfect fifth above the root (the upper interval, from the third to the fifth, is then a minor third).
- **[major scale](days/d07.html#term-major-scale)** — Day 7, Music theory · The major scale and key signatures. A scale whose steps follow the pattern whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half (W W H W W W H).
- **[major term and minor term](days/d61.html#term-major-and-minor-term)** — Day 61, Logic · Celarent, and the major and minor terms. In a syllogism, the major term (P) is the predicate of the conclusion, and the minor term (S) is the subject of the conclusion.
- **[market](days/d14.html#term-market)** — Day 14, Economics & personal finance · Markets and prices. A meeting of buyers and sellers of a particular good or service, in which they can trade.
- **[medium of exchange](days/d29.html#term-medium-of-exchange)** — Day 29, Economics & personal finance · Money: medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value. The job of money as a thing commonly accepted in payment, so that trade does not have to be a direct swap of two particular goods.
- **[metaphor](days/d30.html#term-metaphor)** — Day 30, Elective · Poetry 3: imagery and metaphor, with a public-domain poem. A comparison made by speaking of one thing as if it were another, without a comparing word such as *like* or *as* (those usually mark a simile).
- **[meter (verse)](days/d10.html#term-verse-meter)** — Day 10, Elective · Poetry: meter and the iambic pentameter. The pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in a line of verse.
- **[minor triad](days/d42.html#term-minor-triad)** — Day 42, Music theory · The minor triad, beside the major triad already taught. A three-note chord built from a root, a minor third above the root, and a perfect fifth above the root (or a major third above the third).
- **[modus ponens](days/d46.html#term-modus-ponens)** — Day 46, Logic · Modus ponens and modus tollens. A valid form: If P, then Q; P; therefore Q.
- **[modus tollens](days/d46.html#term-modus-tollens)** — Day 46, Logic · Modus ponens and modus tollens. A valid form: If P, then Q; not Q; therefore not P.
- **[money (three jobs)](days/d29.html#term-money-three-jobs)** — Day 29, Economics & personal finance · Money: medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value. In this course’s working definition, money is whatever, in a given setting, does three jobs: a medium of exchange (you can pay with it), a unit of account (prices and debts can be written in it), and a store of value (it can be kept and later spent, with the usual cautions about inflation and risk, which are named, not computed today).
- **[natural minor scale](days/d22.html#term-natural-minor-scale)** — Day 22, Music theory · The natural minor scale, and relative minor. A seven-note scale whose stepwise pattern of whole and half steps is whole–half–whole–whole–half–whole–whole (W H W W H W W).
- **[necessary condition](days/d16.html#term-necessary-condition)** — Day 16, Logic · Necessary and sufficient conditions. A condition that must hold if the result holds: if P is necessary for Q, then Q → P (equivalently, ¬P → ¬Q).
- **[Nike of Samothrace](days/d28.html#term-nike-of-samothrace)** — Day 28, Art history · Hellenistic sculpture: the Nike of Samothrace, and the Laocoön with a date caution. The marble “Winged Victory” now in the Louvre, a Hellenistic statue of the goddess Nike, usually dated to the second century BCE (often around the early second century; exact year is not settled, and this course will not invent one).
- **[octave as a frame](days/d37.html#term-octave-as-frame)** — Day 37, Music theory · Interval inversion, with the octave as the frame. The octave (from Latin octavus, “eighth”) is the interval of eight letter names, inclusive, for example C to the next C.

The Pantheon in Rome is a circular temple with a concrete dome and a central oculus, as we have it, generally treated as a rebuild under Hadrian in the early second century CE (a conventional range often given around 118–125 CE).

The bony basin at the base of the trunk, a volume that carries the abdomen and meets the thigh at the hip, with a front plane (including the anterior superior iliac spines as useful landmarks) and a tilt of its own.

The amount of money (or other payment) asked or agreed for a good or service, which also serves as a signal: a high price discourages some buying and encourages some selling and production; a low price does the reverse, other things equal.

In this first look, profit is a leftover: the amount that remains when cost is subtracted from the money taken in (the price received, or the takings) in a labeled, invented example.

The minor scale that shares its key signature (its pitch set) with a given major scale, and starts a minor third below the major tonic, or on the sixth degree of the major scale.

The bony thorax as an artistic form: a rounded cage, wider than it is deep in a typical academic model, attaching to the spine, with a lower opening (the costal margin) that is not a straight cut-off.

The amphitheater ordinarily known as the Colosseum, in Platner and Ashby’s entry Amphitheatrum Flavium (1929): built by Vespasian on the site of the stagnum of Nero’s domus Aurea; carried to the second arcade and dedicated before Vespasian’s death in 79; Titus added the third and fourth stories and celebrated the dedication of the enlarged building in 80 with games that lasted one hundred days.

A first naming of the hand after last week’s arm (humerus, radius, and ulna): the wrist bones (the carpals), the palm bones (the metacarpals), and the finger bones (the phalanges).

The seventh scale degree raised by a half step so that it lies a half step below the tonic, often used to build a major V chord in a minor key.

- **[tibia](days/d65.html#term-tibia)** — Day 65, Elective · Anatomy: the leg — femur, tibia, and fibula. The shinbone: the inner (medial) and usually the larger of the two long bones of the lower leg, between the knee and the ankle.

A change of which triad tone is in the bass: root position has the root in the bass; first inversion has the third in the bass; second inversion has the fifth in the bass.
- **[undistributed middle](days/d36.html#term-undistributed-middle)** — Day 36, Logic · A first look at the undistributed middle. An invalid syllogistic form in which the middle term never refers to the whole of its class in either premise, so it does not actually link the other two terms.
