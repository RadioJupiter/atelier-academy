## Lessons by subject (weeks 1–4)

- **Mathematics:** weeks 1–2 as before (bar models through the two-week problem set). Weeks 3–4: compass-and-straightedge constructions (including the perpendicular and angle bisectors); systems of equations (bar model, then substitution and elimination); parallelogram, rectangle, rhombus, and square (a trapezoid in this course has exactly one pair of parallel sides); exponent laws and radicals; chords and tangents of a circle; a mixed set with a first look at arithmetic and geometric progressions.
- **Writing & Rhetoric:** weeks 1–2 as before. Weeks 3–4: sentence combining and transitions; imitatio from the first paragraph of Lincoln’s Gettysburg Address (19 November 1863); the semicolon and the conjunctive adverb; Franklin’s hints on chapter 1 of Johnson’s *Rasselas* (1759); the précis; the two-paragraph outline; a Franklin reconstruction; quotations and attribution; concession and the limit of a claim; a two-paragraph essay check.
- **Latin:** *Familia Romana*, Capitula I–IV in weeks 1–2; Capitula V–VI in weeks 3–4 (ablative with *in*, *ex*, *ab*, *cum*, *sine*; accusative prepositions; town names and the locative; first passives with *ā/ab* for the agent). All reading examples in the lessons are original; the book is a companion, not a text to be copied.
- **Atelier:** weeks 1–2 as before (sight-size, Bargue, the egg, a first cast, critique). Weeks 3–4: the cube (planes and foreshortening), the cylinder (the ellipse), the sphere and hatching, cross-hatching and value control, a second Bargue plate (a feature), a still life of solids, a plaster feature, lost-and-found edges, accents and a point of focus, critique and a two-week plan.
- **Physics:** weeks 1–2 as before (motion through Newton’s third law). Weeks 3–4: friction and the normal force; vectors and components; projectiles; centripetal acceleration; momentum and its conservation; a mixed set with impulse.
- **Science:** weeks 1–2 as before. Weeks 3–4: why oil paint dries (drying oils, autoxidation and polymerization; pigment and binder; a mass-check that oil may gain mass from oxygen, while watercolor loses mass as water evaporates). No lead driers in studio use.
- **History:** weeks 1–2 as before. Weeks 3–4: Old Kingdom and the pyramids (with chronology treated as uncertain at the exact-year level); the Pyramid Texts of Unas at Saqqara; the year-21 treaty of Ramesses II with Ḫattušili III (Akkadian from Hattusa and the Karnak hieroglyphs; a conventional mid-13th-century BCE date, not treated as exact); Alexander, 336–323 BCE, with Arrian (late) in Chinnock’s 1884 translation; Hellenistic and Ptolemaic Egypt to 30 BCE, the Rosetta Stone (196 BCE) named, not read; a Friday synthesis on sources for kingship.
- **Literature:** *Iliad* Books 1–6 in weeks 1–2; Books 7–24 in weeks 3–4 (the embassy; Patroclus; the shield and the death of Hector; the funeral, Priam’s supplication, and a short written response). The poem ends with Hector’s funeral; the fall of Troy is not narrated here. Quotes, when used, are short and attributed, from a public-domain translation.
- **Modern language (Spanish):** weeks 1–2 as before. Weeks 3–4: stem-changing verbs (e → ie, o → ue); *ser* and *estar* contrasted; direct-object pronouns; reflexives; the personal *a*; irregular *yo* forms and *saber*/*conocer*; comparatives; a two-week review and composition. Instruction stays in English; Hear is used for Spanish words.
- **Seminar:** weeks 1–2 as before. Weeks 3–4: De Morgan’s laws; necessary and sufficient conditions; intervals; the triad; Aegean Bronze Age art (with a caution about restoration and about the name ‘Minoan’); the kouros and contrapposto; markets and prices; supply and demand (classroom numbers clearly labeled as invented); artistic anatomy 2 (origin, insertion, tendon; deltoid, biceps, gastrocnemius); poetry 2 (caesura, enjambment, volta), with Shakespeare, Sonnets 18 and 73.

- **Weeks 5–6 (added in full).** Mathematics: inscribed and central angles (Euclid III.20), angles in the same segment and the angle in a semicircle (III.21, III.31, traditionally Thales), cyclic quadrilaterals (III.22), intersecting chords (III.35), sums of arithmetic and geometric progressions, the circumcircle revisited, and a two-week set. Writing: parallelism and imitatio / Franklin hints / a précis of the opening of the Declaration of Independence (1776, public domain); participial phrases; the three-paragraph outline, reconstruction, attribution, concession, and a three-paragraph essay check. Latin: *Familia Romana* Capitula VII–VIII (dative; reflexive *sē*; *in* + accusative or ablative; *hic* and *ille*; the relative *quī, quae, quod*; third-conjugation *-iō* verbs; ablative of price), with original practice sentences. Physics: a first look at work and energy, beyond the Term 1 list (work and the joule; kinetic energy and the work–energy theorem; gravitational potential energy *m g h* near the Earth; mechanical energy with and without friction). Science: the start of *Living color* (why many leaves look green; photosynthesis as a labeled net equation; paper chromatography without invented R<sub>f</sub> values; autumn color, with a caution about anthocyanins). History: sources for early Rome (Livy late; 753 BCE traditional); the Republic (consuls, Senate; Polybius as a second-century analysis); the Twelve Tables as later fragments; the Punic Wars (264–146 BCE; Cannae 216, Zama 202) with Polybius; late-Republican participant sources (Caesar as advocacy as well as primary; Cicero’s letters); a Friday synthesis. Literature: *Odyssey* Books 1–12 (Telemachy; Calypso, the raft, and the Phaeacians; the wanderings as a tale told in Scheria; a written response on design), with short public-domain quotation from Butler (1898) when used. Atelier: a head block-in and working proportions (the plate wins); planes and a value pass; a plaster start; features in context; lost-and-found and accents; critique and a plan. Spanish (present only): e → i stems; the present progressive; indirect-object pronouns; *gustar*; *tener que* / *hay* / *hay que*; demonstratives; telling time; *por* and *para*; a two-week composition. Seminar: converse, inverse, and contrapositive; all / some / none (A, E, I, O) with a first look at the square of opposition; natural minor and relative minor; I, IV, and V and a first authentic cadence; High Classical Greece (Parthenon, Doryphoros type, copies and conventional dates); Hellenistic sculpture (Nike of Samothrace; Laocoön with a date-and-copy caution); specialization and trade (invented table); money’s three jobs (no invented first-coin year); artistic anatomy 3 (rib cage and pelvis); poetry 3 (imagery and metaphor) with Blake, “The Tyger” (1794, public domain).

- **Weeks 7 (added in full).** Mathematics: functions and f(x); graphs of linear functions (slope and intercept); graphs of quadratic functions (parabola, vertex from completing the square or x = −b/(2a)); two loci (circle; perpendicular bisector of AB as PA = PB); a Friday mix. Writing: topic sentences in a three-paragraph essay; imitatio of Bacon, “Of Studies” (1625), one short attributed sentence; a parenthetical interruption that keeps a series parallel; Franklin hints from that opening; a précis of the week’s model. Latin: *Familia Romana* Capitulum IX (third declension; numerals ūnus, duo, trēs; invariable numerals; quot; magnus numerus with the genitive; cēterī), with original practice sentences only. Physics: power P = W/t and the watt; a first look at torque (F times perpendicular distance, unit N·m, not called a joule); a Friday mix. Science: how we see (cornea, pupil, lens, retina; camera analogy as a map; optic nerve and the blind spot), with no invented focal lengths or receptor counts. History: Actium 31 BCE; 27 BCE as the conventional start of the principate (no single “fall of the Republic” day); the Res Gestae as a first-person record and advocacy; Tacitus and Suetonius as later writers; a Friday mix. Literature: Sophocles, *Antigone*, opening situation and two claims (Antigone and Creon), in a named public-domain translation, with no ending spoilers. Atelier: ear, hair as masses, neck and carriage, a second value pass, critique and a plan — still from the plate or plaster. Spanish (present only): *ir a* + infinitive; irregular presents of *ir, dar, ver, hacer, decir, venir*; dates; a week review and a short composition. Seminar: syllogism and Barbara (AAA-1); intervals (major third, perfect fifth); Roman art (Prima Porta as a copy type; Ara Pacis, conventional 13–9 BCE); a labeled invented budget; artistic anatomy of the arm (humerus, radius, ulna), with a caution about the word *radius*.

- **Week 8 (added in full).** Mathematics: the tangent-chord / alternate-segment theorem (Euclid III.32), proved from the radius-to-a-tangent fact and III.20; regular polygons (interior-angle sum (n − 2)×180°, each interior angle of a regular n-gon, and the matching exterior angle 360°/n); Vieta’s formulas for a quadratic; the inverse of a linear function (distinct from the logic inverse); a two-week mix. Writing: integrating a short attributed quotation; a three-paragraph plan that states two claims fairly; a concession paragraph; a three-paragraph draft; a précis of the draft, with a careful comment on the close of *Antigone* only after that close is taught. Latin: *Familia Romana* Capitulum X (further third-declension nouns *homō, leō, pēs*; present infinitives active and passive; *posse* + infinitive), with original practice sentences only. Physics: Newton’s law of gravitation as a first look (F = G Mm / r², inverse square; G as a standard constant); mass and weight (W = mg); a qualitative first look at orbits (Newton’s thought-picture; not every orbit a circle; no invented speed). Science: accommodation of the lens; two eyes as a first cue for judging distance — no invented focal lengths, diopters, or interocular measurements. History: 14 CE as the death of Augustus and the succession of Tiberius (not a second founding); the Julio-Claudian line as a later name, through 68 CE, with anecdotes not treated as simple facts; a two-week mix. Literature: *Antigone* continued (Haemon’s place) and then the close, with short attributed quotations from Jebb (1917). Atelier: the first move from last week’s plan; a likeness check of features; lost-and-found at hair and ear; a few accents; a two-week critique and plan. Spanish (present only): the superlative; weather phrases; *acabar de* + infinitive; object pronouns in two places with a two-verb pattern; a week review and a short composition. Seminar: the undistributed middle; interval inversion with the octave as the frame; the Pantheon (Hadrianic fabric, Agrippan inscription); saving a leftover (invented figures; a labeled invented rate); Shelley, “Ozymandias” (1818), short attributed extracts.

- **Week 9 (added in full).** Mathematics: composition of functions ((f ∘ g)(x) = f(g(x)); order matters); the equation of a circle as the coordinate form of an already-taught locus; composition with an inverse, giving the identity; a line and a circle as a system (two meetings, a tangent, or none); a Friday mix. Writing: because / but / so used on a source caution; a short attributed Dryden quotation; Franklin’s hints from that opening; a précis of the set lines only; a Friday close. Latin: a review of Capitula I–X only (first, second, and third family nouns; the present and the active infinitive; a short original reading; a Friday check), with original sentences, not a new chapter and not a textbook story. Physics: a first look at circular motion and a centripetal (center-seeking) force; the tangent direction if the inward pull stops; no invented orbital speed. Science: photoreceptors and color vision deficiency from National Eye Institute pages, still without a cell count; afterimages as a careful observation without an invented mechanism. History: 69 CE as a civil-war year after 68, not a fall of Rome; “Year of the Four Emperors” as a later name; Tacitus’s *Histories* opening (Loeb 1925, public domain) naming Galba, Otho, Vitellius, and Vespasian, with a career line under the Flavians; Flavian as a later name. Literature: Virgil’s *Aeneid*, Dryden’s 1697 opening only, later books reserved. Atelier: light on a sphere (one lamp; highlight, midtone, core shadow, cast shadow); a check and a turn; a held study; a simple-form critique and a short plan. Spanish (present only): possessive adjectives; tan… como; aquí, ahí, allí; deber + infinitive; a week review. Seminar: affirming the consequent and denying the antecedent; the minor triad; a Flavian later-name habit without an invented portrait; an incentive (invented shop); the hand in three groups (carpals, metacarpals, phalanges), without an invented official count.

- **Week 10 (added in full).** Mathematics: iteration of a function, f(f(x)); the incircle from equal tangents; a line and a parabola as a system; the incenter and A = r s; a Friday mix. Writing: because / but / so used on a Josephus source caution; a short attributed Dryden Book II quotation; Franklin’s hints from that opening; a précis of the set opening only; a Friday close. Latin: a review of Capitula I–X only (adjective agreement; place phrases with in, ad, and ē; quis, quid, and ubi; a short original reading; a Friday check), with original sentences, not a new chapter. Physics: g = G M / R² with sourced NASA Earth Fact Sheet figures (about 9.79 m/s², beside the working 9.8); g on the Moon from the same fact-sheet family (about 1.6 m/s², about one-sixth); a Friday mix; no invented orbital speed. Science: refractive errors from the National Eye Institute (myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism, presbyopia), without invented diopters or a classroom diagnosis. History: 70 CE as the conventional event-year Josephus dates to Vespasian’s second year, with a patronage caution from the *Life*; the Arch of Titus as a later commemoration (Platner and Ashby, 1929), with named spoils and an 1822 Valadier rebuild; a Friday mix. Literature: Dryden’s 1697 Book II opening, then a short attributed steed passage, later of Book II still reserved. Atelier: a cube under one lamp (planes of value, a check, a cast shadow, a few accents, a two-form critique and plan). Spanish (present only): saber vs conocer; me duele / me duelen; desde and hasta; affirmative pensar que / creer que; a week review and a short composition. Seminar: modus ponens and modus tollens; a leading tone in a minor key as a common practice for a major V, not a law; the Arch of Titus as a later relief, not a photograph; profit as a leftover in an invented shop example; Keats, “On First Looking into Chapman’s Homer” (1817).

- **Week 11 (added in full).** Mathematics: a quadratic inequality read from the graph; Euclid III.36 (tangent-secant, PT² = PA × PB); a downward-opening inequality and a perfect square that is never negative; a short mix that keeps the pictures separate; a Friday mix. Writing: because / but / so on a later letter in translation; a short attributed quotation from Firth’s Pliny; Franklin’s hints from the set pine extract; a précis of that extract only; a Friday close.
