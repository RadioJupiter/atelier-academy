# Scope and sequence

A 36-week year in three terms of 12 weeks, five school days a week. Weeks 1–33 are written in full (see [Lessons](lessons.html)); week 12 closes Term 1, week 13 opens Term 2, weeks 14–23 continue it, week 24 closes Term 2, and weeks 25–33 continue Term 3. The rest is planned here and will be written in the same format. The Term 2 science module *Living color* is begun in weeks 5–6 rather than inventing a filler topic. Regular polygons receive a first look in week 8 (interior and exterior angles); the fuller Term 2 treatment of regular-polygon area (and the apothem) is in week 16, and the circumference and area of a circle are in week 17. Week 9 adds composition of functions and the coordinate equation of a circle, and continues *How we see* from weeks 7–8 (photoreceptors and a careful afterimage observation, still without invented cell counts). Week 10 continues that seeing work with refractive errors (National Eye Institute), derives g = G M / R² from sourced NASA figures, and opens *Aeneid* Book II with short, attributed Dryden extracts only. Week 11 adds quadratic inequalities and Euclid III.36, computes g on Mars from the same formula, continues Dryden with a short hollow-side extract, and begins Pliny the Younger to Tacitus in Firth’s 1900 translation with a set pine-tree cloud passage only. Week 12 is the Term 1 consolidation: Mon–Thu re-teach already-taught methods (no new chapter, no Capitulum XI, no new Dryden or Pliny extract), and Friday (with Thursday science and literature) holds the Term 1 examinations, plus a vocabulary review mixing weeks 1–12. Week 13 opens Term 2: similar triangles and AA; a Toulmin analysis (grounds as the Day 8 evidence job) on a set Bliss-copy Lincoln extract; Capitulum XI (third-declension neuters and the accusative and infinitive); rotational equilibrium and the lever; the Water module (melting and boiling at 1 atm, then the triple point); Augustine on the sack of 410, and 476 as a conventional marker with the East continuing; the opening of Inferno in Longfellow (Canto I, lines 1–9); a return to the head as a full-head sitting; the preterite of regular -ar verbs; Celarent; triad inversion; the Arch of Constantine from Platner; credit and a bank, in labeled invented figures; and the leg (femur, tibia, fibula). Week 14 continues Term 2: the Pythagorean theorem and its converse; the précis; Capitulum XII (third-declension adjectives, the dative of possession, comparatives, and a first look at the fourth declension); the center of mass and stability; surface tension (IAPWS R1-76(2014)) and capillary rise; Justinian and the church called Sophia from Procopius, *Buildings*, in Dewing; Inferno I, Longfellow, lines 16–60; features on the blocked-in head; the regular -er and -ir preterite; Darii; I–IV–V–I; simple interest; and the arm (humerus, radius, ulna). Week 15 continues Term 2: area of a triangle, including a height found by Pythagoras; informal fallacies (straw man, false dilemma, ad hominem); Capitulum XIII (the year and the months; Kalendae, Nonae, and Īdūs; named days and inclusive counting, after Allen and Greenough); stable, unstable, and neutral equilibrium, and a center of mass by suspension; solutions (IUPAC); Anthemius and Isidorus, and the church’s light and proportion, from Buildings I.1.23–30 in Dewing, with no invented measurements; Inferno I, Longfellow, lines 61–90 and 112–136 (91–111 reserved); a value pass on the placed features; the irregular preterite of ser, ir, and hacer; Ferio; authentic and plagal cadences; the amount after simple interest; and the hand (carpus, metacarpals, phalanges). Week 16 continues Term 2: area of a parallelogram, a trapezoid, and a regular polygon (with the apothem; circle area reserved); circular reasoning and post hoc, then a multi-paragraph outline for the already-named analytical essay; Capitulum XIV (pair words, the present participle, mēcum, rēs); oscillation and the simple pendulum (OpenStax 16.4); saturated solutions and solubility (IUPAC); Buildings I.1.31–35 in Dewing; Inferno I.91–111 and II.1–42 in Longfellow; grisaille; the uv-stem preterite; Cesare; the deceptive cadence; finding the principal from the amount; and the foot. Week 17 continues Term 2: circumference and area of a circle, a sector, and a ring; the analytical essay assembled from last week’s outline; Capitulum XV (present persons, licet, sī and nisi); Hooke’s law and the mass-spring period (OpenStax 16.1, 16.3); density and why ice floats; Buildings I.1.36–49 in Dewing; Inferno II.52–142 in Longfellow; a grisaille lay-in; pude, puse, supe, and quise; Camestres; a musical period; the pendentive; a comparison of simple and compound interest; and the seven tarsal names from Gray. Week 18 continues Term 2: volume of a right prism and a right cylinder; Franklin’s method on the Inferno III gate, then a précis of Dewing I.1.50–57; Capitulum XV leftovers and the opening of Capitulum XVI (deponents and fiō); elastic potential energy and its conversion to kinetic energy (OpenStax 16.1 Example 16.2); the enthalpy of fusion and the specific heat of ice from Harvey/NIST; Buildings I.1.50–57 in Dewing; Inferno III.1–69 in Longfellow; modeling in the light, still monochrome; dije and traje; Festino; contrasting and parallel periods; equal installments on a known invented total; and the eight carpal names from Gray. Week 19 continues Term 2: volume of a pyramid, a cone, and a sphere, with the sphere’s surface area; Franklin’s method on the Inferno III leaves simile, then a précis of Dewing I.1.58–63; the ablative absolute; energy in simple harmonic motion and vmax (OpenStax 16.5, Example 16.6); the enthalpy of sublimation of ice from Harvey/NIST; Buildings I.1.58–63 in Dewing; Inferno III.70–136 in Longfellow; reflected light in the monochrome head, already taught on the egg; vi and di; Baroco; a musical sequence; simple interest on a declining principal; and the skull from Gray. Week 20 continues Term 2: surface area of a pyramid and a cone, then composite volume and surface; Franklin’s method on Inferno IV.25–42, then a précis of Dewing I.1.64–73; the perfect-participle ablative absolute; the projection of uniform circular motion as SHM (OpenStax 16.6); the latent heat of vaporization of water from OpenStax Table 14.2, kept distinct from Harvey’s 0 °C figures; Buildings I.1.64–73 in Dewing; Inferno IV.1–102 in Longfellow; the underplane of the jaw and the mouth as a slot, still monochrome; pidió and durmió; the third figure and Darapti; inversion of a short invented figure; a three-year repayment table; and the mandible from Gray. Week 21 continues Term 2: volume and surface of a conical frustum, then similar solids; Franklin’s method on Inferno IV.121–151, then a précis of Dewing I.1.74–78; first- and second-person present deponents; damped harmonic motion (OpenStax 16.7, including Example 16.7); Lv of water at 37.0 °C from OpenStax Table 14.2 footnotes, kept distinct from last week’s 100.0 °C row and from Harvey’s 0 °C figures; Buildings I.1.74–78 in Dewing; Inferno IV.103–151 in Longfellow; the eye as a volume in the socket, still monochrome; the regular imperfect (hablaba, comía, vivía); Felapton; retrograde of a short invented figure; a simple discount, invented figures; and the vertebral column from Gray. Week 22 continues Term 2: sine and cosine in a right triangle, then complementary angles, degrees only; Franklin’s method on Inferno V.1–72, then a précis of Dewing I.2.1–16; the deponent perfect; forced oscillations and resonance (OpenStax 16.8); the three methods of heat transfer (OpenStax 14.4); Buildings I.2.1–16 in Dewing; Inferno V in Longfellow; the ear as a volume, still monochrome; the irregular imperfects iba, era, and veía; Ferison; retrograde inversion of a short invented figure; successive discounts, invented figures; and the curves of the vertebral column from Gray, with official lengths reserved. Week 23 continues Term 2: tangent in a right triangle, then tan A = sin A / cos A and tan(90° − A) = 1 / tan A, degrees only; Franklin’s method on Inferno VI.1–57, then a précis of Dewing I.2.17–19; the deponent imperfect; waves, wavelength, and wave velocity (OpenStax 16.9), then transverse and longitudinal waves; the rate of conductive heat transfer (OpenStax 14.5), then Example 14.5; Buildings I.2.17–19 in Dewing; Inferno VI in Longfellow; the neck as a volume, still monochrome; choosing preterite versus imperfect; Datisi; a short invented sequence; a markup, then a discount, invented figures; and a typical vertebra from Gray, with official lengths reserved. Week 24 is the Term 2 consolidation and term examinations: already-taught methods are retaught Monday through Thursday, with the science and literature examinations on Thursday and the remaining Term 2 examinations on Friday; under 80% means re-teaching before Term 3; review lessons recall earlier vocabulary cards and add no new “close” or examination names to the glossary. Week 25 opens Term 3 with first looks in space geometry, the deponent pluperfect, a fable, OpenStax 16.10, ochre and hematite, Inter Caetera (May 3, 1493), Pride and Prejudice Chapter 1, a limited palette, the simple future, equally likely outcomes, binary form, Dewing I.3.1–5, a sales tax on invented figures, and the foramen transversarium. Week 26 continues Term 3 with the unit circle in degrees, a narrative after the fable, the present of sequor and ēgredior, OpenStax 16.11, lapis lazuli and ultramarine, Inter Caetera (May 4, 1493), Pride and Prejudice Chapter 2, a mixed earth hue, irregular Spanish futures, complementary events, ternary form, Dewing I.3.6–9 at Pegê, an income tax on invented figures, and the atlas. Week 27 continues with related angles and the law of sines, a chreia after the narrative, the pluperfect of sequor and ēgredior, OpenStax 17.1–17.2, azurite and malachite, the Treaty of Tordesillas, Pride and Prejudice Chapter 3, a cloth study, the Spanish conditional, mutually exclusive events, rondo form, Dewing I.3.10–15 at the Heraeum, insurance on invented figures, and the axis. Week 28 continues with the law of cosines, a maxim after the chreia, the future of sequor and ēgredior, OpenStax 17.3 and the decibel, cinnabar, the first-voyage letter to Luis de Santangel, Pride and Prejudice Chapter 4, a rounded form in the limited palette, the present subjunctive of regular verbs, independent events, theme and variations, Dewing I.3.16–18 on the rebuilt shrine of Michael, a deductible on invented figures, and the seventh cervical vertebra. Week 29 continues with the radian, a refutation after the maxim, the perfect of sequor and ēgredior, OpenStax 17.4 and the Doppler effect, electric current and a simple circuit, the first-voyage letter’s 178 leagues and rivers bearing gold, Pride and Prejudice Chapter 5, reflected light in the limited palette, the irregular present subjunctives of ser, ir, and estar, the addition rule for overlapping events, minuet and trio, Dewing I.4.1–8 on Peter and Paul and Saints Sergius and Bacchus, a simple budget on invented figures, and the thoracic vertebrae. Week 30 continues with the ambiguous case, a confirmation after the refutation, the future perfect of sequor and ēgredior, OpenStax 17.5 and air columns, Ohm’s law, the first-voyage letter’s 107 leagues along Juana and 188 leagues on one side of Española, Pride and Prejudice Chapter 6, two objects in the limited palette, the present subjunctive of haber, conditional probability, a waltz on an invented figure, Dewing I.4.9–16 on the church of the Apostles, saving on invented figures, and the first thoracic vertebra. Week 31 continues with the area of a sector in radians, a commonplace after the confirmation, the present subjunctive of sequor and ēgredior, OpenStax 25.1–25.2 on rays and the law of reflection, resistivity and official Example 20.5, the first-voyage letter’s fortress at Villa de Navidad and its February and March dates, Pride and Prejudice Chapter 7, a cylindrical form in the limited palette, the present perfect with haber, independent events, a march in duple time, Dewing I.4.25–26 on the Church of Acacius, investing on invented figures, and the twelfth thoracic vertebra. Week 32 continues with a first look at a limit, an encomium after the commonplace, a purpose clause with ut, OpenStax 25.3 on refraction, n = c / v, official Example 25.1, and Snell’s law, electric power and official Example 20.7, the second-voyage letter from Cadiz to the third of November, Pride and Prejudice Chapter 8, a second simple object beside the cylinder, irregular participles dicho, hecho, and escrito, expected value on an invented cube, binary form on an invented figure, Dewing I.5.1–6 on the sea around Byzantium, a gain returned to a leftover, and the lumbar vertebrae. Week 33 continues with the limit of a sum, a product, and a quotient, an invective after the encomium, a purpose clause with nē, official Example 25.3, alternating current and official household 120 V, the second-voyage letter from six islands to a fall of water, Pride and Prejudice Chapter 9, a background plane behind the two forms, the irregular participle visto, a mix of expected value and of binary and ternary form, Dewing I.5.7–13 on three straits and a bay of more than forty stades, a sales tax on an invented leftover, and the sacrum. Each Friday ends with a cumulative check, and weeks 12, 24 and 36 are consolidation weeks with term examinations.

## Mathematics

| Term | Geometry strand (Kiselev) | Algebra / analysis strand (Gelfand, Russian problem tradition) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (wk 1–12) | Axioms, angles, congruence, parallels, triangle inequalities, loci, constructions with compass and straightedge, quadrilaterals, the circle (chords, tangents, inscribed angles) | Bar-model modeling, identities and factoring, quadratics and Vieta, inequalities and AM–GM, functions and graphs, systems, exponents and radicals, arithmetic and geometric progressions |
| 2 (wk 13–24) | Similarity, the Pythagorean theorem (several proofs), area, regular polygons and the circumference and area of a circle, geometric constructions and impossibility (discussion) | Trigonometry (Gelfand & Saul): unit circle, identities, laws of sines and cosines; exponential and logarithmic functions; mathematical induction; complex numbers (introduction) |
| 3 (wk 25–36) | Solid geometry selections (Kiselev, *Stereometry*): lines and planes in space, prisms, pyramids, solids of revolution | Calculus in the Russian tradition: limits, continuity, the derivative and its rules, applications (tangents, optimisation, motion), the integral and the fundamental theorem; daily short problem sets in the style of Demidovich |

## Latin

Ørberg, *Lingua Latina per se Illustrata, Pars I: Familia Romana*: Capitula I–X in Term 1, XI–XVIII in Term 2, XIX–XXV in Term 3, with daily reading aloud, original exercises, and the book's own exercises (*pensa*) as additional practice. *Familia Romana* has 35 chapters; the remainder continues next year. Pace is set by mastery, not by the calendar.

## Physics (Peer Instruction)

Term 1: kinematics, Newton's laws, friction, motion in two dimensions, circular motion, momentum. Term 2: work and energy, rotation, gravitation and orbits, oscillations. Term 3: waves and sound, geometric optics (lenses, the eye, the camera), electric charge, circuits, magnetism.

## Science (phenomenon modules)

Each module starts from a real phenomenon and draws on chemistry, biology, physics and Earth science, with olympiad-style problem sets.
- Term 1: *Fire and air* (combustion, gases) · *The color of the sky* (light, scattering, polarization) · *Why oil paint dries* (oxidation and polymerization of drying oils; the chemistry of pigments)
- Term 2: *Living color* (photosynthesis, plant pigments, chromatography) · *How we see* (eye, retina, color vision, illusions) · *Water* (states, surface tension, solutions)
- Term 3: *From rock to pigment* (minerals, ochres, lapis lazuli to ultramarine) · *Electricity at home* · *Weather and climate*

## History (chronological, primary sources)

Term 1: the ancient Near East, Egypt, Greece, the Hellenistic world, Rome (Republic and Empire). Term 2: late antiquity, Byzantium, the Islamic world, medieval Europe, the Renaissance and Reformation. Term 3: exploration and empires, the Scientific Revolution, the Enlightenment, the age of revolutions, the 19th and 20th centuries.

## Literature (great books)

Term 1: Homer, *Iliad* and *Odyssey*; Sophocles, *Antigone*; Virgil, *Aeneid* (selected books). Term 2: Dante, *Inferno*; Chaucer (selections); Shakespeare, *Macbeth* and the sonnets; Cervantes, *Don Quixote* (selections). Term 3: Austen, *Pride and Prejudice*; Mary Shelley, *Frankenstein*; Tolstoy, *The Death of Ivan Ilyich*; a poetry anthology from Wordsworth to the 20th century.

## Seminar rotation

- **Logic:** propositional logic (Term 1); categorical syllogisms and informal fallacies (Term 2); probability and evidence (Term 3).
- **Music theory:** notation, scales, key signatures, intervals (Term 1); triads, chords, cadences, simple harmony (Term 2); form and listening (Term 3), with ear training throughout.
- **Art history:** chronological alongside History: prehistoric to Roman (Term 1), medieval to Renaissance (Term 2), Baroque to modern (Term 3).
- **Economics & personal finance:** scarcity, choice, markets, prices (Term 1); money, banking, interest, budgets, credit (Term 2); investing, taxes, insurance, and introductory macroeconomics (Term 3).
- **Electives:** see [Electives](electives.html).

## Modern language

The language of instruction for the whole course is English. Spanish is taught as a daily subject (see [Electives](electives.html) if another modern language is preferred): present tense and core vocabulary (Term 1), past tenses (Term 2), future, conditional and introduction to the subjunctive (Term 3), with graded readers from Term 2. Latin is a separate daily subject, taught from *Lingua Latina per se Illustrata*.
