<p>Dewing continues. The Emperor built the church “not with money alone,” but also “with labour of the mind and with the other powers of the soul, as I shall straightway shew.” One of the arches toward the east had been built up from either side, but was not wholly completed in the middle. The piers, unable to carry the mass, suddenly began to crack, and seemed on the point of collapsing. Anthemius and Isidorus, “terrified at what had happened,” having “come to have no hope in their technical skill,” carried the matter to the Emperor. He, “impelled by I know not what, but I suppose by God (for he is not himself a master-builder),” commanded them to carry the curve to its final completion. “For when it rests upon itself,” he said, “it will no longer need the props beneath it.” If the story were without witness, Dewing says, it would have seemed flattery and incredible; but there are many witnesses. The artisans carried out the instructions, “and the whole arch then hung secure, sealing by experiment the truth of his idea.”</p>
<p>Keep “labour” and “shew” inside quotations. Do not invent a width for the crack, or a modern engineering proof Dewing does not give.</p>
