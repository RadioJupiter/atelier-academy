Last week named the arm, **palm-forward**: humerus; radius on the thumb side; ulna on the little-finger side. Today continues into the **hand**, still palm-forward.

Three groups, not a first list of every small name:

- The **carpus** is the wrist, treated today as a **group of short bones** between the forearm and the palm. The eight individual carpal names remain reserved.
- The **metacarpals** are the long bones of the palm; one metacarpal leads toward each digit.
- The **phalanges** are the bones of the digits. Each of the four fingers has three (proximal, middle, distal); the **thumb has two** (proximal and distal).

The thumb sits on the **radius** side, already taught. This lesson does not invent official lengths in centimeters, and it does not begin the muscles of the hand.

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>A model answer for a near-miss item, so the others can be written in the same shape.</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Question: What is the area of a triangle, and why is “½ times base times a slanted side” a miss?</li>
<li>The area is ½bh, where h is the perpendicular height to the chosen base. A triangle is half of a rectangle with the same base and the same perpendicular height.</li>
<li>A slanted side is not h, because it does not meet the base at a right angle. A 6-8-10 right triangle has area ½ × 6 × 8 = 24; the hypotenuse 10 is not the height to a leg.</li>
<li>A near-miss is to drop the one-half (area 48 for that triangle), or to use 5 as the height of a 5-5-6 isosceles triangle instead of finding h = 4.</li>
</ol>

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. A = ½bh, with h perpendicular. The base 6 splits into 3 and 3; 3² + h² = 5², so h = 4; A = ½ × 6 × 4 = 12.
2. Kalendae are the first day of a Roman month. The Īdūs of March fall on 15 March. These facts follow Allen and Greenough, New Latin Grammar, §631.
3. Ferio is EIO: no M are P; some S are M; therefore some S are not P. The minor is only particular, so the conclusion stays “some S are not P,” not the universal “no S are P.”
4. Longfellow: “Not man; man once I was.” Dewing: “the most learned man in the skilled craft which is known as the art of building.”
5. ser/ir: fui, fue. hacer: hice, hizo. The z is part of the él/ella/usted form hizo, which is not *hico and not the yo form hice.
6. An authentic cadence arrives from V to I. A plagal cadence arrives from IV to I. Both arrive at the tonic; they differ in the leading chord.
7. I = 200 × 0.05 × 3 = 30; A = 200 + 30 = 230. The rate is not an official rate at a named bank.
8. Carpus: the wrist, as a group of short bones (individual names reserved). Metacarpals: the long bones of the palm, one toward each digit. Phalanges: the bones of the digits; the thumb has two, the other fingers three.
