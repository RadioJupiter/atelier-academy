## Mathematics · Geometry LXXXIV: arc length in radians, s = rθ {#math}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: s = rθ when θ is in radians; invented figures labeled invented</p>

<p>Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.</p>
</div>
<p>From the definition of a radian, the arc length s cut off by a central angle θ, when θ is measured in radians, is s = rθ. That is the definition rearranged: θ = s/r, so s = rθ. The already-taught degree form of arc length used a different conversion; today the radian form is used, and the angle must be in radians, not in degrees, before the product is taken.</p><p>On an invented circle of radius 3 with a central angle of 2 radians, s = 3 × 2 = 6. On an invented circle of radius 6 with a central angle of π/3 radians (that is, 60°), s = 6 × (π/3) = 2π. If an invented arc is π and the radius is 2, then θ = s/r = π/2 radians, which is 90°. The radii and the 2 radians are invented classroom numbers. Do not multiply a degree measure by the radius as if it were already in radians, and do not invent an official arc on a real wheel.</p>

</div>

## Seminar · Music: minuet and trio, a first look, on an invented figure {#seminar}
