<p>Ēgredior is a deponent of the third conjugation in -iō. Its present, taught two weeks ago, is ēgredior, ēgrederis, ēgreditur; its pluperfect, taught last week, is ēgressus eram. Today the future: ēgrediar, I shall go out; ēgrediēris; ēgrediētur; ēgrediēmur; ēgrediēminī; ēgredientur. The -iē- in the middle forms is the -iō future marker, not the ē of sequētur. Puer ēgrediētur is he will go out, still ahead. Sequar remains I shall follow. Sentences are original, not copied from Ørberg. Identity in a sentence is given with Puer or Puella, not with an unquoted English “boy” or “girl.”</p>

## Mathematics · Geometry LXXX: the law of cosines with an obtuse included angle {#math}

<p>When the included angle is obtuse, its cosine is negative, so the term −2ab cos C becomes an addition, and the opposite side is longer than it would be in an acute or right triangle with the same two sides. Cos 120° = −1/2, from last week’s related angles, because 120° = 180° − 60°, and cos(180° − θ) = −cos θ.</p>

</div><p>On an invented triangle with sides 4 and 4 and included angle 120°, c² = 16 + 16 − 2·4·4·(−1/2) = 32 + 16 = 48, so c = √48 = 4√3. Do not take cos 120° as +1/2, and do not switch to radians.</p>

## Modern language · Spanish: the present subjunctive of regular -er and -ir verbs {#language}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: coma and viva; contrasted with the indicative and with hable</p>

<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-present-subjunctive-of-regular-er-and-ir-verbs">

<dl class="frayer">

## Seminar · Music: theme and variations, a first look {#seminar}

<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>Theme and variations is a form in which a short theme is stated and then repeated with changes, each repetition a variation, while the theme remains recognizable. It is not rondo form, and it is not a copyrighted score.</dd></div>
