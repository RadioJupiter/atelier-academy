## Physics · Waves, wavelength, and wave velocity, a first look {#physics}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: a first look at a wave, λ, and vw = λ/T = fλ, from OpenStax College Physics 2e, section 16.9, including Example 16.8</p>
<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>
<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-wave-wavelength-and-wave-velocity">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="wave, wavelength, and wave velocity" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear wave, wavelength, and wave velocity">Hear</button> wave, wavelength, and wave velocity</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>In OpenStax College Physics 2e, section 16.9, a wave is a disturbance that propagates, or moves from the place it was created. Wavelength, λ, is the distance between adjacent identical parts of the wave. Wave velocity vw is the speed at which the disturbance moves, which is one wavelength in one period: vw = λ / T, or vw = fλ, since f = 1/T, already taught. This relation holds for all types of waves named in the section. The medium itself need not travel with the disturbance.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>OpenStax Example 16.8: if the distance between wave crests is 10.0 m and the time for a sea gull to bob up and down is 5.00 s, then λ = 10.0 m, T = 5.00 s, and vw = 2.00 m/s.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not last week’s resonance of a system that stays in place while it is driven. Not a claim that a water wave far from shore pushes the water itself from one place to another; OpenStax warns that the energy moves forward and the water, far from shore, stays in one location aside from a local motion. Superposition and interference remain reserved for a later section.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Already-taught period and frequency, f = 1/T, and last week’s forced oscillation, which is not yet a travelling disturbance.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
</div>
<p>Last week’s forced oscillator was driven in place. Today, from OpenStax 16.9, a wave is a disturbance that propagates, or moves from the place it was created. For a simplified water wave, the surface is disturbed up and down; a sea gull bobs in simple harmonic motion as crests and troughs pass, and the time for one complete up-and-down motion is the wave’s period T, already taught, with f = 1/T. The wave moving to the right is the disturbance moving to the right, not the water itself.</p>
<p>Wavelength λ is the distance between adjacent identical parts of the wave, parallel to the direction of propagation. Wave velocity vw is the speed at which the disturbance moves: one wavelength in one period, so vw = λ / T, or vw = fλ. OpenStax notes that this holds for all types of waves, including water waves, sound, and visible light; official measured speeds of sound or of light are not required today and are not invented. <div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>Datisi is a valid third-figure mood, AII-3: All M are P; some M are S; therefore some S are P. It is distinguished from Darapti (AAI-3), already taught, which has two universal premises, from Felapton (EAO-3), and from Ferison (EIO-3).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>All classroom stamps are inked. Some classroom stamps are square. Therefore some square things are inked. The stamps are the middle term M, invented for the example.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not a first look at the third figure, or at Darapti, Felapton, or Ferison, already taught. Not first-figure Darii, which has the same vowels but a different figure. Not a claim about an official fact outside the invented terms.</dd></div>

<p>Last week’s Ferison was EIO-3: No M are P; some M are S; therefore some S are not P. Today a first look at Datisi, AII-3, still in the third figure, so M is the subject of both premises: All M are P; some M are S; therefore some S are P.</p>
<p>An invented classroom example: All classroom stamps are inked. Some classroom stamps are square. Therefore some square things are inked. Darapti, already taught, also concludes some S are P, but from two universal premises (All M are P; all M are S). Felapton and Ferison conclude a particular negative. First-figure Darii has the vowels AII but a different placement of M, and is not today’s mood. Invented terms stay invented.</p>
