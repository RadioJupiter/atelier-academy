<p>Monday named the three kinds of damping. OpenStax Example 16.7 now supplies sourced figures for one underdamped case, and a sourced contrast.</p>
<p>A 0.200 kg object is connected to a spring of k = 50.0 N/m, on a horizontal surface, with simple friction μk = 0.0800. It is released from rest at X = 0.100 m from equilibrium. The frictional force is f = μk mg. OpenStax computes f = (0.0800)(0.200 kg)(9.80 m/s²) = 0.157 N. The 9.80 m/s² is the value used in that example, not a different official g blended in from another page.</p>
<p>The motion is horizontal, so gravitational potential energy is not in the account. The energy begins as PE_el,i = ½kX². Friction removes energy by Wnc = −fd, where d is the total distance traveled, back and forth. OpenStax finds d = 1.59 m. The system is underdamped: it crosses equilibrium more than d/X = 15.9 times in name only as a comparison, because the amplitude is falling, and it does not finish exactly at x = 0, because static friction will at last exceed the restoring force.</p>
<p>OpenStax’s own contrast: if this system had a damping force 20 times greater, it would move only 0.0484 m toward equilibrium from the same 0.100 m start. That 0.0484 m is sourced, not invented.</p>

The figures 0.157 N, 1.59 m, and 0.0484 m are OpenStax’s. Invented classroom cases, if used below, are labeled invented.</p>

3. The first-person and third-person singular share the form, as in the -ar imperfect. The subject, or the rest of the sentence, tells them apart.

<p>This week’s history lessons already read Dewing I.1.74–78. Today that close of Book I, chapter 1, is reread as a question of how a work is repaired, and then left to stand, rather than as a first look at the passage.</p>
<p>The south and north arches were, in Dewing’s word, “labouring under their load.” The columns threw off tiny flakes, “as if they had been planed.” The master-builders were dismayed once more, and reported to the Emperor. The remedy was to remove the strained upper masonry in contact with the arches, and to put it back later, when the dampness had abated enough to bear it. The structure then stood secure. The Emperor enjoys “a kind of testimonial from the work.”</p>
<p>This is not last week’s cure, in which the eastern arch was completed so that the work could rest upon itself. Today’s reread holds the delayed replacement, and the testimonial, without inventing a flake width or an official waiting-time.</p>
