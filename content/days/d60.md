<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>A model answer for a near-miss item, so the others can be written in the same shape.</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Question: How is a quadratic inequality read from the graph of y = (x − 2)(x − 6), and how is that different from Euclid III.36?</li>
<li>The parabola opens upward. y < 0 between the roots: 2 < x < 6. y > 0 outside the roots.</li>
<li>III.36 is a different picture: from an exterior point, PT² = PA × PB. The 2 and the 6 in the inequality are roots, not automatically a tangent and a secant.</li>

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. puer is nominative (the one who gives); rosam is accusative (the thing given); puellae is dative (the person to whom). Term 1 stays in Capitula I–X; a new chapter is not begun early.
2. No real solution. A square is never negative; it is 0 only at x = 3, and positive elsewhere.
3. PT² = PA × PB, so 36 = 4 × PB, and PB = 9. 10 would be a sum (4 + 6), which is not the theorem.
4. A sound sentence: the letter can mention about the seventh hour because Firth’s wording says the mother pointed the cloud out then, but it is a later letter in a 1900 translation, so I will not treat the phrase as a stopwatch reading, and I will not invent Pompeii.
5. About 1.6 m/s², about one-sixth of 9.8, from the already-used NASA fact-sheet family, with two significant figures respected.
6. Myopia: far-away objects look blurry. Hyperopia: nearby objects look blurry. Neither is a classroom diagnosis.
7. Firth: the cloud “more closely resembled a pine-tree than anything else, for what corresponded to the trunk was of great length and height, and then spread out into a number of branches.” The set extract does not name Pompeii, and it does not give a death toll.
8. The tool is a brush (P or Q; not P; therefore Q). Under this course’s default inclusive “or,” P does not give not-Q unless the problem says exactly one.
9. A half cadence pauses on V; it does not close. An authentic cadence moves V to I and can close. Still a major key, with I, IV, and V only, in this course’s school pattern.
10. The inward eye is a figure of later memory (“They flash upon that inward eye / Which is the bliss of solitude”). “Ten thousand saw I at a glance” is a figure in the poem, not a counted census, and not a biology term.
