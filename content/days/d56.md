**The jobs, already taught.** Nominative: the subject (*puella habet*). Accusative: the object (*rosam habet*). Genitive: ‘of’ (*rosa puellae*, the girl’s rose, when that pattern has been taught). Dative: the person to or for whom (*puellae dat*). Ablative: already-taught phrases such as *in hortō* (in the garden). They are different jobs.

**A model.** *Puer puellae rosam dat.* The boy gives the girl a rose. *Puer* names the giver (nominative). *rosam* names the thing given (accusative). *puellae* names the person to whom (dative).

Week 11 taught how to **read a quadratic inequality from the graph**. Today is a close of that method, not a first look and not a new kind of number.

**The method, already taught.** Write one side as zero. The roots split the number line. If the parabola opens upward (a > 0), y < 0 *between* the roots, and y > 0 *outside* them. If it opens downward, those regions swap. A square (x − a)² is never negative: (x − a)² < 0 has no real solution, and this course does not begin complex numbers to “solve” it.

**The same worked shape.** y = (x − 2)(x − 6) opens upward. y < 0 when **2 < x < 6**. The shading in the figure is that region.

Keep this picture separate from Euclid III.36. Roots are not automatically a tangent and a secant.

The formula is the one already taught: **g = G M / R²**, with **G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²** (three significant figures). Today is a close of the three already-computed fields, not a first look and not a new formula.

**Earth**, from the NASA Earth Fact Sheet mass and equatorial radius used in week 10: about **9.79 m/s²**, beside this course’s working **9.8 m/s²**. The computation is limited by G’s three significant figures.

**The Moon**, from the Planetary Fact Sheet family: about **1.6 m/s²**, about one-sixth of 9.8, limited by the two significant figures in the sheet’s mass, and sitting beside NASA Science’s “one-sixth.” Those wordings are not blended into a homemade official third number.

**Mars**, from last week: mass **0.642 × 10²⁴ kg**, diameter **6,792 km** so R = **3,396 km**. g comes out about **3.71 m/s²**, reported as **about 3.7 m/s²**, the same two-figure value the fact sheet lists. 3.7 / 9.8 ≈ 0.38, about 19/50, a little less than 2/5.

We will not invent an orbital speed for any of the three, and we will not claim more significant figures than G and M support.
