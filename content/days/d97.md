## Mathematics · Geometry LII: the surface area of a right cone {#math}

## Science · The latent heat of vaporization of water, from OpenStax {#science}

<p class="meta">40 min · Method: OpenStax College Physics 2e, Table 14.2 — Lv of water is 2256 kJ/kg at 100.0 °C, and Q = mLv. This is the official boiling figure last week would not invent from Harvey’s 0 °C rows</p>

<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>OpenStax, College Physics 2e, Table 14.2, lists the latent heat of vaporization of water as 2256 kJ/kg (540 kcal/kg) at the normal boiling point, 100.0 °C. The energy to vaporize a mass m, without a temperature change during the change of phase, is Q = mLv. In this table’s units, 2256 kJ/kg is 2256 J/g. This is not Harvey/NIST’s 0 °C table, and it is not last week’s difference 2834 − 333.4, which this course refused to treat as an official 100 °C figure.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>OpenStax’s own example: it would take 2256 kJ to change 1 kg of liquid water at the normal boiling point into steam. An invented classroom mass of 10 g, or 0.010 kg, would need 22.56 kJ, and is labeled invented.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not Harvey’s ΔfusH(0 °C) = 333.4 J/g, or ΔsublH(0 °C) = 2834 J/g, already taught from a different table. OpenStax also lists Lf = 334 kJ/kg for water; that is close to Harvey’s 333.4 J/g, but the two figures are from different tables and are not blended. Evaporation below the boiling point, which OpenStax also discusses, is reserved.</dd></div>

## Elective · Music: inversion of a short invented figure {#seminar}
