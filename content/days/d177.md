<p>Yesterday reviewed purpose clauses. Today reviews already-taught result clauses. A result clause marks what happens as a result, often with tam, ita, or sic: ut for a positive result, ut nōn for a negative result, not nē. Puella tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur: she comes so quickly that she follows him. Puella tam lentē venit ut eum nōn sequātur: she comes so slowly that she does not follow him. The verb is still the present subjunctive, not the future. No new construction. Original classroom sentences only.</p>
