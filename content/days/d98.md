<p class="meta">45 min · Method: the volume of a composite solid is the sum (or difference) of already-taught volumes, with a shared face not counted twice</p>

<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>A composite solid is made by joining or cutting already-taught solids, such as a cube and a pyramid, or a cone and a hemisphere. ## Physics · The speed of the projection, from similar triangles {#physics}

<p>History has already read I.1.67–73. Today the same stretch is reread as Dewing’s claim that the church was a work of mind, “not with money alone.”</p>
<p>The example is the eastern arch, unfinished in the middle, the piers cracking, the master-builders having “no hope in their technical skill,” and the Emperor, “not himself a master-builder,” commanding them to complete the curve, “For when it rests upon itself, it will no longer need the props beneath it.” The arch then “hung secure, sealing by experiment the truth of his idea.”</p>
<p>Keep “labour” and “shew” inside quotations. Do not invent a modern official span or a later technical name as if Dewing had used it. The rereading is of his claim and his example, not a replacement of them by a modern report. The south and north arches remain reserved.</p>
