## Mathematics · Geometry LXXXVIII: the two-triangle case of SSA, with exact invented sides {#math}

<p>Yesterday, on invented figures with acute A, a was compared with h = b sin A. Today the case h &lt; a &lt; b, which gives two triangles. On invented figures, A = 30°, b = 2, and a = √2. Then h = 1, and 1 &lt; √2 &lt; 2, so there are two triangles. The figures are invented, not official measurements.</p><p>The law of sines gives sin B / b = sin A / a, so sin B = b sin A / a = 2 × (1/2) / √2 = 1/√2. So B is 45°, or 180° − 45° = 135°. Both can occur, because a is shorter than b, and B may be acute or obtuse. If B = 45°, then C = 180° − 30° − 45° = 105°. If B = 135°, then C = 180° − 30° − 135° = 15°. There are two different triangles: 30°-45°-105°, and 30°-135°-15°. Taking only 45° and ignoring 135° is a miss. 30° is also π/6, and 45° is also π/4, already taught; the work today is kept in degrees.</p>
