<p>Last week’s purpose clause used ut and the present subjunctive to mark an intended purpose: Puella venit ut eum sequātur, she comes in order that she follow him. Today’s first look is a result clause. A result clause marks what happens as a result, not what is intended. It is often signaled by tam, ita, or sic in the main clause. Puella tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur: she comes so quickly that she follows him. The verb in the ut-clause is still the already-taught present subjunctive, sequātur with ā, not the future sequētur with ē. The first-person sequar is still spelled the same as the future, so a result clause is clearer in the other persons. The sentences are original classroom Latin, not copied from Ørberg. Identity is handled with Puer and Puella, and with he and she in the English gloss, not with an unquoted English “boy” or “girl.” A negative result uses ut nōn, not nē, and waits until tomorrow. Nē still marks a negative purpose.</p>

## Physics · Total internal reflection, and the critical angle, a first look {#physics}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: OpenStax 25.4; total internal reflection when n₁ > n₂ and the incident angle is greater than θc; θc = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁); official Example 25.4 reserved until Wednesday</p>
<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>
<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-total-internal-reflection-and-the-critical-angle-a-first-look">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="total internal reflection, and the critical angle, a first look" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear total internal reflection, and the critical angle, a first look">Hear</button> total internal reflection, and the critical angle, a first look</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>Total internal reflection, in this first look from OpenStax 25.4, occurs when a ray in a medium of index n₁ strikes a boundary with a medium of smaller index n₂, n₁ > n₂, at an incident angle greater than the critical angle. The critical angle θc is the incident angle that produces an angle of refraction of 90°. From Snell’s law, already taught, θc = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁), when n₁ > n₂. Official numerical examples, including official Example 25.4, wait until Wednesday. There is no total internal reflection when the second medium has the larger index.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>The largest the angle of refraction can be is 90°. The incident angle that produces that 90° refraction is the critical angle. If the incident angle is greater than that critical angle, all of the light is reflected, in the book’s wording. The formula is reserved for official numbers on Wednesday.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not official Example 25.4, which waits until Wednesday. Not a claim that total internal reflection can occur when n₂ is larger than n₁, for example from air into water. Not last week’s official Example 25.3 as if it had already named the critical angle. Not an invented official index.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Last week’s official Example 25.3, and earlier Snell’s law. Official Example 25.4, with official 42.2°, waits until Wednesday.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
</div>
<p>Last week’s official Example 25.3 used Snell’s law for a ray going from air into diamond, with official θ₂ = 11.9°. OpenStax 25.4 now asks what happens as the incident angle increases, when a ray is in a medium of larger index n₁ and meets a medium of smaller index n₂. The angle of refraction θ₂ also increases. The largest θ₂ can be is 90°. The critical angle θc for a combination of materials is defined to be the incident angle θ₁ that produces an angle of refraction of 90°. If the incident angle is greater than the critical angle, then, in the book’s wording, “all of the light is reflected.” That is total internal reflection. From already-taught Snell’s law, with θ₂ = 90° and sin 90° = 1, the book’s formula is θc = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁), used when n₁ > n₂. There is no total internal reflection for rays going in the other direction, for example from air to water, because the second medium must have the smaller index. Official numerical values, including official Example 25.4, wait until Wednesday. Official discussion angles such as water-to-air remain reserved.</p>

</div>
