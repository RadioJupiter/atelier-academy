A **regular polygon** is already known from Day 37: all sides equal, all interior angles equal. Today’s job is its **area**, not a second first look at the name.

From the center, the polygon divides into **n congruent isosceles triangles** (n is the number of sides). The perpendicular from the center to a side is the **apothem**. It is the height of each of those triangles (the altitude meets the side at its midpoint, as in last week’s isosceles work).

Area of one triangle: ½ × (one side) × (apothem). All n of them: **A = ½ × (perimeter) × (apothem)**.

## Science · Water: solubility, at a specified temperature {#science}

<p class="meta">45 min · Science · solubility from IUPAC Gold Book S05740; no official table is invented</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

<div class="keyterms">

<article class="term" id="term-solubility">

<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Yesterday’s saturated solution is the solution at that equilibrium. Today names the composition of that solution as solubility, from S05740. Temperature still has to be specified, as in S05471.</dd></div>

Yesterday a **saturated solution** was defined at a specified temperature and pressure. Today names the **composition** of that solution.

The IUPAC Gold Book (S05740) says: the analytical composition of a saturated solution, expressed as the proportion of a designated solute in a designated solvent, is the **solubility** of that solute. It may be written as a concentration, a molality, a mole fraction, or another composition unit.

In school language: solubility is **how much solute is in the saturated solution**, for that solute and that solvent, at the stated conditions. It is a quantity, not a second word for leftover solid.

If the temperature changes, the solubility may change. That is why the definition sits on a specified temperature (and pressure). This lesson does **not** invent an official percentage, a textbook table, or a molarity. If a later source gives a tabulated solubility, it will be cited then.

A classroom statement such as ‘this sugar-water looks stronger when warm’ is an observation, not an official number, and must be labeled as a classroom observation if you use it.

<p class="note">Definitions of a saturated solution and of solubility follow the IUPAC Gold Book: a saturated solution is a solution which has the same concentration of a solute as one that is in equilibrium with undissolved solute at specified values of the temperature and pressure (Gold Book S05471, https://doi.org/10.1351/goldbook.S05471, https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/S05471.html); solubility is the analytical composition of a saturated solution, expressed as the proportion of a designated solute in a designated solvent (Gold Book S05740, https://doi.org/10.1351/goldbook.S05740, https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/S05740.html). No official percentage, solubility table, or molarity figure is invented this week. Classroom dishes and leftover solid, if used as a demonstration, are labeled invented.</p>
