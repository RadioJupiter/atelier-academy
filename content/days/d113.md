## Mathematics · Geometry LXV: tangent of complementary angles {#math}

<div class="keyterms">

<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="complementary tangents" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear complementary tangents">Hear</button> complementary tangents</h5>

<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>If A and B are the two acute angles of a right triangle, already known to add to 90°, then tan A = 1 / tan B, because the side opposite A is adjacent to B, and the side adjacent to A is opposite B. ## Physics · Transverse and longitudinal waves, from OpenStax 16.9 {#physics}

<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>In OpenStax 16.9, a transverse wave, or shear wave, has its disturbance perpendicular to the direction of propagation, as in a wave on a cord, a guitar string, or visible light. A longitudinal wave, or compressional wave, has its disturbance parallel to the direction of propagation, as in sound in air or water. Amplitude is independent of the wave speed vw. </dl></article>

<p>Monday’s wave velocity still holds for both kinds of wave named today. The new distinction, from the same OpenStax section 16.9, is the direction of the disturbance relative to the direction of propagation.</p>
<p>Transverse, or shear: the disturbance is perpendicular to propagation. Waves on the strings of musical instruments are transverse, and so are electromagnetic waves such as visible light. Longitudinal, or compressional: the disturbance is parallel to propagation. Sound waves in air and water are longitudinal, because fluids do not have appreciable shear strength. Sound in solids can be both. Water waves are actually a combination; the simplified Monday figure showed no longitudinal motion of the bird. Amplitude X is independent of vw. Earthquake waves under Earth’s surface have both components, called compressional or P-waves and shear or S-waves, and OpenStax notes that they propagate at different speeds. Those official speeds are not given as numbers here and are not invented. Superposition remains reserved.</p>
