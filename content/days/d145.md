<p>Friday mixes this week’s sea, vaya, and esté with last week’s regular present subjunctives, the future, and the conditional. “Quiere que” in these invented sentences takes the subjunctive. “Mañana” without that frame often takes the future. “En tu lugar” often takes the conditional. <p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Sea, vaya, and esté are irregular present subjunctives. They are not es, va, or está; not será, irá, or estará; and not sería, iría, or estaría.

## Seminar · Anatomy: the thoracic vertebrae, from Gray 1918, a first look {#seminar}

<p>Gray’s 1918 <em>Anatomy of the Human Body</em>, as posted by Theodora, is still the artistic-anatomy set text. Last week ended with the seventh cervical vertebra, vertebra prominens. Today the thoracic vertebrae, in Gray’s wording. “The thoracic vertebræ are intermediate in size between those of the cervical and lumbar regions; they increase in size from above downward, the upper vertebræ being much smaller than those in the lower part of the region.” “Vertebræ” is Gray’s spelling and stays inside the quotation. Teaching prose uses US spelling, “vertebrae.”</p><p>“They are distinguished by the presence of facets on the sides of the bodies for articulation with the heads of the ribs, and facets on the transverse processes of all, except the eleventh and twelfth, for articulation with the tubercles of the ribs.” “The bodies in the middle of the thoracic region are heart-shaped, and as broad in the antero-posterior as in the transverse direction.” “The spinous process is long, triangular on coronal section, directed obliquely downward, and ends in a tuberculated extremity.” Gray does not give official classroom lengths here, and none are invented.</p><p>“The first, ninth, tenth, eleventh, and twelfth thoracic vertebræ present certain peculiarities, and must be specially considered.” Those peculiarities are not today’s first look and remain reserved. The lesson is artistic anatomy from a public-domain text, not a medical diagnosis, and no body is being described from life. Official lengths remain reserved. Lumbar vertebrae remain reserved.</p>

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>A model answer for a near-miss item, so the others can be written in the same shape.</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Question: What is a radian, and why is using a degree measure in s = rθ a miss?</li>
<li>A radian is the angle at the center of a circle for which the arc length equals the radius. The full turn is 2π radians, which is 360°.</li>
<li>In s = rθ, θ must be in radians, not in degrees. Multiplying r by 90, for a right angle, is a miss.</li>
<li>A second miss is to replace π with 3.14 in exact work, when the exact value, such as s = 2π, is wanted.</li>
</ol>

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. A radian is the central angle whose arc equals the radius; 2π rad = 360°, so π rad = 180°, and π/3 = 60°. Arc length s = rθ = 6 × (π/3) = 2π.
2. Secūtus sum, I followed, or I have followed; sequor, I follow; sequar, I shall follow; secūtus eram, I had followed. Ēgressus sum is I went out, or I have gone out, the perfect of ēgredior, a different verb.
3. Sea; vaya; esté. Vaya: “that she go” is ir, not ser, and not the indicative va.
4. For a moving source, f_obs = f_s × v_w / (v_w ± v_s), minus toward the observer, plus away. Official Example 17.4: approaching 167 Hz, receding 136 Hz, and the engineer hears 150 Hz. 340 m/s is that example’s official speed of sound, and is not to be replaced by 331 or 343.
5. Current is I = ΔQ/Δt, charge passing a point per unit time; 1 A = 1 C/s. Example 20.1(a): 720 C in 4.00 s is 180 A.
6. “One hundred and seventy-eight full leagues due east.” “188 leagues” is Major’s editorial footnote, not the letter’s body, and is not taught as an official correction. Do not convert the 178 leagues into official modern miles.
7. “I could easily forgive his pride, if he had not mortified mine.” Mary: “Vanity and pride are different things, though the words are often used synonimously.” “Pride relates more to our opinion of ourselves, vanity to what we would have others think of us.” “Synonimously” is the 1813 wording.
8. P = 3/6 + 3/6 − 2/6 = 2/3. The remainder is $10, because 40 + 20 + 10 = 70 and 80 − 70 = 10. A deductible is borne first only if a named event happens; the $10 is leftover income after invented expenses, not a deductible.
