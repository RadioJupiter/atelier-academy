## Mathematics · Algebra XI: Vieta’s formulas for a quadratic {#math}

<p class="meta">60 min · Method: expand a factored quadratic, then read the sum and the product of the roots from the coefficients</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-vieta-quadratic">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="Vieta’s formulas (quadratic)" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear Vieta’s formulas (quadratic)">Hear</button> Vieta’s formulas (quadratic)</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>For a quadratic equation, the sum and the product of the roots can be read from the coefficients. For x² + bx + c = 0, the sum of the roots is −b and the product is c. For ax² + bx + c = 0 with a ≠ 0, the sum is −b/a and the product is c/a. The name honors François Viète (Vieta).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>If the roots are 2 and −5, the monic quadratic is (x − 2)(x + 5) = x² + 3x − 10, so the sum is −3 (which is −b) and the product is −10 (which is c).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not a substitute for solving when you are asked to find the roots, and not a claim that every quadratic has two real roots. If there are no real roots, today’s reading of a real sum and product from a real factored form does not apply in that way; this lesson works with quadratics that have two real roots (possibly repeated).</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Day 3 factored quadratics, Day 5 solved them, and Day 33 graphed them. The x-intercepts are the roots; when there are two, the vertex’s x-coordinate is their average, which is also half the sum.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
</div>


**From the factored form, first.** If the roots are r and s,

**(x − r)(x − s) = x² − (r + s)x + rs**.

Compare with **x² + bx + c**. Then:

- r + s = −b
- rs = c

That pair is **Vieta’s formulas** for a monic quadratic (leading coefficient 1). If the quadratic is **ax² + bx + c = 0** with a ≠ 0, first divide by a, or remember:

- r + s = −b/a
- rs = c/a

**A pictorial check.** On the graph, the roots are the x-intercepts. Their midpoint is −b/(2a), which is also the x-coordinate of the vertex (Day 33). That is the same sum, halved.

**A near-miss.** The sum is **−b/a**, not b/a. The minus sign is easy to drop.


</div>

A concession is a **real grant**. Write one middle paragraph:

- A topic sentence that names the point you are granting, in fair terms.
- Two or three sentences that make the grant specific (a reason, a short attributed quotation if you have one already taught, an example).
- A last sentence that returns to your essay’s job without erasing the grant.

If you cannot state the other claim in words that claim’s defender would still recognize, the concession is not yet real.

</dl></article>
</div>


**Mass** is a property of the body (kilograms). **Weight** is the gravitational force on it (newtons).

Near the Earth, in the model you already have:

**W = mg**, with **g = 9.8 N/kg**.

The same mass in a place where *g* is smaller has a **smaller weight**. A commonly taught first figure is that *g* on the Moon is **about one sixth** of *g* near the Earth; that is an approximate first figure, not a number this class measured.

Monday’s *G* is still not *g*. Weight near the Earth can be seen, in Newton’s formula, as the pull between the Earth and the body; this lesson does not ask you to compute that pull from *G* and the Earth’s mass.
