## Seminar · Logic: conditional probability, P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B), a first look {#seminar}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: invented fair cube; distinguished from the addition rule and from independence</p>
<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<div class="keyterms">

<article class="term" id="term-conditional-probability">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="conditional probability" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear conditional probability">Hear</button> conditional probability</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>The conditional probability of A given B, written P(A|B), is P(A and B) / P(B), provided P(B) is not 0. It is the probability of A, once B is known to have happened. It is not last week’s addition rule for “or,” and not an official probability of a real event.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>On an invented fair cube, P(even | greater than 3) = P(even and greater than 3) / P(greater than 3) = (2/6) / (3/6) = 2/3. The faces greater than 3 are 4, 5, and 6; the even ones among them are 4 and 6. The cube is an invented classroom figure.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not P(A or B), last week’s addition rule. Not P(A and B) left un-divided, which would be 2/6, not the conditional. Not an official probability of a real event. Not a claim that every pair of events is independent.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Last week’s addition rule for overlapping events, and earlier independent and mutually exclusive events. Official probabilities of real events remain reserved.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
</div>
<p>Last week, overlapping events used P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B). Today a different question: once B is known to have happened, what is the probability of A? That is the conditional probability of A given B, written P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B), provided P(B) is not 0.</p><p>On an invented fair cube, let A be “even” and B be “greater than 3.” P(B) = 3/6, the faces 4, 5, and 6. P(A and B) = 2/6, the faces 4 and 6. So P(A|B) = (2/6) / (3/6) = 2/3. Once the roll is known to be greater than 3, two of those three faces are even. P(A and B) alone, 2/6, is not the conditional. Last week’s P(A or B) = 2/3 happens to equal this week’s P(A|B) on this invented cube, but the two questions are different, and they need not agree on another invented example. The cube is invented, not an official experiment.</p>

### Exercises

1. On the same invented cube, find P(odd | greater than 3).
2. Why is P(even and greater than 3) = 2/6 not already the conditional?
3. What official claim must you not add?

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B), when P(B) is not 0. It is the probability of A, once B is known to have happened.
2. Even and greater than 3: 4 and 6, so 2/6. Greater than 3: 4, 5, and 6, so 3/6. P = 2/3.
3. Last week’s rule answers “or.” Today’s rule answers “given.” The questions are different, even when the numbers happen to match on this invented cube.
4. Model: **conditional probability.** Own words should match this idea: The conditional probability of A given B, written P(A|B), is P(A and B) / P(B), provided P(B) is not 0. It is the probability of A, once B is known to have happened. It is not last week’s addition rule for “or,” and not an official probability of a real event. Near-miss: Not P(A or B), last week’s addition rule. Not P(A and B) left un-divided, which would be 2/6, not the conditional. Not an official probability of a real event. Not a claim that every pair of events is independent. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

**Exercises.**
1. Greater than 3: 4, 5, 6. Odd among them: 5 only. P = (1/6) / (3/6) = 1/3.
2. 2/6 is the chance that both happen, out of all six faces, not the chance of even once the roll is already known to be greater than 3.
3. Do not invent an official probability of a real event.
