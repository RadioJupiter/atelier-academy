## Physics · The maximum speed in simple harmonic motion, vmax = X√(k/m) {#physics}

<p class="meta">40 min · Method: vmax = X√(k/m) from OpenStax 16.5, with sourced Example 16.6 and invented classroom checks</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-maximum-speed-in-shm">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="maximum speed in SHM" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear maximum speed in SHM">Hear</button> maximum speed in SHM</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>In undamped simple harmonic motion, the maximum speed occurs at x = 0, and OpenStax 16.5 gives vmax = X√(k/m). It is proportional to the amplitude X and to the square root of k, and inversely proportional to the square root of m.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>OpenStax Example 16.6: a 900 kg car, k = 6.53 × 10⁴ N/m, amplitude 0.100 m, no damping, vmax = 0.852 m/s. Those figures are OpenStax’s, not invented.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not a first look at Monday’s conservation equation, or at last week’s ½kx² = ½mv² as a one-step conversion. Not a period, and not a claim that a real car’s shock absorbers are undamped (OpenStax assumes no damping in the example).</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>The formula follows from ½m vmax² = ½kX², which is Monday’s statement at x = 0.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
</div>


<p>Monday’s conservation, at x = 0, is ½m vmax² = ½kX². Solving for the speed gives OpenStax 16.5’s result:</p>
<p>vmax = X√(k/m).</p>
<p>OpenStax Example 16.6 uses a car of mass 900 kg, a suspension with k = 6.53 × 10⁴ N/m, and amplitude 0.100 m, with no damping:</p>
<p>vmax = 0.100 × √(6.53 × 10⁴ / 900) = 0.852 m/s.</p>
<p>Those figures are OpenStax’s. Invented Together figures, labeled invented: k = 200 N/m, m = 0.50 kg, X = 0.10 m, so vmax = 0.10 × √(200/0.50) = 0.10 × 20 = 2.0 m/s. A check: ½mv² = ½ × 0.50 × 4.0 = 1.0 J, which matches Monday’s ½kX² = 1.0 J.</p>
<p class="note">OpenStax, <em>College Physics 2e</em>, section 16.5, “Energy and the Simple Harmonic Oscillator” (CC BY): https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/16-5-energy-and-the-simple-harmonic-oscillator The conservation statement ½mv² + ½kx² = ½kX², the result vmax = X√(k/m), and Example 16.6 (a 900 kg car, k = 6.53 × 10⁴ N/m, amplitude 0.100 m, vmax = 0.852 m/s, no damping) are in that section. Last week’s PE_el = ½kx² and the conversion ½kx² = ½mv², from section 16.1 Example 16.2, are not first looks. Other classroom masses, amplitudes, and constants are invented and labeled invented.</p>
