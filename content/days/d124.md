## Mathematics · Geometry LXX: skew lines, a first look {#math}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: two lines that do not meet, are not parallel, and do not lie in one plane</p>
<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>
<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-skew-lines">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="skew lines" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear skew lines">Hear</button> skew lines</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>Two lines in space are skew if they are not parallel, do not meet, and do not lie in one and the same plane. Skew lines are a space relation; two lines in a single plane are either parallel or intersecting, and cannot be skew.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>On the invented box, the front bottom edge AB and the top back edge (the edge of the top face farthest from AB) are skew: they are not parallel, they do not meet, and no single plane contains both.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not two parallel edges, which never meet but do lie in one plane (or in many, if one wishes to name a plane of two parallels). Not two edges that meet at a corner. Not two lines drawn on one sheet of paper.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>A line and a plane, a line perpendicular to a plane, and two planes in space.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
</div>
<p>In a single plane, two distinct lines either meet or are parallel. In space a third relation appears: two lines may fail to meet and also fail to be parallel, because they do not lie in one plane. Such lines are called skew. On the invented box, take the front bottom edge AB and the top back edge. Those two edges do not meet, are not parallel (one runs along the front of the floor, the other along the back of the top, in a different direction of depth), and no one face of the box contains both. They are skew.</p>
