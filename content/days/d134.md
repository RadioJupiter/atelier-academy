## Mathematics · Geometry LXXVIII: the law of sines in a non-right triangle {#math}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: an invented isosceles triangle 30°-30°-120°, using sin 120° = sin 60° from Monday’s related angles</p>

<p>Yesterday the law of sines was checked on a right triangle. Today a non-right invented triangle, labeled invented: the angles are 30°, 30°, and 120°, so the triangle is isosceles, and the sides opposite the 30° angles are equal. Monday’s related-angle work gives sin 120° = sin(180° − 60°) = sin 60° = √3/2. Let each equal side be 4, invented. The side opposite 120° is found from 4 / sin 30° = c / sin 120°, so 4 / (1/2) = c / (√3/2), and c = 4 × 2 × (√3/2) = 4√3. The common ratio is 8. A miss would be to take sin 120° as −√3/2, which is cos 120°, or to treat 120° as if it were 60° in the triangle itself. The ambiguous case, and the law of cosines, remain reserved. Radians remain reserved. Invent no official length.</p>
