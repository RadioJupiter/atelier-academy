## Mathematics · Geometry LXXIV: signs of sine and cosine in the four quadrants, a first look {#math}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: the quadrant gives the signs; a first-quadrant reference angle gives the sizes</p>

<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-signs-of-sine-and-cosine-in-the-four-quadrants">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="signs of sine and cosine in the four quadrants" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear signs of sine and cosine in the four quadrants">Hear</button> signs of sine and cosine in the four quadrants</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>On the unit circle, cosine is the x-coordinate and sine is the y-coordinate, so their signs follow the signs of x and y. In the first quadrant both are positive. In the second, sine is positive and cosine is negative. In the third, both are negative. In the fourth, sine is negative and cosine is positive. A reference angle in the first quadrant gives the size of the coordinates; the quadrant gives the signs.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>At 150°, the reference angle is 30°, so the coordinates have the size √3/2 and 1/2, and the point is in the second quadrant: P is (−√3/2, 1/2). Cos 150° = −√3/2, sin 150° = 1/2.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not a new set of absolute values for 30°, 45°, and 60°. Not a first look at radians. Not a claim that sine is positive in every quadrant.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>The unit circle, the axis angles, and yesterday’s 30°, 45°, and 60° values.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
</div>
<p>The four quadrants now give signs. Cosine is x, sine is y. First quadrant: both positive. Second: y positive, x negative, so sine positive and cosine negative. Third: both negative. Fourth: y negative, x positive, so sine negative and cosine positive. For 150°, which is 180° − 30°, the reference angle is 30°. The sizes are yesterday’s: √3/2 and 1/2. The point is in the second quadrant, so P is (−√3/2, 1/2): cos 150° = −√3/2, sin 150° = 1/2. For 330°, which is 360° − 30°, the fourth quadrant gives P = (√3/2, −1/2). Radians remain reserved. Invent no official turning of a real wheel.</p>

## Seminar · Economics: an income tax on invented figures, distinguished from a sales tax {#seminar}
