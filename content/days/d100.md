<p>Last week, Gray’s skull was a cranium of eight and a face of fourteen, twenty-two bones in all, with the mandible the movable exception. Today that bone is read more closely, still from Gray 1918.</p>
<p>“The mandible, the largest and strongest bone of the face, serves for the reception of the lower teeth. It consists of a curved, horizontal portion, the body, and two perpendicular portions, the rami, which unite with the ends of the body nearly at right angles.”</p>
<p>“The mandible articulates with the two temporal bones.” That is the movable joint already implied by last week’s exception.</p>
<p>On the body, “below the second premolar tooth, on either side,” is “the mental foramen, for the passage of the mental vessels and nerve.” On the upper border of the ramus, Gray names “two processes, the coronoid in front and the condyloid behind, separated by a deep concavity, the mandibular notch.”</p>
<p>A first closer day requires the largest-and-strongest claim, the body and two rami, the articulation with the two temporals, and a recognition of the mental foramen, the coronoid and condyloid processes, and the mandibular notch. It does not require a memorized recitation of every fossa, muscle attachment, or ossification center, or of the changes Gray describes at birth, in childhood, in the adult, and in old age, which remain reserved.</p>
