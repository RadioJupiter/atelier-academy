## Physics · The law of reflection, a first look, from OpenStax 25.2 {#physics}

<p>OpenStax, College Physics 2e, 25.2, states the law of reflection: “the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence—θᵣ = θᵢ.” The angles “are measured relative to the perpendicular to the surface at the point where the ray strikes the surface.” That perpendicular is the normal. The angle of incidence, θᵢ, is the angle between the incident ray and the normal, not the angle between the ray and the surface. The angle of reflection, θᵣ, is the angle between the reflected ray and the same normal. On an invented figure, θᵢ = 30°, so θᵣ = 30°. The 30° figure is invented and labeled invented; the law is the book’s official statement. The book also notes that a rough surface reflects light in many directions, or is diffused, which is why a sheet of paper can be seen from many angles, while a smooth mirror reflects “at specific angles.” The law of refraction remains reserved. No official instrument length is used.</p>
