<p>This sitting mixes yesterday’s and Monday’s continuity with last week’s algebra of limits. No new rule. On invented functions f(x) = 2x and g(x) = x + 1, as x approaches 3, last week found lim f = 6, lim g = 4, lim (f + g) = 10, lim (f g) = 24, and lim (f / g) = 3/2. Both f and g are defined at 3, with f(3) = 6 and g(3) = 4, so both are continuous at 3, and so are the sum, the product, and the quotient, because (f + g)(3) = 10, (f g)(3) = 24, and (f / g)(3) = 6/4 = 3/2, each matching its limit. The quotient is allowed here because g(3) = 4 ≠ 0. The functions are invented. A formal epsilon argument remains reserved.</p>
