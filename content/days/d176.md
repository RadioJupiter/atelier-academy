<p>This week reviews already-taught Term 3 Latin. No new construction. Today reviews purpose clauses. A purpose clause marks an intended purpose: ut for a positive purpose, nē for a negative purpose. The verb is the already-taught present subjunctive, not the future. Puella venit ut eum sequātur: she comes in order that she follow him. Puella manet nē eum sequātur: she stays in order that she not follow him. A result clause is tomorrow’s review, not today’s job. Original classroom sentences only. Identity is handled with Puer and Puella, and with he and she in the English gloss.</p>

<p>Term 3 opened with space geometry. Today reviews already-taught relations of a line and a plane, and of a line perpendicular to a plane. No new theorem. In space, a line and a plane have three possible relations: the line may lie in the plane, meet the plane at exactly one point, or be parallel to the plane and never meet it. A line is perpendicular to a plane if it meets the plane at a point P, the foot, and is perpendicular to every line in the plane that passes through P. Later Term 3 work on trig, limits, continuity, and the derivative waits until later sittings this week.</p>

<p>Today reviews already-taught expected value on an invented fair cube. If the six faces are equally likely, and the already-taught invented payoffs are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6, the already-taught expected value is (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6) / 6 = 21/6 = 7/2. No new term. The cube and the payoffs are invented, not official measurements. A later official probability model remains reserved.</p>
