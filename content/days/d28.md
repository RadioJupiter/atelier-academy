**The rule, restated.** If the words or the claim come from a source, say so before or as you use them. Then explain, in your own sentences, why the excerpt or précis is there.

## Physics · Mechanical energy, conserved when friction does no work {#physics}

<p class="meta">50 min · Method: KE + PE constant in the no-friction model; v = √(2gh) from rest, after it is shown</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-mechanical-energy">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="mechanical energy (no friction)" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear mechanical energy (no friction)">Hear</button> mechanical energy (no friction)</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>In this course’s first conservation model, mechanical energy is the sum KE + PE, with PE = m g h near the Earth. When no friction (and no other force that takes energy out of this account) does work, the sum is constant: ½ m v² + m g h stays the same along the path.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>A 2 kg object starts from rest at h = 2.5 m: PE = 49 J, KE = 0. At the floor (h = 0), PE = 0, so KE = 49 J, and v = 7 m/s, because ½ × 2 × v² = 49, v² = 49, v = 7.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not a claim that energy is “used up” when the object stops in your hand, and not a license to ignore friction when friction is actually present. That case is Friday’s caution and is already previewed: friction’s work decreases the mechanical-energy sum as this course is keeping it.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Mass cancels when you go from m g h to ½ v² (if you start from rest): v = √(2 g h). You may use that once you have shown it.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
<article class="term" id="term-conservation-mechanical">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="conservation (mechanical, first model)" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear conservation (mechanical, first model)">Hear</button> conservation (mechanical, first model)</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>The statement that KE + PE is constant when only gravity (in the PE = m g h model) and forces that do no work (such as a normal force perpendicular to the motion) act. It is a first model, not a complete account of every form of energy.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>From a height of 2.5 m, starting at rest, speed at the bottom is 7 m/s in the no-friction model, independent of mass.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not “energy is always the same number on every line of every problem,” and not a refusal of the work–energy theorem: conservation of mechanical energy is that theorem, plus PE, when gravity’s work is already stored as ΔPE.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Wednesday’s sitting is this model. Friday’s two-week check will include a problem where friction does work, so the sum is not constant.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
</div>


**The bookkeeping.** Write KE + PE at two points, with one reference level. If the model’s conditions hold, the sums are equal.

**The clean example.** m = 2 kg, h = 2.5 m, at rest: 0 + 49 = 49 J. At h = 0: ½(2)v² + 0 = 49, v = 7 m/s.

**Mass cancels, when it should.** From rest, m g h = ½ m v², so v = √(2 g h) = √(2 × 9.8 × 2.5) = √49 = 7 m/s, for any mass in this model.

**A mid-height check.** At h = 0.5 m, PE = 2 × 9.8 × 0.5 = 9.8 J, so KE = 39.2 J, v = √(39.2) = √(196/5) = 14/√5 m/s (leave exact, or about 6.3 m/s, labeled approximate). Prefer a height that stays clean when you can: at h = 0, v = 7 m/s is the check to know by heart for these numbers.


</div>

**Hellenistic, as a contrast, not as a complete survey.** After the High Classical fifth century, Hellenistic art (conventionally from the later fourth century toward the first) often shows stronger motion, more elaborate drapery, and — in some famous works — a more theatrical pathos. Two type examples, both with cautions, are enough today.

**Nike of Samothrace.** A winged Nike in motion, now in the Louvre, usually placed in the second century BCE, fragmentary, and displayed far from its original setting. Describe the stride and the drapery; date it carefully; name the losses.

**The Laocoön group.** A tightly wound group, intense faces, a famous later life in the Vatican. Then the caution: date and original-or-copy status are debated. Describe what you see; do not use the emotion of the scene as a dating method.

**The same habit as last week.** A museum photograph is not the ancient setting. A marble may be a later version. A conventional century is not an exact year.
