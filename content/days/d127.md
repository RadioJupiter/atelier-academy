## Mathematics · Geometry LXXII: sine and cosine at 180° and 270°, a first look {#math}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: the same unit circle; P at (−1, 0) and at (0, −1); degrees only</p>
<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>
<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-sine-and-cosine-at-the-axis-angles">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="sine and cosine at the axis angles" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear sine and cosine at the axis angles">Hear</button> sine and cosine at the axis angles</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>On the unit circle, the four axis angles in degrees have these points: 0°, (1, 0); 90°, (0, 1); 180°, (−1, 0); 270°, (0, −1). Then cos 180° = −1, sin 180° = 0, cos 270° = 0, and sin 270° = −1. The same circle of radius 1 is used; only the direction of the ray has changed.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>Cos 180° = −1 and sin 180° = 0, because the terminal ray meets the unit circle at (−1, 0).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not a first look at radians. Not a claim that sine is always positive. Not a new radius. The 30°, 45°, and 60° points wait until tomorrow.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Yesterday’s unit circle, and last term’s sine and cosine in a right triangle, which already gave the first-quadrant agreement.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
</div>
<p>Yesterday the unit circle gave cos θ = x and sin θ = y, with P at 0° and at 90°. Today the same method at 180° and 270°. At 180°, the terminal ray points along the negative x-axis, so P is (−1, 0): cos 180° = −1, sin 180° = 0. At 270°, the ray points along the negative y-axis, so P is (0, −1): cos 270° = 0, sin 270° = −1. Sine is not always positive, and cosine is not always positive. Radians remain reserved. The 30°, 45°, and 60° coordinates wait until tomorrow. Invent no official turning of a real wheel.</p>
