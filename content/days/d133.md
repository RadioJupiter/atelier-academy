## Mathematics · Geometry LXXVII: the law of sines, a first look {#math}

<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>The law of sines says that in any triangle, a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C, where side a is opposite angle A, side b opposite B, and side c opposite C. <div><dt>Example</dt><dd>An invented 30°-60°-90° triangle with hypotenuse 2 has sides 1, √3, and 2. Then 1/sin 30° = 1/(1/2) = 2, and 2/sin 90° = 2/1 = 2, and √3/sin 60° = √3/(√3/2) = 2. The three ratios are equal, as the law says. The 2 is invented, not an official length.</dd></div>

## Physics · The speed of sound, frequency, and wavelength, from OpenStax 17.2 {#physics}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: vw = fλ, the book’s air formula, Table 17.1, and official Example 17.1 (348.7 m/s, 17 m, 1.7 cm)</p>

<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>

<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>In OpenStax 17.2, the speed of sound, frequency, and wavelength are related by the already-taught wave equation vw = fλ. Perception of frequency is called pitch. For air at sea level, the book gives vw = 331 m/s × √(T/273 K), with T in kelvin. At 0°C the official table value is 331 m/s; at 20.0°C the book gives 343 m/s. Official Example 17.1 uses 30.0°C air, taken as 303 K, and finds vw = 348.7 m/s, then λ = 17 m at 20 Hz and λ = 0.017 m, or 1.7 cm, at 20,000 Hz, the extremes of the audible range, with those frequencies accurate to two significant figures.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>The book’s official Example 17.1: in 30.0°C air, vw = 331 m/s × √(303/273) = 348.7 m/s; at 20 Hz, λmax = 17 m; at 20,000 Hz, λmin = 1.7 cm.</dd></div>

<p>OpenStax 17.2, “Speed of Sound, Frequency, and Wavelength,” continues Monday’s first look. Access for free at the OpenStax 17.2 page. The flash of fireworks is seen before the sound is heard, “implying both that sound travels at a finite speed and that it is much slower than light.” Perception of frequency is called pitch. The wave equation is the one already taught: vw = fλ. The speed of sound depends on the medium: “the more rigid (or less compressible) the medium, the faster the speed of sound.” Table 17.1, official values, includes air at 0°C, 331 m/s; fresh water at 20°C, 1480 m/s; and steel, 5960 m/s. For air at sea level, the book gives vw = 331 m/s × √(T/273 K), with T in kelvin. “At 0°C, the speed of sound is 331 m/s, whereas at 20.0°C it is 343 m/s, less than a 4% increase.” The speed “is nearly independent of frequency” in open air for sounds in the audible range, 20 to 20,000 Hz.</p>
<p>Official Example 17.1: wavelengths at the extremes of the audible range, 20 and 20,000 Hz, in 30.0°C air, with those frequencies accurate to two significant figures. T = 303 K, and vw = 331 m/s × √(303/273) = 348.7 m/s. Then λmax = 348.7 m/s ÷ 20 Hz = 17 m, and λmin = 348.7 m/s ÷ 20,000 Hz = 0.017 m = 1.7 cm. An invented practice frequency, labeled invented, may use the book’s official 343 m/s at 20.0°C: if f = 686 Hz, invented, then λ = 343 / 686 = 0.500 m. Invent no official speed of light, and no official living air temperature other than the book’s. Decibels remain reserved.</p>
