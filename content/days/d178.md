<p>Today reviews already-taught limits. A limit, in the first look already taught, is a value that a quantity approaches as an input approaches a chosen number. On invented f(x) = 2x, lim x→3 2x = 6. A limit may exist even when the function is not defined at the point: invented (x² − 4)/(x − 2) is not defined at 2, but the limit is 4, by factorization. If lim f = L and lim g = M, then lim (f + g) = L + M, lim (f g) = L M, and, if M ≠ 0, lim (f / g) = L / M. On invented f(x) = 2x and g(x) = x + 1, as x approaches 3, those limits are 10, 24, and 3/2. The quotient rule does not apply when M = 0. Continuity and the derivative wait until tomorrow’s review. A formal epsilon argument remains reserved. The functions are invented.</p>
