## Science · OpenStax Example 14.5: conduction through an ice box {#science}

<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>OpenStax Example 14.5 applies Q/t = kA(T2 − T1)/d to a Styrofoam ice box, then uses the already-taught heat of fusion to find how much ice melts in one day. <p>Yesterday’s formula is now used on OpenStax Example 14.5, recomputed by hand, not treated as a first look at the equation. A Styrofoam ice box has total area 0.950 m² and walls of average thickness 2.50 cm. It contains ice, water, and canned beverages at 0 °C, and is kept in a car trunk at 35.0 °C. How much ice melts in one day?</p>
<p>Convert d = 2.50 cm = 0.0250 m, and t = 1 day = 86,400 s. Styrofoam k = 0.010 J/s·m·°C, from Table 14.3. Q/t = 0.010 × 0.950 × (35.0 − 0) / 0.0250. First, 0.010 × 0.950 = 0.00950; times 35.0 is 0.3325; divided by 0.0250 is 13.3 J/s. Q = 13.3 J/s × 86,400 s = 1,149,120 J, which OpenStax reports as 1.15 × 10⁶ J. Ice melts with Q = m Lf, and Lf = 334 × 10³ J/kg, already used in earlier latent-heat work, so m = 1.15 × 10⁶ / (334 × 10³) = 3.44 kg. Example 14.6, the aluminum pan, remains reserved. Official household R-factor numbers remain reserved.</p>
