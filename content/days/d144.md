## Seminar · Economics: a simple budget on invented figures, a first look {#seminar}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: invented income, expenses, and remainder; distinguished from a tax, a premium, and a deductible; not official</p>

<p>A simple budget, in this invented classroom arrangement, is a plan that places an invented income beside invented expenses and names the remainder: income minus the total of the expenses. On invented figures, income is $80. Expenses are $40, $20, and $10, which total $70. The remainder is $80 − $70 = $10. If the expenses were $90, the remainder would be negative, $80 − $90 = −$10, and the invented plan would not yet balance. The $80, the expenses, and the $10 are invented classroom numbers, not an official wage, rent, or household account.</p><p>The remainder is not a tax: an income tax was a percent taken from invented income, and a sales tax was a percent added to an invented price. It is not a premium: last weeks’ invented premium was paid in advance, $10 a month, whether or not a named event happened. It is not a deductible: a deductible was borne first only if the named event happened. Official budgets, official wages, and official costs of living remain reserved.</p>
