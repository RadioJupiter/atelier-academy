</div>
<p>Today mixes both uses of the law of cosines. From two sides and the included angle, the opposite side is found from c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C. From three sides, an angle is found from cos C = (a² + b² − c²)/(2ab). On an invented triangle with sides 6 and 6 and included angle 60°, c² = 36 + 36 − 2·6·6·(1/2) = 36, so c = 6. On an invented triangle with sides 6, 6, and 6, the angle is 60°, which checks the first result. The figures are invented. The law of sines remains available as a check when a side-angle pair is already known, but it is not a substitute for the included-angle job. Radians and the ambiguous case remain reserved.</p>
