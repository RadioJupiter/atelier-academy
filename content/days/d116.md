<p>This is review, not a first look. The three already-taught deponent tenses still look passive and are translated as active. The imperfect is the ongoing or habitual past; the perfect presents the action as completed; the present is for action now. The already-taught absolute, such as puerō locūtō, is a loosely attached phrase, with no sum, and is not a fourth tense of a main-clause verb. <p>This is review, not a first look. From OpenStax 16.8, already taught: a forced oscillation is driven by a periodic driving force. The natural frequency is the frequency with no driving and no damping. Resonance is driving at the natural frequency, when energy is transferred most efficiently and the amplitude is greatest. OpenStax’s graph shows that all the curves peak at the natural frequency, and that less damping gives a higher and narrower peak. Heavy damping lowers the amplitude and spreads the response. Official measured pitches, peak heights, and wind speeds are not invented. A named 1940 bridge is cited only as OpenStax cites it, without an invented official wind speed. Waves wait until Wednesday.</p>

<p>This is review, not a first look. In the third figure, already taught, M is the subject of both premises. Darapti (AAI-3): All M are P; all M are S; therefore some S are P. Felapton (EAO-3): No M are P; all M are S; therefore some S are not P. Ferison (EIO-3): No M are P; some M are S; therefore some S are not P. Datisi (AII-3): All M are P; some M are S; therefore some S are P. First-figure moods with the same vowels, such as Darii or Ferio, are near-misses of figure, not of today’s work. Invented terms stay invented.</p>
