<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Similar triangles keep corresponding sides in the ratio k. Sin A is opposite over hypotenuse, cos A is adjacent over hypotenuse, tan A is opposite over adjacent, and tan A = sin A / cos A. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, if A is opposite 3, tan A = 3/4. A similar 6-8-10 triangle has the same tan A.

<p>This is review, from the already-set public-domain Gray via Theodora, not a first look. The column is formed of 33 vertebræ: 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, and 4 coccygeal. The four curves: cervical and lumbar convex forward, thoracic concave forward, pelvic concavity downward and forward; thoracic and pelvic primary, cervical and lumbar secondary or compensatory. A typical vertebra has a body and a vertebral or neural arch, enclosing the vertebral foramen; the arch has pedicles and laminæ, and seven processes: four articular, two transverse, and one spinous. Stacked bodies form a pillar; stacked foramina form a canal for the medulla spinalis (spinal cord); intervertebral foramina transmit nerves and vessels. Keep vertebræ and laminæ. Official 71 cm and 61 cm lengths, a slight lateral curvature, and regional special features remain reserved.</p>
