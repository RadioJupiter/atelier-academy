## Physics · Standing waves, nodes, and the fundamental, a first look {#physics}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: OpenStax 16.10, λ1 = 2L and f1 = vw / (2L), on an invented string</p>
<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>
<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-standing-wave">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="standing wave" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear standing wave">Hear</button> standing wave</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>A standing wave, in OpenStax 16.10, is the result of two identical waves moving in opposite directions, superposed so that the oscillations stay at fixed locations. The pattern does not travel; it alternates between constructive and destructive interference in place. On a string fixed at both ends, the ends must be nodes.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>The fundamental on a string of length L has wavelength λ1 = 2L and frequency f1 = vw / (2L). An invented string with L = 2.00 m and vw = 80.0 m/s has λ1 = 4.00 m and f1 = 20.0 Hz.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not a traveling wave that carries a crest along the string. Not a reserved official frequency of a named instrument. The invented L and vw are classroom figures, not a measured guitar.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Superposition, constructive and destructive interference, and last term’s vw = fλ.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
<article class="term" id="term-node-of-a-standing-wave">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="node (of a standing wave)" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear node (of a standing wave)">Hear</button> node (of a standing wave)</h5>
<dl class="frayer">

<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>On the fundamental, there are nodes at the two fixed ends, and an antinode in the middle.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not an antinode, which is a location of maximum amplitude. Not a claim that the wave has ceased to exist on the rest of the string.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Standing waves on a string fixed at both ends, from OpenStax 16.10.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
<article class="term" id="term-antinode">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="antinode" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear antinode">Hear</button> antinode</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>An antinode is a location of maximum amplitude in a standing wave.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>On the fundamental, the midpoint of the string is an antinode. On the first overtone, with λ2 = L, there are two antinodes, and a node at the midpoint as well as at the ends.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not a node, where the disturbance is zero. Not a traveling crest that moves along the string.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Node, standing wave, and the fundamental and first overtone.</dd></div>
</dl></article>

<p>OpenStax 16.10 next treats standing waves. Two identical waves moving in opposite directions superpose and can look as if a wave is standing in place. Nodes are points where the disturbance is zero; antinodes are locations of maximum amplitude. On a string fixed at both ends, the ends must be nodes, because the string cannot move there.</p>
<p>The lowest frequency, the fundamental, has the longest wavelength, λ1 = 2L, so f1 = vw / λ1 = vw / (2L). The next, the first overtone or second harmonic in the book’s numbering, has λ2 = L, so f2 = vw / L = 2f1. Then f3 = 3f1, and so on. Today’s figures are invented: L = 2.00 m, vw = 80.0 m/s. Then λ1 = 4.00 m, f1 = 20.0 Hz; λ2 = 2.00 m, f2 = 40.0 Hz. Invent no official frequency of a named instrument. Beats wait until Friday.</p>

</div>

<blockquote><p>we, of our own accord, not at your instance nor the request of anyone else in your regard, but of our own sole largess and certain knowledge and out of the fullness of our apostolic power, by the authority of Almighty God conferred upon us in blessed Peter and of the vicarship of Jesus Christ which we hold on earth, do by tenor of these presents give, grant, and assign forever to you and your heirs and successors, kings of Castile and Leon, all and singular the aforesaid countries and islands thus unknown and hitherto discovered by your envoys and to be discovered hereafter, provided however they at no time have been in the actual temporal possession of any Christian owner, together with all their dominions, cities, camps, places, and villages, and all rights, jurisdictions, and appurtenances of the same.</p></blockquote>
<p>Read what the clause does and what it already limits. It grants lands discovered and to be discovered, forever, to the kings of Castile and León and their heirs, with a proviso about a Christian owner, and with a statement that a right already acquired by a Christian prince is not to be taken away. It does not, in this May 3 text, draw a line of leagues; that belongs to a later reserved bull. Invent no official map from this wording, and invent no official quantity of gold. The bull also mentions earlier apostolic grants to kings of Portugal in “Africa, Guinea, and the Gold Mine,” as a comparison, not as a second grant you are asked to quote in full today.</p>

## Modern language · Spanish: the simple future, contrasted with the present {#language}
<p class="meta">40 min · Method: mañana hablaré contrasted with hoy hablo; the future is not a present with a new ending only</p>
<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>
<div class="keyterms recall-terms" markdown="0">
<h4>Key terms (from earlier lessons)</h4>
<ul>
<li><a href="d121.html#term-simple-future-of-a-regular-verb">simple future of a regular verb</a> — Day 121</li>
</ul>
<p>Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.</p>
</div>
<p>The new tense is now contrasted with the already-taught present. Hoy hablo, I speak today, or I am speaking today. Mañana hablaré, I will speak tomorrow. The future keeps the infinitive; the present does not. Ella habla is present; ella hablará is future. Nosotros comemos is present; nosotros comeremos is future. Do not let a time word such as mañana sit next to a present and call that a simple future. The near future with ir a remains a contrast, not today’s form to practice as if it were new. Irregular futures remain reserved.</p>

</div>
