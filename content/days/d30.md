<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<div class="keyterms">

<article class="term" id="term-two-week-physics-check">

<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>W = F s cosθ, KE = ½ m v², PE = m g h, W_net = ΔKE, and, with no friction, KE + PE constant. With friction, W_friction = Δ(KE + PE) in the sense that the mechanical sum changes by that work.</dd></div>

</dl></article>

**A reminder, then a mixed sitting.**

- W = F s cosθ, in joules. Holding still is zero work. A perpendicular force does zero work.
- KE = ½ m v²; W_net = ΔKE.
- PE = m g h near the Earth, one chosen zero, g = 9.8 N/kg.
- No friction: KE + PE constant. From rest, v = √(2 g h) at a drop h.
- With friction opposite a displacement s: W_friction = −F_friction s, and the mechanical sum changes by that work.

**A friction example you should be able to do.** A 2 kg object starts from rest at 2.5 m (49 J of PE). Friction does −9 J of work on the way down. Then KE + PE at the floor is 40 J, so KE = 40 J, v² = 40, v = √40 = 2√10 m/s (about 6.3 m/s, approximate). The 9 J has left the mechanical account; the work–energy theorem still holds if that work is included in W_net.

## History · Two-week synthesis: how we know the Roman Republic {#history}

<p class="meta">45 min · Two-week check: early tradition, mid-Republican analysis, late participant sources; one habit throughout</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-how-we-know-the-republic">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="how we know the Republic" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear how we know the Republic">Hear</button> how we know the Republic</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>A synthesis question: early Rome and early law are known through later tradition, archaeology, and fragments; the mid-Republic can be analyzed with writers such as Polybius; the late Republic speaks in participant sources that are also advocacy. The same habit of dating and classifying sources runs through all three.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>A careful paragraph might move from the Twelve Tables as later fragments, through Polybius on government and on the Punic Wars, to Caesar’s commentaries as primary and interested.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not a claim that we know nothing, and not a claim that we know the early city as clearly as the 50s BCE. The quality of the evidence changes, and the wording should change with it.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>This is the two-week history check: a short synthesis, not a new period.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
</div>


**Three bands of evidence, one habit.**

1. **Early:** Livy and other late narratives; traditional dates (753, 509, mid-fifth-century Tables) labeled as tradition; archaeology as independent, limited evidence; the Twelve Tables as later fragments.

<li>An AP sum uses the average of the first and last terms: S = n/2 · (a + ℓ). A finite GP sum uses S = a(r<sup>n</sup> − 1)/(r − 1) when r ≠ 1, or S = n a when r = 1.</li>
<li>You need the common ratio r, not only a common difference d, and you must not use the AP pairing formula on a GP.</li>
<li>A near-miss is to add n times the first term, or to apply n/2 · (a + ℓ), to a geometric sequence.</li>
</ol>

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. S = n/2 · (first + last), or n/2 · (2a + (n − 1)d). Pairing first with last makes each pair sum to a + ℓ, so 2S = n(a + ℓ). The formula is that counting, not a separate trick.
2. ille, illa, illud is the far demonstrative, “that.” hic, haec, hoc is the near one, “this.” They are different pointing words; the adverb hīc (“here”) is a third, long-ī form.
3. 6 CO<sub>2</sub> + 6 H<sub>2</sub>O + light energy → C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub> + 6 O<sub>2</sub>. It is a net summary, not a one-step mechanism, and C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub> is a representative carbohydrate.
4. With verbs such as cōnstāre, the price is ablative without a preposition (decem sēstertiīs). When the price is the predicate of pretium est, it is nominative.
5. A solvent carries the pigments up the paper at different rates because they cling to the paper and dissolve in the solvent differently, so they form colored bands. R<sub>f</sub> values depend on the exact setup; this course will not invent them.
6. Money is taught as a medium of exchange, a unit of account, and a store of value. A numbered paper that cannot do those jobs (or a token in a game that is not exchangeable) is not money in this working sense, even if it looks like a bill.
7. nostos is a homecoming or return. Books 9–12 are earlier wanderings narrated by Odysseus after we have already met him at Calypso’s and among the Phaeacians, so the earlier years arrive as a story inside the story.
8. Any two careful cautions: Livy is late for the kings; Polybius is a second-century analysis, not a transcript of 509 BCE; the Twelve Tables survive as later fragments; Caesar writes as a commander about his own campaigns (primary and advocacy); a Doryphoros in marble is a later copy.
