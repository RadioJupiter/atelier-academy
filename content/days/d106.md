## Mathematics · Geometry LIX: sine of an acute angle, a first look {#math}

<article class="term" id="term-sine-of-an-acute-angle">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="sine of an acute angle" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear sine of an acute angle">Hear</button> sine of an acute angle</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>In a right triangle, the sine of an acute angle A, written sin A, is the length of the side opposite A divided by the length of the hypotenuse. The ratio is the same for a given angle in any right triangle, because those triangles are similar, from the already-taught AA similarity and scale factor. A classroom 3-4-5 right triangle, invented lengths, has sin A = 3/5 if A is opposite the side of 3. Sine is a ratio, not a length. Degrees only; the radian remains reserved. The unit circle remains reserved.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>In that 3-4-5 triangle, if A is opposite 3 and adjacent to 4, sin A = 3/5. A second right triangle with the same angle A, sides 6, 8, and 10, has sin A = 6/10, which is the same ratio 3/5.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not a first look at similar triangles, the scale factor, or the Pythagorean theorem, already taught. Not cosine, which is tomorrow. Not a trigonometric table of official measured angles, and not a unit-circle definition. Classroom lengths are invented.</dd></div>

</dl></article>

<p>A right triangle has one right angle. Each of the other two angles is acute. Today a new name is given to a ratio already sitting in that triangle.</p>
<p>The sine of an acute angle A, written sin A, is the opposite side over the hypotenuse. Why is the ratio the same whenever the angle is the same? Two right triangles that share an acute angle also share the right angle, so they are similar by AA, already taught. Corresponding sides are in the same ratio, already taught as a scale factor. So opposite over hypotenuse does not change when the triangle is enlarged or reduced.</p>
