<p>This is review. In a right triangle, sin A is opposite over hypotenuse, and cos A is adjacent over hypotenuse. The ratios stay the same for a given angle because of already-taught AA similarity and a scale factor. Sin(90° − A) = cos A, because the two acute angles add to 90° and the sides swap. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, invented lengths, if A is opposite 3, sin A = 3/5, cos A = 4/5, and sin(90° − A) = 4/5. Tangent waits until tomorrow. Degrees only. The radian and the unit circle remain reserved.</p>

<p>This is review, still on short invented figures, not a copyrighted score. Retrograde: the figure backwards. Inversion: the figure upside down. Retrograde inversion: invert first, then backwards, as already taught. A sequence: the same invented figure repeated starting on a new degree, keeping the shape and moving the starting place. Invented example already used: C–D–E–G, sequenced one step higher, becomes D–E–F–A. The retrograde of C–D–E–G is G–E–D–C, which is not a sequence. Invertible counterpoint remains reserved.</p>
