## Mathematics · Algebra X: graphs of quadratic functions {#math}

<p class="meta">60 min · Method: a table of values, then the curve; the vertex from completing the square, already taught</p>

<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-parabola">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="parabola" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear parabola">Hear</button> parabola</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>The graph of a quadratic function, y = ax² + bx + c with a ≠ 0. It is a curve with a single turning point, the vertex, not a straight line.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>y = x² − 4 has vertex (0, −4), opens upward (a = 1 > 0), and crosses the x-axis at (−2, 0) and (2, 0), because x² − 4 = (x − 2)(x + 2).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not a linear graph, and not a circle. A parabola is not “any curved graph.”</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Day 5 already solved quadratics and completed the square. Today those solutions become points and a vertex on a graph. The etymology of the name is left aside if a short, clearly sourced origin is not being repeated here.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
<article class="term" id="term-vertex-parabola">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="vertex of a parabola" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear vertex of a parabola">Hear</button> vertex of a parabola</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>The turning point of the graph of a quadratic. For y = x² + px + q, completing the square (Day 5) shows the x-coordinate of the vertex; the y-coordinate is the value of the function there. For y = ax² + bx + c, a first formula, once the square is completed or the axis of symmetry is named, is x = −b/(2a).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>For y = x² − 4, b = 0, so the vertex is at x = 0, and y = −4. For y = x² − 2x − 3, completing the square or x = −b/(2a) = 1 gives the vertex (1, −4).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not an x-intercept automatically (the vertex lies on the x-axis only when the root is repeated). And not the slope of a line.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>The axis of symmetry is the vertical line through the vertex. That is a line of symmetry of the graph, not a claim about a crater or a drawing of a head.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
</div>

**A quadratic function has a curved graph, a parabola**, not a straight line. Plot a table for y = x² − 4:

| x | y |
|---|---|
| −2 | 0 |
| −1 | −3 |
| 0 | −4 |
| 1 | −3 |
| 2 | 0 |

The points rise the same way on both sides of (0, −4). That turning point is the **vertex**. The curve crosses the x-axis at ±2, which matches the factors (x − 2)(x + 2) = 0 already available from Day 5.

**The vertex from completing the square, or from x = −b/(2a).** For y = x² − 2x − 3, x = −(−2)/2 = 1, and y(1) = 1 − 2 − 3 = −4, so the vertex is (1, −4). As a check, y = (x − 1)² − 4 after completing the square.

**Do not force a line through a parabola.** If the points are not collinear, that is the point of today’s graph.

## Writing & Rhetoric · A parenthetical interruption that does not break the series {#writing}

<p class="meta">45 min · Method: explicit repair of a series or a sentence broken by an insertion</p>

<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-parenthetical-interruption">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="parenthetical interruption" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear parenthetical interruption">Hear</button> parenthetical interruption</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>A word, phrase, or clause inserted into a sentence, often set off by commas or dashes, that adds a comment without taking over the grammatical job of the sentence.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>“Studies serve for delight, for ornament — and, when they are used, for ability.” The series is still three <em>for</em>-phrases; the interruption is a comment.</dd></div>

<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Yesterday’s Bacon series is the test case. Parallelism (week 5) still binds the three parts; the interruption must not un-match them.</dd></div>
</dl></article>

A comment may sit inside a sentence if the sentence can still be read without it and if a series remains parallel. Use commas or dashes honestly; do not let the interruption become a second essay.

Test: remove the interruption. If the sentence still works, and if a three-part series is still three matching parts, the insertion is doing its job.

## Physics · A first look at torque {#physics}

<p class="meta">50 min · Method: concrete lever, then the product of force and perpendicular distance</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

<div class="keyterms">
<h4>Key terms</h4>
<article class="term" id="term-torque-first-look">
<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="torque (first look)" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear torque (first look)">Hear</button> torque (first look)</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>A measure of how effectively a force tends to turn an object about a point or an axis: the product of the force and the perpendicular distance from the point (or axis) to the line of the force. In this first look, τ = F × (perpendicular distance).</dd></div>
<div><dt>Example</dt><dd>A 4 N force whose line is 0.5 m perpendicular from a pivot gives a torque of 4 N × 0.5 m = 2 N·m about that pivot.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Not this</dt><dd>Not the same as work (also a force times a distance, but along the displacement), and not a new unit called a joule. The unit here is the newton-meter (N·m), named as a torque, not as an energy.</dd></div>
<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>A door handle far from the hinge is a picture of a larger perpendicular distance, and so a larger torque for the same force. A force through the pivot gives no torque.</dd></div>
</dl></article>
</div>


A force can turn a body about a pivot. **Torque**, in this first look, is

**τ = F × d⊥**

where d⊥ is the **perpendicular** distance from the pivot to the line of the force. The unit is the **newton-meter (N·m)**. That product has the same dimensions as a joule, but we do **not** call a torque a joule. The joule remains a unit of work or energy.

If the line of the force goes through the pivot, d⊥ = 0, and the torque is zero. If the force is not perpendicular to a given radius, you must use the perpendicular distance to the force’s line (or the perpendicular component of the force); today’s numbers will be given so that the perpendicular distance is clear.


</div>

## History · How we know: later writers, and a first-person record {#history}

<h5><button type="button" class="listen listen-inline no-speak" data-say="Res Gestae as advocacy" data-lang="en-US" aria-label="Hear Res Gestae as advocacy">Hear</button> Res Gestae as advocacy</h5>
<dl class="frayer">
<div><dt>Definition</dt><dd>The Res Gestae Divi Augusti is a first-person record of deeds attributed to Augustus, known from later copies (notably an inscription at Ankara). It is a primary statement of how he wished to be seen, and therefore advocacy as well as a record.</dd></div>

<div class="wide"><dt>Connects to</dt><dd>Use it with later writers, not instead of them, and not as a simple confirmation of them.</dd></div>
</dl></article>

Two later writers, often used for this period, are **Tacitus** (early second century CE) and **Suetonius** (early second century CE). They wrote **about a century** after Augustus’s death (14 CE). They are evidence; they are not eyewitness diaries of 27 BCE.

**The Res Gestae** is different in kind: a first-person record of deeds, copied in inscriptions. Read it as **advocacy** as well as a list: it tells you what was claimed, in a public voice, about those deeds.

A careful habit: date the source, name its kind, and ask what it can show. Do not flatten all three into “what happened.”
