Remember

When Hooke’s law holds, the stored elastic potential energy is PE_el = ½kx², the area of the triangle under the kx-against-x graph. OpenStax Example 16.2: k = 50.0 N/m, x = 0.150 m, PE_el = 0.563 J. This is not a first look at F = −kx, work, or energy as kinds.

Key terms: elastic potential energy.

1. Using OpenStax’s k = 50.0 N/m and x = 0.150 m, show that PE_el = 0.563 J.

2. An invented classroom spring has k = 80 N/m and is compressed 0.050 m. What is PE_el?

3. Why is “PE_el = kx” a miss?

4. For each of today's key terms (elastic potential energy), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
