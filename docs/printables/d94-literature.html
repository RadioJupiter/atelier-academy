Literature · The leaves, “My son,” and the close of Inferno III (lines 112–136)

Week 19 · Day 94 · Thursday · one-lesson review

Remember

The people leave the margin as autumn leaves, one by one, at signals, as a bird unto its lure. The guide says, “My son,” and explains why they pass and why Charon complained. The canto ends when the dusk champaign trembles, a vermilion light overmasters the speaker, and he falls as one whom sleep has seized.

Key terms: the leaves simile (Inferno III), “My son,” in Inferno III.

1. Write out, or closely recall, the first three lines of the leaves simile, or the “in similar wise…” three lines.

2. What does the guide, addressing the speaker as “My son,” say about a good soul, and about Charon’s complaint?

3. How does Canto III end, in Longfellow’s wording?

4. For each of today's key terms (the leaves simile (Inferno III), “My son,” in Inferno III), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
