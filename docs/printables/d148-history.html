Remember

The letter gives “one hundred and eighty-eight great leagues” on one side of Española, a figure in the letter’s body, not Major’s earlier footnote on the 178 leagues. “Villa de Navidad” is the name he says he gave. Official areas, conversions, and gold quantities are not invented.

1. In Major’s wording, what comparison does the letter draw between Española and Spain, and what league figure does it give for one of the four sides?

2. Why is this 188-league figure not Major’s footnote on last week’s 178 leagues?

3. What does the letter say of Villa de Navidad and of gold mines, and what official claims must you not add?

4. Quote the “greater circumference than all Spain” clause, keeping Major’s place-names.

5. How does this still differ from the Treaty of Tordesillas, already taught?

6. What remains reserved?
