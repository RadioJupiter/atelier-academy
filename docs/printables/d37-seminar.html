Remember

An octave (from octavus, “eighth”) is the frame. A fifth inverts to a fourth, a third to a sixth (numbers add to nine). Perfect stays perfect; major inverts to minor. Not the math or logic inverse.

Key terms: interval inversion, octave as a frame.

1. What is the inversion of a perfect fourth, and of a minor third (E–G, already used as a minor third in this course)?

2. In one sentence, how the octave is the frame for these pairs.

3. For each of today's key terms (interval inversion, octave as a frame), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Why “inversion” in this lesson is not f⁻¹ and not the logic inverse.
