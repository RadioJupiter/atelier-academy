Carry the commonplace through to a close that returns to the type of fault. Do not open a new claim at the end, and do not name a living person.

1. What should the close of this week’s commonplace do, and what must it not do?

2. Give a fair two-sentence close on the invented type.

3. Why is opening a new untried claim at the end a miss?

4. Write the commonplace as a short continuous piece: opening, two amplifying reasons, a contrast, and a close, without naming a living person.

5. In one sentence, distinguish a commonplace from a confirmation.

6. What remains reserved?
