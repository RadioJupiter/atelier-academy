Remember

Books 11–16: the chiefs are wounded, the ships are in danger, Patroclus goes out in Achilles’ armor with a limit he does not keep, and Hector kills him. An aristeia is a hero’s run of excellence; it does not guarantee survival. The armor is not Achilles’ return.

Key terms: aristeia, Patroclus in Achilles’ armor.

1. In your own words, what is an aristeia, and how does Patroclus’s aristeia end differently from Diomedes’ in Book 5?

2. What does the death of Sarpedon, a son of Zeus, add to Book 16, if even Zeus cannot simply keep him alive without breaking the poem’s order?

3. For each of today's key terms (aristeia, Patroclus in Achilles’ armor), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Write a close reading (about a page) of Achilles’ charge to Patroclus and of one later moment when the limit is visibly gone.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
