Remember

In a major key, I, IV, and V are the primary triads (all major). In C: C, F, G major. A first authentic cadence is V going to I. Not a full catalogue of cadences, and not a minor-key lesson today.

Key terms: I, IV, and V (major key), authentic cadence (first look).

1. Play or sing I–IV–V–I in C major, slowly, and write one sentence on how V–I feels different from IV–I, in your ear, without forcing a metaphor.

2. Why is V in G major D–F♯–A, not D–F–A?

3. For each of today's key terms (I, IV, and V (major key), authentic cadence (first look)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Name the scale degrees of I, IV, and V, and one reason they are called tonic, subdominant, and dominant (a first naming, not a history of the terms).

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
