Remember

Netherfield is let, in the novel, to a single man named Bingley, “four or five thousand a year” in Mrs. Bennet’s report. Mr. Bennet prefers Lizzy’s “quickness,” and treats “nerves” as “old friends.” These are the book’s figures and jokes, not official facts.

1. In the 1813 wording, who has taken Netherfield, and what income does Mrs. Bennet name? Why is that income not an official salary?

2. What does Mrs. Bennet want Mr. Bennet to do, and why, in her wording?

3. What does Mr. Bennet say of Lizzy, and how does he answer the complaint about nerves?

4. Quote the phrase “four or five thousand a year,” and the phrase about Lizzy’s “quickness.”

5. Why must “girls” and “daughters,” when they appear, stay clearly inside the novel?

6. What official identification must you still not add?
