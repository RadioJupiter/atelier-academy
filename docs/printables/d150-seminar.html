Remember

Gray’s first thoracic vertebra has an entire facet for the first rib and a demi-facet for the second, a body “like that of a cervical vertebra,” and a spinous process “thick, long, and almost horizontal.” Official lengths are not invented, and the lesson is not a diagnosis.

1. In Gray’s wording, what facets does the first thoracic vertebra have for the ribs, and how is the body described?

2. What does Gray say of the spinous process, the transverse processes, and the upper vertebral notches?

3. Why is the lesson not a diagnosis, what official figure must you not invent, and what remains reserved?

4. For each of today's key terms (the first thoracic vertebra (Gray, 1918)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
