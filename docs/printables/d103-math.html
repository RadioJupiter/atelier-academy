Remember

Similar solids: corresponding lengths scale by k, areas by k², volumes by k³. The scale factor itself is already taught. Classroom lengths are invented. Exact π answers stay in terms of π.

Key terms: similar solids.

1. If corresponding lengths of similar solids scale by k, how do corresponding areas and volumes scale?

2. Two classroom cubes have edges 2 and 6. What is k, and how do their volumes compare?

3. Yesterday’s similar cones have radii 3 and 6. What is k, and how do their volumes compare, using k³?

4. Two similar classroom cubes have edges 3 and 6. The smaller has volume 27. What is the volume of the larger?
