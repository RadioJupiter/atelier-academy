Remember

A commonplace amplifies a general, already-acknowledged fault of a type. It is not a confirmation, not a refutation, and not a trial of a living person. This week’s invented type is treating a fable as if it were history.

1. What is a commonplace in this course, and how does it differ from a confirmation and from a refutation?

2. What invented type is used this week, and what already-taught reason can open the amplification?

3. Why is naming a living person a miss?

4. For each of today's key terms (commonplace (progymnasmata)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
