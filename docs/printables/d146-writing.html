Remember

A confirmation shows why a claim holds. The set invented claim is “If two sides of a triangle are equal, the angles opposite those sides are equal.” It is already-taught isosceles geometry, not a new theorem and not a refutation.

1. What is a confirmation, and how does it differ from a refutation?

2. State the set invented claim, and give one reason, from already-taught geometry, why it holds.

3. Why is this not a first look at the isosceles theorem, and why must no living person be introduced?

4. For each of today's key terms (confirmation (progymnasmata)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
