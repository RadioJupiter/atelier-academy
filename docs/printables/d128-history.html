Remember

The May 4 proviso uses the bull’s wording: not in actual possession of any Christian king or prince up to the birthday from which 1493 begins. A later sentence keeps a right already acquired before that birthday from being withdrawn.

1. Quote or closely restate the May 4 proviso about possession, including the bull’s wording for the date.

2. What does the later sentence say about a right already acquired before that birthday?

3. How does this differ from the May 3 proviso, and what official conversions and later treaties remain reserved?

4. What does a later clause already forbid, even to imperial and royal persons, without a special permit?

5. Why keep the bull’s wording “the birthday … from which the present year … 1493 begins,” rather than only a later short name?

6. What official figures must you still not invent?
