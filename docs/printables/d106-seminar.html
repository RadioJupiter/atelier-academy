Remember

Ferison is EIO-3: No M are P; some M are S; therefore some S are not P. The conclusion is particular and negative. Last week’s Felapton had all M are S, two universals. Ferio is first figure, already taught, not today’s mood. Invented examples are classroom terms.

1. State Ferison’s two premises and its conclusion, and name the figure.

2. How does Ferison differ from last week’s Felapton?

3. How does Ferison differ from first-figure Ferio, already taught?

4. Give an invented classroom syllogism in Ferison, and say why it is invented.
