Remember

The pluperfect of a deponent is the perfect participle plus the imperfect of sum: locūtus eram, I had spoken. Locūtus sum is the perfect. Loquēbar is the imperfect, with no participle.

1. Give the six pluperfect forms of loquor, with meanings.

2. In Puer locūtus erat, et tum tacēbat, why is locūtus erat used, not loquēbātur and not locūtus est?

3. Why is puerō locūtō not a pluperfect main-clause form?

4. For each of today's key terms (pluperfect of a deponent), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
