Remember

Disjunctive syllogism: P or Q; not P; therefore Q. Default “or” is inclusive: from P you may not infer not-Q, unless the problem says exactly one. This is not modus ponens or modus tollens. Day 16’s necessary and sufficient conditions are already taught, not reteached today.

Key terms: disjunctive syllogism, inclusive or, in a first look.

1. In your own words, what is disjunctive syllogism, and how is it different from modus ponens?

2. Why is “it is a pencil, so it is not a brush” not given by the default “or”?

3. For each of today's key terms (disjunctive syllogism, inclusive or, in a first look), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Give a fresh “or” pair (not the pencil and brush) and apply disjunctive syllogism in one direction.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
