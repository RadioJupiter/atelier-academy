Remember

Damping removes mechanical energy: Wnc = Δ(KE + PE), and Wnc is negative. With small damping, the period is nearly unchanged and the amplitude falls. Underdamped: overshoots and crosses one or more times. Critically damped: returns as quickly as possible, overshooting at most once. Overdamped: slower than critical, without a train of oscillations. Last week’s undamped energy equation is the contrast in which those losses were neglected. Section 16.8 is reserved.

Key terms: damped harmonic motion, underdamped, critically damped, and overdamped.

1. What does a damping force do to mechanical energy, and how is that written as Wnc?

2. With a small amount of damping, what happens to the period, and what happens to the amplitude?

3. Distinguish underdamped, critically damped, and overdamped motion, in OpenStax’s sense.

4. Which of the three returns to equilibrium as quickly as possible?
