Remember

Hammurabi's stele is a public primary source: a law code and a royal self-portrait. Sections 196–199 apply lex talionis in full only to a victim of full status; lower status brings a payment instead. When two reliable sources disagree (Johns: 'diorite'; the Louvre: basalt), prefer the one with better access to the object and note that knowledge can change.

Key terms: stele, lex talionis.

1. Johns (1903) called the stele 'black diorite'; the Louvre now describes it as basalt. How should a historian handle that disagreement?

2. For each of today's key terms (stele, lex talionis), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

3. Sourcing. Who made the stele, for what audience and for what purpose? Why might a king set up his laws in public stone?

4. Close reading. What principle governs section 196? How do sections 198 and 199 change it? What does that tell you about Babylonian society?
