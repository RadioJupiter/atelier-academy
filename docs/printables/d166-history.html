Remember

The letter gives twenty-five leagues as the apparent length of the island on the side where they arrived, and more than two leagues along the shore in search of a “harbour.” “Harbour” stays inside the quotation. No official modern conversion is taken.

1. What does the letter say about the length of the island, and about the distance sailed along the shore?

2. Why does “harbour” stay inside a quotation in this course’s prose?

3. What later sentences of the same paragraph wait until Wednesday?

4. For each of today's key terms (twenty-five leagues, and a search for a “harbour,” from the second-voyage letter), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
