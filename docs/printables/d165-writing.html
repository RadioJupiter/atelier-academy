Remember

A commonplace amplifies a general fault. An encomium praises. An invective blames a type, or an already-taught fault of a type. Do not substitute one job for another, and do not name a living person.

1. What is an invective in this course, and how does it differ from an encomium and from a commonplace?

2. What invented subject was blamed this week, and what already-taught reason was used?

3. Why must a living person not be named?

4. Write a short mixed piece: a brief commonplace, a brief encomium, and a brief invective, clearly distinguished, without naming a living person.

5. Name one near-miss for each of the three jobs.

6. What remains reserved?
