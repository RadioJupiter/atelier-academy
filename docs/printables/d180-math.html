Five Check items. You need four out of five to move on. Not a score or a prize. A line and a plane; sine, cosine, and tangent; limits; continuity; the derivative of invented f(x) = x². Invented lengths and functions. A formal epsilon argument remains reserved.

1. State the three already-taught relations of a line and a plane in space, and say when a line is perpendicular to a plane.

2. In an invented 3-4-5 triangle, with A opposite 3, give sin A, cos A, and tan A, and check that tan A = sin A / cos A.

3. Give lim x→3 2x, and lim x→2 (x² − 4)/(x − 2). On invented f(x) = 2x and g(x) = x + 1, as x approaches 3, give lim (f + g), lim (f g), and lim (f / g).

4. State the two conditions for continuity at a point, and say why invented (x² − 4)/(x − 2) is not continuous at 2, even though the limit is 4.

5. For invented f(x) = x², write the already-taught rule f'(x), and find f'(3) and f'(0). Why is continuity at 3 not the same statement as f'(3) = 6?
