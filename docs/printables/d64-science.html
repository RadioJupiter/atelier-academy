Remember

The triple point of water is the unique condition where ice, liquid, and vapor coexist: 273.16 K on ITS-90, which is 0.01 °C. The ice point at one atmosphere is a different condition, about 0 °C. They are not two names for the same point. The source is the IAPWS FAQ.

Key terms: triple point.

1. What three phases are present at a triple point, and why is the point called unique?

2. Convert 273.16 K to degrees Celsius, and name the condition it describes for water.

3. How is the ice point at one atmosphere different from the triple point?

4. What is the sourced ITS-90 atmospheric boiling temperature, and why is it not the triple point?
