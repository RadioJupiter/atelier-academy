Remember

Independent events satisfy P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Mutually exclusive events cannot both happen, so their “and” probability is 0, not the product.

1. What are independent events, and what is P(A and B) when A and B are independent?

2. On two invented fair coins, tossed independently, what is P(both heads)? On an invented fair cube and fair coin, independent, what is P(6 and heads)?

3. Why is P(4 and 5) on one invented fair cube not the product (1/6)×(1/6)?

4. For each of today's key terms (independent events), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
