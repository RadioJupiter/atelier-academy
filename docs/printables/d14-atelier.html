Atelier · Cross-hatching and control of value

Week 3 · Day 14 · Thursday · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

NameDate

Remember

Cross-hatching is a second form-following layer, at a shallow angle, used only where value must go down. Control means hitting an intended step on the value scale and keeping the families. Do not cover a highlight, and do not hide a bad contour.

Key terms: cross-hatching, control of value.

1. In your own words, how does cross-hatching differ from the first hatch, and when should you refuse to add it?

2. Why should a cross on a cube’s plane still lie on that plane, rather than curve as if the cube were a sphere?

3. For each of today's key terms (cross-hatching, control of value), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Continue or redraw one solid: first hatch, then a controlled cross only in the darker families, plus the cast shadow. Keep a nine-step strip in the margin. About 35 minutes.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
