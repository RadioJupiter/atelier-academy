Remember

Gray names the first cervical vertebra the atlas because it supports the globe of the head. It has no body and no spinous process; it is ring-like, with an anterior arch, a posterior arch, and two lateral masses. Official lengths remain reserved.

1. Why is the first cervical vertebra called the atlas, and what is its chief peculiarity?

2. What other peculiarities does Gray name, and how is the ring made up?

3. What official figure must you not invent, and what remains reserved?

4. For each of today's key terms (the atlas (first cervical vertebra)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
