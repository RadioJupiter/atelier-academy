Remember

V = B × h for a right prism or a right cylinder. B may be ℓw, ½bh, πr², or π(R² − r²). Exact cylinder answers stay in terms of π. No new solid is introduced.

1. A right triangular prism has a base of area 18 and height 7. What is V?

2. A classroom cylinder has diameter 8 and height 3. What is V, in terms of π?

3. Why must 45π and 60 not be compared as if both were plain whole numbers?

4. A classroom tube has R = 6, r = 4, and h = 3. What is the volume of the material, in terms of π?

5. A classroom box 10 × 2 × 3 is stacked on another box of the same size, making one taller prism. What is the combined volume?

6. A classroom cylinder has volume 100π cubic units and height 4. What is its radius?
