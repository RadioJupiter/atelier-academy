Remember

A All S are P; E No S are P; I Some S are P; O Some S are not P. “Some” means at least one. A and O, E and I are contradictories. A and E are contraries. The traditional square assumes there is at least one S.

Key terms: categorical sentences (A, E, I, O), square of opposition (first look).

1. Write the contradictory of “All cyclic quadrilaterals have opposite angles supplementary,” and say whether that contradictory is true or false, given Day 23.

2. A student hears “some” as “only some, not all.” Repair the meaning for the I-sentence “Some squares are rectangles.”

3. For each of today's key terms (categorical sentences (A, E, I, O), square of opposition (first look)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Give a true A-sentence and its false O-contradictory, from this course’s geometry or language facts.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
