Remember

A limit is a value approached. lim x→3 2x = 6; lim x→2 (x² − 4)/(x − 2) = 4, though the expression is not defined at 2; lim n→∞ 1/n = 0, and ∞ is not a number to substitute.

1. State lim x→3 2x, lim x→2 (x² − 4)/(x − 2), and lim n→∞ 1/n, and say which one is not defined at the point approached.

2. Why is ∞ not to be substituted, and why is a formal epsilon argument still a miss?

3. For the invented h(x) = (x² − 1)/(x − 1), x ≠ 1, what is lim x→1 h(x)?

4. On an invented table, as x takes 4.9, 4.99, 5.01, and 5.1, the values of x + 1 are 5.9, 5.99, 6.01, and 6.1. What is lim x→5 (x + 1)?

5. Why is writing f(2) = 4 a miss for f(x) = (x² − 4)/(x − 2)?

6. What remains reserved?
