Atelier · Accents and a point of focus

Week 4 · Day 19 · Thursday · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

Remember

Accents are a few small, decided dark (or sharp) touches at real contacts or deep shadows, placed after the families are right. A point of focus is the most particular passage; quiet the rest without falsifying it. A drawing full of accents has no accent.

Key terms: accent, point of focus.

1. In your own words, why does a drawing full of accents have no accent?

2. Name two places on a plaster nose or on a group of solids that are plausible accents, and one place that is probably not.

3. For each of today's key terms (accent, point of focus), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Finish the study already in progress: two or three accents, one chosen focus, a stand-back, and no new invented light. About 35 minutes.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
