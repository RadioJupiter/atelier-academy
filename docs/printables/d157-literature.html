Remember

In Chapter 8, Elizabeth is called “an excellent walker.” Miss Bingley speaks of a “petticoat, six inches deep in mud,” and “above her ancles in dirt.” The walk is said to “shew an abominable sort of conceited independence.” Bingley answers, “It shews an affection for her sister that is very pleasing.” “Ancles,” “shew,” and “shews” stay inside quotations.

1. In Chapter 8, how is Elizabeth’s walk described by Miss Bingley, and what does Bingley say it shows?

2. Why do “ancles,” “shew,” and “shews” stay inside quotations?

3. How does this conversation continue last week’s Chapter 7 without treating the walk as a new first look?

4. For each of today's key terms (Pride and Prejudice, Chapter 8, Elizabeth’s walk and the muddy petticoat), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
