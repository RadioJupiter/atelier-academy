Remember

The sacrum is a large triangular bone, a wedge between the two hip bones, its apex meeting the coccyx. The coccyx is usually four rudimentary “vertebræ,” the first largest, the last a nodule. Official lengths remain reserved, and the lesson is not a diagnosis.

1. How does the sacrum meet the coccyx, in the already-taught wording?

2. Of how many rudimentary “vertebræ” is the coccyx usually formed, and which piece is a nodule of bone?

3. Why is this not a new first-look term, and why is the lesson not a diagnosis?

4. In your own words, keep the sacrum and the coccyx distinct, quoting “vertebræ” when you use Gray’s spelling.

5. What remains reserved?
