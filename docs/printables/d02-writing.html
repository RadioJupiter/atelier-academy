Remember

Copy exactly first (spelling, punctuation, line breaks if asked). Then vary: same skeleton, new nouns and verbs. Do not paraphrase loosely until the copy is accurate.

1. What do you do first in imitatio: copy or vary?

2. What is copia, in one sentence?

3. Copy the passage by hand, exactly, including the 18th-century spellings try'd and compleat. Then proofread letter by letter against the original. Count your errors.

4. Analyse. (a) The first four sentences are short; the fifth is long. What does that change of rhythm do? (b) In the long sentence, list the actions in order. How does Franklin keep a long sentence clear?

5. Imitate. Write your own sentence with the same structure as the long sentence ("With this view I took …, and, making …, laid …, and then, without …, tried to …, by …"), describing how you would learn a skill of your own choice.

6. Copia. Write ten different English sentences that mean "Your letter pleased me greatly". Vary vocabulary, voice (active/passive), sentence type (question, exclamation) and figures of speech.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
