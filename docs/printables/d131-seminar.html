Remember

If A and B cannot both happen, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B). On an invented fair cube, P(4 or 5) = 1/3, and P(even or 5) = 2/3. If they overlap, the simple sum is a miss.

1. What does it mean for two events to be mutually exclusive, and how do you find P(A or B) then?

2. On an invented fair cube, give P(4 or 5) and P(even or 5), with the sums.

3. Why is P(even or greater than 3) not 1, and why must you not invent an official probability of the weather?

4. For each of today's key terms (mutually exclusive events), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
