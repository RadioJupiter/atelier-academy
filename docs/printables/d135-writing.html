Writing · Friday mix: a chreia distinguished from a narrative and from a fable

Week 27 · Day 135 · Friday · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

Remember

A chreia attributes a saying or deed to a named person on a brief occasion. A narrative tells an event in order, without a required saying. A fable has a stated moral.

1. What is a chreia, and how does it differ from a narrative and from a fable?

2. Retell the set Diogenes chreia, quoting the 1859 saying, and add one reason it fits.

3. What remains reserved, and what official claim must you not invent?

4. Write a three-part original chreia, invented figures labeled invented, and say why it is not a fable.

5. Why does “any thing,” if quoted, stay inside the quotation?

6. Which of the three exercises has a stated moral, and which has a required famous or attributed saying?
