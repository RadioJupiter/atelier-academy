History · A clove-scented leaf, two hours, and a fall of water, from the second-voyage letter

Week 33 · Day 165 · Friday · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

Remember

The letter describes a leaf with “the finest smell of cloves,” “like a laurel leaf,” a stay of only two hours, a later island at “seven or eight leagues,” and a fall of water “of the breadth of an ox” that “appeared to fall from the sky.” A footnote naming “Guadaloupe” is Major’s note, not the body.

1. What does the letter say of the leaf, and how long did they remain on that island?

2. How does the letter describe the fall of water, and what wager does it record?

3. Why is a later official island name in a footnote a miss if the job is the body of the letter?

4. For each of today's key terms (a clove-scented leaf, two hours, and a fall of water), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
