Remember

Formal analysis describes what is there (line, shape, mass, value, color, texture, space, composition) before any interpretation. Interpretation must be labeled and paired with testable evidence. Chauvet paintings are more than 30,000 years old; Lascaux about 17,000. Animals dominate; artists used the rock's volume, charcoal, and mineral pigments. Scholars still disagree about purpose.

Key terms: formal analysis, interpretation.

1. Name four properties you must describe in a formal analysis before you interpret a cave painting.

2. For each of today's key terms (formal analysis, interpretation), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

3. Find a good photograph of the "Hall of the Bulls" at Lascaux (the French Ministry of Culture's Lascaux site has them). Write a formal analysis of one animal in 150 words: line, shape, value, color, use of the rock surface. No interpretation yet.

4. Then write two sentences of interpretation, clearly marked as interpretation, and state what evidence would be needed to test them.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
