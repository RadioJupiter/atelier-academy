Remember

A kouros is an Archaic standing youth: nude, left foot forward, even weight, often an Archaic smile. Contrapposto shifts the weight to one leg and counter-tilts hips and shoulders; the Kritios Boy (Acropolis, early fifth century BCE) is the type example in this lesson. Compare with Egypt without erasing differences.

Key terms: kouros, contrapposto.

1. What is the Archaic smile, and what is a sound way not to read it?

2. The heads of the Lion Gate lionesses are missing (Day 13), and some kouroi have lost attributes or paint. How should missing parts change what you claim?

3. For each of today's key terms (kouros, contrapposto), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Write a looking-note on a kouros (any well-photographed example): stance, hair, face, and one limit of what the photograph will not give you (scale, paint, back of the statue, find-spot).
