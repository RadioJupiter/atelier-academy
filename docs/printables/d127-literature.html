Remember

In the 1813 Chapter 2, Mr. Bennet was among the earliest to wait on Bingley. He had always intended to visit, while assuring his wife that he should not go, and she has no knowledge of it until the evening after the visit is paid.

1. In the 1813 wording, what had Mr. Bennet always intended, what had he assured his wife, and when does she first know of the visit?

2. How does the disclosure begin, and why is “since we are not to visit” already behind the facts?

3. What must stay inside quotations, and what remains reserved until Thursday?

4. For each of today's key terms (the undisclosed visit (Pride and Prejudice, Chapter 2)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
