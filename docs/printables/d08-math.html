Remember

Larger side ↔ opposite larger angle. For sides a, b, c: a + b > c, and the two similar inequalities. A 'triangle' with sides 2, 3, 6 is impossible.

1. Can 2, 3 and 6 be the sides of a triangle? Why or why not?

2. In a triangle, the largest angle is opposite which side?

3. Can a triangle have sides (a) 3, 4, 8; (b) 5, 7, 11; (c) 6, 6, 12?

4. Two sides of a triangle are 4 and 9. What lengths can the third side have?

5. A triangle has angles 50°, 60° and 70°. Order its sides from shortest to longest, naming them by their opposite angles.

6. Prove theorem 3 using theorem 1.
