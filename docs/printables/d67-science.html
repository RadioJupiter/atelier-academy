Remember

Surface tension is a tension in a liquid surface, reported for example in mN/m. For ordinary water at 20 °C, IAPWS R1-76(2014), Table 1, gives 72.74 mN/m, with an uncertainty of 0.36 mN/m. A classroom needle is not that official figure. The surface is not a separate solid film.

Key terms: surface tension.

1. What values does the same table give at 0.01 °C and at 25 °C?

2. Why will this lesson not invent an official surface tension at 22 °C from an average of 20 °C and 25 °C?

3. For each of today's key terms (surface tension), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
