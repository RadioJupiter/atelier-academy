Remember

For overlapping events, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B). On an invented fair cube, P(even or greater than 3) = 2/3. Mutually exclusive events are the special case in which the overlap is 0.

1. State the addition rule for overlapping events, and say why the overlap is subtracted.

2. On an invented fair cube, find P(even or greater than 3), naming the faces in each part.

3. When does the rule reduce to P(A) + P(B), and why is P(A) × P(B) not automatically the overlap?

4. For each of today's key terms (the addition rule for overlapping events), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
