Remember

The set extract is Procopius, Buildings I.1.6, in Dewing’s 1940 translation (public domain). Justinian, in this passage, takes over a state harassed by disorder and makes it greater in extent and more illustrious, including by expelling barbarians. These are Procopius’s claims in a work of praise. Later sections remain reserved.

Key terms: Justinian, The Buildings (Procopius).

1. Why does this lesson not treat “greater in extent” as a measured official area this course has computed?

2. A student writes that Justinian was a western emperor who ended Rome in 476. What two confusions is that?

3. For each of today's key terms (Justinian, The Buildings (Procopius)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Why does Procopius mention the Books on the Wars in this sentence, and will this week open those books?
