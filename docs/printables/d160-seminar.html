Remember

In Gray, 1918, the lumbar vertebrae are “the largest segments of the movable part of the vertebral column,” distinguished by the absence of a foramen in the transverse process and the absence of facets on the sides of the body. The body is large, a little thicker in front than behind; the vertebral foramen is triangular; the spinous process is thick, broad, and somewhat quadrilateral, and projects backward. “Vertebræ” stays inside the quotation. Official lengths remain reserved. The lesson is not a diagnosis.

1. In Gray, 1918, how are the lumbar vertebrae distinguished, and how is the body described?

2. What does Gray say of the vertebral foramen and of the spinous process?

3. Why is an official length, or a diagnosis, a miss?

4. For each of today's key terms (the lumbar vertebrae (Gray, 1918)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
