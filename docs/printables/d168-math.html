Remember

If f and g are continuous at a, then f + g and f g are continuous at a, and f / g is continuous at a when g(a) ≠ 0, because the algebra of limits matches the values. This is a mix of already-taught rules, not a new first look.

1. On invented f(x) = 2x and g(x) = x + 1, why is f g continuous at 3?

2. Why does the quotient rule of last week not, by itself, settle continuity of (x² − 4)/(x − 2) at 2?

3. On invented p(x) = 3x and q(x) = 1, is p / q continuous at 2? Give the value and the limit.

4. On invented u(x) = x and v(x) = 4, show that u + v and u v are continuous at 5.

5. On invented p(x) = 3x and q(x) = x − 2, why is p / q not continuous at 2?
