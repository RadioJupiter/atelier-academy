Remember

The named day itself is ablative (Kalendīs, Nonīs, Īdibus) with an agreeing month. Prīdiē takes the accusative and means only the day immediately before that named day.

Key terms: the ablative of the named day, prīdiē.

1. Write prīdiē Kalendās Iānuāriās as a numbered date, and explain why the month is not January.

2. Why is *prīdiē Īdibus Mārtiīs not the construction taught today?

3. For each of today's key terms (the ablative of the named day, prīdiē), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Give the Latin, with case, for “on the Nones of October” and for “the day before the Nones of October.”
