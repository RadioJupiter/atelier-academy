Remember

A maxim is a short general precept. The set example is “Eat to live, and not live to eat.” from Poor Richard, 1733. It is not a chreia.

1. What is a maxim in this course, and what is the set example, in the 1733 wording?

2. How does a maxim differ from a chreia, from a narrative, and from a fable’s moral?

3. Why must the lesson not be read as an official diet, and what remains reserved?

4. For each of today's key terms (maxim (progymnasmata)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
