Remember

The difference quotient of invented f(x) = x² is 2x + h, for h ≠ 0. The derivative is the limit as h approaches 0, so f'(x) = 2x.

1. Write the difference quotient of invented f(x) = x² at a general x, and the derivative f'(x).

2. Find f'(3), f'(2), and f'(0), and say how each can be seen both from the rule and from a limit.

3. Why is a derivative of a different function still reserved?

4. Find f'(8) and f'(−1).

5. On invented f(x) = x², is f continuous at 2? Give f(2) and the limit. Is that the same job as f'(2) = 4?
