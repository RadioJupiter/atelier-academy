Remember

Cōnātus eram, I had tried, is the pluperfect of cōnor. The participle agrees with the subject. It is not cōnābar and not cōnātus sum.

1. Give cōnātus eram, cōnāta erat, and cōnātī erāmus, with meanings.

2. In Puella cōnāta erat, et tum rīsit, why is cōnāta erat used, not cōnābātur?

3. Why does the participle change from cōnātus to cōnāta in that sentence?

4. For each of today's key terms (pluperfect of a deponent), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
