Remember

Imagery appeals to the senses. A metaphor speaks of one thing as if it were another, without like or as (those usually mark a simile). Quote shortly, attribute, and say what the language is doing. Blake (1794) is the model; a full theology of the poem is not required.

Key terms: imagery, metaphor.

1. Quote another short phrase from the opening stanza (or from a later stanza of the same poem, if you actually open the public-domain text), and label it imagery, metaphor, both, or a question that is neither yet.

2. Why is “the forest is dark” not automatically a metaphor in this lesson’s terms?

3. For each of today's key terms (imagery, metaphor), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Write two sentences of your own: one with a clear image and no metaphor, and one with a metaphor, on a geometry or studio subject.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
