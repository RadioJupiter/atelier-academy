Remember

I–IV–V–I uses the tonic, subdominant, and dominant triads, and returns to the tonic. A triad may be inverted without changing its name. The last two chords are an authentic cadence (V–I). Inversion was taught last week.

Key terms: a I–IV–V–I progression.

1. Write I–IV–V–I in G major, in root position (G, C, D, G).

2. Why is I–IV–V–I not a new name that replaces “authentic cadence”?

3. For each of today's key terms (a I–IV–V–I progression), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. A student says a first-inversion V is now a new chord, not the dominant. What was last week’s distinction?
