Dewing’s stones are a meadow of named colors. The experience comes again on later visits, as though the sight were new, and no one has had a surfeit. Both claims are already in this week’s history reading. No invented counts.

Key terms: meadow, colors, and the sight new each time (Dewing).

1. To what does Dewing compare the columns and stones, and which colors does he name?

2. What does I.1.62 say of each successive occasion?

3. Why must the two claims not be blended into one sentence that drops either the colors or the repeated visit?

4. For each of today's key terms (meadow, colors, and the sight new each time (Dewing)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
