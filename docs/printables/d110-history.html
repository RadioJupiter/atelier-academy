History · Friday mix: Dewing I.2.1–16, the statue, Eirenê, and Samson

Week 22 · Day 110 · Friday · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

Remember

I.2.1–6: the Augusteum; seven courses used as seats; a column not a monolith; brass softer than pure gold, in Dewing’s wording; a bronze horse facing east. I.2.7–12: a figure habited like Achilles, a globe with a cross, no sword or spear, the right hand toward the rising sun; “So much, then, for this statue.” I.2.13–16: Eirenê, scarcely second save to Sophia; the hospice of Samson, burned and rebuilt, and endowed. I.2.17 remains reserved. Invent no official height, size, or income. Quote “colour.”

1. What is the Augusteum, and what must you keep about the column and the brass?

2. How is the figure habited, what does it carry, and what does it not carry?

3. What two buildings does I.2.13–16 treat, and how is Eirenê compared?

4. Why must you not invent an official height or income, and why does I.2.17 remain reserved?

5. Which way does the horse face, and which way does the figure look?

6. How are the seven courses used?
