Remember

The play ends with the deaths of Antigone, Haemon, and Eurydice, and with Creon alive, seeing the cost too late. A short Jebb (1917) choral close may be used, attributed. Name the cost; do not make a list of wounds.

Key terms: the close of Antigone.

1. Copy the Jebb closing sentences, then attribute them correctly (author, play, translator, year).

2. How does today’s close change what a fair essay may now say, and what it still should not do (a plot dump)?

3. For each of today's key terms (the close of Antigone), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. In your own words, one sentence each for Antigone, Haemon, and Creon at the end, without a gore-first wording.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
