Remember

On invented f(x) = x², the difference quotient is 2x + h, for h ≠ 0, and f'(x) = 2x.

1. Write the definition of f'(a), and the rule f'(x) for invented f(x) = x².

2. Find f'(3), f'(0), and f'(6).

3. Why may h not be 0 in the difference quotient, and why may ∞ not be substituted for h?

4. Find f'(9) and f'(1/4).

5. On invented f(x) = x², why is f continuous at 3, and why is that not the same statement as f'(3) = 6?
