Remember

in + accusative = into; in + ablative = in (place where). The preposition stays; the case changes. Town names still use the exception from last week.

Key terms: in + accusative, in + ablative.

1. Write in Latin: ‘The boys enter into the garden’ (use intrant or eunt).

2. Correct the second form if the meaning is location, not motion: Mārcus in aquam natat when you mean he is swimming in the water.

3. For each of today's key terms (in + accusative, in + ablative), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Give ‘into the water’ and ‘in the water.’

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
