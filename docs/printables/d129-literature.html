Remember

Lizzy dates the ball “To-morrow fortnight,” the book’s wording. Mr. Bennet then reveals that he has “actually paid the visit.” The astonishment is “just what he wished.” 1813 forms stay inside quotations.

1. In the 1813 wording, how does Lizzy date the next ball, and what does Mr. Bennet say he has already done?

2. What does the narrator say of the astonishment, and what does Mrs. Bennet then declare?

3. Which 1813 forms must stay inside quotations, and why must “To-morrow fortnight” not be replaced in the quotation itself?

4. Quote “as I have actually paid the visit, we cannot escape the acquaintance now.”

5. Why must “girls” and “neices,” when they appear, stay clearly inside the novel?

6. What remains reserved, and what official identification must you not add?
