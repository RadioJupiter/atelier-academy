Remember

The seventh cervical vertebra is named vertebra prominens for its long, prominent, not bifurcated spinous process, in Gray 1918. Official lengths remain reserved. The lesson is not a diagnosis, and no body is being described from life.

1. In Gray’s wording, what is the most distinctive characteristic of the seventh cervical vertebra, and what is it named?

2. What does Gray say about the spinous process and about the usual course of the artery and vein?

3. Why is the lesson not a diagnosis, and what official figure must you not invent?

4. For each of today's key terms (the seventh cervical vertebra (vertebra prominens)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
