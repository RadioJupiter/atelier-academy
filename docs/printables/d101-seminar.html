Remember

Felapton is EAO-3: No M are P; all M are S; therefore some S are not P. The conclusion is particular and negative, not “no S are P,” and not Darapti’s “some S are P.” The middle term is the subject of both premises. Invented examples are classroom terms, not official claims.

Key terms: Felapton.

1. State Felapton’s two premises and its conclusion, and name the figure.

2. Why is the conclusion “some S are not P,” and not “no S are P”?

3. How does Felapton differ from last week’s Darapti?

4. Give an invented classroom syllogism in Felapton, and say why it is invented.
