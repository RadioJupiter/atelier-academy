Remember

The deponent perfect is locūtus sum, locūtus es, locūtus est, locūtī sumus, locūtī estis, locūtī sunt, active in meaning. Cōnātus sum follows the same pattern. The participle agrees in number. This is not last week’s present table, and not the already-taught ablative absolute, which has no sum. Classroom sentences only.

1. Give the six perfect forms of loquor, and translate them as active past or present-perfect English.

2. Give the six perfect forms of cōnor, and translate them.

3. Why must the participle be locūtī, not locūtus, in nōs locūtī sumus?

4. How does locūtus est differ from puerō locūtō?
