Remember

The candle-under-the-jar phenomenon is not automatically explained by 'oxygen is a fifth of the air'. Burning wax consumes oxygen but produces CO₂ and water; a candle goes out before the oxygen is gone. Watch when the water rises, and test with more candles and a pre-warmed jar. Safety: do this with an adult present, on a non-flammable surface, away from anything that can catch fire.

Key terms: phenomenon, model (scientific).

1. Using observations from the procedure, which explanation does the evidence support: (a) oxygen is removed and nothing replaces it; or (b) the gas is heated and expands (some escapes as bubbles), then cools and contracts after the flame dies? Give two observations.

2. For each of today's key terms (phenomenon, model (scientific)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

3. Record your three heights as a fraction of the jar's height. Compare with 21%.

4. Using the observations from steps 1–3, decide which explanation the evidence supports: (a) oxygen is removed and nothing replaces it; (b) the gas is heated and expands (some escapes as bubbles), then cools and contracts after the flame dies. Give two observations that support your choice.
