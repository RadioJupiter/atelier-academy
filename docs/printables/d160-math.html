Remember

A limit is a value approached. Substitution may agree, but is not the definition. A limit may exist where the function is not defined. As n grows large, 1/n approaches 0, and ∞ is not a number to substitute.

1. What is a limit in this week’s work, and what does lim x→a f(x) = L mean?

2. State lim x→2 (x² − 4)/(x − 2) and lim n→∞ 1/n, and say why the first expression is not defined at 2.

3. Why is “1/∞ = 0” a miss as a substitution?

4. On an invented table, n = 10, 100, and 1000, what are 1/n, and what is lim n→∞ 1/n?

5. For the invented k(x) = (x² − 9)/(x − 3), x ≠ 3, what is lim x→3 k(x)?

6. What remains reserved?
