Remember

In Dewing I.4.25–26, the Church of Acacius had “fallen into ruin” and was rebuilt “from the foundations.” It is “carried on all sides on columns of astonishing whiteness,” and the floor of similar stone gives “the impression that the whole church is coated with snow.” Two stoas stand in front, “one of them making a court (peristylos), the other facing the market-place.” “Marvellous” stays inside the quotation. Official lengths are not invented. I.4.17–24 remains reserved.

1. In Dewing I.4.25–26, what had happened to the Church of Acacius, and how was it rebuilt?

2. What does Dewing say of the columns and of the floor?

3. What are the two stoas, and why is an official length a miss?

4. For each of today's key terms (the Church of Acacius (Dewing, I.4.25–26)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
