Science · Fusion and sublimation at 0 °C, compared, from the same table

Week 19 · Day 94 · Thursday · one-lesson review

Remember

At 0 °C, Harvey gives ΔfusH = 333.4 J/g and ΔsublH = 2834 J/g. Sublimation takes about 8.50 times as much energy per gram as fusion, at that temperature in this table. A 100 °C official vaporization figure is not invented.

1. State both of Harvey’s 0 °C phase-transition figures used this week and last, and what each one does.

2. An invented 15 g of ice, already at 0 °C, is sublimed at 0 °C. What energy is required?

3. Why is 2834 − 333.4 not to be reported as the official 100 °C vaporization figure in this lesson?

4. Show that 2834 / 333.4 is about 8.50, and say in words what the ratio compares.

5. An invented 10 g of ice is warmed by 1 °C, using 2.10, and a second invented 10 g is melted at 0 °C. Find the two energies, and say why they are different kinds of change.

6. If 14,170 J sublimes an invented sample already at 0 °C, what was the mass?
