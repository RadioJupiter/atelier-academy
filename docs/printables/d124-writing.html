Shorten the action, keep one moral at the end, and cut a mid-story lecture.

1. Write a tighter version of an original fable, with a short action and one moral at the end.

2. Name one sentence you cut, and why it was a lecture or a second moral.

3. How can you tell that the moral still follows from the tightened action?

4. Compare your tighter version with Townsend’s crow: what shape do they share, without sharing the story?

5. Why is a mid-story lecture a miss even if the advice is sound?

6. Write the moral alone, as one sentence.
