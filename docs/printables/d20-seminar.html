Remember

Caesura: a pause inside the line. Enjambment: the sentence runs over the line end. Volta: the turn in a sonnet, often at line 9 or at the couplet in a Shakespearean sonnet — find it by the thought, not by a superstition. Sonnet 18’s ‘But thy eternal summer shall not fade’ is a standard place to hear the turn.

Key terms: caesura and enjambment, volta.

1. In your own words, how is a caesura different from a line break, and how is enjambment different from a typographical accident?

2. What is the rhyme scheme of a Shakespearean sonnet, and where might you look first for a volta?

3. For each of today's key terms (caesura and enjambment, volta), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Mark a short stretch of Sonnet 18 (four lines) for meter (Day 10), for one caesura, and for the volta if it falls in your stretch or just after it.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
