Remember

The second-voyage letter, in Major’s 1870 wording, says he left Cadiz “on the twenty-fifth of September, of the year,” with the year missing in the body. Major’s footnote says “it should say of the year 1493,” which is the note, not the body. They reached the Great Canary and “put into harbour” to repair a ship “which made a great deal of water.” “Favourable” and “harbour” stay inside quotations.

1. How does the second-voyage letter, in Major’s 1870 wording, date the departure from Cadiz, and what does Major’s footnote say about the missing year?

2. What does the letter say they did at the Great Canary, and why do “favourable” and “harbour” stay inside quotations?

3. Why is an official modern conversion of “nearly fifty leagues” a miss?

4. For each of today's key terms (the second-voyage letter, Cadiz and the Great Canary), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
