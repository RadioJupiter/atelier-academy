Remember

In Dewing I.5.7–13, “the sea forms a garland about the city.” The bay “is always calm,” so as “to do honour to the city.” “The circuit of the bay extends to a distance of more than forty stades.” “Honour” stays inside the quotation. “More than forty stades” is Dewing’s wording, not a modern official conversion.

1. In Dewing I.5.7–13, how is the sea said to form a garland, and what does Dewing say of the bay’s calm?

2. What wording does Dewing use for the circuit of the bay, and why is an official modern conversion a miss?

3. Why is last week’s I.5.1–6 a miss if the job is this extract?

4. For each of today's key terms (three straits and a bay of more than forty stades (Dewing, I.5.7–13)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
