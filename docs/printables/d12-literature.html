Remember

Books 7–9: an indecisive duel, a Greek retreat, then the embassy of Odysseus, Ajax, and Phoenix in Book 9. Achilles refuses. Phoenix’s Meleager story is a paradeigma, a story used to persuade, not a detour. Read the speeches as arguments about honor.

Key terms: embassy (to Achilles), paradeigma (the Meleager story).

1. What is decided, and what is left undecided, at the end of the Hector–Ajax duel in Book 7?

2. Does Achilles, in Book 9, still say he will leave for home? What, if anything, has shifted by the end of the book? Use a short attributed phrase if you quote.

3. For each of today's key terms (embassy (to Achilles), paradeigma (the Meleager story)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. For one of the three envoys, write a half-page close reading: what is asked, what is offered, and where the speech fails or almost works.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
