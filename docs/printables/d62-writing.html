On the set Bliss sentences, name a claim, the grounds (quoted accurately), and a warrant that is a rule, not a restated claim. Later of the address remains reserved. Do not treat a paraphrase as a quotation.

1. Write a Toulmin analysis of the second set sentence: claim, grounds (quoted), and warrant.

2. In the Bliss copy, how does the first sentence begin? Quote the first nine words exactly.

3. Why is “Lincoln thinks the war is important” too loose to be the claim of the second sentence?

4. What remains reserved today, and why does that matter for a quotation?

5. Give one phrase from the first sentence that “so conceived and so dedicated” depends on, and quote it exactly.

6. A student quotes “all men are created equal” as the grounds for the claim that “we are engaged in a great civil war.” What has gone wrong?
