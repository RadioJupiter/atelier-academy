Remember

In AcI, the subject of the reported clause is accusative, and the verb is an infinitive. Puerum loquī audiō is not today’s model if loquī as a complementary infinitive would confuse the job; a fair already-taught shape is: Magister puerum adesse dīcit, the schoolmaster says that the boy is present, when adesse / esse has been taught. Keep the jobs distinct from loquēbātur and locūtus est.

1. What is AcI, as already taught, and how does AcI with esse fit that pattern?

2. Why is puer locūtus est not an AcI sentence?

3. Why is puerō locūtō not an AcI sentence?

4. Write one original AcI sentence with esse, and translate it. Do not copy a textbook sentence.

5. Label each as AcI, a finite deponent, or an absolute: puerum adesse dīcit; puer loquēbātur; puerō locūtō.
