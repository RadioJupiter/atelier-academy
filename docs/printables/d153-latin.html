Latin · Sequor and ēgredior: present subjunctive against the future

Remember

Sequātur (ā) is the present subjunctive of sequor; sequētur (ē) is the future. Ēgrediātur is the present subjunctive of ēgredior; ēgrediētur is the future. Sequar and ēgrediar are spelled the same in both tenses. The two verbs are not interchangeable.

1. Gloss “Puella eum sequētur” and “Puer ēgrediātur,” and name the tense of each.

2. Why can “sequar,” standing alone, not settle whether the future or the present subjunctive is meant?

3. Why is ēgrediātur a miss for “she will go out”?

4. Write four short original sentences using sequātur, sequētur, ēgrediātur, and ēgrediētur, with glosses.

5. Give the third-person singular present subjunctive of both verbs, with glosses.

6. What remains reserved?
