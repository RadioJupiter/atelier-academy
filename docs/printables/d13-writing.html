Remember

Two complete sentences may be joined by a period, by a comma plus and/but/so, or by a semicolon (often with a conjunctive adverb: ; however, ). A comma alone is a splice. A colon introduces an explanation, a list, or a quotation.

Key terms: semicolon, conjunctive adverb.

1. Add a legal conjunctive adverb: ‘The two tangent segments are equal. They are drawn from the same outside point.’

2. Which mark fits, a semicolon or a colon? ‘The three questions are these ubi, quō, and unde.’ (Supply the mark.)

3. For each of today's key terms (semicolon, conjunctive adverb), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Write two new sentence pairs from this week’s studies, one joined by ; however, and one by ; therefore,
