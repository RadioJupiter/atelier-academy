Remember

The letter compares the foliage to Spain in May, names nightingales in November, and calls Española a wonder, with harbors and rivers “most of them bearing gold.” Those are the letter’s claims, not official modern quantities.

1. In Major’s wording, what does the letter say of the trees in May and of the birds in November?

2. What does it say of Española as “a wonder,” including the harbors and the rivers?

3. Why must “gold,” “a thousand different kinds,” and “population innumerable” not be treated as official modern figures?

4. Quote the “palm-trees of six or eight kinds” sentence, and say why it is not an official botanical list.

5. How does this voyage report still differ from last week’s Treaty of Tordesillas?

6. What official claim must you not add?
