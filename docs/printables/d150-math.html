Remember

SSA does not always determine one triangle. On the invented acute figures, a = 1/2, 1, √2, and 2 give none, one right, two, and one isosceles triangle. On the invented obtuse figures, a must be greater than b for a triangle to occur.

1. State the acute SSA rule, including h = b sin A, and the obtuse SSA rule.

2. On invented figures with A = 30° and b = 2, give the four results for a = 1/2, a = 1, a = √2, and a = 2.

3. On invented figures with A = 120° and b = 2, what happens if a = 2, and what triangle appears if a = 2√3?

4. Show that, in the two-triangle case a = √2, sin B = 1/√2, and name both triangles.

5. Why is taking only the acute B a miss in that case, and why is inventing a second triangle when A = 120° and a = 2√3 also a miss?

6. What official figure must you not invent?
