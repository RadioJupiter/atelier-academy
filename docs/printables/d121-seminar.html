Remember

If n outcomes are equally likely, and one must happen, the probability of one named outcome is 1/n. An invented fair six-faced cube gives 1/6 for a single face, and 3/6 = 1/2 for an even face.

1. What does it mean for outcomes to be equally likely, and what is the probability of one named outcome if there are n of them?

2. On an invented fair six-faced cube, give the probability of the face 4, and of an even face, as fractions.

3. Why must you not invent an official probability of the weather, or treat this as a gambling lesson?

4. For each of today's key terms (equally likely outcomes), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
