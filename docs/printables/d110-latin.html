Remember

The deponent perfect is locūtus sum and cōnātus sum, active in meaning, with the participle agreeing in number. The present, loquor, is a different tense. The ablative absolute has no sum: a present participle is contemporaneous; a perfect participle is already complete.

1. Give the six perfect forms of loquor and of cōnor, and translate them as active.

2. Translate Ego locūtus sum and Nōs loquimur, and name each tense.

3. Why must the participle be locūtī in puerī locūtī sunt?

4. Contrast magistrō recitante with urbe captā, and say why neither is the perfect tense.

5. Translate Tū cōnātus es and Vōs cōnāminī.

6. Translate Puerō locūtō, magister respondet, and name the construction.
