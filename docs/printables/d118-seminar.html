Remember

A second percent is taken off the current price, not off the original again, and not as a simple subtraction of percents. Two 10 percent discounts on $100 leave $81. Ten percent up, then 10 percent down, on $100, leaves $99.

1. An invented $100 takes two successive 10 percent discounts. What is the final price, and why is $80 a miss?

2. An invented $100 is marked up 10 percent, then discounted 10 percent. What is the final price, and why are $100 and $81 both misses?

3. An invented $200 is marked up 10 percent, then discounted 25 percent. What is the final price?

4. How do the two $100 paths differ?

5. Why must you not treat 10 percent up and 10 percent down as a net of zero?

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
