Remember

Egyptian relief and painting: composite view (head in profile, eye from the front, shoulders from the front, legs in profile); grids for proportion; hierarchical scale; registers (horizontal bands). These conventions were stable for some three thousand years, with a striking exception under Akhenaten (14th century BCE). The aim was the most complete, lasting form of each part, not a snapshot from one viewpoint.

Key terms: composite view, hierarchical scale.

1. What is hierarchical scale, and why is it not the same as perspective?

2. For each of today's key terms (composite view, hierarchical scale), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

3. Find a good reproduction of an Egyptian tomb painting or relief (for example, from the Metropolitan Museum of Art's online collection). Write a formal analysis (150 words): line, shape, composition, scale, color.

4. Draw one figure from it using the composite view, then redraw the same figure as it would actually appear from a single viewpoint. What is lost and what is gained?

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
