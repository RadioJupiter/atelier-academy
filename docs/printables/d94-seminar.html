Remember

Each year’s simple interest is computed on the principal still outstanding, after a labeled invented principal repayment. Invented example: $300 at 10%, year 1 interest $30, then a $100 principal repayment, year 2 interest $20. This is not last week’s equal installment of a known total, and not compound interest. No official bank rate is invented.

Key terms: simple interest on a declining principal.

1. In the invented $300 example at 10%, what is year 1’s interest, what principal remains after the $100 repayment, and what is year 2’s interest?

2. Why is “year 2’s interest is also $30” a miss in that example?

3. Why is this not a first look at simple interest, and not last week’s equal installment as a first look?

4. For each of today's key terms (simple interest on a declining principal), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
