Remember

An ablative absolute, in this first look, is a noun plus a present participle, both ablative. Deponent participles stay active in meaning. Fiō is a different verb. The perfect-participle absolute is reserved.

1. What is an ablative absolute in this week’s school sense, and which participle is used?

2. Translate: Magistrō recitante, puerī tacent. Puerō loquente, magister tacet.

3. In Discipulīs ēgredientibus, tempestās fit, what is the absolute, and what does fit mean?

4. Why is “with the boy being spoken” a miss, and why is urbe captā reserved?

5. Write one original sentence with an ablative absolute and a deponent or fiō in the main clause.
