Remember

Many leaves look green because chlorophyll absorbs red and blue more than green, so more of the leftover green light reaches the eye. That is an absorption story, not a scattering story like the blue sky. No invented percentages.

Key terms: chlorophyll, pigment.

1. Why is “plants prefer green light, and that is why they are green” a near-miss?

2. A thin leaf held up to the sun can look green in transmission (light passing through) as well as in reflection. Does that fit the leftover-light account? Explain.

3. For each of today's key terms (chlorophyll, pigment), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Name the pigment that dominates the color of an ordinary midsummer leaf, and the two regions of the spectrum it absorbs more strongly than green, as taught today.
