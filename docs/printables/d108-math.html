Remember

In a right triangle the two acute angles add to 90°. The side opposite A is adjacent to 90° − A, so sin A = cos(90° − A), and sin(90° − A) = cos A. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, if A is opposite 3, sin A = 3/5 = cos B, and cos A = 4/5 = sin B. Degrees only.

1. Why do the two acute angles in a right triangle add to 90°?

2. Why is sin A equal to cos(90° − A)?

3. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, A is opposite 3. Give sin A, cos A, sin(90° − A), and cos(90° − A).

4. Why is this not a first look at sine or at cosine?
