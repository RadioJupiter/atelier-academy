Remember

A confirmation shows why a claim holds. A refutation shows why a claim does not. The set isosceles claim is confirmed; last week’s “told many times” claim was refuted. The earlier shapes remain distinct.

1. What is a confirmation, and how does it differ from a refutation?

2. State the set invented claim, and give one already-taught reason why it holds.

3. How do a maxim, a chreia, a narrative, and a fable each differ from a confirmation?

4. Confirm, in two tight sentences, the second invented claim about s = rθ, or say why you would instead confirm the isosceles claim.

5. Why is a living person still not needed?

6. What remains reserved?
