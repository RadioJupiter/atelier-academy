Remember

ΔfusH(0 °C) = 333.4 J/g, from Harvey/NIST at 101.325 kPa: the energy to melt one gram of ice at 0 °C, with no temperature change. An invented 100 g needs 33.34 kJ. This is not a first look at melting as a change of state, and not last week’s density work as a first look.

Key terms: enthalpy of fusion of ice.

1. What does ΔfusH(0 °C) = 333.4 J/g mean, and where is the figure from?

2. An invented 10 g of ice, already at 0 °C, is melted at 0 °C. What energy is required?

3. Why is “the ice is then at 333.4 °C” a miss?

4. For each of today's key terms (enthalpy of fusion of ice), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
