Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation

Week 1 · Day 1 · Monday · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

NameDate

Remember

Part-whole: A + B = W. Comparison: larger = smaller + difference. Change: start ± change = end. Draw the bars before you write the equation.

1. A chemist used 3/8 of a bottle in the morning and 1/5 of the remaining solution in the afternoon. 28 mL were left. How much was in the bottle at the start?

2. Pencils and pens were in the ratio 5 : 8. After 12 more pencils were bought, there were equally many pencils and pens. How many pencils were there at first?

3. A painter used 2/7 of a tube of white on Monday and 1/3 of what remained on Tuesday. 20 mL were left. How much was in the tube?

4. Sketchbooks and canvases were in the ratio 4 : 7. After 15 more sketchbooks were bought, there were equal numbers of each. How many sketchbooks were there at first?

5. Two numbers add to 96. The larger is 4 less than three times the smaller. Find both.

6. One account held three times as much as another. After $40 was added to each, the first held twice as much as the second. Find the original amounts.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
