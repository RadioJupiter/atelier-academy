Remember

A refutation examines a claim and gives reasons why it should not be accepted. The set claim is invented: “A story is true because it has been told many times.” Repetition is not evidence. Confirmation remains reserved.

1. What is a refutation in this course, and what is the set invented claim?

2. Give one reason why that invented claim should not be accepted.

3. How does a refutation differ from a maxim, from a chreia, and from a narrative? What remains reserved?

4. For each of today's key terms (refutation (progymnasmata)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
