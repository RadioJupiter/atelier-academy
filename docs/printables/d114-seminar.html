Remember

A markup is a percent of the current price, added on. A discount after a markup is a percent of the new price, not of the original, and not the difference of the two percents. An invented $100, up 10 percent, then down 10 percent, leaves $99, not $100. Last week’s two 10 percent discounts left $81.

1. For each of today's key terms (a markup, then a discount), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

2. An invented $100 is marked up 10 percent, then discounted 10 percent. Give the two steps and the final price, and say why $100 and $81 are both misses.
