Remember

Continuous at a means defined at a, with the limit equal to the value. A hole is not a point of continuity. The functions are invented.

1. State the two conditions for continuity at a point, and give one invented function that meets them at 3 and one that fails them at 2.

2. Why is a limit of 4 not enough, by itself, to make (x² − 4)/(x − 2) continuous at 2?

3. On invented r(x) = x + 1, is r continuous at 0? Give r(0) and the limit.

4. On invented f(x) = 2x and g(x) = x + 1, is f + g continuous at 3? Give the value and the limit.

5. On invented k(x) = (x² − 16)/(x − 4), at which of 4 and 5 is k continuous, and why?
