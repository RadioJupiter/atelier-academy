Logic · Necessary and sufficient conditions

Remember

P sufficient for Q means P → Q. P necessary for Q means Q → P (‘Q only if P’). Both together: P ⇔ Q. ‘Only if’ is not ‘if’.

Key terms: sufficient condition, necessary condition.

1. Symbolize: ‘The angle is right only if the lines are perpendicular.’ Name which condition is necessary for which.

2. Give a case (from geometry already taught) of a condition that is sufficient but not necessary, and one that is necessary but not sufficient.

3. For each of today's key terms (sufficient condition, necessary condition), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Rewrite in ‘if…then’ form: ‘You can construct the perpendicular bisector only if the compass opening is more than half the segment.’ Then symbolize.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
