Remember

The letter, in Major’s 1870 wording, says he fortified Villa de Navidad with a fortress, “which by this time will be quite completed,” and left in it a sufficient number of men with arms, artillery, and provisions for more than a year, a barge, and a sailing master “skilful in the arts necessary for building others.” “Skilful” stays inside the quotation. No official head-count is taken from later accounts.

1. In Major’s 1870 wording, how does the sentence that named Villa de Navidad continue, and what does the letter say was left there?

2. Why is “a sufficient number of men” not replaced by an official head-count here?

3. Why does “skilful” stay inside the quotation?

4. For each of today's key terms (Villa de Navidad, the fortress in the letter), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
