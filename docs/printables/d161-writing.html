Remember

An invective blames a general type, or an already-taught fault of a type, with already-taught reasons. It is not a commonplace, not an encomium, and not a blame of a living person. This week’s invented subject is “the careless geometer,” as a type, who claims a result without an already-taught reason.

1. What is an invective in this course, and how does it differ from an encomium and from a commonplace?

2. What invented subject is used this week, and what already-taught reason can open the blame?

3. Why is naming a living person a miss?

4. For each of today's key terms (invective (progymnasmata)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
