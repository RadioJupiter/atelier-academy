Remember

Sin A, in a right triangle, is the opposite side over the hypotenuse. The ratio is the same for a given angle because the right triangles are similar. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, if A is opposite 3, sin A = 3/5. Sine is a ratio, not a length. Degrees only. Cosine, the radian, and the unit circle remain reserved.

1. What is sin A, in this lesson’s school sense, in a right triangle?

2. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, A is opposite 3. What is sin A?

3. A similar classroom triangle has sides 6, 8, and 10, with the same angle A opposite 6. What is sin A, and why is it not a new ratio?

4. Why is sine not a length, and why do the radian and the unit circle remain reserved?
