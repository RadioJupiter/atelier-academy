Remember

Number: count letter-names inclusively. From a major-scale tonic: 1, 4, 5, 8 are perfect; 2, 3, 6, 7 are major. A minor interval is a major one reduced by a half step, same letters. Half step = minor second.

Key terms: interval, perfect, major, and minor.

1. Name the intervals from C to each degree of the C-major scale, C–D through C–C′.

2. Which is larger, a major third or a minor third, and by how much? Keep the letter-names the same in your explanation.

3. For each of today's key terms (interval, perfect, major, and minor), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Name: D–A (in the sense of the white-key span from D up to A), and F–B, and E–C (up). Give number and quality; if you are not yet sure of quality, give the number honestly and then count half steps.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
