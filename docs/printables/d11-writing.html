Remember

Combine two short sentences so that no fact is lost and the joining word matches the relation (because, but, so, a relative, an appositive, or a transition). Do not splice with a comma alone.

Key terms: sentence combining, transition.

1. Combine: ‘The boys walk to the town. The town is near Rome.’ Use a relative clause or an appositive.

2. Rewrite the pair with a transition that marks contrast: ‘The locative looks like a genitive. It does not show possession here.’

3. For each of today's key terms (sentence combining, transition), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Combine these three kernels into one or two sentences, keeping every fact: the treaty is in two languages; one copy is from Hattusa; the other is on a wall at Karnak.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
