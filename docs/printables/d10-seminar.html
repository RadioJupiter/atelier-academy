Remember

Meter is the pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables. A foot is the unit: iamb (da-DUM), trochee (DUM-da), anapest, dactyl, spondee. Iambic pentameter is five iambs, the common English line (Shakespeare's sonnets and blank verse). Variation (a spondee, a shifted first foot) is often deliberate. US spelling: meter, not metre, for verse in this course. (The SI unit is a different word of the same spelling.)

Key terms: meter (verse), iambic pentameter, foot (verse).

1. What is a spondee, and what effect does 'Rough winds' have in line 3?

2. For each of today's key terms (meter (verse), iambic pentameter, foot (verse)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

3. Scan all four lines: mark each syllable as stressed (/) or unstressed (×), and divide the feet.

4. Where does the rhythm vary from strict iambs? What effect does the variation have?
