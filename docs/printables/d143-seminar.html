Remember

Dewing I.4.1–8 places a church of Peter and Paul at Hormisdas, later joined to the Palace, and the shrines of Saints Sergius and Bacchus at an angle, sharing entrances, neither superior nor inferior in beauty or in size. Official measurements are not invented. Later I.4 remains reserved.

1. In Dewing’s wording, where is the church of Peter and Paul built, and what happens to Hormisdas when he becomes emperor?

2. What does Dewing say of Saints Sergius and Bacchus, of the angle, and of equality in beauty and in size?

3. In what one respect do the churches differ, what do they share, and what official figure must you not invent?

4. For each of today's key terms (the churches of Peter and Paul and of Saints Sergius and Bacchus (Dewing, Buildings I.4.1–8)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with.
