Remember

The same infinitive: future endings -é, -ás, -á, -emos, -éis, -án for will; conditional endings -ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -ían for would.

1. How do the regular future and the regular conditional share a stem, and how do their endings differ?

2. Write original sentences with hablaré, hablaría, and comeríamos.

3. Why is *hablaré a miss for I would speak, and what remains reserved until tomorrow?

4. Translate, invented only: Mañana viviré aquí. Ellos comerían. Nosotros hablaríamos.

5. Give vivirías, comerán, and hablaríamos, with meanings, and label each as future or conditional.

6. Change vivo and hablamos into one future and one conditional first-person sentence.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
