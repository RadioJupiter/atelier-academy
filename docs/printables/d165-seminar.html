Remember

In Gray, 1918, the sacrum is “a large, triangular bone,” “inserted like a wedge between the two hip bones,” its base articulating with the last lumbar vertebra, its apex with the coccyx. It is “curved upon itself and placed very obliquely.” The pelvic surface is “concave from above downward,” with “four transverse ridges.” “Vertebræ” stays inside the quotation. Official lengths remain reserved. The lesson is not a diagnosis.

1. In Gray, 1918, how is the sacrum described, and what do its base and apex articulate with?

2. What does Gray say of the pelvic surface?

3. Why is an official length, or a diagnosis, a miss?

4. For each of today's key terms (the sacrum (Gray, 1918)), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
