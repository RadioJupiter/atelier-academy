Remember

Saber in the preterite uses sup-: supe, supiste, supo, supimos, supisteis, supieron. The school sense here is “found out” on a completed occasion, not a general state of knowing, and not pude or puse.

Key terms: supe.

1. Give the six preterite forms of saber.

2. Translate the invented sentence: Ayer supe la verdad.

3. Why is “supe just means I knew, as a state” too rough for this lesson?

4. For each of today's key terms (supe), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
