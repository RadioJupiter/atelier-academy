Remember

A short Toulmin plan is three labeled lines: a claim, grounds (quoted if they come from a set text), and a warrant that is a rule rather than a restated claim. This is not a new outline form, and it is not the three-paragraph outline from Day 26.

1. Write a three-line plan (claim, grounds, warrant) of an invented argument that a window has been left open.

2. If your grounds come from the set Lincoln extract, why must they be quoted?

3. Take your warrant from question 1 and write the restated-claim version of it, labeled as a near-miss.

4. How is today’s plan different from the three-paragraph outline taught on Day 26?

5. Write a three-line plan of the second set Lincoln sentence, with quoted grounds.

6. A plan has a claim and a warrant but no grounds. What is still missing, and why does it matter?

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
