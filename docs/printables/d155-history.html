History · The letter’s date, and the Lisbon postscript, from Major 1870

Week 31 · Day 155 · Friday · one-lesson review

Remember

The letter is dated, in Major’s wording, “on the fifteenth of February, fourteen hundred and ninety-three,” “off the Canary Islands.” The postscript records a south-west wind and a run into the port of Lisbon, says the weather in the Indies was “like that in the month of May,” repeats “I reached them in thirty-three days,” and is “Done on the fourteenth day of March.” “Returned in twenty-eight” is the letter’s wording, not a verified official duration. “Castille” and “marvellous” stay inside quotations.

1. In Major’s 1870 wording, how is the letter dated, and how is the postscript dated?

2. What does the postscript say about a wind and about the port of Lisbon, and what does it say about the weather in the Indies?

3. Why is “returned in twenty-eight” not taught as a verified official duration?

4. For each of today's key terms (the date of the first-voyage letter, and the Lisbon postscript), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
