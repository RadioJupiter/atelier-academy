Remember

Annus is a year; mēnsis is a month, third declension. There are twelve Latin month names, from Iānuārius to December. Quīntīlis was renamed Iūlius, and Sextīlis Augustus, as Allen and Greenough §630 notes. Kalendae, Nonae, and Īdūs have not yet been today’s job.

Key terms: mēnsis, the Latin month names.

1. Write one original Latin sentence that uses both annus and mēnsis.

2. List the twelve month names in order, in Latin.

3. For each of today's key terms (mēnsis, the Latin month names), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Which four months will later have later Nonae and Īdūs? (You may use the English names for this check if you also give the Latin.)

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
