Remember

Scarcity: resources are limited relative to wants, so every choice gives something up. Opportunity cost is the value of the best alternative given up, not the sum of every alternative. Sunk costs are already paid and should not affect a forward-looking decision. Think at the margin: one more unit, extra benefit versus extra cost.

Key terms: scarcity, opportunity cost, sunk cost.

1. Why is the opportunity cost of a choice not the sum of every alternative you did not take?

2. For each of today's key terms (scarcity, opportunity cost, sunk cost), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

3. You can spend Saturday afternoon (a) working a paid shift for $60, (b) attending a free museum lecture, or (c) painting. You choose painting. Which is the opportunity cost? Why not "$60 plus the lecture"?

4. You paid $40 for a workshop ticket, but on the day you would rather draw at home, which you value more than the workshop. Should the $40 matter? Explain.
