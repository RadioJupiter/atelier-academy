Remember

In 399 BCE Socrates was tried in Athens, charged with not recognizing the city's gods and with corrupting the young, and was sentenced to death. The Apology is a defense speech (apologia), written by Plato, a devoted student who was present. Socrates' gadfly metaphor: Athens is a large, noble, sluggish horse; he is the sting that wakes it. 'The unexamined life is not worth living.'

Key terms: apology, gadfly.

1. Compare Socrates' view of Athens (as Plato presents it) with Pericles' praise in Day 6. How do they differ?

2. For each of today's key terms (apology, gadfly), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

3. Sourcing. Plato was Socrates' devoted student. How should that affect your reading? What other evidence would help?

4. Close reading. Explain the gadfly metaphor: who is the horse, what is the gadfly, and what does it imply about Athens?

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
