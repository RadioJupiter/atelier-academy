Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium III, rhetoric: the three appeals

Week 2 · Day 10 · Friday · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practise only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

Remember

Ethos: why we should trust the speaker. Pathos: the state of the audience. Logos: the proof. Name them; do not let feeling stand in for a missing reason.

1. Name the three appeals.

2. Why is a strong feeling not enough, on its own, to make a good argument?

3. Write a persuasive paragraph (150–200 words) on: Every serious art student should study Latin. Plan it as an SPO first. Use all three appeals and label each in the margin.

4. Revise it with this checklist: topic sentence arguable? Each claim supported? One sentence with although? One appositive? No sentence that could be cut without loss?

5. Identify the main appeal in each: (a) As a surgeon for twenty years, I can tell you that this treatment is safe. (b) Imagine the faces of the children who will have no school. (c) The data from all eight trials show the same effect.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
