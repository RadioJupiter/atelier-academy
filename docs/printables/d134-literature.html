Remember

Darcy, in the 1813 wording, calls Elizabeth “tolerable; but not handsome enough to tempt me,” and tells Bingley he is “wasting your mite with me.” Elizabeth tells the story “with great spirit.” “Mite” and “ancle” stay inside quotations.

1. Quote Darcy’s “tolerable” remark, including “mite,” and say what Elizabeth does with the story.

2. How does Bingley’s praise of Jane contrast with Darcy’s remark about Elizabeth, still in the book’s wording?

3. Why must “mite,” “ancle,” and “humour” stay inside quotations, and what remains reserved?

4. What does Mrs. Bennet finally say of Darcy, and why is “ten thousand a year,” from Tuesday, still not an official salary?

5. Why must “girls” and “daughters,” when they appear, stay clearly inside the novel?

6. Why is Chapter 4 not today’s job?
