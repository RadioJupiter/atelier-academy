Remember

From “If P, then Q,” you may not argue from Q to P (affirming the consequent), or from not-P to not-Q (denying the antecedent). A non-square rectangle is a counterexample. These are not modus ponens and not modus tollens.

Key terms: affirming the consequent, denying the antecedent.

1. Name the invalid move: If P then Q; Q; therefore P.

2. Name the invalid move: If P then Q; not P; therefore not Q.

3. For each of today's key terms (affirming the consequent, denying the antecedent), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Give a valid conclusion from “If it is a square, then it is a rectangle” and “This is a square.”

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
