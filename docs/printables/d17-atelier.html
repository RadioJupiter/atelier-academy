Atelier · A plaster feature: from plane to a more particular form

Week 4 · Day 17 · Tuesday · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

Remember

A plaster feature is built from large planes first, then particular transitions (sharp, soft, or stepped). It is a form in the round under your light, not a symbol and not a copy of yesterday’s plate. White plaster still has a full value range in one light.

Key terms: transition of planes, plaster feature.

1. In your own words, how is a plaster feature different from a Bargue feature, and what method is the same?

2. Give an example of a sharp transition and a soft transition you would look for on a nose or an ear.

3. For each of today's key terms (transition of planes, plaster feature), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Draw the plaster feature: 15-minute block-in, large planes, then a first modeling with controlled hatch or tone. Fixed light and viewing point.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
