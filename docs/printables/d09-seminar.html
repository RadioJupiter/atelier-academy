Remember

Simple interest pays only on the principal P. Compound interest pays on interest already earned: A = P(1 + r)^n when compounded yearly. The rule of 72: doubling time ≈ 72/r years for ordinary rates (about 2–15%). A 24% APR compounded monthly is (1.02)^12 − 1 ≈ 26.8% effective. The same mathematics grows savings and debt.

Key terms: compound interest, principal, rule of 72.

1. Two people save $100 a month until the same date; one starts ten years earlier. Explain in two sentences, using the formula, why the early starter ends with far more than just the extra deposits.

2. For each of today's key terms (compound interest, principal, rule of 72), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

3. $1,000 is invested at 7% a year, compounded yearly. How much after 10 years? Compare with simple interest.

4. Use the rule of 72: about how long does money take to double at 6%? at 9%?
