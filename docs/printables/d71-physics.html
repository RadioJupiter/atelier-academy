Remember

Stable: a small displacement tends to be reversed. Unstable: a small displacement tends to grow. Neutral: a small displacement leads to a new rest nearby. Last week’s tipping test is still the tool for a block and its base; it is not retaught as new.

Key terms: stable equilibrium, unstable equilibrium, neutral equilibrium.

1. A marble on a flat, level table is moved a little and stays in the new place. Which kind is that, and why is it not stable?

2. Why is “the block does not tip” not, by itself, a complete name for one of today’s three kinds?

3. For each of today's key terms (stable equilibrium, unstable equilibrium, neutral equilibrium), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. An invented bowl is steeper than another invented bowl. Does today’s lesson give an official number for that steepness? What does it still let you name?
