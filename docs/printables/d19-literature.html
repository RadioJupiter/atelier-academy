Remember

Books 23–24: funeral games for Patroclus; Priam’s supplication; the ransom and funeral of Hector. The Iliad ends with that funeral, not with the fall of Troy. A response should stay inside this poem and use a specific scene.

Key terms: supplication (Priam), ransom of Hector.

1. What is the last thing the Iliad does, and what does it refuse to do?

2. In one sentence each, what do Andromache, Hecuba, and Helen lament, as the poem gives their voices over Hector? (If your translation’s line-division differs, stay close to the text you have.)

3. For each of today's key terms (supplication (Priam), ransom of Hector), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

4. Write the two-paragraph response: what Book 24 does that Book 22 left undone. One specific scene, a limit, no fall-of-Troy ending.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
