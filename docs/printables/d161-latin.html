Remember

A purpose clause with nē marks a negative purpose and takes the already-taught present subjunctive. Puella manet nē eum sequātur: she stays in order that she not follow him. It is not ut eum sequātur, and not the future sequētur. A result clause remains reserved.

1. What is a purpose clause with nē, in this first look, and how does it differ from last week’s purpose clause with ut?

2. Translate the original sentence “Puer manet nē eam sequātur,” and say why “nē eam sequētur” would be a miss.

3. Why is a result clause a miss for today?

4. For each of today's key terms (a purpose clause with nē, a first look), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
