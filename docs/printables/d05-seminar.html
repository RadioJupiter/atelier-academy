Remember

Subcutaneous landmarks (bone just under the skin) are the reliable measuring points of a figure: clavicles and the jugular notch, acromion, seventh cervical vertebra, sternum, olecranon, iliac crest and ASIS, patella, malleoli (inner higher than outer). In an adult head from the front, the eyes lie at about half the height from chin to crown — lower than beginners usually place them.

Key terms: subcutaneous landmark, clavicle.

1. In an adult head seen from the front, at about what fraction of the chin-to-crown height do the eyes lie, and what mistake do beginners usually make?

2. For each of today's key terms (subcutaneous landmark, clavicle), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

3. On your own body, find and touch each landmark listed. On a sheet, draw a simple standing figure and mark each landmark with a dot and label.

4. Look at a photograph of an adult face from the front. Measure the chin-to-crown height and the eye level. What fraction is it?

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
