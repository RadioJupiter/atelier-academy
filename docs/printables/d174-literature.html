Remember

A ball at Netherfield is “quite a settled thing.” Bingley says Darcy may go to bed “if he chuses.” Darcy calls vanity a weakness, and says that pride, “where there is a real superiority of mind,” will be “under good regulation.” “Chuses” stays inside the quotation.

1. What is “quite a settled thing,” and what may Darcy do, in Bingley’s wording, “if he chuses”?

2. Quote Darcy’s distinction between vanity and pride.

3. Why do “chuses” and “any thing” stay inside quotations?

4. For each of today's key terms (Pride and Prejudice, Chapter 11: a ball at Netherfield, and vanity and pride), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).
