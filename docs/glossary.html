Mathematics

  • a downward-opening quadratic inequality — Day 53, Mathematics · Algebra XVIII: a downward-opening parabola, and a square. When the x² coefficient is negative, the already-taught parabola opens downward, so the regions swap: y > 0 between the roots, and y < 0 outside them.
  • a height found by the Pythagorean theorem — Day 72, Mathematics · Geometry XXXI: a height from Pythagoras, then the area. When a perpendicular height of a triangle is not given, but it forms a right triangle with known sides, that height can be found from a² + b² = c², and then the area is ½bh.
  • a line and a parabola, as a system — Day 48, Mathematics · Algebra XVI: a line and a parabola, as a system. Two equations together, one the already-taught parabola y = ax² + bx + c (often y = x² in a first look) and one a line, solved by substitution.
  • a line and a plane in space — Day 121, Mathematics · Geometry LXVII: a line and a plane in space, a first look. In space, a line and a plane have three possible relations: the line may lie in the plane, meet the plane at exactly one point, or be parallel to the plane and never meet it.
  • a line perpendicular to a plane — Day 122, Mathematics · Geometry LXVIII: a line perpendicular to a plane, a first look. A line is perpendicular to a plane if it meets the plane at a point P, the foot, and is perpendicular to every line in the plane that passes through P.
  • a missing side in a right triangle — Day 68, Mathematics · Geometry XXXI: a missing side, from a² + b² = c². A leg or the hypotenuse of a right triangle, found from the Pythagorean theorem when the other two sides are known.
  • a perfect-square inequality — Day 53, Mathematics · Algebra XVIII: a downward-opening parabola, and a square. A quadratic that is a square, such as (x − 3)², is never negative.
  • a short mix of this week’s algebra and geometry — Day 54, Mathematics · Geometry XXIV: a short mix — a tangent-secant, and an inequality. A sitting that uses Monday’s and Wednesday’s quadratic inequalities and Tuesday’s tangent-secant theorem, each in its own picture, without mixing the theorems into one invented hybrid.
  • AA similarity — Day 62, Mathematics · Geometry XXVI: AA similarity. If two angles of one triangle are equal to two angles of another triangle, the third angles are equal as well (because the angle sum is 180°), and the triangles are similar.
  • alternate segment theorem (III.32) — Day 36, Mathematics · Geometry XVII: the tangent-chord angle (Euclid III.32). The angle between a tangent and a chord, at the point of contact, equals the inscribed angle in the alternate segment of the circle (the segment on the other side of the chord).
  • altitude to the hypotenuse — Day 67, Mathematics · Geometry XXX: a similar-triangle proof of the Pythagorean theorem. The perpendicular from the right angle to the hypotenuse in a right triangle.
  • AM–GM inequality — Day 7, Mathematics · Algebra III: inequalities and the mean. For a, b ≥ 0, (a + b)/2 ≥ √(ab), with equality only when a = b.
  • angle bisector — Day 12, Mathematics · Geometry VI: the angle bisector and a parallel through a point. The ray that divides an angle into two equal angles.
  • angle in a semicircle — Day 22, Mathematics · Geometry XI: the angle in a semicircle, and angles in the same segment. An angle inscribed in a semicircle — the angle at the point C on the circle when AB is a diameter — is a right angle.
  • angles in the same segment — Day 22, Mathematics · Geometry XI: the angle in a semicircle, and angles in the same segment. Inscribed angles that stand on the same arc (and so lie in the same segment of the circle) are equal.
  • annulus — Day 84, Mathematics · Geometry XLI: mixed circle measure, and a ring. An annulus, or ring, is the region between two circles that share a center.
  • apothem — Day 79, Mathematics · Geometry XXXVII: a regular polygon’s area, and the apothem. The apothem of a regular polygon is the perpendicular from the center to the middle of a side.
  • area of a circle — Day 82, Mathematics · Geometry XXXIX: the area of a circle. The area of a circle of radius r is A = πr², with the same π taught yesterday as C / d.
  • area of a parallelogram — Day 76, Mathematics · Geometry XXXIV: the area of a parallelogram. The area of a parallelogram is the base times the corresponding perpendicular height: A = b × h.
  • area of a regular polygon — Day 79, Mathematics · Geometry XXXVII: a regular polygon’s area, and the apothem. A regular polygon divides into n congruent isosceles triangles from the center.
  • area of a right triangle — Day 73, Mathematics · Geometry XXXII: the area of a right triangle. In a right triangle the two legs are perpendicular, so either leg may be taken as the base and the other as the height.
  • area of a trapezoid — Day 78, Mathematics · Geometry XXXVI: the area of a trapezoid. The area of a trapezoid is the average of the two parallel bases times the perpendicular height: A = ½(a + b) × h.
  • area of a triangle — Day 71, Mathematics · Geometry XXX: the area of a triangle. The number of unit squares a triangle covers.
  • area of a triangle as (1/2)ab sin C — Day 132, Mathematics · Geometry LXXVI: the area of a triangle as (1/2)ab sin C, a first look. The area of a triangle is (1/2)ab sin C, where C is the included angle between sides a and b.

  • complementary tangents — Day 113, Mathematics · Geometry LXV: tangent of complementary angles. If A and B are the two acute angles of a right triangle, already known to add to 90°, then tan A = 1 / tan B, because the side opposite A is adjacent to B, and the side adjacent to A is opposite B.
