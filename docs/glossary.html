Mathematics

Physics

  • acceleration aavg = Δv / Δt — Day 3, Physics · Acceleration and velocity–time graphs. The rate of change of velocity: how quickly velocity increases, decreases, or changes direction.
  • action–reaction pair — Day 10, Physics · Newton's third law and the two-week check. The two forces named by the third law: they are equal, opposite, simultaneous, of the same type, and they act on two different bodies.
  • centripetal — Day 41, Physics · A first look at circular motion, and a center-seeking force. Center-seeking: a force (or an acceleration) directed toward the center of the circle, needed if the motion is to stay on that circle.
  • centripetal acceleration a = v²/r — Day 16, Physics · Uniform circular motion and centripetal force. The inward, center-seeking acceleration of a body that moves at constant speed on a circle, of size a = v²/r (or a = 4π²r/T², where T is the period).
  • centripetal force F = mv²/r — Day 16, Physics · Uniform circular motion and centripetal force. The net force toward the center that produces the centripetal acceleration: F_net, inward = m v²/r.
  • circular motion — Day 41, Physics · A first look at circular motion, and a center-seeking force. Motion along a circular path.
  • component vx, vy — Day 13, Physics · Vectors and components. The projection of a vector along a chosen axis.
  • conservation (mechanical, first model) — Day 28, Physics · Mechanical energy, conserved when friction does no work. The statement that KE + PE is constant when only gravity (in the PE = m g h model) and forces that do no work (such as a normal force perpendicular to the motion) act.
  • conservation of momentum — Day 18, Physics · Momentum. If two (or more) bodies interact and the net external force on the system is zero (or is negligible for a short collision), the total momentum of the system does not change.
  • displacement Δx = xfinal − xinitial — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. The change in position: final minus initial.
  • free fall g ≈ 9.8 m/s², downward — Day 5, Physics · Free fall. Motion under gravity alone, with air resistance neglected.
  • friction f = μN — Day 11, Physics · Friction and the normal force. A force between surfaces in contact that resists their relative sliding.
  • Friday mix (circular motion) — Day 45, Physics · Friday mix: circular motion, and a center-seeking force. A Friday mix of this week’s first look: circular motion, tangent velocity, a centripetal (center-seeking) force, and what happens if that force stops — still with no invented speed.
  • Friday mix: power and torque — Day 35, Physics · Friday mix: power and a first look at torque. A Friday mix, not a new topic: compute a power in watts, and a torque in newton-meters, keeping the units distinct.
  • g from G M / R² — Day 46, Physics · g from G M / R², with sourced Earth figures. Near a sphere, the gravitational field g is G M / R², because F = G M m / R² and F = m g, so the m cancels.
  • g on Mars, from the same formula — Day 51, Physics · g on Mars, from the same formula and sourced figures. The already-taught formula g = G M / R², applied to NASA fact-sheet figures for Mars, giving about 3.7 m/s², which matches the fact sheet’s own listed gravity 3.7 m/s².
  • g on the Moon, a first sourced look — Day 48, Physics · g on the Moon, from the same formula and sourced figures. The same formula g = G M / R², now used with sourced Moon figures, compared with a NASA table value and with a NASA Science qualitative comparison.
  • gravitational potential energy (near Earth) — Day 26, Physics · Gravitational potential energy near the Earth. In this course’s first model, the gravitational potential energy of an object near the Earth, treated as a particle, is PE = m g h, where h is the height above a chosen reference level, g is 9.8 N/kg (or 9.8 m/s²), and PE is in joules.
  • gravity g — Day 5, Physics · Free fall. The attraction that gives a free-falling object its downward acceleration g near Earth.
  • impulse J = Δp — Day 20, Physics · Two-week check: friction to momentum. The product of a net force and the time it acts, equal to the change in momentum: J = F_net Δt = Δp, when the force is constant (or using an average force).
  • independence of components — Day 20, Physics · Two-week check: friction to momentum. The rule, used for projectiles, that the horizontal motion (constant v_x, if air is neglected) and the vertical motion (acceleration g) can be solved separately, and that a change in one component does not by itself change the other.
  • inertia — Day 6, Physics · Newton's first law: inertia. The tendency of a body to keep its velocity — to stay at rest, or to keep moving in a straight line at constant speed — unless a net force acts on it.
  • inverse-square (gravitation) — Day 36, Physics · A first look at gravitation: an inverse-square force. A pattern in which a quantity is proportional to 1/r²: if r is multiplied by 2, the quantity is multiplied by 1/4; if r is multiplied by 3, the quantity is multiplied by 1/9.
  • joule — Day 21, Physics · Work done by a constant force. The SI unit of work (and of energy): one joule (J) is one newton-meter, the work done by a one-newton force along one meter of displacement in its own direction.
  • kinetic energy — Day 23, Physics · Kinetic energy, and the work–energy theorem. The energy an object has because it is moving.
  • mass (mechanics) — Day 38, Physics · Mass and weight. A measure of the amount of matter, and of how strongly the body resists a change of motion (inertia), in this first look.
  • mass m — Day 8, Physics · Newton's second law. A measure of how much a body resists acceleration (inertial mass).
  • mechanical energy (no friction) — Day 28, Physics · Mechanical energy, conserved when friction does no work. In this course’s first conservation model, mechanical energy is the sum KE + PE, with PE = m g h near the Earth.
  • momentum p = mv — Day 18, Physics · Momentum. The product of mass and velocity, p = m v, a vector in the same direction as the velocity.
  • net force Fnet — Day 6, Physics · Newton's first law: inertia. The vector sum of all forces on a body.
  • net work — Day 25, Physics · Friday mix: work, friction, and kinetic energy. The sum of the work done by every force acting on the object along the path, equal (in this course) to the work done by the net force along the path, and equal to the change in kinetic energy.
  • Newton's second law Fnet = m a — Day 8, Physics · Newton's second law. The net force on a body equals its mass times its acceleration.
  • Newton's third law — Day 10, Physics · Newton's third law and the two-week check. If body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts a force on A that is equal in size, opposite in direction, and of the same type.
  • Newton’s law of gravitation (first look) — Day 36, Physics · A first look at gravitation: an inverse-square force. Every two masses attract each other with a force along the line joining them.
  • normal force N — Day 11, Physics · Friction and the normal force. The force a surface exerts on an object, perpendicular to the surface.
  • orbit (first look) — Day 40, Physics · Friday mix: gravitation, and a first look at orbits. A path in which a body keeps falling toward a larger body and yet does not arrive, because it also has motion to the side.
  • position x — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. Where something is, measured from a chosen origin along a line, with a sign for direction.
  • power (mechanics) — Day 31, Physics · Power: work over time. The rate at which work is done: power is work divided by the time in which that work is done.
  • projectile vx constant; ay = g — Day 15, Physics · Projectile motion. A body in free flight under gravity alone (air resistance neglected in this course), with a constant horizontal velocity and a vertical acceleration of g downward.
  • range (of a projectile) — Day 15, Physics · Projectile motion. The horizontal distance traveled from the launch point to the landing point, equal to vx times the time of flight, when the landing is at the same vertical level or at a stated lower level.
  • reference level — Day 26, Physics · Gravitational potential energy near the Earth. The height you choose as h = 0 when you write PE = m g h.
  • tangent velocity, kept — Day 43, Physics · If the inward pull stops: the tangent direction. The direction the motion already has at a point of the circle: along the tangent.
  • torque (first look) — Day 33, Physics · A first look at torque. A measure of how effectively a force tends to turn an object about a point or an axis: the product of the force and the perpendicular distance from the point (or axis) to the line of the force.
  • two-week physics check — Day 30, Physics · Two-week check: work, energy, and a first look at conservation. A mixed check of work, kinetic energy, gravitational potential energy, and mechanical energy, including one problem in which friction does work so that KE + PE is not constant.
  • vector v — Day 13, Physics · Vectors and components. A quantity that has both a size (magnitude) and a direction, such as displacement, velocity, acceleration, or force.
  • velocity vavg = Δx / Δt — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. Displacement divided by the time taken (average velocity).
  • velocity–time graph — Day 3, Physics · Acceleration and velocity–time graphs. A graph with time on the horizontal axis and velocity on the vertical axis.

  • week's physics mix — Day 50, Physics · Friday mix: g = G M / R² on the Earth and on the Moon. A Friday mix of this week's gravitation work: g = G M / R², the sourced Earth computation (about 9.79 m/s²), the course's working 9.8 N/kg, and the sourced Moon value about 1.6 m/s² (about one-sixth).
