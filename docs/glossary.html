Mathematics

  • AM–GM inequality — Day 7, Mathematics · Algebra III: inequalities and the mean. For a, b ≥ 0, (a + b)/2 ≥ √(ab), with equality only when a = b.
  • angle bisector — Day 12, Mathematics · Geometry VI: the angle bisector and a parallel through a point. The ray that divides an angle into two equal angles.
  • angle in a semicircle — Day 22, Mathematics · Geometry XI: the angle in a semicircle, and angles in the same segment. An angle inscribed in a semicircle — the angle at the point C on the circle when AB is a diameter — is a right angle.
  • angles in the same segment — Day 22, Mathematics · Geometry XI: the angle in a semicircle, and angles in the same segment. Inscribed angles that stand on the same arc (and so lie in the same segment of the circle) are equal.
  • arithmetic progression — Day 20, Mathematics · Two-week problem set: constructions to tangents, and a first look at progressions. A sequence in which each term after the first is obtained by adding the same fixed number, the common difference d.
  • axiom — Day 2, Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs. A starting statement accepted without proof, so that theorems can be proved from it.
  • bar model — Day 1, Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation. A drawing of quantities as bars of equal units, so the relationship is visible before any equation is written.
  • central angle — Day 21, Mathematics · Geometry X: central and inscribed angles. An angle whose vertex is the center of a circle, and whose sides pass through two points of the circle.
  • chord — Day 18, Mathematics · Geometry IX: the circle, chords. A segment joining two points of a circle.
  • completing the square — Day 5, Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations. Rewriting x² + px as a square plus or minus a constant, so a quadratic can be solved or a parabola’s vertex read off.
  • congruence △ABC ≅ △DEF — Day 4, Mathematics · Geometry II: congruent triangles and the isosceles triangle. A matching of two figures so that corresponding sides and corresponding angles are equal; one can be placed exactly on the other.
  • construction — Day 11, Mathematics · Geometry V: constructions with compass and straightedge. A drawing made with only an unmarked straightedge and a compass, justified by axioms and earlier theorems, not by measuring with a ruler.
  • cyclic quadrilateral — Day 23, Mathematics · Geometry XII: cyclic quadrilaterals. A quadrilateral whose four vertices all lie on a single circle.
  • diagonal — Day 14, Mathematics · Geometry VII: the parallelogram. A segment joining two vertices of a polygon that are not already joined by a side.
  • discriminant D = b² − 4ac — Day 5, Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations. The quantity that decides how many real roots a quadratic has: two if D > 0, one if D = 0, none if D < 0.
  • distance from the center — Day 18, Mathematics · Geometry IX: the circle, chords. The perpendicular distance from the center of a circle to a chord.
  • exponent an — Day 16, Mathematics · Algebra V: the laws of exponents. The small number that says how many times a base is used as a factor: in aⁿ, a is the base and n is the exponent.
  • exterior angle — Day 6, Mathematics · Geometry III: parallel lines and the angle sum. An angle formed by one side of a triangle and the extension of an adjacent side.
  • factor — Day 3, Mathematics · Algebra I: identities and factoring. To write an expression as a product; or one of those pieces.
  • function y = f(x) — Day 9, Mathematics · Functions and their graphs. A rule that assigns to each allowed input exactly one output.
  • geometric progression — Day 20, Mathematics · Two-week problem set: constructions to tangents, and a first look at progressions. A sequence in which each term after the first is obtained by multiplying by the same fixed number, the common ratio r.
  • heuristic — Day 10, Mathematics · Two-week problem set. A deliberate method of attack, not a guess: for example, draw a bar, try a smaller case, or write a short proof plan before computing.
  • identity — Day 3, Mathematics · Algebra I: identities and factoring. An equality that is true for every allowed value of its letters, and that must be proved by expanding or by using a known identity.
  • inequality — Day 7, Mathematics · Algebra III: inequalities and the mean. A statement that one quantity is greater or less than another.
  • inscribed angle — Day 21, Mathematics · Geometry X: central and inscribed angles. An angle whose vertex lies on the circle, and whose sides are chords of the circle.
  • intersecting chords — Day 24, Mathematics · Geometry XIII: intersecting chords. If two chords of a circle meet at a point E inside the circle, then the products of the segments of each chord are equal: AE · EC = BE · ED.
  • isosceles — Day 4, Mathematics · Geometry II: congruent triangles and the isosceles triangle. A triangle with two sides equal.
  • locus — Day 8, Mathematics · Geometry IV: inequalities in triangles. The set of all points that satisfy a given condition.
  • opposite angles supplementary — Day 23, Mathematics · Geometry XII: cyclic quadrilaterals. In a cyclic quadrilateral, each pair of opposite angles sums to 180°.
  • parallel through a point — Day 12, Mathematics · Geometry VI: the angle bisector and a parallel through a point. The unique line through a given point P, not on a line ℓ, that is parallel to ℓ.
  • parallelogram — Day 14, Mathematics · Geometry VII: the parallelogram. A quadrilateral whose opposite sides are parallel (both pairs).
  • perpendicular bisector — Day 11, Mathematics · Geometry V: constructions with compass and straightedge. The line that cuts a segment in half and meets it at a right angle.
  • power — Day 16, Mathematics · Algebra V: the laws of exponents. The result of raising a base to an exponent, or the expression aⁿ itself.
  • radical — Day 17, Mathematics · Algebra VI: radicals. An expression that uses a root symbol, such as √a or ³√a.
  • ratio — Day 1, Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation. A comparison of two quantities by units of the same size, written a : b.
  • rectangle — Day 15, Mathematics · Geometry VIII: rectangle, rhombus and square. A parallelogram with a right angle.
  • remainder — Day 1, Mathematics · Modeling: from bar model to equation. What is left of a quantity after a part of it has been taken.
  • rhombus — Day 15, Mathematics · Geometry VIII: rectangle, rhombus and square. A parallelogram with all four sides equal.
  • square root √a — Day 17, Mathematics · Algebra VI: radicals. For a ≥ 0, the square root √a is the unique number ≥ 0 whose square is a.
  • substitution — Day 13, Mathematics · Algebra IV: systems of linear equations. A method for solving a system: use one equation to express one unknown in terms of the other, and put that expression into the second equation.
  • sum of an arithmetic progression — Day 25, Mathematics · Algebra VII: the sum of an arithmetic progression. The sum of the first n terms of an arithmetic progression with first term a and last term ℓ is Sn = n/2 · (a + ℓ).
  • system of equations — Day 13, Mathematics · Algebra IV: systems of linear equations. Two or more equations that must be true at the same time, about the same unknowns.
  • tangent to a circle — Day 19, Mathematics · Geometry X: tangents to a circle. A line that meets a circle at exactly one point, called the point of contact.
  • theorem — Day 2, Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs. A statement that is proved from definitions, axioms, and earlier theorems.
  • transversal — Day 6, Mathematics · Geometry III: parallel lines and the angle sum. A line that crosses two or more other lines, forming corresponding, alternate interior, and same-side interior angles.
  • triangle inequality — Day 8, Mathematics · Geometry IV: inequalities in triangles. In a triangle, each side is less than the sum of the other two (and greater than their difference).
  • two tangents from a point — Day 19, Mathematics · Geometry X: tangents to a circle. From a point outside a circle, the two tangent segments to the points of contact are equal.
  • unit (in a bar or ratio) — Day 10, Mathematics · Two-week problem set. One equal part in a bar model or a ratio.
  • vertex — Day 9, Mathematics · Functions and their graphs. The turning point of a parabola.
  • vertical angles ∠AOB and ∠COD — Day 2, Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs. A pair of angles whose sides are the extensions of each other’s sides: the opposite angles formed when two lines meet.
  • Vieta's formulas r + s = −p, rs = q — Day 5, Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations. For the roots r and s of x² + px + q = 0, the sum of the roots is −p and the product is q.

Physics

  • acceleration aavg = Δv / Δt — Day 3, Physics · Acceleration and velocity–time graphs. The rate of change of velocity: how quickly velocity increases, decreases, or changes direction.
  • action–reaction pair — Day 10, Physics · Newton's third law and the two-week check. The two forces named by the third law: they are equal, opposite, simultaneous, of the same type, and they act on two different bodies.
  • centripetal acceleration a = v²/r — Day 16, Physics · Uniform circular motion and centripetal force. The inward, center-seeking acceleration of a body that moves at constant speed on a circle, of size a = v²/r (or a = 4π²r/T², where T is the period).
  • centripetal force F = mv²/r — Day 16, Physics · Uniform circular motion and centripetal force. The net force toward the center that produces the centripetal acceleration: F_net, inward = m v²/r.
  • component vx, vy — Day 13, Physics · Vectors and components. The projection of a vector along a chosen axis.
  • conservation of momentum — Day 18, Physics · Momentum. If two (or more) bodies interact and the net external force on the system is zero (or is negligible for a short collision), the total momentum of the system does not change.
  • displacement Δx = xfinal − xinitial — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. The change in position: final minus initial.
  • free fall g ≈ 9.8 m/s², downward — Day 5, Physics · Free fall. Motion under gravity alone, with air resistance neglected.
  • friction f = μN — Day 11, Physics · Friction and the normal force. A force between surfaces in contact that resists their relative sliding.
  • gravity g — Day 5, Physics · Free fall. The attraction that gives a free-falling object its downward acceleration g near Earth.
  • impulse J = Δp — Day 20, Physics · Two-week check: friction to momentum. The product of a net force and the time it acts, equal to the change in momentum: J = F_net Δt = Δp, when the force is constant (or using an average force).
  • independence of components — Day 20, Physics · Two-week check: friction to momentum. The rule, used for projectiles, that the horizontal motion (constant v_x, if air is neglected) and the vertical motion (acceleration g) can be solved separately, and that a change in one component does not by itself change the other.
  • inertia — Day 6, Physics · Newton's first law: inertia. The tendency of a body to keep its velocity — to stay at rest, or to keep moving in a straight line at constant speed — unless a net force acts on it.
  • joule — Day 21, Physics · Work done by a constant force. The SI unit of work (and of energy): one joule (J) is one newton-meter, the work done by a one-newton force along one meter of displacement in its own direction.
  • kinetic energy — Day 23, Physics · Kinetic energy, and the work–energy theorem. The energy an object has because it is moving.
  • mass m — Day 8, Physics · Newton's second law. A measure of how much a body resists acceleration (inertial mass).
  • momentum p = mv — Day 18, Physics · Momentum. The product of mass and velocity, p = m v, a vector in the same direction as the velocity.
  • net force Fnet — Day 6, Physics · Newton's first law: inertia. The vector sum of all forces on a body.
  • net work — Day 25, Physics · Friday mix: work, friction, and kinetic energy. The sum of the work done by every force acting on the object along the path, equal (in this course) to the work done by the net force along the path, and equal to the change in kinetic energy.
  • Newton's second law Fnet = m a — Day 8, Physics · Newton's second law. The net force on a body equals its mass times its acceleration.
  • Newton's third law — Day 10, Physics · Newton's third law and the two-week check. If body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts a force on A that is equal in size, opposite in direction, and of the same type.
  • normal force N — Day 11, Physics · Friction and the normal force. The force a surface exerts on an object, perpendicular to the surface.
  • position x — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. Where something is, measured from a chosen origin along a line, with a sign for direction.
  • projectile vx constant; ay = g — Day 15, Physics · Projectile motion. A body in free flight under gravity alone (air resistance neglected in this course), with a constant horizontal velocity and a vertical acceleration of g downward.
  • range (of a projectile) — Day 15, Physics · Projectile motion. The horizontal distance traveled from the launch point to the landing point, equal to vx times the time of flight, when the landing is at the same vertical level or at a stated lower level.
  • vector v — Day 13, Physics · Vectors and components. A quantity that has both a size (magnitude) and a direction, such as displacement, velocity, acceleration, or force.
  • velocity vavg = Δx / Δt — Day 1, Physics · Motion: position, displacement, velocity. Displacement divided by the time taken (average velocity).
  • velocity–time graph — Day 3, Physics · Acceleration and velocity–time graphs. A graph with time on the horizontal axis and velocity on the vertical axis.
  • work — Day 21, Physics · Work done by a constant force. In this course, a force does work on an object when the object is displaced and the force has a component along that displacement.
  • work–energy theorem — Day 23, Physics · Kinetic energy, and the work–energy theorem. The net work done on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy: Wnet = ΔKE = KEfinal − KEinitial.

Science

  • autoxidation — Day 14, Science · Autoxidation and the oil film. A reaction in which a substance combines with oxygen in the air, without being set on fire.
  • binder — Day 17, Science · Pigment and binder. The substance that holds the pigment particles in a film and attaches the film to the support.
  • carbon dioxide — Day 24, Science · Photosynthesis as a net process. A gas, CO2, that is one of the carbon sources for the sugars made in photosynthesis.
  • chlorophyll — Day 22, Science · Living color: why many leaves look green. The green pigment in leaves (and in other photosynthetic parts) that absorbs some of the light the plant uses.
  • combustion — Day 4, Science · Combustion and gases: the quantitative model. The chemical burning of a fuel with oxygen, producing new gases (and often water) and releasing heat.
  • drying oil — Day 12, Science · Phenomenon: why oil paint dries. A plant oil, such as linseed oil (from flax seed), that can form a solid film by reacting with oxygen in the air, rather than by the evaporation of water.
  • evaporation — Day 12, Science · Phenomenon: why oil paint dries. The change in which a liquid becomes a gas at its surface.
  • frequency f = c / λ — Day 9, Science · Light: polarization and the quantitative model. The number of wave cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz).
  • mass check (of a film) — Day 19, Science · The oil-paint module: a quantitative check and a short synthesis. A measurement that can distinguish evaporation from autoxidation: a film that is mainly losing a liquid to the air should lose mass; a drying-oil film can gain mass as it takes up oxygen (before any later, slower losses).
  • model (scientific) — Day 2, Science · Phenomenon: the candle under the jar. A reasoned picture of what is happening that makes a testable prediction.
  • model of oil-paint drying — Day 19, Science · The oil-paint module: a quantitative check and a short synthesis. The short account this module has built: a drying-oil binder, open to the air, undergoes autoxidation and then polymerization, forming a solid film that holds the pigment; a solvent, if used, may evaporate, but that is not what makes the oil itself into a film; watercolor is a different system, with water leaving mainly by evaporation.
  • phenomenon — Day 2, Science · Phenomenon: the candle under the jar. A real event or appearance that you can observe, and that you then try to explain with a model and a test.
  • photosynthesis — Day 24, Science · Photosynthesis as a net process. The process by which green plants (and some other organisms) use light to build sugars from carbon dioxide and water, and release oxygen as a product.
  • pigment — Day 17, Science · Pigment and binder. A finely divided solid that gives a paint its color (and often its opacity) and that remains as particles in the film.
  • pigment — Day 22, Science · Living color: why many leaves look green. A substance that absorbs some wavelengths of light more than others, and so gives a material a color when white light falls on it.
  • polarization — Day 9, Science · Light: polarization and the quantitative model. The preferring of one direction of vibration in a light wave.
  • polymerization — Day 14, Science · Autoxidation and the oil film. A process in which small molecules join into much larger ones (polymers).
  • quantitative — Day 4, Science · Combustion and gases: the quantitative model. Described with measured numbers and a relation, not only with words such as ‘more’ or ‘less’.
  • Rayleigh scattering intensity ∝ 1/λ⁴ — Day 7, Science · Phenomenon: why is the sky blue and the sunset red?. The scattering of light by particles much smaller than the wavelength.
  • wavelength λ — Day 7, Science · Phenomenon: why is the sky blue and the sunset red?. The distance from one crest of a wave to the next.

History

  • Alexander of Macedon — Day 16, History · Alexander and the end of the Achaemenid empire. The Macedonian king who, from 336 BCE (the death of his father, Philip II) until his own death at Babylon in 323 BCE, conquered the Achaemenid Persian empire.
  • apology — Day 8, History · The trial of Socrates. A defense speech. Plato’s Apology is not an admission of fault; the Greek apologia means a defense.
  • Arrian — Day 16, History · Alexander and the end of the Achaemenid empire. A Greek historian (2nd century CE) whose Anabasis of Alexander is a main surviving narrative of the campaign.
  • citizenship — Day 6, History · Athens: Pericles' Funeral Oration. Membership in the political community, with its rights and duties.
  • consul — Day 23, History · The Republic: consuls, Senate, and Polybius as a source. One of the two chief annual magistrates of the Roman Republic.
  • corroboration — Day 1, History · How historians work. Checking whether an independent source agrees, in part or in whole, with the claim you are testing.
  • democracy — Day 6, History · Athens: Pericles' Funeral Oration. In Pericles’ usage (as Thucydides reports it), a constitution whose administration ‘favors the many instead of the few’.
  • diplomatic copy — Day 15, History · The Egyptian–Hittite treaty of year 21 of Ramesses II. A version of an agreement written for one court, often in a lingua franca (here Akkadian on the tablet from Ḫattuša) or in the court’s own script (Egyptian at Karnak), after the terms have been negotiated.
  • early Rome (tradition and evidence) — Day 21, History · Sources for early Rome: Livy, tradition, and archaeology. The period of the Roman kings and the first generations of the Republic, for which later literary tradition (Livy and others) must be read beside archaeology and inscriptions.
  • gadfly — Day 8, History · The trial of Socrates. A biting fly; in Socrates’ metaphor, the stinging critic who keeps a large, sluggish horse — the city — awake.
  • Hellenistic — Day 18, History · The Hellenistic world and Ptolemaic Egypt. The period, after Alexander’s death in 323 BCE, in which his empire was divided among successor kingdoms (the Diadochi and their heirs) whose courts used Greek as a language of rule, while older cultures continued.
  • historiē — Day 5, History · Greece: Herodotus and the idea of "inquiry". The Greek word (ἱστορίη) meaning inquiry, investigation.
  • inquiry — Day 5, History · Greece: Herodotus and the idea of "inquiry". A systematic asking of questions in order to find out and to explain, not only to list events.
  • judgment — Day 10, History · Synthesis: law, citizenship and judgment. The act of deciding a case, or the standard by which people are judged.
  • kingship (Egyptian, this period) — Day 13, History · Kingship and the afterlife: the Pyramid Texts of Unas. The office of the Egyptian king (often called pharaoh in later usage) as both a political ruler and a figure in religion: the king’s tomb, titles, and afterlife texts present him as the one who maintains order and who will join the gods.
  • lex talionis — Day 3, History · Babylon: the laws of Hammurabi. The rule of retaliation in kind: the penalty matches the injury, ‘an eye for an eye’.
  • Livy — Day 21, History · Sources for early Rome: Livy, tradition, and archaeology. Titus Livius, a Roman historian writing in the late first century BCE and early first century CE.
  • Old Kingdom — Day 11, History · Egypt: the Old Kingdom and the pyramids. The period of Egyptian history in which the great pyramids of Giza were built, conventionally the Third to Sixth Dynasties.
  • periodization — Day 20, History · Two-week synthesis: sources for kingship, from Khufu to the Ptolemies. The decision to cut a long past into named periods (Old Kingdom, New Kingdom, Hellenistic) so that change can be talked about.
  • primary source — Day 1, History · How historians work. A source produced at the time by someone involved or present: a law, a letter, a coin, an inscription, a painting, a speech written down in the period.
  • Ptolemaic Egypt — Day 18, History · The Hellenistic world and Ptolemaic Egypt. The Hellenistic kingdom in Egypt ruled by the dynasty founded by Ptolemy, a companion of Alexander (the same Ptolemy Arrian later preferred as a source), from the late 4th century BCE until 30 BCE.
  • pyramid (Egyptian) — Day 11, History · Egypt: the Old Kingdom and the pyramids. A royal tomb of this period, with a square base and four sloping faces, built in stone, of which the Great Pyramid of Khufu at Giza is the largest.
  • Pyramid Texts — Day 13, History · Kingship and the afterlife: the Pyramid Texts of Unas. Religious texts, in the Egyptian language, carved on the walls of royal pyramids beginning with the pyramid of Unas (Wenis), last king of the 5th Dynasty, at Saqqara.
  • Roman Republic — Day 23, History · The Republic: consuls, Senate, and Polybius as a source. The period of Roman government, after the traditional end of the kingship, in which annually elected magistrates (including two consuls) and assemblies, together with the Senate, shared the public business of the city and, later, of an empire.
  • secondary source — Day 1, History · How historians work. A source that interprets the past afterwards: a textbook, a modern biography, a documentary, a history written long after the events.
  • Senate — Day 23, History · The Republic: consuls, Senate, and Polybius as a source. The council of leading Romans that advised magistrates and, in Republican practice, carried great weight in policy, finance, and foreign affairs.
  • stele — Day 3, History · Babylon: the laws of Hammurabi. An upright stone slab with a relief or an inscription, set up to be seen in public.
  • synthesis — Day 10, History · Synthesis: law, citizenship and judgment. A reasoned putting-together of several sources around an idea, not a tour of one document after another.
  • synthesis (this week) — Day 20, History · Two-week synthesis: sources for kingship, from Khufu to the Ptolemies. A short argued piece that puts more than one source, from more than one period, under a single thesis, with sourcing sentences and a limit.
  • treaty of year 21 — Day 15, History · The Egyptian–Hittite treaty of year 21 of Ramesses II. A peace treaty between Ramesses II of Egypt and Ḫattušili III of Ḫatti, dated on the Egyptian side to year 21 of Ramesses II.
  • Twelve Tables — Day 25, History · The Twelve Tables as a class of source. Rome’s early written law code, in the tradition dated to the mid-fifth century BCE (often 451–450 BCE).

Literature

  • aidōs — Day 7, Literature · Iliad, Books 4–6. Shame before others; a sense of honor that answers to the community’s gaze.
  • analytical essay — Day 9, Literature · Writing about the Iliad: the analytical essay. A short argued piece in which every paragraph begins with a claim, every quotation is introduced and explained, and the other side is acknowledged.
  • aristeia — Day 7, Literature · Iliad, Books 4–6. A hero’s ‘best moment’: a stretch of the poem in which one warrior dominates the battlefield.
  • catalogue — Day 4, Literature · Iliad, Books 2–3. A formal list built into a poem.
  • ekphrasis — Day 17, Literature · Iliad, Books 17–22: the shield, the return, the death of Hector. A set-piece description of a work of art inside a poem.
  • embassy (to Achilles) — Day 12, Literature · Iliad, Books 7–9: the embassy to Achilles. The episode in Iliad Book 9 in which Odysseus, Ajax, and Phoenix go to Achilles’ camp with Agamemnon’s offers, and Achilles refuses to return.
  • epithet — Day 2, Literature · Homer, Iliad, Book 1. A fixed descriptive phrase attached to a name, used and reused in oral poetry (‘swift-footed Achilles’, ‘rosy-fingered Dawn’).
  • in medias res — Day 2, Literature · Homer, Iliad, Book 1. ‘Into the middle of things’: the story begins not at the first event, but in the midst of an already ongoing action.
  • in medias res (Odyssey) — Day 24, Literature · Odyssey, Books 5–8: Calypso, the raft, and the Phaeacians. Starting a narrative in the middle of the larger story.
  • invocation — Day 2, Literature · Homer, Iliad, Book 1. The opening move of an epic in which the poet asks the Muse to sing, and names the subject of the poem.
  • paradeigma (the Meleager story) — Day 12, Literature · Iliad, Books 7–9: the embassy to Achilles. A story told inside another story as an example meant to persuade: Phoenix tells Achilles the tale of Meleager, who also withdrew in anger and was later begged to save his city, as a warning and a model.
  • Patroclus in Achilles’ armor — Day 14, Literature · Iliad, Books 11–16: Patroclus. The decision, in Book 16, that Patroclus will put on Achilles’ armor, lead the Myrmidons, and drive the Trojans from the ships, but go no further — a command Achilles gives and Patroclus does not keep.
  • Phaeacians — Day 24, Literature · Odyssey, Books 5–8: Calypso, the raft, and the Phaeacians. The people of Scheria, who receive Odysseus after his raft wreck, host him, hear his name, and (in the poem’s design) will send him home.
  • ransom of Hector — Day 19, Literature · Iliad, Books 23–24: the funeral, the ransom, a last written response. The return of Hector’s body to Priam, for burial, after the supplication and the giving of gifts, with a pause in the fighting agreed for the funeral.
  • simile — Day 4, Literature · Iliad, Books 2–3. A comparison that names itself with ‘like’ or ‘as’, often extended over several lines in Homer, usually from nature or ordinary work.
  • suitors — Day 22, Literature · Odyssey, Books 1–4: the Telemachy. The men who occupy Odysseus’s house in Ithaca, pressing Penelope to remarry and consuming the household’s stores, while Telemachus is not yet able to drive them out.
  • supplication (Priam) — Day 19, Literature · Iliad, Books 23–24: the funeral, the ransom, a last written response. The scene in Book 24 in which Priam comes by night to Achilles’ shelter, kisses his hands, and asks for Hector’s body, appealing to Achilles’ own father, Peleus.
  • Telemachy — Day 22, Literature · Odyssey, Books 1–4: the Telemachy. The opening movement of the Odyssey (Books 1–4), whose foreground is Telemachus in Ithaca and on his journey to Pylos and Sparta, rather than Odysseus’s travels at sea.
  • the death of Hector — Day 17, Literature · Iliad, Books 17–22: the shield, the return, the death of Hector. The climax of Book 22: Achilles, now back in the war, chases Hector around the walls of Troy and kills him, then mistreats the body.
  • thesis — Day 9, Literature · Writing about the Iliad: the analytical essay. One sentence that can be argued against, stating the judgment the essay will defend.

Writing & Rhetoric

  • although-sentence — Day 9, Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin's method, part 3: verse into prose. A sentence that concedes a real point on the other side, then states the judgment it can still defend.
  • appositive — Day 3, Writing & Rhetoric · Appositives and subordinating conjunctions. A noun phrase set beside another noun to rename it: ‘Leonardo, the draughtsman, …’.
  • attribution — Day 18, Writing & Rhetoric · Quotations and attribution. The naming of who said or wrote the words, and in what work or on what occasion, so that a reader can find the source.
  • claim — Day 8, Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium II, logic: claim, evidence, warrant. A sentence that can be argued with, which the rest of the paragraph or essay must support.
  • clause — Day 5, Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium I, grammar: the anatomy of a sentence. A group of words with a subject and a verb.
  • comparison step — Day 17, Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin's method: reconstruction from the hints. The last step of a reconstruction: laying your version beside the original and naming, in writing, two or three specific choices the author made that you did not — a verb, a delay, an apposition, a transition — not a vague judgment that the original is ‘better’.
  • concession — Day 19, Writing & Rhetoric · Concession in a two-paragraph argument. A real point on the other side that you state fairly, before you show why your thesis still stands.
  • concluding sentence — Day 16, Writing & Rhetoric · From one paragraph to two: the two-paragraph outline. The last sentence of a paragraph or of a short essay that does not merely repeat the topic sentence, but closes the thought — often by stating a consequence, a limit, or a return to the thesis in new words.
  • conjunctive adverb — Day 13, Writing & Rhetoric · The semicolon and the conjunctive adverb. A word such as however, therefore, moreover, meanwhile, or otherwise that shows the relation between two complete sentences.
  • coordinated series — Day 21, Writing & Rhetoric · Parallelism in a series. Two or more elements joined by a coordinating conjunction (and, or, but) or by punctuation that does the same job, which must be parallel if they share a single grammatical slot.
  • copia — Day 2, Writing & Rhetoric · Imitatio: copywork and copia. Plenty of expression: many accurate ways of saying the same idea, produced on purpose.
