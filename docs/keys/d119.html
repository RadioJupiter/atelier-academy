Latin · A short Term 2 mix

Check. 1. Ego cōnābar: I was trying (imperfect). Nōs locūtī sumus: we spoke or have spoken (perfect). Puer loquitur: the boy speaks (present). 2. It is an absolute, not the imperfect loquēbātur. 3. The accusative is the subject of the reported clause; the infinitive is that clause’s verb.

Exercises. 1. Model: Ego loquor / loquēbar / locūtus sum, and Magister puerum adesse dīcit. 2. Cōnāminī, you (plural) try; cōnātī estis, you (plural) tried or have tried.

Mathematics · A short Term 2 mix: similarity and right-triangle ratios

Check. 1. Sin A = 3/5, cos A = 4/5, tan A = 3/4, tan(90° − A) = 4/3. 2. The sides are 9 and 12. Tan A = 9/12 = 3/4. 3. 3/5 is sine, opposite over hypotenuse, not tangent.

Exercises. 1. Sin D = 12/13, cos D = 5/13, tan D = 12/5, and (12/13)/(5/13) = 12/5. 2. They have not been taught as first looks. Degrees only.

Writing · A short Term 2 mix: hints, a reconstruction, and a précis

Science · Term 2 examination (heat transfer and conduction)

Check. 1. Conduction, convection, and radiation. Radiation is responsible for most of the heat transferred into the room. 2. Q/t = kA(T2 − T1)/d. The factors are k, A, (T2 − T1), and d. When T2 = T1, the net rate is zero. 3. Copper 390 J/s·m·°C; Styrofoam 0.010 J/s·m·°C. A third k not in the table would be invented. 4. A = 0.950 m², d = 0.0250 m, T1 = 0 °C, T2 = 35.0 °C, t = 86,400 s, k = 0.010 J/s·m·°C. Q/t = 13.3 J/s. 5. Q = 1.15 × 10⁶ J, m = 3.44 kg, using Lf = 334 × 10³ J/kg. Example 14.6 remains reserved.

Literature · Term 2 examination (Inferno V and VI)

Check. 1. “Who reason subjugate to appetite.” The speaker falls “even as a dead body falls.” 2. “There is no greater sorrow than to be mindful of the happy time in misery.” 3. “The rain / Eternal, maledict, and cold, and heavy.” The Conductor throws earth into the rapacious gullets, as already taught. 4. “You citizens were wont to call me Ciacco.” Do not add an official historical identification beyond the name the poem gives. 5. “The just are two, and are not understood there.” The three sparks are Envy, Arrogance, and Avarice. The poem does not name those two here. The canto ends with “Plutus the great enemy.” Canto VII remains reserved.

Modern language · Spanish: a short Term 2 mix

Check. 1. Preterite: completed, ayer comí or ayer fui. Imperfect: habitual or background, cada mañana iba or era. 2. Ayer, mientras comía, vi el cielo: while I was eating, I saw the sky. 3. They share the preterite fui, but their imperfects are different.

Exercises. 1. Model: Cada mañana iba al mercado. Ayer, mientras iba, vi el cielo. 2. Mientras marks a background, so comía; a completed seeing is vi.

Seminar · A typical vertebra, and the column, reviewed

Check. 1. 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, and 4 coccygeal. 2. Cervical and lumbar convex forward; thoracic concave forward; pelvic concavity downward and forward. Thoracic and pelvic are primary; cervical and lumbar are secondary or compensatory. 3. Stacked bodies form a pillar. Stacked vertebral foramina form a canal for the medulla spinalis (spinal cord). Intervertebral foramina transmit spinal nerves and vessels.

Exercises. 1. They are Gray’s forms in the set text. 2. Official 71 cm and 61 cm lengths, a slight lateral curvature, and regional special features.
