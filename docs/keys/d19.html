Latin · Capitulum VI, part 5: a first look at the passive

Check. 1. ā servō after a passive verb names the doer (‘by the slave’). cum servō is accompaniment (‘with the slave’), as in est cum servō. 2. Servus saccum portat. 3. Model: passive. Own words should match this idea: A verb form in which the subject is the person or thing that the action happens to, not the one who does it. In this chapter you meet third-person forms such as portātur (‘is carried’) and portantur (‘are carried’). Near-miss: The passive is not a past tense. portātur is still a present-tense form. And ā servō (by the slave) is not the same as cum servō (with the slave). ablative of agent. Own words should match this idea: The construction ā or ab + ablative that names the person who does the action of a passive verb. Near-miss: This is not motion from, even though it uses the same preposition ab. In a passive sentence ā servō names the doer, not a place left behind. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Saccī ā servīs portantur.
  2. Use ā servō, not cum servō, for the agent.
  3. Any correct pair, for example Puer ā dominō vocātur / Dominus puerum vocat, if those verbs have been met; otherwise stay with portāre.
  4. Accurate identification and clear reading.
  5. Present. The lesson treats portātur as the passive partner of the present portat, not as a past tense.

Mathematics · Geometry X: tangents to a circle

Check. 1. OP = √(8² + 15²) = √(64 + 225) = √289 = 17. 2. PB = 9, because the two tangent segments from the same outside point are equal. 3. Model: tangent to a circle. Own words should match this idea: A line that meets a circle at exactly one point, called the point of contact. The radius to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent. Near-miss: A tangent is not a chord (a chord meets the circle twice), and a line that ‘just misses’ the circle on the page is not a tangent. two tangents from a point. Own words should match this idea: From a point outside a circle, the two tangent segments to the points of contact are equal. The two radii to those points, the two tangents, and the line from the center to the outside point make two congruent right triangles. Near-miss: This is not ‘any two tangents of the same circle are equal’ — only the two drawn from the same outside point. Parallel tangents on opposite sides are a different figure. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. OP = √(12² + 16²) = √(144 + 256) = √400 = 20. Area of △OAP = (1/2) × 12 × 16 = 96.
  2. Tangent = √(15² − 9²) = √(225 − 81) = √144 = 12.
  3. If the radius were not perpendicular to the line, a perpendicular from the center to the line would be shorter than the radius, and the line would cut the circle at two points, contradicting the definition of a tangent.
  4. The two right triangles (radius, tangent, line OP) share the hypotenuse OP and a radius as a leg, so they are congruent by Hypotenuse–Leg, and the tangent segments are equal.
  5. Congruent right triangles give equal angles at P and at O, and the shared line OP is then also perpendicular to AB at its midpoint.

Writing & Rhetoric · Concession in a two-paragraph argument

Check. 1. The although-sentence names a specific contrary point and a remaining thesis; it usually opens paragraph 2, or, if it limits the whole, the close. 2. The concluding sentence states a boundary and restates, in new words, what the evidence still supports. 3. Model: concession. Own words should match this idea: A real point on the other side that you state fairly, before you show why your thesis still stands. In this course it often takes the form of an although-sentence (Day 9), and in a two-paragraph essay it often opens the second paragraph or appears in the concluding sentence. Near-miss: A concession is not a fake weakness (‘although some people may disagree’), and it is not a surrender of the thesis. If you ignore the point after stating it, it was only decoration. limit of a claim. Own words should match this idea: A precise statement of what the thesis does not try to prove, so that the argument stays as strong as the evidence. A limit is not a concession of a contrary fact; it is a boundary around your own claim. Near-miss: A limit is not timidity, and it is not ‘we can never know anything’. It is a way of not claiming more than the sources will bear (Day 1’s sourcing habit). The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. The points after the concession should answer it.
  2. Specific facts replace the fake concession.
  3. The science pair should be accurate (for example: although a drying oil does lose some volatile matter, the film forms mainly by reaction with oxygen, not by evaporation like a watercolor).
  4. The close is exact, not inflated and not a surrender.
  5. The outline is saved.

Science · The oil-paint module: a quantitative check and a short synthesis

Check. 1. The oil can take up oxygen, so its mass can rise; watercolor loses water by evaporation, so its mass should fall. 2. A model first sentence: Oil paint ‘dries’ because the drying-oil binder reacts with oxygen and the molecules link into a solid film, not because the oil evaporates. 3. Model: mass check (of a film). Own words should match this idea: A measurement that can distinguish evaporation from autoxidation: a film that is mainly losing a liquid to the air should lose mass; a drying-oil film can gain mass as it takes up oxygen (before any later, slower losses). The check is the direction of the change, not a promised percentage. Near-miss: A mass check is not a license to invent a number you did not measure, and a single weighing on a coarse kitchen scale may not be sensitive enough. If the scale cannot show the change, say so; do not fake a gain. model of oil-paint drying. Own words should match this idea: The short account this module has built: a drying-oil binder, open to the air, undergoes autoxidation and then polymerization, forming a solid film that holds the pigment; a solvent, if used, may evaporate, but that is not what makes the oil itself into a film; watercolor is a different system, with water leaving mainly by evaporation. Near-miss: The model is not a complete chemistry of every additive, and it is not a rule about a fixed number of hours. It is a model: it explains the smears, the skin on a lump, and a possible mass gain, and it has limits. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Either a real pair of masses, or an honest ‘not measured’ with predicted directions (oil: possible gain; watercolor: loss).
  2. A short synthesis that uses the terms correctly and states a limit (no fixed time; not every additive; no toxic driers used).
  3. An honest end to the record.
  4. The oil binder and the pigment are already in the model; the solvent’s evaporation is already distinguished; the chemistry of a particular modern drier would need more than this module.
  5. The thicker film, because oxygen has farther to go, so the interior remains short of oxygen after the surface has formed a skin.

Literature · Iliad, Books 23–24: the funeral, the ransom, a last written response

Check. 1. It buries Hector and stops. It does not narrate Achilles’ death or the fall of Troy. 2. A careful answer, close to the text: Andromache often laments as a wife and as the mother of a child now unprotected; Hecuba as a mother of many dead; Helen as the one who was kindlier treated by Hector than by others in Troy. Exact wording depends on the translation; the three voices should not be collapsed into one. 3. Model: supplication (Priam). Own words should match this idea: The scene in Book 24 in which Priam comes by night to Achilles’ shelter, kisses his hands, and asks for Hector’s body, appealing to Achilles’ own father, Peleus. It is a formal and dangerous asking, not a casual request. Near-miss: Supplication is not ‘Priam apologizes for the war’, and it is not a conversion of Achilles into a gentle hero. He is still dangerous; the poem also shows pity and a shared grief. ransom of Hector. Own words should match this idea: The return of Hector’s body to Priam, for burial, after the supplication and the giving of gifts, with a pause in the fighting agreed for the funeral. It is the close of the Iliad; the fall of Troy is not narrated in this poem. Near-miss: The ransom is not a happy ending in a modern sense, and it is not a cancellation of Book 22. The body, which was dragged, is washed and burned, and the women lament. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A two-paragraph piece that meets the prompt: Book 24 returns the body, allows lament and a burial, and shows a shared grief, without cancelling Book 22 or ending the war forever.
  2. The games honor Patroclus, display the army still as a community, and delay, then prepare, the turn toward Hector’s body. A passing answer uses the book, not a generic ‘comic relief’.
  3. Book 9’s gifts were to buy a return to war and failed; Book 24’s gifts go with a body returned for burial, after supplication, and they are not a reversal of the whole war.
  4. A sentence that keeps his danger, his grief, and his pity in view together.
  5. Because this poem’s last act is a funeral, not a sequel; the ending is a design, not a cliffhanger the reader is obliged to fill in at once.

Atelier · Accents and a point of focus

Check. 1. Because an accent works by contrast with what is not accented. If every shadow is equally black and sharp, nothing stands out, and the eye is not guided. 2. Plausible: the contact with the table; the deepest part of a nostril or of an overlap. Probably not: a random corner of the background cloth, or the whole length of a contour. 3. Model: accent. Own words should match this idea: A small, especially dark (or, more rarely, especially sharp or especially light) touch that sits at a point of contact, a deep hole of shadow, or a decisive edge. Accents are few. They are placed after the large families are right, not as a first sprinkle of black. Near-miss: An accent is not ‘shade everything darker’, and it is not a black outline. A drawing full of accents has no accent. point of focus. Own words should match this idea: The place in the drawing you want the eye to arrive at first: usually the most particular passage of form, with a slightly stronger contrast or a slightly sharper found edge, while the surroundings are relatively quieter. Near-miss: A focus is not a spotlight invented after the fact, and it is not the same as a highlight (the highlight may or may not be at the focus). It is a decision about where the drawing is most specific. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A finished pass with a few real accents and a readable focus, families still intact, no invented spotlight.
  2. Marks and a sentence that name a genuinely quieter passage.
  3. Three sentences that give reasons, including a refusal.
  4. A short, practical note for a second start, not a wish list.
  5. A specific comparison with earlier work, not a vague ‘it is better’.

Modern language · Spanish: comparatives

Check. 1. La clase es menos difícil que el examen, y es tan larga como una hora. 2. mejor (drop más); for ‘as many books as’ do not use tan libros — rewrite with más/menos or wait for tantos; más de diez páginas. 3. Model: comparative. Own words should match this idea: A pattern that compares two people or things. In Spanish: más + adjective (or adverb or noun) + que; menos + … + que; and tan + adjective or adverb + como for ‘as … as’. Near-miss: Más que is not tan como, and más de is a different pattern used before a number (más de diez), not before the second person or thing being compared. irregular comparative. Own words should match this idea: A small set of comparatives that do not use más: mejor (better), peor (worse), mayor (older or greater), menor (younger or smaller). They still take que: mejor que, peor que. Near-miss: Do not write *más mejor or *más peor. Mayor/menor for age are the usual choice in this course, rather than *más viejo for a person when you mean ‘older’. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Four grammatical sentences, one of each required pattern.
  2. E.g. Ana es mayor que Luis. Luis es menor que Ana. Invented names only.
  3. Tengo más de veinte lápices, pero menos libros que cuadernos.
  4. Sé más lecciones que ayer, y conozco esta ciudad. (saber vs conocer both appear.)
  5. Four grammatical comparisons, with agreement and the right connecting word (que / como / de).

Economics & personal finance · Supply and demand

Check. 1. At $5 there is a surplus of 15; unsold sandwiches put downward pressure on the price, in the model. 2. E.g. a new timetable gives students a longer lunch (demand shifts out); the equilibrium price would rise if supply does not also shift. The example must be labeled as invented. 3. Model: demand and supply. Own words should match this idea: Demand is the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity buyers are willing and able to buy, other things equal. Supply is the relationship between the price and the quantity sellers are willing and able to offer, other things equal. Each is a whole schedule or curve, not a single number. Near-miss: Demand is not ‘what people wish they could have’, and supply is not ‘how many exist in a warehouse’. Willingness and ability, at a price, are part of the definition. equilibrium, shortage, and surplus. Own words should match this idea: The equilibrium price is the price at which the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied. At a price below that, there is a shortage (quantity demanded greater than quantity supplied). At a price above it, there is a surplus (quantity supplied greater than quantity demanded). Near-miss: Equilibrium is not ‘the fair price’ and not ‘a price that never changes’. It is the matching price in this model, other things equal. A change in tastes, costs, or income can shift a whole schedule and move the equilibrium. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. quantity demanded equals quantity supplied (both 30).
  2. falls (usually; in this table it falls as you read up the price column).
  3. Supply shifts (less offered at each price); the equilibrium price rises.
  4. Fairness is a moral or legal question. Equilibrium is only the matching price in the model, and it can change when schedules shift.
  5. A clear six-sentence account that uses the table correctly and says the numbers are invented.
