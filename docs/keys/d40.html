Physics · Friday mix: gravitation, and a first look at orbits

Check. 1. Weight 39.2 N; mass still 4 kg. 2. F becomes F/9. 3. Model: orbit (first look). Own words should match this idea: A path in which a body keeps falling toward a larger body and yet does not arrive, because it also has motion to the side. In Newton’s thought-picture (a throw from a high mountain, often illustrated from his System of the World), a faster sideways throw curves with the Earth instead of hitting the ground nearby. This lesson gives no invented orbital speed, and it does not treat every orbit as a circle. Near-miss: Not a claim that a circular orbit is the only possible path, and not a measured speed from this classroom. A picture of a throw is a thought-picture, not a classroom experiment from a real mountain. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. No sourced classroom measurement, and no invented figure, is on offer; the lesson’s point is the path, not a speed.
  2. Useful: a simple first picture of a closed path. Misleading: if it is taken to mean that every real orbit is a perfect circle.
  3. G is 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² in the general formula; g is 9.8 N/kg near the Earth.
  4. A fair set: the Moon is falling, because the Earth pulls on it; but it also has motion to the side, so it stays in orbit rather than arriving.
  5. F becomes 4F.

Vocabulary review

Check. 1. Sum of roots = −b; product = c. The near-miss is to take the sum as b, dropping the minus sign. (For ax² + bx + c = 0, sum = −b/a, product = c/a.) 2. Each interior angle is ((n − 2)/n)×180°. 120° is the interior angle of a regular hexagon; a hexagon that is not regular is not forced to that angle. 3. F = G Mm / r², with G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² as a standard constant, not a classroom measurement. When r doubles, F becomes F/4. 4. Mass is in kilograms; weight is a force, in newtons. Near the Earth, W = mg with g = 9.8 N/kg. 5. Accommodation is the change of the lens’s shape so that a near or far object can be focused on the retina. The lesson refuses invented focal lengths and diopter numbers. 6. 14 CE: death of Augustus and succession of Tiberius, not a second founding. 68 CE: end of Nero’s principate in the conventional Julio-Claudian list, and a year of civil war, not a “fall of the Republic” day. 7. Antigone, Haemon, and Eurydice are dead; Creon remains, seeing the cost too late. The close was quoted from Sophocles, Antigone, trans. R. C. Jebb, 1917. 8. An invalid syllogism in which the middle term never covers its whole class, so it does not link S to P (for example, All dogs are mammals; all cats are mammals; therefore all cats are dogs). Barbara is All M are P; All S are M; therefore All S are P, where M is distributed in the first premise.
