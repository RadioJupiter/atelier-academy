Latin · Term 2 examination (deponents, the absolute, and AcI)

Check. 1. Loquor, I speak; loquēbar, I was speaking or used to speak; locūtus sum, I spoke or have spoken. Cōnor, I try; cōnābar, I was trying or used to try; cōnātus sum, I tried or have tried. 2. Loquēbar, loquēbāris, loquēbātur, loquēbāmur, loquēbāminī, loquēbantur. Loquēbar: I was speaking, or I used to speak. Loquēbantur: they were speaking, or used to speak. 3. Ego cōnābar: I was trying (imperfect). Nōs locūtī sumus: we spoke or have spoken (perfect). Puer loquitur: the boy speaks (present). 4. Puerō locūtō is an absolute, a loosely attached phrase with no sum and no infinitive. The imperfect is loquēbātur. AcI would have an accusative subject and an infinitive verb. 5. The accusative is the subject of the reported clause; the infinitive is that clause’s verb. Model: Magister puerum adesse dīcit. The schoolmaster says that the boy is present.

Physics · Term 2 examination

Check. 1. Resonance is driving a system at its natural frequency, when energy is transferred most efficiently and the amplitude is greatest. Less damping gives a higher and narrower peak. OpenStax 16.8 does not give an official wind speed for that bridge; a number would be invented. 2. A wave is a disturbance that propagates from the place it was created. vw = λ / T = fλ. 3. λ = 10.0 m, T = 5.00 s, vw = 2.00 m/s. That is the speed of the disturbance, not the gull’s changing vertical speed. 4. Transverse: disturbance perpendicular to propagation; a guitar string, or visible light. Longitudinal: disturbance parallel to propagation; sound in air or water. 5. vw = 8.0 / 4.0 = 2.0 m/s, f = 0.25 Hz. Official P-wave and S-wave speeds are not given as numbers in this section for use here, so a number would be invented.

Modern language · Spanish: Term 2 examination (preterite and imperfect)

Check. 1. The preterite presents a past action as completed: ayer comí, ayer fui, ayer vi. The imperfect presents it as ongoing, habitual, or background: cada mañana comía, cada mañana iba, era, veía. 2. Comí, I ate; comía, I was eating or used to eat. Fui, I went; iba, I was going or used to go. Fui, I was (completed); era, I was or used to be. Vi, I saw; veía, I was seeing or used to see. Ir and ser share fui, not an imperfect. 3. Ayer fui al mercado (completed). Cada mañana iba al mercado (habitual). Cada mañana el cielo era claro (description, imperfect of ser). 4. Ayer, mientras comía, vi el cielo: yesterday, while I was eating, I saw the sky. Mientras comí would present the eating as a completed event, not as a background. 5. The miss swapped the jobs: mientras marks a background, so comía, and a completed seeing is vi, not veía. Vía drops the stem e; the already-taught imperfect is veía.

Seminar · Term 2 examination: a mood, a discount, and a typical vertebra

Check. 1. AII-3: All M are P; some M are S; therefore some S are P. M is the subject of both premises. Conclusion: some spades are metal. That is a conclusion from invented classroom terms, not an official fact about tools. 2. Darapti also concludes some S are P, but from two universal premises. Ferison is EIO-3 and concludes some S are not P. 3. Successive discounts leave $81; $80 would be a single 20 percent drop from the original. The markup path leaves $99; $100 would treat the percents as cancelling, and $81 is the other already-taught path. 4. A body and a vertebral or neural arch, enclosing the vertebral foramen. The arch has pedicles and laminæ, and seven processes: four articular, two transverse, and one spinous. Keep vertebræ and laminæ. 5. Stacked bodies form a pillar. Stacked vertebral foramina form a canal for the medulla spinalis (spinal cord). Intervertebral foramina transmit spinal nerves and vessels. Already-taught contrasts: the count of 33, and the four curves. Reserved: official 71 cm and 61 cm lengths, and regional special features.
