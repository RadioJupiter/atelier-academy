History · Babylon: the laws of Hammurabi

  1. Made for Hammurabi, displayed publicly in a temple context, addressed to his subjects, to the gods and to future kings. Purposes: to present the king as a just ruler chosen by the gods, to publish standards for judgement, and to preserve his reputation. Stone is permanent and public.
  2. Retaliation in kind (lex talionis) for injury to a man of full status. For lower-status free people the penalty becomes a fixed payment, and for a slave half the slave's value, payable (it is implied) to the owner. Punishment depended on the social rank of the victim: the society was formally hierarchical.
  3. Identify which source has the better access to the evidence (the museum holding the object, with modern geological analysis) and prefer the more recent expert identification, while noting that the older term was the standard description at the time. Neither source is "lying"; knowledge changed.
  4. Either side is acceptable if argued from the text. A strong answer notes that the penalty is limited to equivalence (no more than an eye), which restrains blood feud, while also noting that it is applied unequally by class.
