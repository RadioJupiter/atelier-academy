Latin · Sequor and ēgredior, a second mixed practice of future, present, and pluperfect

Check. 1. You (pl.) will follow; she had gone out. 2. I shall not go out. The friends will follow me. 3. Ēgrediuntur is the present, they go out, not the future ēgredientur.

Exercises. 1. Sequeris, you follow, present; sequēris, you will follow, future; secūtus erās, you had followed, pluperfect. Each sentence should keep that time. 2. A fair sentence keeps ēgrediar, I shall go out, and sequētur, he or she will follow, both still ahead, but as two different verbs. 3. Later deponent work, including any future perfect, remains reserved. Radians are a mathematics reservation, not a Latin one.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXXII: the law of cosines, a second mixed practice

Check. 1. 6, because c² = 36. 2. 120°, because the cosine is −1/2. 3. The law of sines can check a side once an opposite angle is known. It does not use the cosine of the included angle, which is today’s formula.

Exercises. 1. 5, because c² = 9 + 16 − 0 = 25. 2. 60°, because the cosine is 1/2. 3. Radians and the ambiguous case remain reserved. The side lengths and angles are classroom numbers, not official measurements.

Science · Cinnabar contrasted with azurite and malachite, still not a studio material

Check. 1. Cinnabar is HgS, Manhattan, Nevada. Azurite is Cu3(CO3)2(OH)2; malachite is Cu2CO3(OH)2. The last two are copper carbonates, not mercury sulfide. 2. No official mercury or copper percentage of a classroom stone, and no official price. 3. The limited palette remains the four already-taught paints. Cinnabar is mercury sulfide and is not handled as a studio material in this course.

Exercises. 1. Cinnabar is the sulfide. Azurite and malachite are the copper carbonates. 2. It is the record’s figure for that impure sample, HS133, not a general official iron percentage of all cinnabar. 3. A studio recipe, synthetic verditer, and official prices remain reserved. Electricity at home has not yet been today’s job.

Literature · Pride and Prejudice, Chapter 4: Bingley and Darcy contrasted, and the assembly recalled

Check. 1. “Nearly an hundred thousand pounds,” the novel’s figure, not an official salary. He is “established only as a tenant.” 2. Bingley’s manners are inviting; “Bingley was sure of being liked wherever he appeared.” Darcy is “haughty, reserved, and fastidious,” and “continually giving offence.” 3. Bingley: “an angel more beautiful.” Darcy: “pretty, but she smiled too much.” The sisters “pronounced her to be a sweet girl.”

Exercises. 1. Bingley “could not conceive an angel more beautiful.” Darcy “acknowledged [her] to be pretty, but she smiled too much.” 2. It is the novel’s wording about the assembly, and it stays inside quotation or clearly in the book’s household. 3. Chapter 5 remains reserved. Official identifications of characters with living people, and official salaries, are not added.

Modern language · Spanish: regular present subjunctives in short invented sentences

Check. 1. That they speak; that you (pl.) eat; that I live, or that he or she live. 2. “Quiere que nosotros hablemos.” Hablamos is present indicative, not the subjunctive. 3. Vivirán, the future, they will live tomorrow. Vivan would need a subjunctive frame such as “quiere que.”

Exercises. 1. Fair invented sentences keep “quiere que” and the matching subjunctive form. 2. Hable, not habla or hablaría; coma, not come or comería; viva, not vive or vivirá. 3. Irregular subjunctives remain reserved.

Seminar · Economics: a deductible on invented figures, a first look

Check. 1. An amount the holder bears first when the named event happens, before the invented payment is made. $400 is paid, because $500 − $100 = $400. 2. $120, paid in advance, whether or not the event happens. 3. A tax is not an amount borne first after a named event in this arrangement, and it is not this premium. The figures are invented classroom numbers, not an official policy or rate. 4. Model: a deductible (invented figures). Own words should match this idea: A deductible, in this invented classroom arrangement, is an amount the holder bears first when the named event happens, before the invented payment is made. It is not a premium, not a tax, and not an official policy. Near-miss: Not the premium, which is paid in advance, invented here as $10 a month. Not a tax. Not a game of stakes. Not an official deductible, rate, or policy. Not last week’s $500 paid in full, unless the invented arrangement has no deductible. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Nothing is paid, and in this invented arrangement the deductible is not charged if the event does not happen. 2. $450, because $500 − $50 = $450. The $50 is invented. 3. Official insurance rates and official deductibles remain reserved.
