Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations

Check. 1. x = 2 or x = 3. 2. A product (or a square) can be zero in more than one way; the graph can cross the axis twice.

  1. (x + 3)² = 16 ⇒ x + 3 = ±4 ⇒ x = 1 or x = −7.
  2. x = (3 ± √(9 + 16))/4 = (3 ± 5)/4 ⇒ x = 2 or x = −1/2. Vieta on x² − 1.5x − 1 = 0: sum 1.5 ✓, product −1 ✓.
  3. D = k² − 36 = 0 ⇒ k = ±6.
  4. r + s = 5, rs = 3. r² + s² = 25 − 6 = 19; 1/r + 1/s = (r + s)/rs = 5/3.
  5. Sum 4, product (2 + √3)(2 − √3) = 4 − 3 = 1 ⇒ x² − 4x + 1 = 0.
  6. ac < 0 ⇒ −4ac > 0 ⇒ D = b² − 4ac > 0. ∎
  7. Let y = x²: y² − 5y + 4 = 0 ⇒ y = 1 or 4 ⇒ x = ±1, ±2.

Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium I, grammar: the anatomy of a sentence

Check. 1. The subject and the finite verb of the main clause. 2. No: it is a relative (subordinate) clause.

  1. (a) S I, V bought, DO it. (b) S I, V thought, DO the writing, object complement excellent. (c) S The Mind, V sprouts up; dependent clause that lies fallow but a single Day modifies Mind.
  2. (a) compound; (b) complex; (c) complex (after the flame went out is a dependent clause); (d) simple.
  3. Compound: The candle went out, and the gas cooled, so the water rose. Complex: When the candle went out, the gas cooled and the water rose. Simple: After the flame's death, the cooling gas drew the water upward.

Physics · Free fall

Check. 1. Neither: both have g. 2. 10 m/s downward.

  1. C. The acceleration does not depend on mass.
  2. C. Horizontal motion does not change vertical motion; both start with zero vertical velocity.
  3. B. Without air resistance, motion up and down is symmetric.
  4. t = √(2 · 20/9.8) ≈ 2.0 s; v = gt ≈ 20 m/s (19.8).
  5. h = v²/2g = 225/19.6 ≈ 11.5 m; t = 15/9.8 ≈ 1.5 s.
  6. t = √(0.30/9.8) ≈ 0.17 s. Typical results are 0.15–0.25 s.

History · Greece: Herodotus and the idea of "inquiry"

  1. So that human deeds are not forgotten; so that great achievements of both Greeks and non-Greeks keep their fame; and, above all, so that the causes of the war are remembered.
  2. No: "works great and marvellous … some by Hellenes and some by Barbarians" gives both equal credit. (Barbaroi was not yet as pejorative as "barbarian" is in English.)
  3. The stele glorifies one ruler and commands; Herodotus writes as a private inquirer, explains causes, and gives credit to both sides. The stele is a primary source for Hammurabi's self-image; Herodotus is a secondary source for earlier events and a primary source for 5th-century Greek thought.
  4. A chronicle lists what happened; history asks why. Seeking causes means weighing evidence and explaining, which is what historians still do.

Atelier · Bargue plate: modeling and completion

Check. 1. The big light and the big shadow. 2. The edge where the form turns from light into shadow.

The finished copy should match the plate's value pattern when both are viewed squinting from the marked spot. Typical beginner faults: shadows too dark and too varied, light side over-modeled, outlines left as hard lines where the plate has none. One Bargue plate commonly takes students many hours; finishing in a week is an achievement. Do not rush accuracy for speed.
