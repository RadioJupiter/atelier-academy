Latin · Friday mix: the deponent imperfect, with present and perfect contrasts

Check. Loquēbar, loquēbāris, loquēbātur, loquēbāmur, loquēbāminī, loquēbantur. Loquēbar: I was speaking, or I used to speak. Loquēbātur: he or she was speaking, or used to speak. Ego cōnābar: I was trying (imperfect). Nōs locūtī sumus: we spoke or have spoken (perfect). Puer loquitur: the boy speaks (present). Puerō locūtō is an absolute, not the imperfect loquēbātur.

Exercises. Model: Ego loquor / loquēbar / locūtus sum, with honest translations. Cōnāminī, you (plural) try; cōnātī estis, you (plural) tried or have tried.

Mathematics · Friday mix: tangent, with sine and cosine

Physics · Friday mix: waves, wave velocity, and the two kinds of wave

Check. A wave is a disturbance that propagates from the place it was created. vw = λ / T = fλ. Example 16.8: vw = 2.00 m/s, the speed of the disturbance, not the gull’s changing vertical speed. Transverse: a guitar string, or visible light. Longitudinal: sound in air or water.

Exercises. vw = 6.0 / 2.0 = 3.0 m/s, f = 0.50 Hz. OpenStax says P-waves and S-waves propagate at different speeds but does not give those official numbers in this section for use here, so a number would be invented.

Modern language · Spanish: Friday mix of preterite versus imperfect

Check. The preterite presents a past action as completed: ayer comí, ayer fui, ayer vi. The imperfect presents it as ongoing, habitual, or background: cada mañana comía, cada mañana iba, era, veía. Ayer, mientras comía, vi el cielo: while I was eating, I saw the sky. Ir and ser share the preterite fui, but their imperfects are different: iba and era.

Exercises. Model: Cada mañana iba al mercado. Ayer, mientras iba, vi el cielo. The miss swapped the jobs: mientras already marks a background, so comía, and the seeing, if completed, is vi, not veía.

Seminar · Artistic anatomy: a typical vertebra, from Gray 1918

Check. Model: a typical vertebra, from Gray. Own words should match this idea: Gray 1918, in the public-domain text used here from Theodora, describes a typical vertebra as consisting of two essential parts: an anterior segment, the body, and a posterior part, the vertebral or neural arch; these enclose a foramen, the vertebral foramen. The vertebral arch consists of a pair of pedicles and a pair of laminæ, and supports seven processes: four articular, two transverse, and one spinous. When the vertebræ are articulated, the bodies form a pillar for the support of the head and trunk, the vertebral foramina constitute a canal for the protection of the medulla spinalis (spinal cord), and between every pair are two intervertebral foramina, for the transmission of the spinal nerves and vessels. Keep Gray’s spellings vertebræ and laminæ. Official lengths remain reserved. Regional special features of cervical, thoracic, or lumbar vertebræ remain reserved. Near-miss: Not a first look at the count of 33 vertebræ, already taught, or at the four curves, already taught last week. Not official 71 cm or 61 cm lengths, which remain reserved. Not the special features of a cervical, thoracic, or lumbar vertebra, which remain reserved. Bartleby no longer serves the page; the set text is Theodora, https://theodora.com/anatomy/general_characteristics_of_a_vertebra.html. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Stacked bodies form a pillar for the head and trunk. Stacked vertebral foramina form a canal for the medulla spinalis (spinal cord). Intervertebral foramina transmit spinal nerves and vessels. Already-taught contrasts: the count of 33, and the four curves. Reserved: official lengths, and regional special features. Bartleby is not the set text; Theodora is.

Exercises. They are Gray’s forms in the set text, as vertebræ has been kept in earlier lessons. Those official lengths were reserved last week and have not been taken up with a sourced first look today, so using them would invent today’s job.
