Elective · Anatomy: the foot — tarsus, metatarsals, and phalanges

Check. 1. A long bone of the midfoot, between tarsus and the toe phalanges. A metacarpal is the parallel bone in the hand; the names are not interchangeable. 2. They are reserved for a later sitting, from a named source, as the carpal names were reserved last week. 3. Model: tarsus. Own words should match this idea: The tarsus is the group of bones in the posterior foot, the counterpart of the carpus in the hand. In this lesson it is taught as a group. The seven individual tarsal names are reserved, as the eight carpal names were reserved last week. Near-miss: Not a first look at the carpus, metacarpals, or hand phalanges, which were taught last week. Not a claim that the tarsus is a single bone. The individual names (for example the calcaneus as a later name) are reserved, not invented unofficially today. metatarsal. Own words should match this idea: A metatarsal is one of the long bones of the midfoot, between the tarsus and the phalanges of the toes, the counterpart of a metacarpal in the hand. Near-miss: Not a phalanx, and not a tarsal bone. Not a metacarpal renamed without the foot’s own name. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Two phalanges in the hallux, as two in the thumb; three in each of the other toes, as in the other fingers.
  2. The course does not put identifying personal information on the site, and no particular living foot is required for the grouping.

Vocabulary review

Check. 1. A = b × h, with h perpendicular to the base. Trapezoid: ½(6 + 10) × 4 = 32. 2. The apothem is the perpendicular from the center of a regular polygon to the middle of a side. Area = ½ × 48 × 7.2 = 172.8, invented measurements. 3. Uterque: each of the two (often with a singular verb). The present participle names a same-time action and agrees with a noun; it is not the main verb. 4. Tuve, tuviste, tuvo, tuvimos, tuvisteis, tuvieron. Estuve is the preterite of estar; fui is the preterite of ser or ir. 5. T = 2π√(L/g), for amplitudes less than about 15°. T depends on length and on g, not on mass. 6. Solubility is the composition of that saturated solution (S05740), not the leftover solid itself. 7.
