Latin · Deponent pluperfect, imperfect, and perfect, mixed

Check. 1. Locūtus eram: the speaking is already completed before I was silent. 2. Loquēbar: cotīdiē marks a habitual past, I used to speak, not a prior completed action. 3. Both use locūtus. Sum makes the perfect, I spoke or I have spoken. Eram makes the pluperfect, I had spoken, already done before another past moment.

Exercises. 1. She had spoken (pluperfect). She was speaking or used to speak (imperfect). She spoke or has spoken (perfect). 2. A fair sentence: Puer locūtus erat, et tum tacēbat. The first verb is pluperfect, the second imperfect. 3. Cōnātus eram; cōnābar; cōnātus sum.

Mathematics · Geometry LXIX: two planes in space, a first look

Check. 1. They either intersect in a line, or they are parallel and do not meet. 2. In the line AB, the front bottom edge. 3. If two planes share a point, and they are not the same plane, their intersection is a line through that point, not an isolated point. 4. Model: two planes in space. Own words should match this idea: In space, two distinct planes either intersect in a line, or they are parallel and do not meet. They cannot meet at only a single point, and they cannot meet in a short segment that then stops. Near-miss: Not two planes that meet at a single point. Not a claim that a real ceiling is officially parallel to a real floor. The box is an invented model. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. In the line BF, the right-hand bottom edge. 2. Yes. They are opposite faces of the rectangular box and do not meet. 3. The box is an invented model. Parallelism here is a geometric relation of the model’s faces, not a measured official room.

Writing · An original fable, in the same shape

Check. 1. A fair answer is a short invented narrative, not the crow, ending in one moral sentence that follows from the action. The model ink-jar fable is one acceptable shape, not the required story. 2. A moral announced before any action; a mid-story lecture; a retelling of Townsend; or a scene so long that it is no longer a fable. 3. If the moral could be attached to any story, or contradicts the action, it is not the moral of this fable.

Exercises. 1. The moral should be one sentence after the last action, not a new event and not a paragraph of advice. 2. Do not invent an official habit of a living animal, or an official school or legal rule, and present it as a fact. 3. It still has two parts: a short invented narrative, and a stated moral after the action.

Physics · Standing waves, nodes, and the fundamental, a first look

Check. 1. A standing wave is the superposition of two identical waves moving in opposite directions, with oscillations at fixed locations. A node is a point of zero disturbance; an antinode is a location of maximum amplitude. 2. λ1 = 4.00 m, f1 = 20.0 Hz; λ2 = 2.00 m, f2 = 40.0 Hz. 3. The string cannot move at a fixed end, so the disturbance is zero there. 20.0 Hz comes from invented L and vw, not from a measured official instrument. 4. Model: standing wave. Own words should match this idea: A standing wave, in OpenStax 16.10, is the result of two identical waves moving in opposite directions, superposed so that the oscillations stay at fixed locations. The pattern does not travel; it alternates between constructive and destructive interference in place. On a string fixed at both ends, the ends must be nodes. Near-miss: Not a traveling wave that carries a crest along the string. Not a reserved official frequency of a named instrument. The invented L and vw are classroom figures, not a measured guitar. node (of a standing wave). Own words should match this idea: A node is a point in a standing wave where the disturbance is zero, and the string does not move. The fixed ends of a string must be nodes. Near-miss: Not an antinode, which is a location of maximum amplitude. Not a claim that the wave has ceased to exist on the rest of the string. antinode. Own words should match this idea: An antinode is a location of maximum amplitude in a standing wave. Near-miss: Not a node, where the disturbance is zero. Not a traveling crest that moves along the string. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. f3 = 3f1 = 60.0 Hz. Also λ3 = 2L / 3 = 4.00 / 3 m, and f3 = 80.0 / (4/3) = 80.0 × 3 / 4 = 60.0 Hz. 2. Nodes at both fixed ends and at the midpoint. There are two antinodes, one in each half. 3. The standing pattern is the sum, in place, of two traveling waves. The disturbances still add; they do not replace one another.

History · Inter Caetera: the grant to Castile and León

Check. 1. To the kings of Castile and León and their heirs and successors, forever: lands already discovered by their envoys and lands “to be discovered hereafter.” 2. The lands must not have been in the actual temporal possession of any Christian owner. A right already acquired by any Christian prince is not to be understood as withdrawn or taken away. 3. This May 3 clause does not draw a line of leagues. The later May 4 bull remains reserved.

Exercises. 1. The grant is not only for lands already named as discovered; it also claims lands not yet found, still under the same proviso. 2. It notes that some kings of Portugal, “likewise by apostolic grant made to them,” have similarly discovered and taken possession of islands in Africa, Guinea, and the Gold Mine, and it extends similar favors by comparison. That is a comparison in this bull, not a full reading of a Portuguese grant. 3. An official island count, an official quantity of gold, and an official line of leagues.

Atelier · An earth-red scale, and a value match with ochre

Check. 1. Earth red, white, and black. Four or five even steps are enough. 2. An earth-red mixture that has the same lightness as a chosen ochre step, checked by half-closing the eyes or by comparing the two as values. 3. Mixing the two hues and calling that a match, or matching hue instead of value. A portrait, and a fifth tube, remain reserved.

Exercises. 1. Today’s job is to see that value can match across different hues. Blending the two hues makes a third color and hides the check. 2. Value is lightness, already taught in monochrome. Half-closing the eyes reduces the pull of hue so the lightness can be compared. 3. The job is two scales and one match, not a likeness of a face.

Modern language · Spanish: the simple future, contrasted with the present

Check. 1. Hablaré: mañana names a later time, and the simple future is the form taught for that use today. 2. Habla: hoy names the present, so the already-taught present is the right form. 3. Hablaré still shows the infinitive hablar, plus -é. Hablo is a present form, built on a stem, not on the whole infinitive with a future ending.

Exercises. 1. A fair pair: Hoy como en casa. Mañana comeré en casa. 2. A fair pair: Ahora vivo aquí, an invented place. Un día viviré cerca del río, also invented. 3. Mañana hablo still uses the present form hablo. The simple future is hablaré. A time word does not by itself make a present into a future tense.
