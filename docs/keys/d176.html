Latin · Purpose clauses, reviewed

Check. 1. She comes in order that she follow him (positive purpose). She stays in order that she not follow him (negative purpose). He rises in order that he go out (positive purpose). 2. Sequētur is the future, not the present subjunctive. 3. An intended purpose. A result clause is tomorrow’s review.

Exercises. 1. Model: Puella venit ut eum sequātur, and Puella manet nē eum sequātur. 2. Puer surgit ut ēgrediātur: he rises in order that he go out. Puer sedet nē ēgrediātur: he sits in order that he not go out.

Mathematics · A line and a plane in space, reviewed

Check. 1. The line may lie in the plane, meet it at exactly one point, or be parallel to it and never meet it. 2. It meets the plane at a foot P and is perpendicular to every line in the plane through P. 3. This week reviews already-taught methods and holds examinations. No new first look is opened.

Exercises. 1. Model: a line drawn on the table plane lies in it; a line parallel to the table, above it, never meets it. 2. It might meet at one point without being perpendicular to every line through the foot.

Writing · Encomium, reviewed

Check. 1. It praises one subject, with already-taught reasons. 2. “The Crow and the Pitcher,” as a fable well made. The school rule is that the subject is invented, not a living person. 3. An invective, and a comparison.

Exercises. 1. Model: a short praise of the already-used invented fable, with already-taught reasons. 2. It treats one subject by praise, not two subjects side by side.

Physics · Refraction and official Example 25.3, reviewed

Check. 1. n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂. Sin θ₂ = (0.413)(0.500) = 0.207, θ₂ = 11.9°, official. 2. Official n₂ = 1.33. 3. Total internal reflection and official Example 25.4.

Exercises. 1. Official 11.9° is Example 25.3, a ray from air into diamond. Official 42.2° is Example 25.4, the critical angle for polystyrene to air, Wednesday’s review. 2. Official book figures are taken from the book, not invented as if official.

History · Twenty-five leagues, a port, and the admiral’s resolve, reviewed

Check. 1. “twenty-five leagues in length”; “more than two leagues along the shore in search of a harbour”; “after proceeding two leagues we found a port late in the evening.” 2. “That night the admiral resolved that some of the men should land at break of day in order to confer with the natives.” 3. The letter does not give one, and official conversions are not invented. Later events remain reserved, and this week opens no new extract.

Exercises. 1. It is the book’s spelling, kept inside a quotation. 2. They were already reserved, and no new extract is opened this week.

Atelier · The already-taught group, reviewed

Check. 1. Two forms on the table, still four paints, one light, value first. 2. As a change of plane. 3. This week reviews already-taught methods only. A portrait and a fifth tube remain reserved.

Exercises. 1. Model: the already-taught group, with an already-taught closer pass, not a new first-look name. 2. The already-taught group remains four paints.

Spanish · Visto and puesto, reviewed

Check. 1. He/has/ha/hemos/habéis/han visto, and he/has/ha/hemos/habéis/han puesto. 2. The already-taught irregular forms are visto, from ver, and puesto, from poner. 3. Vuelto, from volver.

Exercises. 1. Model: He visto el libro, and Ha puesto el libro en la mesa, each translated. 2. They are already-taught irregular participles of other verbs, not of ver or poner.

Seminar · Expected value, reviewed

Check. 1. E = (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6) / 6 = 21/6 = 7/2. 2. They are classroom figures, not official measurements. 3. This week reviews already-taught methods only.

Exercises. 1. E = 2, because every invented payoff is 2. It is not the already-taught 7/2 model. 2. Expected value is a method, not a prize or a score.
