Elective · Anatomy: the skull, from Gray 1918 — cranium and face

Check. 1. Twenty-two: eight in the cranium, which lodges and protects the brain, and fourteen in the skeleton of the face. 2. Occipital, two parietals, frontal, two temporals, sphenoidal, and ethmoidal. 3. The mandible. Gray mentions the hyoid in this section, but it is not one of the twenty-two skull bones in the count he has just given. 4. Model: the skull, cranium and face (Gray). Own words should match this idea: Gray, 1918, writes: “The skull is supported on the summit of the vertebral column, and is of an oval shape, wider behind than in front. It is composed of a series of flattened or irregular bones which, with one exception (the mandible), are immovably jointed together. It is divisible into two parts: (1) the cranium, which lodges and protects the brain, consists of eight bones, and (2) the skeleton of the face, of fourteen.” The eight cranial bones he then names are the occipital, the two parietals, the frontal, the two temporals, the sphenoidal, and the ethmoidal. Near-miss: Not a first look at last week’s eight carpals, or at the earlier carpus as a group, though those lists may be contrasted as already-taught bone names in the hand. Not a Basle regrouping of some nasal bones as cranial, which Gray notes and which is a gloss only. Not a required recitation of every facial name on this first day, beyond the count of fourteen and the mandible as the movable exception; the fourteen as Gray lists them may be read from the table. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Two nasals, two maxillae, two lacrimals, two zygomatics, two palatines, two inferior nasal conchae, vomer, and mandible. Today’s required work is the division, the eight cranial names, and the mandible as the exception; the fourteen are to be recognized from Gray’s list.
  2. Last week named eight carpals in the wrist, from Gray’s hand chapter. Today is a different region, the skull, from Gray’s skull chapter. The earlier list is a contrast, not a new first look.
  3. In the Basle nomenclature, certain bones of the nasal capsule are grouped as cranial, not facial. That is Gray’s report of another grouping, not today’s required list.
  4. Gray’s wording is a cranium of eight and a face of fourteen, twenty-two in all. This course does not invent a further bone to memorize today.
