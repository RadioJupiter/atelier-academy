Mathematics · Geometry XXII: the incenter, and A = r s

Check. 1. The incenter is equally far from the sides (center of the incircle). The circumcenter is equally far from the vertices (center of the circumcircle). 2. A = 24, s = 12, r = 2, and A = r s. 3. Model: incenter. Own words should match this idea: The center of a triangle’s incircle: the point where the angle bisectors meet, equally far from all three sides. That common perpendicular distance is the inradius r. Near-miss: Not the circumcenter (the center of the circumcircle), which is equally far from the three vertices, not from the three sides. inradius. Own words should match this idea: The radius of the incircle: the perpendicular distance from the incenter to any side. In a triangle, A = r s, where A is the area and s is the semiperimeter (half the perimeter). Near-miss: Not the circumradius (the radius of the circumcircle). A = r s is for the incircle; it is not a new area formula for a rectangle. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. r = 18 / 9 = 2.
  2. s = 15, A = 30, r = 2.

Science · Refractive errors continued: astigmatism and presbyopia

Check. 1. Myopia: distant blur; hyperopia: near blur; astigmatism: far and near blur or distortion; presbyopia: close work hard for middle-aged and older adults. 2. Eyeglasses or contact lenses may be prescribed. The lesson still refuses surgery instruction, invented diopters, and a classroom diagnosis. 3. Model: astigmatism. Own words should match this idea: A refractive error that, in the NEI's wording, can make far-away and nearby objects look blurry or distorted. Near-miss: Not “just myopia plus hyperopia” as a slogan, and not a classroom diagnosis. The NEI wording is about blur or distortion of far-away and nearby objects. presbyopia. Own words should match this idea: A refractive error that, in the NEI's wording, makes it hard for middle-aged and older adults to see things up close. The same page says it is common in adults ages 45 and older, and lists aging of the lens among the causes of refractive errors. Near-miss: Not myopia, and not a claim that every older adult has the same sight. The lesson does not invent a mechanism beyond the NEI's “aging of the lens.” The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A complete Together answer should match this idea: Astigmatism: blur or distortion at far and near. Presbyopia: close work hard for middle-aged and older adults. They are different NEI types. The NEI lists aging of the lens as a cause; that may be named beside accommodation, without a new invented mechanism.
  2. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card; the near-miss should be the one the lesson named, or a close equivalent.
