Latin · Review of Capitula I–X: place phrases with in, ad, and ē

Check. 1. Puella in hortō est. 2. in hortum mixes the wrong form into a place-where sentence. Place-where is Puer in hortō est. If the idea is “he goes to the garden,” the sentence is Puer ad hortum it. 3. Model: place phrases (in, ad, ē). Own words should match this idea: Short preposition phrases you already have: in + the place-where form (in hortō, in the garden); ad + the toward form (ad hortum, to the garden); ē or ex + the place-from form (ē hortō, out of the garden). Near-miss: Not a new case name from a later chapter, and not a mixed-up pair (in hortō is not “to the garden”). Today reviews three place jobs already used in Capitula I–X. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Puer ad hortum it.
  2. Puer ē hortō it.

Mathematics · Geometry XXI: the incircle, from equal tangents

Check. 1. The incircle is tangent to the sides; the circumcircle passes through the vertices. They are different circles, except in special cases this lesson does not claim as a general rule. 2. x + y + z = 8; the lengths are 8 − 6 = 2, 8 − 6 = 2, and 8 − 4 = 4. Check: 2 + 2 = 4, 2 + 4 = 6, 4 + 2 = 6. 3. Model: incircle. Own words should match this idea: The unique circle that sits inside a triangle and is tangent to all three sides. The two tangent segments from any one vertex to the points of contact are equal, by the already-taught equal-tangents theorem. Near-miss: Not the circumcircle (already taught), which passes through the three vertices. The incircle touches the sides; it does not have to pass through the vertices. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. It is also 5, because the two tangent segments from the same point are equal (Day 19).
  2. x + y + z = 12; the lengths are 12 − 9 = 3, 12 − 8 = 4, and 12 − 7 = 5. Check: 4 + 5 = 9, 5 + 3 = 8, 3 + 4 = 7.

Science · Refractive errors: myopia and hyperopia

Check. 1. Eyeglasses or contact lenses. Surgery is listed by the NEI but is not taught here, and no diopter number is invented. 2. A camera-like eye has to focus an image on the retina; a refractive error, in this account, is a failure of that focus because of the eye's shape. 3. Model: refractive error. Own words should match this idea: A vision problem in which the shape of the eye keeps light from focusing correctly on the retina, so it is hard to see clearly. The National Eye Institute (NEI) uses this definition, and names eyeglasses or contact lenses as a treatment a doctor can prescribe. Surgery is listed by the NEI but is not taught in this lesson. Near-miss: Not a classroom diagnosis of anyone in the room, and not an invented diopter number or a surgery lesson. The word refraction is used here as the NEI uses it (the bending of light as it is focused on the retina), not as a new unsourced experiment. myopia. Own words should match this idea: Nearsightedness: a refractive error that, in the NEI's wording, makes far-away objects look blurry. Near-miss: Not farsightedness, which the NEI describes as making nearby objects look blurry, and not a classroom test with invented distances. hyperopia. Own words should match this idea: Farsightedness: a refractive error that, in the NEI's wording, makes nearby objects look blurry. Near-miss: Not myopia, and not today's later types (astigmatism and presbyopia), which Thursday will take from the same page. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A complete Together answer should match this idea: Myopia: distant blur; hyperopia: near blur; the focus should be on the retina. Myopia, in the NEI wording, because the far-away sign is blurry. It is an illustration, not a diagnosis of a real person in the room.
  2. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card; the near-miss should be the one the lesson named, or a close equivalent.

Modern language · Spanish: me duele and me duelen

Check. 1. Me duelen los pies. 2. The body part is the subject: Me duele la cabeza. 3. Model: me duele and me duelen. Own words should match this idea: A present-tense pattern like gustar: the thing that hurts is the subject, and me marks the person affected. Me duele is used with a singular subject; me duelen with a plural subject. Near-miss: Not Yo duele la cabeza, which copies English and makes yo the subject of the wrong verb. The body part is the subject; the verb agrees with it. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A complete Together answer should match this idea: Me duele la cabeza. Me duelen los pies. ¿Te duele la cabeza?
  2. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card; the near-miss should be the one the lesson named, or a close equivalent.

Music theory · A leading tone in a minor key, and a major V

Check. 1. Tonic: A–C–E (minor). V with the leading tone: E–G♯–B (major). 2. The minor third (A to C) makes the tonic triad minor. The leading tone is a raised seventh (G♯ below A), used to make V major. They are different degrees, with different jobs. 3. Model: the leading tone in a minor key, a first look. Own words should match this idea: The seventh scale degree raised by a half step so that it lies a half step below the tonic, often used to build a major V chord in a minor key. This is a common practice, not a law that every minor melody must follow, and not a claim that natural minor (already taught) is being cancelled. Near-miss: Not a new minor scale taught as the only minor, and not a claim about every folk or later melody. Natural minor remains as taught; today is one common way of making a V chord in minor. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A complete Together answer should match this idea: Natural seventh G; leading tone G♯; V is E–G♯–B. Because natural minor remains taught, and some minor music keeps the natural seventh. Today is a common practice for a major V, not a law.
  2. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card; the near-miss should be the one the lesson named, or a close equivalent.
