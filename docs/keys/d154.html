Mathematics · Geometry XCIV: the area of a circular segment, a first look

Check. 1. The region between an arc and the chord that joins the arc’s ends. Its area is the sector area minus the area of the triangle formed by the two radii and that chord. 2. π − 2 square units. The figure is invented. 3. Dewing used “segment of a circle” for an architectural meeting of stone and a dome, already taught. Today’s segment is a measurable region of a disk, sector minus triangle. 4. Model: area of a circular segment. Own words should match this idea: A circular segment, in this lesson, is the region between an arc and the chord that joins the arc’s ends. Its area is the area of the corresponding sector minus the area of the triangle formed by the two radii and that chord. Near-miss: Not the sector itself, which still includes the triangular piece. Not Dewing’s already-taught “segment of a circle” under a dome, an architectural use of the word, not this area. Not a first look at a new degree formula. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Sector 6π, triangle 9√3, segment 6π − 9√3 square units. The figure is invented. 2. Sector: (1/2) × 16 × π = 8π. The triangle with included angle π is flat and has area (1/2) × 16 × sin π = 0, so the segment is 8π, which is half the disk 16π. The figure is invented. 3. The sector still includes the triangular piece between the two radii and the chord. The segment is what remains after that triangle is removed.

Science · Official Example 20.5, a headlight filament, from OpenStax 20.3

Check. 1. R = 0.350 Ω, L = 4.00 cm = 4.00 × 10⁻² m, ρ = 5.6 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m. The official area is 6.40 × 10⁻⁹ m². 2. D = 9.0 × 10⁻⁵ m, using the book’s 3.14. 3. Official household voltage remains reserved. Today is official Example 20.5, a filament diameter from resistivity. 4. Model: official Example 20.5, a headlight filament. Own words should match this idea: OpenStax, College Physics 2e, official Example 20.5, takes a tungsten car-headlight filament with a cold resistance of 0.350 Ω and a length of 4.00 cm, and asks for the diameter. The official resistivity from Table 20.1 is 5.6 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m. The book finds A = ρL / R = 6.40 × 10⁻⁹ m², and then, using 3.14 for π, D = 9.0 × 10⁻⁵ m. Near-miss: Not an invented filament, and not a replacement of the book’s 3.14 by a different approximation in this official example. Not official household voltage, which remains reserved. Not the temperature coefficient, which remains reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. A = (5.6 × 10⁻⁸)(4.00 × 10⁻²) / 0.350 = 6.40 × 10⁻⁹ m²; D = 2√(6.40 × 10⁻⁹ / 3.14) = 9.0 × 10⁻⁵ m. 2. The book’s official arithmetic uses 3.14 and quotes D to two digits. Replacing 3.14 would no longer be the book’s official working for this example. 3. Official household voltage, and the temperature coefficient of resistivity, remain reserved.

Literature · Pride and Prejudice, Chapter 7: the rain, and a walk of three miles

Check. 1. Mrs. Bennet says she had better go on horseback, “because it seems likely to rain; and then you must stay all night.” “Jane had not been gone long before it rained hard,” and she could not come back. 2. “The distance is nothing, when one has a motive; only three miles.” She arrives “with weary ancles, dirty stockings, and a face glowing with the warmth of exercise,” and “was shewn into the breakfast-parlour.” 3. They are 1813 spellings, and British or period spellings stay inside quotations. 4. Model: Jane’s ride in the rain, and Elizabeth’s three miles, in Chapter 7. Own words should match this idea: In the 1813 wording of Chapter 7, Jane asks, “Can I have the carriage,” and Mrs. Bennet answers, “No, my dear, you had better go on horseback, because it seems likely to rain; and then you must stay all night.” Jane is “obliged to go on horseback,” and “Jane had not been gone long before it rained hard.” The next morning Elizabeth walks to Netherfield: “The distance is nothing, when one has a motive; only three miles.” She arrives “with weary ancles, dirty stockings, and a face glowing with the warmth of exercise,” and “She was shewn into the breakfast-parlour.” “Ancles,” “shewn,” and “to-day,” if quoted from Miss Bingley’s earlier note, stay inside the 1813 quotations. Near-miss: Not an official modern measurement of the walk, and not an official medical claim about Jane’s cold. The apothecary’s later remarks, if named at all, stay the novel’s wording. Not Chapter 8, which remains reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. “That she should have walked three miles so early in the day, in such dirty weather, and by herself, was almost incredible to Mrs. Hurst and Miss Bingley.” 2. The three miles are the novel’s wording, not an official modern measurement added here. 3. Chapter 8 remains reserved.
