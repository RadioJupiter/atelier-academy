Latin · Deponent present, imperfect, and perfect, mixed

Check. Model: deponent tenses, mixed. Own words should match this idea: A mixed practice of the three deponent tenses already taught: present loquor, imperfect loquēbar, and perfect locūtus sum, still looking passive and translated as active, with the two already-taught absolutes kept distinct from the finite tenses. Near-miss: Not a first look at any of those forms. Not a new deponent verb beyond loquor and cōnor. The first persons of sequor and ēgredior remain reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Nōs loquēbāmur: we were speaking, or used to speak (imperfect). Vōs locūtī estis: you (plural) spoke, or have spoken (perfect). Agricolae loquuntur: the farmers speak, or are speaking (present). Puerō locūtō is an absolute, a loosely attached phrase with no sum. Puer locūtus est is a main-clause perfect.

Exercises. Model: Loquuntur, they speak. Locūtī sunt, they spoke or have spoken. A fair sentence: Puerō locūtō, nōs loquēbāmur — with the boy having spoken, we were speaking.

Mathematics · Geometry LXV: tangent of complementary angles

Check. Model: complementary tangents. Own words should match this idea: If A and B are the two acute angles of a right triangle, already known to add to 90°, then tan A = 1 / tan B, because the side opposite A is adjacent to B, and the side adjacent to A is opposite B. Equivalently, tan(90° − A) = 1 / tan A. This uses only the already-taught definition of tangent and the already-taught complementary pair. Degrees only. Near-miss: Not a first look at sin(90° − A) = cos A, already taught last week. Not a reciprocal name such as cotangent, which remains reserved. Degrees only. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Tan A = 3/4, tan(90° − A) = 4/3, and sin(90° − A) = 4/5, which is last week’s complementary-sine fact. Tan(90° − A) would be 3/4 only if the sides had not swapped; that value is tan A, not the complement.

Exercises. Tan A = 6/8 = 3/4, tan(90° − A) = 8/6 = 4/3. Tan(90° − E) = 12/5. No official degree measure was given, and inventing one would not be a calculation from the sides.

Physics · Transverse and longitudinal waves, from OpenStax 16.9

Check. Model: transverse and longitudinal waves. Own words should match this idea: In OpenStax 16.9, a transverse wave, or shear wave, has its disturbance perpendicular to the direction of propagation, as in a wave on a cord, a guitar string, or visible light. A longitudinal wave, or compressional wave, has its disturbance parallel to the direction of propagation, as in sound in air or water. Amplitude is independent of the wave speed vw. Water waves are a combination of the two; the simplified water wave used on Monday showed no longitudinal motion of the bird. Sound in solids can be both. Earthquake waves under the surface have a longitudinal or P-wave component and a transverse or S-wave component, which OpenStax notes propagate at different speeds; official measured speeds are not invented here. Near-miss: Not a first look at λ or vw, already taught on Monday. Not superposition or interference, which remain reserved. Official measured earthquake-wave speeds are not invented. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Transverse: disturbance perpendicular to propagation; a guitar string, or visible light. Longitudinal: disturbance parallel to propagation; sound in air or water. Water waves are a combination. Earthquake waves under the surface have P (longitudinal) and S (transverse) components that propagate at different speeds; those official speeds are not invented. Amplitude is independent of vw.

Exercises. vw = 2.0 × 4.0 = 8.0 m/s, and the wave is transverse. Amplitude is the size of the disturbance and, in OpenStax 16.9, is completely independent of vw.

Modern language · Spanish: era and fui, contrasted in use

Check. Model: era and fui, contrasted in use. Own words should match this idea: Ser already has two taught past forms that must not be treated as synonyms: fui, the completed preterite, and era, the imperfect. Ir shares fui with ser, but iba is not a form of ser. Today those already-taught forms are chosen in short invented sentences. Instruction remains in English. Near-miss: Not a first look at era or fui as forms. Not a claim that ir and ser share an imperfect. Not vía for veía. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Era: I was, or I used to be, or it was (description). Fui as ser: I was, completed. Fui as ir: I went, completed. Iba: I was going, or I used to go. Ir and ser share fui, not the imperfect. Cada mañana el cielo era claro (description, habit). Un día el cielo fue distinto (completed change).

Exercises. Model: El mercado era grande. The market was large. Ayer fui al mercado. Yesterday I went to the market. Cada mañana already marks a habit, so iba, not fui, is the tense that fits the going.
