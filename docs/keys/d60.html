Vocabulary review

Check. 1. puer is nominative (the one who gives); rosam is accusative (the thing given); puellae is dative (the person to whom). Term 1 stays in Capitula I–X; a new chapter is not begun early. 2. No real solution. A square is never negative; it is 0 only at x = 3, and positive elsewhere. 3. PT² = PA × PB, so 36 = 4 × PB, and PB = 9. 10 would be a sum (4 + 6), which is not the theorem. 4. A sound sentence: the letter can mention about the seventh hour because Firth’s wording says the mother pointed the cloud out then, but it is a later letter in a 1900 translation, so I will not treat the phrase as a stopwatch reading, and I will not invent Pompeii. 5. About 1.6 m/s², about one-sixth of 9.8, from the NASA fact-sheet family already used, with two significant figures respected. 6. Myopia: far-away objects look blurry. Hyperopia: nearby objects look blurry. Neither is a classroom diagnosis. 7. Firth: the cloud “more closely resembled a pine-tree than anything else, for what corresponded to the trunk was of great length and height, and then spread out into a number of branches.” The set extract does not name Pompeii, and it does not give a death toll. 8. The tool is a brush (P or Q; not P; therefore Q). Under this course’s default inclusive “or,” P does not give not-Q unless the problem says exactly one. 9. A half cadence pauses on V; it does not close. An authentic cadence moves V to I and can close. Still a major key, with I, IV, and V only, in this course’s school pattern. 10. The inward eye is a figure of later memory (“They flash upon that inward eye / Which is the bliss of solitude”). “Ten thousand saw I at a glance” is a figure in the poem, not a counted census, and not a biology term.
