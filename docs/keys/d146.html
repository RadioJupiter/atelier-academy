Latin · The future perfect of sequor, a first look

Check. 1. Secūtus erō, secūtus eris, secūtus erit, secūtī erimus, secūtī eritis, secūtī erunt. It marks a following that will already be completed at some later time. 2. She will have followed me. Secūta est is the perfect, she followed, or she has followed, already completed in the past, not the future perfect. 3. Ēgressus erō is the future perfect of ēgredior, a different verb, and waits until tomorrow. 4. Model: future perfect of sequor. Own words should match this idea: The future perfect of sequor is secūtus erō, secūtus eris, secūtus erit, secūtī erimus, secūtī eritis, secūtī erunt. It marks a following that will already be completed at some later time. It is not the future sequar, not the perfect secūtus sum, and not the pluperfect secūtus eram. Near-miss: Not sequar, I shall follow, which is still ahead and not yet completed. Not secūtus sum, I followed, or I have followed, which is already completed in the past. Not secūtus eram, I had followed, already completed before another past time. Not ēgressus erō, the future perfect of ēgredior, which waits until tomorrow. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. You (s.) will have followed; we shall have followed. Fair original sentences may use Puer or Puella, with he or she in the gloss. 2. I follow; I shall follow; I followed, or I have followed; I had followed; I shall have followed. 3. The future perfect of ēgredior waits until tomorrow.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXXVII: the ambiguous case (SSA), a first look

Check. 1. SSA is two sides and a non-included angle, the angle opposite one of those sides. When A is acute, h = b sin A. 2. h = 1. If a = 1/2, there is no triangle. If a = 1, there is one right triangle. 3. The comparisons are being done in degrees, as set. 30° = π/6 may be recalled, but the height uses sin 30° = 1/2, an exact already-taught value. 4. Model: the ambiguous case (SSA). Own words should match this idea: The ambiguous case is the SSA arrangement: two sides and a non-included angle, the angle opposite one of those sides. When the given angle A is acute, the height h = b sin A is compared with the opposite side a. If a < h, there is no triangle; if a = h, there is one right triangle; if h < a < b, there are two triangles; if a ≥ b, there is one triangle. The figures in this week’s checks are invented. Near-miss: Not SAS, two sides and the included angle, already handled by the law of cosines. Not ASA or AAS, already handled by the law of sines with one triangle. Not an official measured triangle. Not the obtuse-A case, which remains reserved until later in the week if treated at all, and is not today’s first look. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. No. 0.9 < 1, so a < h, and the side does not reach. 2. SAS gives two sides and the included angle, which determines one triangle by the law of cosines. SSA does not always determine one triangle. 3. The two-triangle case, the case a ≥ b, and an obtuse given angle remain reserved until later in the week.

Writing · A confirmation, a first look, after last week’s refutation

Check. 1. A confirmation answers a claim and shows why it holds. A refutation shows why a claim does not hold. 2. “If two sides of a triangle are equal, the angles opposite those sides are equal.” If two sides are equal, the triangle is isosceles, and the already-taught base angles, opposite those sides, are equal. 3. The isosceles fact has already been taught. Today’s job is the confirmation shape, not a new theorem. No living person is used as an example or as an authority. 4. Model: confirmation (progymnasmata). Own words should match this idea: A confirmation, in this course, answers a claim and shows why it holds. It is the counterpart of last week’s refutation, which showed why a claim did not hold. The set invented claim, labeled invented as a claim to be confirmed, is already-taught geometry: “If two sides of a triangle are equal, the angles opposite those sides are equal.” A confirmation is not a maxim, not a chreia, and not a refutation. Near-miss: Not a refutation, which shows why a claim does not hold. Not a maxim, a short general precept. Not a chreia, which needs a person, an occasion, and a saying or act. Not a first look at the isosceles theorem, which has already been taught. Not a living person. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. A maxim is a short general precept, not a reasoned defense of a claim. A chreia needs a person, an occasion, and a saying or act. 2. A refutation would try to show that the claim does not hold. The claim does hold, from already-taught geometry, so a refutation of it would be a miss for this set claim. 3. A longer later elaboration remains reserved. No official Aphthonius or Heath wording is claimed.

Physics · Resonance in a closed air column, a first look, from OpenStax 17.5

Check. 1. A node at the closed end, an antinode at the open end, and λ = 4L for the fundamental. 2. f_n = n v_w / (4L), with n = 1, 3, 5, …. The fundamental is n = 1, f_1 = v_w / (4L). The first overtone is n = 3. 3. The book does not give an official classroom length here, and none is invented. Official Example 17.5 waits until Wednesday; open tubes wait until Friday. 4. Model: resonance in a closed air column. Own words should match this idea: In OpenStax, College Physics 2e, 17.5, a tube closed at one end has a node of air displacement at the closed end and an antinode at the open end. The fundamental standing wave has λ = 4L, and the resonant frequencies are f_n = n v_w / (4L), with n = 1, 3, 5, …. The lowest resonant frequency is the fundamental; higher resonant frequencies are overtones. Official instrument lengths are not invented. Near-miss: Not a tube open at both ends, whose formula waits until Friday. Not last week’s Doppler effect. Not an official length of a classroom tube or of a named instrument. Not official Example 17.5, reserved until Wednesday. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. 3 f_1, because n = 3 gives three times the fundamental. 2. Because the resonant frequencies depend on v_w, and v_w depends on temperature, as the book notes. 3. Official Example 17.5, open tubes, official instrument lengths, and later optics remain reserved until taught.

History · The first-voyage letter: one hundred and seven leagues along Juana, in Major’s wording

Check. 1. “One hundred and seven leagues in following the sea-coast of Juana in a straight line from west to east.” 2. “The island is larger than England and Scotland together,” a comparison from that survey, not an official modern area, and no official square miles are invented. 3. Two unvisited provinces to the west, “one of which is called Avan,” “cannot be less in length than from fifty to sixty leagues.” Those are the letter’s wording, not official identifications, official lengths, or conversions.

Exercises. 1. The 107 leagues are Juana, west to east. The 178 leagues were the north coast of Española, a different figure in a different sentence. 2. Do not convert the leagues, and do not invent official modern areas or identifications. 3. Española compared with Spain, and a letter figure of 188 leagues on one of its sides, wait until Thursday.

Modern language · Spanish: the present subjunctive of haber, a first look

Check. 1. Haya, hayas, haya, hayamos, hayáis, hayan. 2. I hope that there is silence, or I hope that there be silence. Hay is the present indicative, not the subjunctive haya after “espero que.” 3. Sea is the subjunctive of ser. For a “that there be” frame with haber, the form is haya. 4. Model: present subjunctive of haber. Own words should match this idea: The present subjunctive of haber, in the impersonal use already begun from hay, is irregular: haya, hayas, haya, hayamos, hayáis, hayan. In this week’s invented classroom frames it often means that there be, or that there should be. It is not hay, the present indicative, not the present subjunctive sea, and not vaya or esté. Near-miss: Not hay, there is, or there are, the present indicative. Not sea, the present subjunctive of ser. Not vaya or esté. Not a future or a conditional of haber, not yet taught. Not an official quotation. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Fair invented sentences: “Espero que haya tiempo.” “Quiere que haya silencio.” 2. Hay, there is, or there are; haya, that there be. 3. Haber as a helping verb, and other irregular subjunctives not yet taught, remain reserved.

Seminar · Logic: conditional probability, P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B), a first look

Check. 1. P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B), when P(B) is not 0. It is the probability of A, once B is known to have happened. 2. Even and greater than 3: 4 and 6, so 2/6. Greater than 3: 4, 5, and 6, so 3/6. P = 2/3. 3. Last week’s rule answers “or.” Today’s rule answers “given.” The questions are different, even when the numbers happen to match on this invented cube. 4. Model: conditional probability. Own words should match this idea: The conditional probability of A given B, written P(A|B), is P(A and B) / P(B), provided P(B) is not 0. It is the probability of A, once B is known to have happened. It is not last week’s addition rule for “or,” and not an official probability of a real event. Near-miss: Not P(A or B), last week’s addition rule. Not P(A and B) left un-divided, which would be 2/6, not the conditional. Not an official probability of a real event. Not a claim that every pair of events is independent. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Greater than 3: 4, 5, 6. Odd among them: 5 only. P = (1/6) / (3/6) = 1/3. 2. 2/6 is the chance that both happen, out of all six faces, not the chance of even once the roll is already known to be greater than 3. 3. Do not invent an official probability of a real event.
