Elective · Anatomy: the eight carpal bones, from Gray 1918

Check. 1. Eight, in two rows. 2. Navicular, lunate, triangular, and pisiform. 3. Greater multangular, lesser multangular, capitate, and hamate. 4. Model: the eight carpal bones. Own words should match this idea: Gray, 1918, names eight carpal bones in two rows. “Those of the proximal row, from the radial to the ulnar side, are named the navicular, lunate, triangular, and pisiform; those of the distal row, in the same order, are named the greater multangular, lesser multangular, capitate, and hamate.” Near-miss: Not a first look at the carpus as a group, already taught on Day 75 with the metacarpals and the phalanges. Not last week’s seven tarsal names as a first look, though the two lists may be contrasted. Not a later set of names (scaphoid, triquetrum, trapezium, trapezoid) required as today’s list; those may be heard as a gloss only. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Scaphoid, triquetrum, trapezium, trapezoid, as a gloss only. Today’s set wording is Gray 1918, as last week used Gray’s tarsal wording.
  2. Day 75 already taught the carpus as a group between the forearm and the metacarpals, and reserved the eight names. Today is those reserved names.
  3. Gray names seven tarsals in the foot and eight carpals in the wrist. The two lists belong to two different regions already placed. Last week’s seven names are a contrast, not a new first look.
  4. Gray’s wording is “eight in number.” This course does not invent a further bone to memorize today.
