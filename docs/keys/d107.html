Science · Three methods of heat transfer, a first look

Check. 1. Whenever there is a temperature difference. By only three methods: conduction, convection, and radiation. 2. Heat transfer through stationary matter by physical contact. Heat transferred between an electric burner and the bottom of a pan. 3. Heat transfer by the macroscopic movement of a fluid. A forced-air furnace, or weather systems. 4. Heat transfer by electromagnetic radiation emitted or absorbed, such as the warming of the Earth by the Sun. Quantitative rates belong to later OpenStax modules and are not given here as classroom official figures. 5. Model: conduction, convection, and radiation. Own words should match this idea: OpenStax 14.4 teaches that whenever there is a temperature difference, heat transfer occurs, and that every such process takes place by only three methods. Conduction is heat transfer through stationary matter by physical contact; OpenStax’s example is heat transferred between an electric burner and the bottom of a pan. Convection is heat transfer by the macroscopic movement of a fluid; OpenStax’s examples include a forced-air furnace and weather systems. Heat transfer by radiation occurs when microwaves, infrared radiation, visible light, or another form of electromagnetic radiation is emitted or absorbed; OpenStax’s obvious example is the warming of the Earth by the Sun. Quantitative rates, including a conduction formula, remain reserved in the following OpenStax modules. Near-miss: Not a first look at last week’s latent heat, or at Lv of water, already taught. Not a first look at temperature itself. Not a quantitative conduction, convection, or radiation formula, which 14.4 names as later modules. OpenStax’s brief mention of thermal radiation from the human body is not used as a measured official rate, and is not a claim about a living person in this course. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Latent heat and Lv were taught last week. Today is how heat is transferred, from 14.4.
  2. Conduction: heat travels through the stationary metal by contact.
  3. There is no stationary matter in contact between the Sun and the Earth in the sense of conduction; the transfer is by radiation.
  4. Section 14.4 does not give those official rates for these examples; they would be invented.
