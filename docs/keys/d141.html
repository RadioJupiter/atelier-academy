Latin · The perfect of sequor, a first look

Check. 1. The perfect participle secūtus plus the present of sum: secūtus sum, secūtus es, secūtus est, secūtī sumus, secūtī estis, secūtī sunt. 2. Puella mē secūta est: she followed me, or she has followed me. We have followed him, or we followed him. 3. Locūtus sum is the perfect of loquor, I spoke, or I have spoken. The perfect of ēgredior, ēgressus sum, waits until tomorrow. 4. Model: perfect of sequor. Own words should match this idea: The perfect of sequor uses the already-taught deponent perfect pattern: the perfect participle plus the present of sum. The participle is secūtus. The six forms are secūtus sum, secūtus es, secūtus est, secūtī sumus, secūtī estis, secūtī sunt: I followed, or I have followed, and so on. It is not the present sequor, not the future sequar, and not the pluperfect secūtus eram. Near-miss: Not sequor, I follow. Not sequar, I shall follow. Not secūtus eram, I had followed. Not locūtus sum, I spoke, the perfect of loquor. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. You (s.) followed, or have followed; they followed, or have followed. 2. Secūtus sum; secūtī sunt. 3. The grammar is taught in original sentences. A textbook narrative is not being copied here.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXXIII: the radian, a first look

Check. 1. A radian is the central angle whose arc equals the radius. The whole circumference is 2π r, so the whole central angle is 2π radians. 2. 60° = π/3; 45° = π/4; 90° = π/2. 3. π/3 = 60°; π/2 = 90°; 2π = 360°. 4. Model: the radian. Own words should match this idea: A radian is a measure of a central angle: the angle for which the arc it cuts off has the same length as the radius. A full turn is 2π radians, which is the same turn as 360°, so π radians = 180°, π/2 radians = 90°, π/3 = 60°, π/4 = 45°, and π/6 = 30°. It is not a degree, and it is not an official measured length of a real wheel. Near-miss: Not a degree. Not an approximate stand-in that replaces π with 3.14 in exact work. Not a claim that a classroom wheel has been officially measured in radians. Not the ambiguous case, which remains reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. 180° = π; 30° = π/6. π radians = 180°; π/6 radians = 30°. 2. π radians is exactly 180°. A rounded 3.14 is not the exact conversion, and exact work keeps π. 3. The ambiguous case remains reserved. Official measured angles on a real wheel are not invented.

Writing · A refutation, a first look, after last week’s maxim

Check. 1. A refutation examines a claim and gives reasons why it should not be accepted. The set claim, invented, is “A story is true because it has been told many times.” 2. A chreia needs a person, an occasion, and a saying or act. A narrative tells a complete event in order. Confirmation remains reserved. 4. Model: refutation (progymnasmata). Not a narrative of a complete event in order. Not a confirmation, which remains reserved. Not a living person’s argument, and not a copy of a modern handbook. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Physics · The Doppler effect, a first look, from OpenStax 17.4

Check. 1. An alteration in the observed frequency of a sound due to motion of either the source or the observer. The actual change in frequency due to that relative motion is the Doppler shift. 2. f_obs = f_s × v_w / (v_w ± v_s). The minus sign is for motion toward the observer. 3. f_obs = f_s × (v_w ± v_obs) / v_w. The plus sign is for motion toward the source. 4. Model: the Doppler effect. Own words should match this idea: The Doppler effect, in OpenStax 17.4, is an alteration in the observed frequency of a sound due to motion of either the source or the observer. The actual change in frequency due to that relative motion is called a Doppler shift. Relative motion of source and observer toward one another increases the received frequency; relative motion apart decreases it. Near-miss: Not a change in the speed of sound in the medium, which remains v_w whether the source is moving or not. Not last week’s decibel, which is a level of intensity, not a shift of frequency. Not a sonic boom, which waits. Not an official measured frequency of a real train not in the book’s example. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. The wavelength is shorter ahead of the source, and the observed frequency is higher. 2. Official figures in this lesson come from OpenStax 17.4. A street vehicle not in the book has no official classroom frequency invented for it. 3. Official Example 17.4 waits until Wednesday. Sonic booms wait until Friday.

Seminar · Logic: the addition rule for overlapping events, a first look

Check. 1. P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B). The last term is subtracted so outcomes in both A and B are not counted twice. 2. Even: 2, 4, 6. Greater than 3: 4, 5, 6. Both: 4, 6. So 3/6 + 3/6 − 2/6 = 2/3, the faces 2, 4, 5, and 6. 3. When the events are mutually exclusive, the overlap is 0. The product is last week’s rule for “and” when events are independent, and it is not automatically P(A and B) in today’s “or” calculation. 4. Model: the addition rule for overlapping events. Own words should match this idea: When two events can both happen, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B). The last term is subtracted so the overlap is not counted twice. This is not last week’s product rule for independent events, and it is not the earlier addition rule that was used only when events were mutually exclusive. Near-miss: Not P(A) + P(B) with the overlap left in, which would give 1 on this invented cube, a miss already noted in an earlier week. Not P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B) used in place of today’s subtraction, unless independence has been separately established and the question is “and.” Not an official probability of a real event. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Odd: 1, 3, 5, so 3/6. Greater than 3: 4, 5, 6, so 3/6. Both: 5 only, so 1/6. P = 3/6 + 3/6 − 1/6 = 5/6, the faces 1, 3, 4, 5, and 6. 2. 4 and 5 cannot both happen on one roll, so they are mutually exclusive and P(4 and 5) = 0. 3. Do not invent an official probability of a real event.
