Latin · Capitulum XVI: deponent verbs, a first look

Check. 1. A verb whose forms look passive but are translated as active. 2. The boys speak. The pupil tries to recite. 3. Loquitur is deponent: it looks passive, but the meaning is active, “he or she speaks.” 4. Model: deponent verb. Own words should match this idea: A deponent verb has forms that look passive but are translated as active. In this first look, the course uses the third person and the present infinitive only: loquitur, he or she speaks; loquuntur, they speak; loquī, to speak. Near-miss: Not a first look at ordinary active present endings, closed last week, and not a first look at a true passive meaning. A deponent is not “passive in sense.” Do not invent first- and second-person deponent forms today; they are reserved. loquitur and cōnātur. Own words should match this idea: Loquitur means he or she speaks; cōnātur means he or she tries. Both look as if they were passive, but they are deponent and are translated as active. The infinitives are loquī, to speak, and cōnārī, to try. Near-miss: Not recitat used as a first look (the ordinary active was already taught). Not a passive sentence such as “he is spoken.” The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Loquitur, he or she speaks; loquuntur, they speak; loquī, to speak.
  2. A complete sentence such as Puerī recitāre cōnantur.
  3. Cōnātur: tries to. Potest: is able to. Licet (with a dative): is allowed to. Ability, permission, and trying are not the same.
  4. They have not been taught yet and are reserved. Today’s first look is the third person and the infinitive only.

Mathematics · Geometry XLIV: the volume of a right cylinder

Check. 1. V = π × 25 × 2 = 50π cubic units. 2. 36π = π × 9 × h, so h = 4. 3. 2πr is the circumference, a length, not the base area. The base area is πr². Volume uses that area, times h. 4. Model: volume of a right cylinder. Own words should match this idea: A right cylinder has two parallel circular bases and a height perpendicular to those bases. Its volume is V = B × h, with B the area of a circle, already taught as πr². So V = πr²h, in cubic units. Near-miss: Not last week’s circle area taken as a volume, and not yesterday’s triangular prism. Not a first look at π or at A = πr². Not the volume of a sphere, which is reserved. Not a drawing of a cylinder in perspective, already used in the atelier. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. r = 5, so V = π × 25 × 7 = 175π cubic units.
  2. V = π × 36 × 4 = 144π cubic units. A decimal estimate is 144 × 3.14 = 452.16, using π ≈ 3.14, labeled as an approximation.
  3. V is doubled, because V is proportional to h when r is fixed.
  4. The base is a ring of area π(25 − 9) = 16π; times height 4, the volume is 64π cubic units. Invented tube, not a measured pipe.

Physics · From elastic potential energy to kinetic energy

Check. 1. ½ × 0.00200 × v² = 0.563, v² = 563, v = √563 ≈ 23.7 m/s, as OpenStax reports. 2. ½ × 0.020 × v² = 0.10, so v² = 10, v = √10 m/s, or about 3.16 m/s in a labeled school rounding. Invented figures. 3. The joule is a kg·m²/s². Leaving the mass as 2.00 would treat it as 2.00 kg, a thousand times too large, and the speed would be wrong. 4. Model: conversion of elastic potential energy. Own words should match this idea: If friction and other non-conservative losses are neglected, the elastic potential energy stored in a Hooke’s-law spring can be converted into kinetic energy: ½kx² = ½mv². This is the already-taught conservation of mechanical energy, applied to PE_el. Near-miss: Not a first look at kinetic energy, work–energy, or conservation of mechanical energy (Days 21–28), and not a first look at PE_el = ½kx², taught Monday. Not a claim that friction is always negligible, and not last week’s period formula used as a speed. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Some of the stored energy would then go into work against friction or other losses, so less would remain as ½mv². OpenStax’s 23.7 m/s is the no-friction sourced figure.
  2. PE_el = ½mv² = ½ × 0.10 × 16 = 0.80 J. Then ½ × 200 × x² = 0.80, so 100 x² = 0.80, x² = 0.008, |x| = √0.008 m = about 0.089 m, labeled invented, with a school rounding.
  3. T is a period, a time, from OpenStax 16.3. It is not a speed, and it is not today’s conversion.
  4. Kinetic energy ½mv² was already taught in Days 21–28. Today applies it to the spring’s stored energy.
