Latin · The present subjunctive of sequor, a first look

Check. 1. Sequar, sequāris, sequātur, sequāmur, sequāminī, sequantur. Sequar is spelled the same as the future first-person singular. 2. He follows her, in the present subjunctive. Sequētur is the future, he will follow, or she will follow, and has ē, not ā. 3. A purpose clause with ut has not been taught in this first look; today’s job is the forms, contrasted with the future. 4. Model: present subjunctive of sequor. Own words should match this idea: The present subjunctive of sequor is sequar, sequāris, sequātur, sequāmur, sequāminī, sequantur. The first-person singular sequar is spelled the same as the already-taught future sequar; the other persons are distinct (sequātur, not sequētur). The forms are taught here by contrast with the future, not yet as a purpose clause. Near-miss: Not the future sequētur, he will follow, or she will follow, which has ē in the ending. Not secūtus sit, a later perfect subjunctive, which remains reserved. Not the present indicative sequitur. A purpose clause with ut remains reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. You (s.) follow; we follow, both in the present subjunctive. Fair original sentences may use Puer or Puella, with he or she in the gloss. 2. He follows, or she follows, the present indicative; he will follow, or she will follow, the future; he follows, or she follows, the present subjunctive. 3. The present subjunctive of ēgredior waits until tomorrow.

Mathematics · Geometry XCI: the area of a sector in radians, a first look

Check. 1. A = (1/2)r²θ, with θ in radians. Because θ_rad = θ° × π/180, (θ°/360) × πr² = (1/2)r²θ. 2. 6π square units, either way. The two formulas agree on the invented figure. 3. A circular segment is the region between an arc and its chord, and waits until Thursday. Today is the sector, two radii and the included arc. 4. Model: area of a sector in radians. Own words should match this idea: The area of a sector of a circle of radius r, with central angle θ measured in radians, is A = (1/2)r²θ. This is the same area already taught in degrees as A = (θ°/360) × πr². Because a radian measure satisfies θ = s/r, the same area can also be written A = (1/2)r s, where s is the arc length. Near-miss: Not (θ°/360) × πr² applied to a θ that is already in radians, which would treat π/3 as if it were 60 and shrink the area. Not the already-taught arc length s = rθ in place of the area. Not the area of the whole disk, πr², unless θ is 2π. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. A = (1/2) × 16 × (π/2) = 4π. In degrees, (90/360) × π × 16 = 4π, the same area. The figure is invented. 2. A = (1/2) × 4 × π = 2π. That is half the disk, since the full disk is πr² = 4π. The figure is invented. 3. s = rθ is the already-taught arc length, not the area. The area uses that arc as A = (1/2)r s, or A = (1/2)r²θ.

Spanish · The present perfect with haber, a first look

Check. 1. He, has, ha, hemos, habéis, han, plus a regular participle such as hablado, comido, or vivido. 2. We have spoken. Hay hablado mixes “there is” with a participle and is not the present perfect; the helping form needed is hemos, or ha, or another of the six, not hay. 3. Dicho is an irregular participle and remains reserved. Today’s participles are regular: hablado, comido, vivido. 4. Model: present perfect with haber, a first look. Own words should match this idea: The present perfect, in this first look, uses haber as a helping verb with a past participle: he, has, ha, hemos, habéis, han, plus a regular participle such as hablado, comido, or vivido. He hablado is I have spoken. This is not hay, there is, or there are; not haya, the already-taught present subjunctive of haber, “that there be”; and not a first look at an irregular participle such as dicho or hecho, which remains reserved. Near-miss: Not hay, there is, or there are, already taught. Not haya, “that there be.” Not the already-taught future or conditional. Not an irregular participle, which remains reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. You (s.) have lived; they have eaten. Fair original sentences may use a simple subject and a regular participle. 2. There is, or there are; that there be; he has, or she has, as a helping verb before a participle. 3. More mixed practice with hay and haya waits until Wednesday. Seminar · Logic: independent events on invented figures, a first look

Check. 1. The occurrence of one does not change the probability of the other. If they are independent, P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). 2. 1/12. The figures are invented. 3. Conditional probability is P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B), a different job, already taught on one invented cube. 4. Model: independent events (invented figures). Own words should match this idea: Two events are independent, in this first look, when the occurrence of one does not change the probability of the other. For independent events A and B, P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). The figures used here are invented and labeled invented. This is not last week’s conditional probability, and it is not an official probability of a real event. Near-miss: Not last week’s conditional probability P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B), a different job. Not a claim that two events are independent just because they cannot both happen; that would be a confusion with mutually exclusive events, which remain reserved as a first look. Not an official probability of a real event. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.
