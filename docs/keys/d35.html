Mathematics · Friday mix: functions, graphs, and a locus

Check. 1. g(0) = −4; vertex (0, −4). 2. The circle with center Q and radius 5 cm. 3. Model: Friday mix: functions and loci. Own words should match this idea: A Friday mix, not a new topic: evaluate f(x), read a linear or quadratic graph, and name one of the two loci taught yesterday. Near-miss: Not a new kind of function, and not a third locus presented as if it had been taught. Do not mix the perpendicular bisector with the circle on diameter AB. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. For example h(0) = 4, h(2) = 2; m = −1, b = 4.
  2. (−2, 0) and (2, 0), symmetric about the axis x = 0 through the vertex (0, −4).
  3. On the perpendicular bisector of AB (the crossings of the two circles of radius 3 cm about A and about B lie on that bisector).
  4. That circle is a different locus (right angle in a semicircle). The bisector is equidistance from A and B.
  5. A fair summary: a function’s graph is the set of points (x, f(x)); a locus is a set of points named by a condition, such as a circle or a perpendicular bisector.

Vocabulary review

Check. 1. Power is work divided by time, P = W / t. A watt is one joule per second, the SI unit of power, named after James Watt. 2. Torque is force times the perpendicular distance to the line of the force, τ = F × d⊥, in newton-meters. The joule is reserved for work or energy, even though the dimensions match. 3. Actium is 31 BCE; 27 BCE is the conventional start of the principate. Republican offices still had names and some business; no single fall day is taught as a fact. 4. A first-person record of deeds (known from later copies) and advocacy. It cannot, by itself, give a critic’s account of the same years. 5. Antigone and Ismene, after a war in which their brothers have died; an edict forbids burial of one brother. Thursday still refused the end of the play (and a verdict that depends on later scenes). 6. All M are P; All S are M; therefore All S are P. The middle term M appears in both premises and not in the conclusion, and links S to P. 7. 1–3 is a major third; 1–5 is a perfect fifth. Count letter names inclusively (C, D, E is a third; C, D, E, F, G is a fifth). 8. Humerus (upper arm); radius (thumb side of the forearm, palm forward); ulna (little-finger side). The anatomical radius is a bone; the mathematical radius is a length from a center.
