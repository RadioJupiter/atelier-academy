Mathematics · Friday mix: similar triangles

Check. 1. Yes. 9/6 = 12/8 = 13.5/9 = 3/2. The scale factor from the first to the second is 3/2. 2. 10/4 = 15/YZ, so YZ = 6. 3. Yes. In △ABC, ∠C = 70°. In △DEF, ∠F = 55°. Both are 55-55-70. They are similar (isosceles in the same angle pattern). 4. 4/5 = h/15, so h = 12 m.

  1. Two equal pairs of angles force the third pair, by the angle sum, so all corresponding angles are equal and the triangles are similar. The sides will then be proportional; you do not have to measure them first in order to know the triangles are similar.
  2. Not similar. 6/3 = 2, 10/5 = 2, but 16/7 is not 2. The sides are not proportional.
  3. DF corresponds to AC, so DF = 6 × 5/2 = 15.

Writing & Rhetoric · Friday mix: claim, grounds, and warrant

Check. 1. Grounds are the supporting material under a claim (a fact, a detail, or a short accurate quotation). Day 8 called that job evidence. 2. “Four score and seven years ago our fathers.” 3. A model warrant: a civil war of this kind tests whether a nation so conceived and so dedicated can endure. A near-miss: “therefore the war is such a test,” labeled as a restated claim. 4. Because only the two set sentences are assigned. Grounds taken from memory of later lines would not be a quotation from the set extract.

  1. A model: claim, the pencil point is broken; grounds, the lead is snapped and a short piece lies on the desk; warrant, a broken point leaves a snapped lead and a piece on the desk; near-miss, “so the point is broken.” Other accurate invented plans are accepted.
  2. Usually no: the set extract supplies grounds (and the claim being analyzed), not a general joining rule. A quotation of the claim would be a restated claim, not a warrant.
  3. Ask whether it is a general rule someone could dispute, or only the claim with “therefore” or “so” in front of it.

Physics · Friday mix: torque, rotational equilibrium, and a lever

Check. 1. τ = 12 N × 0.25 m = 3.0 N·m. The unit is the newton-meter, named as a torque. 2. The right-hand force is 16 N downward (equal arms). The support supplies 32 N upward. 3. Because that force’s perpendicular distance from the fulcrum is zero, so its torque about the fulcrum is zero. It still matters for ΣF = 0. 4. That is the already-taught market meaning: a price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. Today’s word is rotational equilibrium of a rigid body, Στ = 0.

  1. 36 N × 0.25 m = E × 0.75 m, so E = 12 N downward. Support: 36 N + 12 N = 48 N upward.
  2. Yes, Στ = 0 about the support (equal opposite torques of 5.0 N·m). No, ΣF is not zero without a support: the two downward forces add to 20 N, so a 20 N upward support is needed for rest.
  3. 4 N·m, named as a torque. A joule is the unit of work or energy, not of a turning effect, even though both involve a force and a distance.

Elective · Anatomy: the leg — femur, tibia, and fibula

Check. 1. The thigh bone, the single long bone of the upper leg, between the hip and the knee. 2. The tibia is the inner, usually larger bone (the shinbone). The fibula is the thinner, outer bone. Both run in the lower leg, between the knee and the ankle. 3. The thigh does have one long bone, the femur; the lower leg has two, the tibia and the fibula. The two segments are not built the same way. 4. It records a comparison with a clasp, the fibula as the pin beside the tibia. It does not require every drawing of the leg to look like a brooch. 5. Model: femur. Own words should match this idea: The thigh bone: the long bone of the upper leg, between the hip and the knee. It is the single bone of the thigh. Near-miss: Not the tibia or the fibula, which are the two bones of the lower leg, below the knee. Not a muscle, and not a joint. tibia. Own words should match this idea: The shinbone: the inner (medial) and usually the larger of the two long bones of the lower leg, between the knee and the ankle. Near-miss: Not the femur (the thigh bone, above the knee), and not the fibula (the thinner, outer bone of the same lower-leg pair). fibula. Own words should match this idea: The thinner, outer (lateral) of the two long bones of the lower leg, beside the tibia, between the knee and the ankle. The name comes from a clasp: the two bones together were compared to a brooch, with the fibula as the pin. Near-miss: Not the tibia, and not the femur. A common mix-up is to treat the lower leg as a single bone, as the thigh is; the lower leg has two. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. The tibia (shinbone). The fibula lies beside it, on the outer side.
  2. Femur; then tibia (inner), fibula (outer).
  3. Because this lesson has not sourced a muscle list, and invented origins would be presented as facts they are not. The job today is to distinguish the three long bones.

Vocabulary review

Check. 1. Grounds are the supporting material under a claim (a fact, a detail, or a short accurate quotation). Day 8 called that job evidence. 2. No M are P; All S are M; therefore No S are P. Major term P, minor term S, middle term M. A model example: No metals are gases; all irons are metals; therefore no irons are gases (P gases, S irons, M metals). 3. Nominative and accusative are the same in the singular, and both end in -a in the plural (corpus / corpora; caput / capita). AcI, after vidēre, dīcere, or putāre, gives an accusative subject and an infinitive. posse + infinitive has no second person in the accusative; the same subject is able to do the infinitive. 4. Στ = 0 about a chosen point: clockwise and counterclockwise torques balance. (A body at rest also has ΣF = 0.) A joule is the unit of work or energy; torque is a turning effect, written in N·m and named as a torque. 5. Longfellow: “Midway upon the journey of our life.” Bliss: “Four score and seven years ago our fathers.” 6. hablé; hablaron. Ayer yo hablé. (or: Ayer hablé.) 7. Triad inversion changes which of a triad’s three tones is in the bass (root position, first inversion, second inversion). Interval inversion, already taught, turns a single two-note interval by an octave. 8. Femur: the thigh bone (hip to knee). Tibia: the larger, inner bone of the lower leg (the shinbone). Fibula: the thinner, outer bone of the lower leg.
