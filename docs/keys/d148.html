Latin · Secūtus erō and ēgressus erō, mixed with already-taught deponent tenses

Check. 1. Secūtus erō, I shall have followed; ēgressus erō, I shall have gone out. 2. She will have gone out. He will have followed her. 3. Secūtus erat is the pluperfect, he had followed, already completed before another past time, not a completion still ahead.

Exercises. 1. Ēgressī erimus. 2. Fair original sentences keep the future perfect and use Puer or Puella, with he or she in the gloss. 3. Later deponent tenses not yet taught remain reserved.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXXIX: SSA when a equals b, and when a is greater than b

Check. 1. One isosceles triangle, 30°-30°-120°. 2. Because a > b, B is acute and smaller than A, so only one triangle occurs. 3. Yesterday, h < a < b gave two triangles, 30°-45°-105° and 30°-135°-15°. Today a is not between h and b.

Exercises. 1. a = 1/2: no triangle. a = 1: one right triangle. a = √2: two triangles. a = 2: one isosceles triangle, 30°-30°-120°. 2. When a = b, B equals A and is acute, so a second, obtuse B does not occur. 3. An obtuse given angle remains reserved until tomorrow.

Writing · A short original confirmation, on a second invented claim

Check. 1. “In the formula s = rθ, as already taught, the angle θ must be in radians.” A short confirmation names that formula and recalls that a degree measure in place of θ was a miss. 2. The radian and s = rθ have already been taught. Today’s job is to confirm the claim, not to teach the formula for the first time. 3. The reason is the already-taught method, not a person’s fame.

Exercises. 1. A fair tighter pair states the claim in one sentence and the reason in the next, with no wasted opening. 2. One claim is already-taught isosceles geometry; the other is already-taught arc length in radians. Both hold, and both are confirmed, not refuted. 3. A longer later elaboration remains reserved.

Physics · Official Example 17.5: a closed tube with a 128 Hz fundamental

Check. 1. v_w = 331 m/s × √(295/273) = 344 m/s, with 22.0°C = 295 K. That 344 m/s is this example’s official speed. 2. L = 344 / (4 × 128) = 0.672 m. 3. 1.15 kHz, from n = 9. 340 m/s was last week’s official speed in Example 17.4, a different official example, and is not used here.

Exercises. 1. 344 / 512 = 0.671875, which the book reports as 0.672 m. 9 × 128 = 1152 Hz, which the book reports as 1.15 kHz. 2. In a closed tube the first overtone is n = 3, so 3 × 128 Hz, not n = 2. 3. Open tubes wait until Friday. Official lengths of other tubes, and later optics, remain reserved.

History · The first-voyage letter: 188 leagues on one side of Española, and Villa de Navidad

Check. 1. “A greater circumference than all Spain, from Catalonia by the sea-coast to Fuenterabia in Biscay,” the letter’s comparison, not an official modern area. “One hundred and eighty-eight great leagues” on one side, west to east. 2. The 188 leagues here are in the letter’s body, for this west-to-east line on one side of Española. The footnote “188” was the editor’s note on a different sentence, the earlier 178, and was not taught as official. 3. He says he took possession of a large town “well adapted for the working of the gold mines,” and “gave the name of Villa de Navidad.” Do not invent official gold weights, official areas, official identifications, or conversions of the leagues.

Exercises. 1. “Española, has a greater circumference than all Spain, from Catalonia by the sea-coast to Fuenterabia in Biscay.” The place-names stay inside the quotation as Major prints them. 2. The letter is still a voyage report, earlier than the June 7, 1494 treaty. 3. Later parts of the letter, official modern identifications, official areas, official gold quantities, and conversions remain reserved.

Atelier · The space between two objects, kept as one still life

Check. 1. The cloth or table between the forms, part of one still life, not an empty gap. If it is left unpainted, the objects float and the study splits. 2. Block its value with the objects, and check on the scale that a shadow stays a shadow and a bounce stays darker than the true light. 3. The objects are simple still-life forms, not a head from life. The interval is mixed from paints already in the four.

Exercises. 1. A fair order blocks the two objects and the interval together, under one light, then adds quieter bounces, so the gap cannot be left as a leftover strip. 2. Then the interval competes with the lights, and the still life no longer sits on one ground. 3. A portrait and a fifth tube remain reserved.

Modern language · Spanish: haya, sea, vaya, and esté, mixed with regular present subjunctives

Check. 1. Haya. 2. Haya, that there be; sea, that I be; vaya, that I go; esté, that I be (in a place or state); hable, that I speak. 3. Coma is the regular present subjunctive of comer, not haber. Hay is the indicative, not the subjunctive after “espero que.”

Exercises. 1. Fair invented sentences keep the matching verb: haya, sea, vaya, or viva, after “espero que” or “quiere que.” 2. Haya, not hay or sea; vaya, not va or haya. 3. Haber as a helping verb, and other irregular subjunctives not yet taught, remain reserved.

Seminar · Art history: Dewing, Buildings I.4.9–16, the church of the Apostles

Check. 1. It had been “shaken by the passage of time,” and “would not continue to stand.” “Two straight lines were drawn, intersecting each other at the middle in the form of a cross.” 2. The transverse arms “are equal to each other,” but the west arm “is enough longer than the other to make the form of the cross.” The roof over the sanctuary is “on a plan resembling that of the Church of Sophia, except that it is inferior to it in size.” 3. The dome “seems to float in the air and not to rest upon solid masonry, though actually it is well supported.” Official lengths are not invented. 4. Model: the church of the Apostles (Dewing, Buildings I.4.9–16). Own words should match this idea: In Dewing’s 1940 translation of Procopius, Buildings I.4.9–16, Justinian pulls down the old church of all the Apostles, “shaken by the passage of time,” and rebuilds it “more worthy both in size and in beauty.” “Two straight lines were drawn, intersecting each other at the middle in the form of a cross.” Official lengths are not invented. Near-miss: Not last week’s churches of Peter and Paul and of Saints Sergius and Bacchus, a different extract, I.4.1–8. Not later I.4, including the later notice of the Apostles’ bodies, which remains reserved. Not an official length, official height, or official comparison measured in the classroom. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. “As shewing very special honour to all the Apostles together.” “Shewing” and “honour” are Dewing’s wording; teaching prose uses US spelling. 2. Last week treated Peter and Paul and Saints Sergius and Bacchus at Hormisdas, I.4.1–8. Today’s extract is the rebuilt church of all the Apostles, I.4.9–16, a different building. 3. Later I.4, including the later notice of the Apostles’ bodies, and official measurements, remain reserved.
