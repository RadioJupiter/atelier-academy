Latin · Friday mix: the deponent pluperfect, with imperfect and perfect contrasts

Check. 1. Locūtus eram, I had spoken; locūtus erās, you (s.) had spoken; locūtus erat, he or she had spoken; locūtī erāmus, we had spoken; locūtī erātis, you (pl.) had spoken; locūtī erant, they had spoken. 2. Locūtus erat: the speaking is already completed before he wrote the letter. 3. Loquēbātur: cotīdiē marks a habitual past, he used to speak.

Exercises. 1. I had tried, and then I rested. Daily I used to try, or I was trying. Today I have tried, or I tried. 2. A fair sentence: Puella locūta erat, et tum rīsit. 3. The absolute has no sum and is a loosely attached phrase, not locūtus eram, loquēbar, or locūtus sum.

Mathematics · Friday mix: lines and planes in space

Check. 1. A line may lie in the plane, meet it at one point, or be parallel to it. The line is perpendicular to the plane if it meets the plane at a foot P and is perpendicular to two intersecting lines in the plane through P. 2. They meet in AB. The top does not meet the floor; the two planes are parallel. 3. They do not meet, are not parallel, and do not lie in one plane. AB and DC are parallel and lie in the front plane, so they are not skew.

Exercises. 1. DA is perpendicular to the floor. DC is parallel to the floor. CB meets the floor at B and is perpendicular to it, on this rectangular box. 2. A fair pair of parallel planes is the top and the floor, or the front and the back. A fair intersecting pair is the front and the floor, in AB, or the right-hand plane and the floor, in BF. 3. A sheet of paper is one plane. In one plane, two lines either meet or are parallel.

Writing · Friday mix: a fable retold, and a fable invented

Check. 1. A fair retelling keeps the thirst, the low water, the failed efforts, the stones, the water within reach, and the life saved, then states, “Necessity is the mother of invention.” 2. A fair original fable is a short new action with one moral at the end. It does not reuse the crow as if the story were new. 3. A mid-story lecture, a second moral, a new official fact, or, in the retelling, a change in what happens.

Exercises. 1. “Necessity is the mother of invention.” It is Townsend’s wording, not a new proverb of your own. 2. A précis restates a source already written. A fable is a short invented narrative with a stated moral. 3. Narrative, chreia, and maxim, and the later progymnasmata, remain reserved.

Physics · Friday mix: interference, standing waves, and beat frequency, a first look at beats

Check. 1. f_B = |f1 − f2|. The other frequency is 254 Hz or 258 Hz. 2. f_B = 4 Hz, f_ave = 442 Hz. The average frequency is the frequency of the resulting wave, the pitch you hear when the two are close; the beat frequency is how often the loudness rises and falls. 3. λ1 = 4.00 m, f1 = 20.0 Hz, f2 = 40.0 Hz. A node is a point where the disturbance is zero. 4. Model: beat frequency. Own words should match this idea: The beat frequency, in OpenStax 16.10, is f_B = |f1 − f2|, the frequency at which two waves of similar but not identical frequencies fluctuate in amplitude, from constructive to destructive interference. The resulting wave has the average frequency f_ave of the two. Near-miss: Not the average frequency itself, which is what you hear as the pitch when the two frequencies are close. Not an official pitch standard claimed for a living piano today. 256 Hz is the book’s example, not a classroom invention. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Amplitude 2X, same wavelength. 2. f_B = 6 Hz, f_ave = 303 Hz. 3. 256 Hz is OpenStax’s tuning-fork example; 440 and 444 are invented classroom figures. Neither is being claimed as an official standard in use today.

History · Friday mix: Inter Caetera, the recital and the grant

Check. 1. It claims remote islands and even mainlands “that hitherto had not been discovered by others,” and, in the same sentence, that “therein dwell very many peoples.” 2. To the kings of Castile and León and their heirs, forever, for lands discovered and to be discovered, provided they have not been in the actual temporal possession of any Christian owner. A right already acquired by any Christian prince is not to be understood as withdrawn. 3. The first-voyage letter, the May 4 bull, and any line of leagues. Invent no official island count and no official quantity of gold.

Exercises. 1. In Rome, at St. Peter’s, on the third day of May, 1493, in the first year of the pontificate. 2. It is the bull’s wording in a grant, and the same sentence already names peoples living there. You are not certifying a map. 3. This May 3 text does not draw that line. It belongs to a later reserved bull.

Atelier · Friday mix: the limited palette, two scales, a match, and a block

Check. 1. Yellow ochre, an earth red, black, and white. Each hue has a short even scale with white and black, four or five steps. 2. An earth-red step and an ochre step of the same lightness, checked by half-closing the eyes. On the block: value first, then a lean toward one hue or the other without losing the value. 3. A portrait, a fifth tube, lapis, ultramarine, and a bright modern red. A store tube is not an official mineral identification.

Exercises. 1. So that mixing and value are learned with a small set, not hidden by a large store of ready-made hues. 2. Value families, a half-closed-eye check, and reflected light as a value note, if one is visible. 3. The week’s job is the four-paint method, scales, a match, and a block, not a likeness of a face.

Modern language · Spanish: Friday mix of the simple future, with the present as a contrast

Check. 1. Hablaré, hablarás, hablará, hablaremos, hablaréis, hablarán. The whole infinitive is kept, and those six endings are added. 2. Vivirá: mañana asks for the simple future. The place is invented, not an official address. 3. Hablamos: hoy asks for the present, we speak, or we are speaking, in the class.

Exercises. 1. A fair pair: Mañana comeremos a las dos, invented time. Un día viviré cerca del río, invented place. 2. Comeré, I will eat; comeremos, we will eat; comerán, they will eat. 3. Irregular futures such as haré and iré remain reserved. Hablaré is simple future and still shows the infinitive; hablo is present.

Seminar · Artistic anatomy: a cervical vertebra, and the foramen transversarium, from Gray 1918

Check. 1. By “the presence of a foramen in each transverse process.” 2. It is the opening that pierces each transverse process. In the upper six vertebrae it gives passage to the vertebral artery and vein and a plexus of sympathetic nerves. 3. The atlas, the axis, and the seventh cervical vertebra, as separate first looks. Official lengths, and an official diameter of the foramen, remain reserved. 4. Model: foramen transversarium. Own words should match this idea: In Henry Gray, Anatomy of the Human Body, 20th ed., 1918, “The Cervical Vertebrae,” as posted at theodora.com, the cervical vertebrae “are the smallest of the true vertebrae, and can be readily distinguished from those of the thoracic or lumbar regions by the presence of a foramen in each transverse process.” That opening is the foramen transversarium. In the upper six vertebrae it “gives passage to the vertebral artery and vein and a plexus of sympathetic nerves.” Near-miss: Not the already-taught typical vertebra of last term as if it had no regional mark, and not the mental foramen of the mandible. Not the atlas, the axis, or the vertebra prominens, which Gray says “must be separately described” and which remain reserved. Official lengths remain reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. They “are the smallest of the true vertebrae.” The body of the remaining four “is small, and broader from side to side than from before backward.” 2. The mental foramen was taught with the mandible. Today’s opening is in the transverse process of a cervical vertebra. 3. The typical parts already taught are assumed. Today the regional mark that distinguishes a cervical vertebra is added, from Gray’s own test.

Vocabulary review

Check. 1. A line may lie in the plane, meet it at one point, or be parallel to it. Skew lines do not meet, are not parallel, and do not lie in one plane. 2. Locūtus eram, locūtus erās, locūtus erat, locūtī erāmus, locūtī erātis, locūtī erant. Locūtus eram, I had spoken, is already completed before another past moment. Locūtus sum is the perfect, I spoke or have spoken. Loquēbar is the imperfect, I was speaking or used to speak, with no participle. 3. Hablaré, hablarás, hablará, hablaremos, hablaréis, hablarán. Hablaré: mañana asks for the simple future, not the present hablo. 4. When two or more waves arrive at the same point, their disturbances add. The other frequency is 254 Hz or 258 Hz. For the invented string, f1 = 20.0 Hz, because λ1 = 4.00 m and vw / λ1 = 80.0 / 4.00. 5. Ochre is an earthy iron-oxide pigment; hematite (red) and hydrated oxides (yellow to brown) are the usual minerals in the USGS sources. Hematite is Fe2O3. Invent no official iron percentage of a classroom lump. 6. “It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife.” The “truth” is “so well fixed in the minds of the surrounding families.” 7. In the same sentence the bull says “therein dwell very many peoples.” The grant is to the kings of Castile and León and their heirs, forever, with a proviso about a Christian owner. 8. The tax is $4; the total is $84. $76 treats the 5 percent as a discount, not as a tax added to the price.
