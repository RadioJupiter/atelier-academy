Latin · Capitulum XVI: the ablative absolute with a deponent participle

Check. 1. With the boy speaking, the schoolmaster is silent. 2. With the pupils going out, the dog follows. 3. Loquente is a deponent present participle, active in meaning, like loquitur. 4. Model: deponent present participle in the absolute. Own words should match this idea: A deponent verb, already taught in the third person and the infinitive, also has a present participle that is active in meaning, as deponents are. That participle may stand in an ablative absolute: Puerō loquente, magister tacet. — With the boy speaking, the schoolmaster is silent. Near-miss: Not a first look at the ablative absolute, taught Monday, and not a first look at loquitur or ēgreditur. Not a perfect-participle absolute. The participle loquente means “speaking,” not “being spoken.” The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Mathematics · Geometry XLVIII: the volume of a sphere

Check. 1. V = 36π cubic units. 2. 36π = (4/3)π r³, so r³ = 27, r = 3 (a length is positive). 3. πr² is the area of a circle, already taught, in square units. The sphere’s volume uses r³ and the factor 4/3. 4. Model: volume of a sphere. Own words should match this idea: The volume of a sphere of radius r is V = (4/3)πr³, in cubic units. OpenStax, College Physics 2e, Appendix C, “Useful Information,” Table C9, lists this formula (CC BY). Exact answers stay in terms of π. Near-miss: Not a first look at last week’s cylinder, or at yesterday’s cone, and not the surface area of a sphere, which is reserved for tomorrow. Not an official planetary volume. 36π here is a volume, not last week’s or yesterday’s area or volume with the same numerals. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. r = 2; V = (32/3)π cubic units.
  2. It becomes eight times as large: 36π becomes 288π, because volume scales with r³.
  3. r³ = 8, r = 2.
  4. No official planetary measurement is used or invented here. The radii in this lesson are classroom inventions for the formula.

Physics · The maximum speed in simple harmonic motion, vmax = X√(k/m)

Check. 1. vmax = 0.852 m/s, OpenStax Example 16.6, no damping. 2. vmax = 0.050 × √(80/0.20) = 0.050 × √400 = 0.050 × 20 = 1.0 m/s. Invented figures. 3. 2π / T would be an angular frequency, not the maximum linear speed taught today from energy. T itself is last week’s period, a time. 4. Model: maximum speed in SHM. Own words should match this idea: In undamped simple harmonic motion, the maximum speed occurs at x = 0, and OpenStax 16.5 gives vmax = X√(k/m). It is proportional to the amplitude X and to the square root of k, and inversely proportional to the square root of m. Near-miss: Not a first look at Monday’s conservation equation, or at last week’s ½kx² = ½mv² as a one-step conversion. Not a period, and not a claim that a real car’s shock absorbers are undamped (OpenStax assumes no damping in the example). The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. vmax doubles, because it is proportional to X. The total energy becomes four times as large, because of X². Invented figures, if applied to Monday’s 1.0 J case, would go to 2.0 m/s × 2 and 4.0 J.
  2. 2.0 = 0.10 × √(k/0.50), so 20 = √(k/0.50), 400 = k/0.50, k = 200 N/m. Invented figures.
  3. OpenStax assumes no damping. Real shock absorbers remove energy, so the actual peak speed can be smaller.
  4. The 23.7 m/s was last week’s sourced conversion of 0.563 J for a 2.00 g dart, a one-step leaving of the spring. Today’s vmax is the peak speed in ongoing SHM, from 16.5 and Example 16.6.

Not an invented visitor count. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Modern language · Spanish: the preterite of dar

Check. 1. Di, diste, dio, dimos, disteis, dieron. The I-form is di, not dije. 2. Yesterday I gave the book. (Invented.) 3. Dije is from decir, “I said / I told,” not from dar. The form for “I gave” is di. 4. Model: di. Own words should match this idea: Di is the first-person singular preterite of dar, to give. The forms are di, diste, dio, dimos, disteis, dieron. There is no accent on di or dio in the ordinary school spelling taught here. Di is not last week’s dije, and dio is not dijo. Near-miss: Not a first look at vi, and not a first look at dije. Not the present doy. Not a spelling dió, and not the miss *dije used as if it were dar. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Three complete invented sentences.
  2. Dio, gave, from dar. Dijo, said / told, from decir. Vio, saw, from ver. Trajo, brought, from traer.
  3. Dieron, vieron, dijeron, trajeron.
  4. Vi was Monday’s (and Tuesday’s) first look. Today’s first look is dar.
