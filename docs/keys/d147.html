Latin · The future perfect of ēgredior, a first look

Check. 1. Ēgressus erō, ēgressus eris, ēgressus erit, ēgressī erimus, ēgressī eritis, ēgressī erunt. It marks a going out that will already be completed at some later time. 2. He will have gone out. Ēgrediētur is the future, he will go out, still ahead, not the future perfect. 3. Secūtus erō is the future perfect of sequor, I shall have followed, not a form of ēgredior. 4. Model: future perfect of ēgredior. Own words should match this idea: The future perfect of ēgredior is ēgressus erō, ēgressus eris, ēgressus erit, ēgressī erimus, ēgressī eritis, ēgressī erunt. It marks a going out that will already be completed at some later time. It is not the future ēgrediar, not the perfect ēgressus sum, and not secūtus erō. Near-miss: Not ēgrediar, I shall go out, still ahead. Not ēgressus sum, I went out, or I have gone out. Not ēgressus eram, I had gone out. Not secūtus erō, yesterday’s future perfect of sequor, a different verb. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. You (s.) will have gone out; they will have gone out. Fair original sentences may use Puer or Puella, with he or she in the gloss. 2. I go out; I shall go out; I went out, or I have gone out; I had gone out; I shall have gone out. 3. Later deponent tenses not yet taught remain reserved.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXXVIII: the two-triangle case of SSA, with exact invented sides

Check. 1. h = 1, and 1 < √2 < 2, so h < a < b, and two triangles occur. 2. sin B = 2 × (1/2) / √2 = 1/√2, so B is 45° or 135°. Then C is 105° or 15°. 3. Because a < b, the obtuse B = 135° is also possible, and dropping it leaves out one of the two triangles.

Exercises. 1. 30°-45°-105°, and 30°-135°-15°. 2. Yesterday, a = 1/2 gave no triangle, and a = 1 gave one right triangle. Today a = √2, between h and b, gives two. 3. The case a ≥ b, and an obtuse given angle, remain reserved until later in the week.

Modern language · Spanish: haya beside hay, sea, vaya, and esté

Check. 1. Haya. 2. Sea, that I be; vaya, that I go; esté, that I be (in a place or state); haya, that there be. 3. Vaya is the present subjunctive of ir, “that I go” or “that he or she go,” not haber.
