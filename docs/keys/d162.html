Latin · A purpose clause with nē and ēgredior, a first look

Check. 1. In order that he not go out, or in order that she not go out. Ut ēgrediātur is the positive purpose, in order that he go out, or in order that she go out. 2. She sits in order that she not go out. Ēgrediētur is the future, not the present subjunctive. 3. A result clause remains reserved. Today is a negative purpose with nē and ēgredior. 4. Model: a purpose clause with nē and ēgredior. Own words should match this idea: A negative purpose may also use the already-taught present subjunctive of ēgredior: nē ēgrediātur, in order that he not go out, or in order that she not go out. It is not the future ēgrediētur, and not yesterday’s nē eum sequātur, a different verb. It is not ut ēgrediātur, last week’s positive purpose with the same verb. Near-miss: Not ēgrediētur, he will go out, or she will go out. Not ut ēgrediātur, last week’s positive purpose. Not nē eum sequātur, yesterday’s nē-clause with sequor. Not a result clause, which remains reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Fair original sentences include Puer surgit ut ēgrediātur, he rises in order that he go out; and Puer sedet nē ēgrediātur, he sits in order that he not go out. 2. Both are negative purposes. One uses ēgredior, the other uses sequor. 3. A result clause remains reserved.

Mathematics · Algebra / analysis: the limit of a product, a first look

Check. 1. The limit of a product is the product of the limits, when those limits exist. On the invented functions, the limit of the product is 24. 2. 5 × 6 = 30. 3. The limit of a sum is a different job, already taught yesterday. 4. Model: the limit of a product, a first look. Own words should match this idea: If two quantities have limits, the limit of their product is the product of those limits. If lim x→a f(x) = L and lim x→a g(x) = M, then lim x→a (f(x) g(x)) = L M. A constant may be taken out: lim x→a (c f(x)) = c L. The figures used today are invented and labeled invented. Continuity, and a formal epsilon argument, remain reserved. Near-miss: Not yesterday’s limit of a sum as if it were already this first look. Not a quotient, which waits until tomorrow. Not continuity, which remains reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. lim (u v) = 5 × 4 = 20; lim (2u) = 2 × 5 = 10. The functions are invented. 2. They are invented classroom functions, not official limits of measured real data. 3. The limit of a quotient waits until tomorrow. Continuity, and a formal epsilon argument, remain reserved.
