Latin · Capitulum XIV: rēs, nihil, and inquit

Check. 1. I see nothing. 2. A model: ‘Tēcum labōrō,’ inquit. Other original sentences accepted. 3. Model: rēs. Own words should match this idea: Rēs, reī is a fifth-declension feminine noun meaning a thing, a matter, or an affair. Nominative and accusative plural are rēs; genitive plural is rērum. Near-miss: Not the Res Gestae of Day 33, which is a specific first-person record of deeds, used as if that title were today’s grammar lesson. Today’s rēs is the ordinary noun. inquit. Own words should match this idea: Inquit means says or said, and it is typically inserted inside direct speech, rather than heading the sentence as a plain dīcit might. Near-miss: Not every verb of speaking, and not a participle. Inquit is the inserted speech-verb. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. The Res Gestae is a particular record of deeds used as a history source. Today’s rēs is the ordinary fifth-declension noun meaning a thing or a matter.
  2. A thing / a matter; says (inserted in speech).

Mathematics · Geometry XXXVII: a regular polygon’s area, and the apothem

Check. 1. ½ × 40 × 6 = 120. 2. A side is an edge of the polygon. The apothem is the perpendicular from the center to a side; those two lengths are different jobs, even when a later figure might compare them. 3. Model: apothem. Own words should match this idea: The apothem of a regular polygon is the perpendicular from the center to the middle of a side. It is the height of each of the congruent isosceles triangles that fill the polygon. Near-miss: Not a side, not a radius to a vertex unless those two lengths happen to be compared later, and not a first look at the name ‘regular polygon’ (that name, and the interior angle, were taught on Day 37). Today’s new job is the apothem and the area. area of a regular polygon. Own words should match this idea: A regular polygon divides into n congruent isosceles triangles from the center. Its area is n times the area of one of those triangles, or ½ × (the perimeter) × (the apothem). Near-miss: Not the area of a circle, which is reserved for a later week, and not an interior-angle calculation (Day 37) presented as if it were an area. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Half-base 2; height √(16 − 4) = √12 = 2√3; one triangle ½ × 4 × 2√3 = 4√3; six of them 24√3.
  2. The area of a circle is reserved for a later week. Today’s work stops at a regular polygon, using triangles already taught.

Writing & Rhetoric · From the outline, one paragraph of the essay

Check. 1. A complete paragraph accepted if it opens with a claim, uses a real ground or an accurate quotation, and stays in one job. Invented Longfellow wording is not accepted. 2. A short honest note accepted, for example: ‘I checked for a circle; the because-clause names the turned-back speaker, not the word rescue again.’ 3. Model: paragraph from the outline. Own words should match this idea: A paragraph written from a multi-paragraph outline opens with the planned claim, introduces and explains a short quotation or ground, and stays inside that paragraph’s job. It does not swallow the whole thesis in one paragraph, and it does not support the claim with a fallacy already taught. Near-miss: Not a new first look at a topic sentence or at introducing a quotation (Days 6 and 9). Not the whole essay in one sitting. Not a précis of the canto. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A model: ‘Virgil says, in Longfellow’s translation, “I will be thy guide.”’ Other accurate introductions accepted.
  2. Order alone does not prove cause. The speech’s wording, not merely the fact that it comes later in the canto, is the safer ground.

Science · Water: solubility, at a specified temperature

Check. 1. Concentrated is a qualitative comparison (relatively much solute). Solubility is the composition of the saturated solution, as a quantity, at specified conditions. 2. Saturated names the equilibrium solution (and leftover solid may be present). Solubility names the composition of that solution, not the solid itself. 3. Model: solubility. Own words should match this idea: In the IUPAC Gold Book (S05740), the analytical composition of a saturated solution, expressed as the proportion of a designated solute in a designated solvent, is the solubility of that solute. It may be expressed as a concentration, a molality, a mole fraction, or another composition measure. Near-miss: Not a synonym for ‘saturated,’ and not last week’s qualitative ‘concentrated.’ Solubility is the composition of the saturated solution, as a quantity, not the leftover solid itself. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Solubility is a composition, and composition can be written in more than one kind of unit. The Gold Book lists several.
  2. It is a classroom observation, not a sourced official table. No official percentage is invented.

Literature · Inferno II, lines 1–42: day departing, and a will that unwills

Check. 1. “Regard my manhood, if it be sufficient” (II.11). 2. The “fair, saintly Lady” and the speech that follows. 3. Model: a will that unwills (Inferno II). Own words should match this idea: In Longfellow’s Inferno II, lines 37–42, the speaker compares himself to one “who unwills what he willed, / And by new thoughts doth his intention change, / So that from his design he quite withdraws.” On “that dark hillside,” thinking “consumed the emprise, / Which was so very prompt in the beginning.” Near-miss: Not a first look at Canto I’s dark wood or at Virgil’s offer to be a guide, already taught. Not the later speech of the “fair, saintly Lady” (from II.52), which is reserved for a later sitting. Not an identification of “the Chosen Vessel” beyond the canto’s own contrast with Paul in these lines. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. The lines contrast the speaker with Aeneas and with Paul; “the Chosen Vessel” is read in that contrast, not as a separate research claim begun today.
  2. A bold undertaking that started readily (the journey just agreed) and then, in hesitation, lost its force. Close honest wording accepted.

Atelier · Grisaille: a first look, as monochrome value

Check. 1. Grisaille is monochrome. Color, even if you can imagine it, is not the job today and is reserved for Term 3. 2. A new lamp would invent a new set of families. The map already states this week’s light. 3. Model: grisaille. Own words should match this idea: Grisaille is monochrome value painting: light and shadow stated in one hue, or in gray, rather than in full color. In this course it is the Term 2 step from a drawn value map toward paint, and it may be begun in oil or acrylic if those materials are ready, or rehearsed in a monochrome drawing if they are not. Near-miss: Not a full-color portrait, and not a first look at the light family, the core shadow, or the value map, which this week and earlier weeks already taught. Not a requirement that oil be used today if it is not in the room. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A gray range, or one earth color plus white and a dark, is enough. A full palette is not required and is not the Term 2 job.
  2. It does the same monochrome value job, in drawing, and it is named as a rehearsal rather than as a claim that paint was used.

Elective · Economics: finding the principal from the amount

Check. 1. 1 + r t = 1.08; P = 2,160 / 1.08 = 2,000. 2. 1 + r t = 1.10; P = 1,100 / 1.10 = 1,000. Check: I = 1,000 × 0.10 × 1 = 100; A = 1,000 + 100 = 1,100. 3. Model: principal from the amount. Own words should match this idea: If the amount after simple interest is A = P + I, and I = P × r × t, then A = P(1 + r t), so the principal is P = A / (1 + r t). The rate r is a fraction or a decimal for the time unit used in t. Near-miss: Not a first look at simple interest or at A = P + I, which were taught in weeks 14–15. Not compound interest, which is not begun today. Not an official bank rate. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. They are classroom figures, not an official bank rate or a sourced offer.
  2. Compound interest has not been taught; the check uses I = P × r × t and A = P + I, already taught.
