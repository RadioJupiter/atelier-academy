Mathematics · The difference quotient, a first look

Check. 1. [f(a + h) − f(a)] / h, for h ≠ 0. If h were 0, the denominator would be 0. 2. [ (3 + h)² − 9 ] / h = 6 + h, for h ≠ 0. 3. Today teaches the increment and simplifies it. The limit as h approaches 0, which will be named a derivative, waits until tomorrow. 4. Model: the difference quotient, a first look. Own words should match this idea: The difference quotient of a function f, at a number a, in this first look, is the ratio [f(a + h) − f(a)] / h, for a increment h not equal to 0. On the invented function f(x) = x², at a = 3, [ (3 + h)² − 9 ] / h = (9 + 6h + h² − 9) / h = (6h + h²) / h = 6 + h, for h ≠ 0. That simplified increment, 6 + h, is not yet a derivative; the limit as h approaches 0 waits until tomorrow. The function and the chosen numbers are invented classroom examples, not official data. A formal epsilon argument remains reserved. Near-miss: Not a first look at a derivative, which waits until tomorrow. Not a formal epsilon argument, which remains reserved. Not ∞ treated as a number to substitute, and not h = 0 in the quotient, which is not defined. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. [ (2 + h)² − 4 ] / h = (4 + 4h + h² − 4) / h = 4 + h, for h ≠ 0. 2. 4.1, and 3.9.

Modern language · Spanish: the irregular participle vuelto, a first look

Check. 1. He vuelto, has vuelto, ha vuelto, hemos vuelto, habéis vuelto, han vuelto. I have returned; he has returned, or she has returned. 2. Volver does not form a regular participle *volvido; the form is vuelto. 3. Ha is the helping form of haber. Hay means there is, or there are. Haya is “that there be.” 4. Model: the irregular participle vuelto, a first look. Own words should match this idea: Vuelto is the irregular past participle of volver, to return, used with the already-taught helping forms of haber: he vuelto, has vuelto, ha vuelto, hemos vuelto, habéis vuelto, han vuelto. It is not puesto, last week’s participle of poner, and not visto, dicho, hecho, or escrito. It is not a regular *volvido. The Spanish participle vuelto is the past participle of volver, from Vulgar Latin *voltus, from *volŭtus, from Latin volūtus, as given in the Spanish section of Wiktionary. Other irregular participles, and a present-perfect subjunctive, remain reserved. Near-miss: Not last week’s puesto as if it were today’s participle. Not visto, dicho, hecho, or escrito as if they were new. Not *volvido, a regular form that volver does not use. Not a present-perfect subjunctive, which remains reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Model: He vuelto a casa. Hemos vuelto a casa. 2. Ella ha puesto el libro: she has put the book. Ella ha vuelto a casa: she has returned home.
