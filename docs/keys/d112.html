Latin · The deponent imperfect, used in short sentences

Check. Model: deponent imperfect in use. Own words should match this idea: The imperfect of a deponent, already named yesterday, is used here in short original sentences: loquēbar and cōnābar for the ongoing or habitual past, still looking passive and translated as active. The participle of last week’s perfect is not used in these forms. Near-miss: Not a first look at yesterday’s table, and not a first look at locūtus sum. Not an absolute. Not a claim that every past sentence in Latin must be imperfect. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Ego loquēbar: I was speaking, or I used to speak. Vōs cōnābāminī: you (plural) were trying, or used to try. Puerī loquēbantur: the boys were speaking, or used to speak. Cōnābar fits “I was trying when…,” because the action is ongoing in that past moment. Cōnātus sum would present it as completed.

Exercises. Model: Nōs loquēbāmur, we were speaking. Puerī locūtī sunt, the boys spoke. Loquor, loquēbar, locūtus sum. Cōnor, cōnābar, cōnātus sum.

Mathematics · Geometry LXIV: tangent as sine over cosine

Check. Model: tangent as sine over cosine. Own words should match this idea: From the already-taught definitions, tan A = sin A / cos A, because (opposite over hypotenuse) divided by (adjacent over hypotenuse) is opposite over adjacent. No new triangle is required; the identity is a rewriting of the three ratios in the same right triangle. Degrees only. Near-miss: Not a first look at sine, cosine, or tangent as separate ratios. Not a claim about a unit circle, which remains reserved. Not a reciprocal such as cotangent, which remains reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Sin B = 5/13, cos B = 12/13, (5/13)/(12/13) = 5/12, which is tan B. Dividing 3/5 by 1 ignores cosine; tan A is 3/4, not 3, and not 3/5.

Exercises. Sin A = 6/10 = 3/5, cos A = 8/10 = 4/5, tan A = 6/8 = 3/4, and (3/5)/(4/5) = 3/4. Tan D = 12/5, and (12/13)/(5/13) = 12/5.

Science · The rate of conductive heat transfer, a first look

Check. Model: rate of conductive heat transfer. Own words should match this idea: In OpenStax College Physics 2e, section 14.5, the rate of conductive heat transfer through a slab is Q/t = kA(T2 − T1)/d, where Q/t is in watts (joules per second), k is the thermal conductivity of the material, A is the surface area, d is the thickness, and (T2 − T1) is the temperature difference across the slab. The rate increases with k, with A, and with the temperature difference, and it decreases as the thickness d increases. When the two temperatures are the same, the net rate falls to zero. Values of k are taken from OpenStax Table 14.3, and are not invented. Near-miss: Not last week’s qualitative naming of conduction, convection, and radiation. Not a first look at convection or radiation rates, which remain reserved. Official household R-factor calculations in non-metric units remain reserved. Table k values are not to be replaced with invented ones. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Q/t = kA(T2 − T1)/d. The four factors are k, A, (T2 − T1), and d. When T2 = T1, the net rate is zero. Copper: 390 J/s·m·°C. Styrofoam: 0.010 J/s·m·°C. A third k not in the table would be invented.

Exercises. Q/t = 220 × 0.020 × 15 / 0.010 = 6600 W. Wool: 0.04 × 0.020 × 15 / 0.010 = 1.2 W. Wool has the much smaller rate, so it is the better insulator in this comparison.

Modern language · Spanish: an imperfect background and a preterite event

Check. Model: background and a completed event. Own words should match this idea: The imperfect can set a background, already ongoing, while the preterite names a completed event against that background, using only already-taught forms. The new skill is the pairing, not a new paradigm. Near-miss: Not a first look at yesterday’s choice in a single-tense sentence. Not a new verb. Not an identifying invented person. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Ayer, mientras comía, vi el cielo: Yesterday, while I was eating, I saw the sky. Mientras comí would present the eating as a completed event, not as a background. Cada mañana iba al mercado, pero ayer fui temprano: habitual iba, completed fui.

Exercises. Model: Ayer, mientras iba, vi el cielo. Yesterday, while I was going, I saw the sky. If you swapped the tenses — mientras fui, veía — the going would be treated as a single completed event and the seeing as background, which is not what the sentence was meant to say.
