Latin · Friday mix: the pluperfect of sequor and ēgredior, with already-taught deponent tenses

Check. 1. The perfect participle plus the imperfect of sum. Secūtus eram, secūtus erās, secūtus erat, secūtī erāmus, secūtī erātis, secūtī erant. 2. You (s.) go out, or you are going out. We had followed him. She had spoken. 3. Ēgrediuntur: nunc asks for the present, they go out, not the pluperfect.

Exercises. 1. You (s.) had gone out; they had followed; we had spoken. 2. A fair trio: Ego tē sequor. Ego tē secūtus eram, et tum tacēbam. Puella ēgressa erat. 3. The pluperfect of a deponent is not formed by adding a past ending to the present stem sequ-. It is the participle plus eram.

Writing · Friday mix: a chreia distinguished from a narrative and from a fable

Check. 1. A short, useful anecdote that attributes a saying, a deed, or both to a named person. A narrative tells who, what, when, and where, in order, with no required saying and no stated moral. A fable has a stated moral, and often uses animals. 2. Alexander finds Diogenes lying in the sun and asks whether he wanted anything. Diogenes says, “I would have you stand from between me and the sun.” It fits because he asks for no gift, only that the sun not be blocked. 3. A maxim, and a full later elaboration. Do not invent official facts about Diogenes beyond the set passage, or name a living person.

Exercises. 1. A fair invented chreia has a person, an occasion, and a saying, and no stated moral tag; a fable would end with “The moral is….” 2. It is the 1859 wording. Teaching prose may say anything. 3. The fable has a stated moral. The chreia has an attributed saying or deed. The narrative has neither as a requirement.

Physics · Friday mix: sound, and the speed, frequency, and wavelength of sound

Check. 1. A disturbance of matter transmitted from its source outward, and a wave. In air it is longitudinal because fluids have almost no shear strength. Hearing is the perception of sound. 2. Vw = fλ. In air at sea level, vw = 331 m/s × √(T/273 K). In Example 17.1, vw = 348.7 m/s, λmax = 17 m, λmin = 1.7 cm. 3. 331 m/s; 343 m/s; 5960 m/s in steel. Decibels remain reserved. Invent no official speed of light.

Exercises. 1. λ = 3.487 m, about 3.49 m to three figures, with 100 Hz invented. f = 348.7 Hz, with 1.00 m invented. 2. Sound has a finite speed, much slower than light. The book does not give a speed of light here, and none is being invented. 3. In open air, in the audible range, the speed of sound is nearly independent of frequency.

History · Friday mix: the Tordesillas line, the two sides, and last week’s May 4 bull

Check. 1. A pole-to-pole line 370 leagues west of the Cape Verde Islands, in Davenport’s 1917 translation, Document 09. The treaty was concluded June 7, 1494. 2. East: Portugal and its successors, forever. West: Castile, Leon, etc., and their successors, forever. If Castile discovers land east of the line, it pertains forever to Portugal and its heirs. 3. The May 4 bull measured 100 leagues from the Azores and Cape Verde. Do not convert 100 or 370 leagues into an official modern length or longitude.

Exercises. 1. The line is “at a distance of three hundred and seventy leagues west of the Cape Verde Islands.” Lands east “shall belong to, and remain in the possession of, and pertain forever to, the said King of Portugal and his successors.” 2. It is the editor’s historical note, useful as background, not the legal wording of the 370-league clause. 3. The first-voyage letter, and any official modern longitude of the line.
