Latin · Purpose clauses with sequor and ēgredior, mixed with the futures

Check. 1. She comes in order that she follow him, a purpose clause; he will go out, the future. 2. The purpose clause uses the present subjunctive sequātur, with ā, not the future sequētur, with ē. 3. Ut sequātur is a purpose of following, not of going out. The purpose of going out is ut ēgrediātur.

Exercises. 1. Fair original sentences may use Puer or Puella, with he or she in the gloss. 2. Ut sequātur, in order that he follow, or she follow; ut ēgrediātur, in order that he go out, or she go out. 3. A negative purpose with nē, and a result clause, remain reserved.

Mathematics · Algebra / analysis: a limit as a positive integer grows large, a first look

Check. 1. The values of 1/n approach 0 as n grows large. The limit is 0. 2. ∞ is only a way of saying that n grows large without bound. It is not a number to plug into the formula. 3. 0.1, 0.01, and 0.001. The table is invented. 4. Model: a limit as a positive integer grows large. Own words should match this idea: A limit may also describe what happens as a positive integer n grows large. In this first look, the values of 1/n approach 0 as n grows large, written lim n→∞ 1/n = 0. The symbol ∞ is read “infinity” here only as a way of saying that n grows large without bound; it is not a number to be substituted, and a formal treatment of infinity remains reserved. Near-miss: Not a claim that ∞ is a number that can be substituted into 1/n. Not a formal epsilon argument, and not a first look at a series, both reserved. Not an official limit of measured real data. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. The limit is 0. The table is invented. 2. A formal treatment of infinity remains reserved. Today’s first look is the approaching of 1/n to 0 from an invented table. 3. Yesterday’s limit was as x approached a finite number, 2, and the function was not defined at 2. Today n grows large, and 1/n is defined for each positive integer n in the table.

Spanish · Dicho, hecho, and escrito beside last week’s regular participles
