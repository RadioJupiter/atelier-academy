Elective · Anatomy: the seven tarsal bones, from Gray 1918

Check. 1. “The tarsal bones are seven in number, viz., the calcaneus, talus, cuboid, navicular, and the first, second, and third cuneiforms.” 2. At the lower and back part of the foot (the heel). It transmits the weight of the body to the ground, and forms a strong lever for the muscles of the calf. 3. Middle and upper part of the tarsus, on the calcaneus, supporting the tibia above, articulating with the malleoli on either side, and with the navicular in front. 4. Model: the seven tarsal bones. Own words should match this idea: Gray, Anatomy of the Human Body, 1918, states: “The tarsal bones are seven in number, viz., the calcaneus, talus, cuboid, navicular, and the first, second, and third cuneiforms.” Last week taught the tarsus as a group and reserved these names; today the names are taught from that sentence, not guessed. Near-miss: Not a first look at the tarsus as a group, or at the metatarsals and phalanges, taught last week. Not a first look at the eight carpal names, which remain reserved. Not an official length of a particular foot. calcaneus and talus. Own words should match this idea: The calcaneus (Gray also gives os calcis) is the largest tarsal bone, at the lower and back part of the foot, “serving to transmit the weight of the body to the ground, and forming a strong lever for the muscles of the calf.” The talus (Gray also gives astragalus, ankle bone) is the second largest, “occupying the middle and upper part of the tarsus, supporting the tibia above, resting upon the calcaneus below, articulating on either side with the malleoli, and in front with the navicular.” Near-miss: Not a metatarsal, and not a first look at the tibia as a bone of the leg in a later detailed lesson. Gray’s wording is the source for today’s placements. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Cuboid: lateral side, in front of the calcaneus, behind the fourth and fifth metatarsals. Navicular: medial side, between the talus behind and the cuneiforms in front.
  2. The largest of the three cuneiforms, at the medial side, between the navicular and the first metatarsal; Gray also calls it the internal cuneiform. Medial, intermediate, and lateral may gloss first, second, and third; they are not a second official list invented here.
  3. Last week: tarsus as a group, metatarsals, phalanges (two in the hallux, three in the other toes). Reserved until today: the seven individual tarsal names.
  4. They were reserved last week with the tarsal names, and they remain reserved. Friday is the tarsal list, not a first look at the carpals.
