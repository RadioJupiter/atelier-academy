Physics · The law of refraction, Snell’s law, and official Example 25.2, from OpenStax 25.3

Check. 1. n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂. The angles are measured from the perpendicular to the surface at the point where the ray crosses it, the normal. 2. n₁ = 1.00, air; θ₁ = 30.0°; θ₂ = 22.0°. Official n₂ = 1.33, which the book identifies as water. 3. Official Example 25.3, a ray from air into diamond, remains reserved. Today is Snell’s law and official Example 25.2. 4. Model: law of refraction (Snell’s law). Own words should match this idea: In OpenStax, College Physics 2e, 25.3, the law of refraction, also called Snell’s law, is n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂. The angles “are measured relative to a perpendicular to the surface at the point where the light ray crosses it,” the same normal already used for reflection. Official Example 25.2 takes medium 1 as air, n₁ = 1.00, θ₁ = 30.0°, θ₂ = 22.0°, and finds n₂ = (1.00 × 0.500) / 0.375 = 1.33, which the book identifies as the index of refraction for water. Near-miss: Not last week’s law of reflection, θᵣ = θᵢ, a different job for light that bounces off a surface. Not official Example 25.3, which remains reserved. Not an invented official index for a real substance. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. n₂ = (1.00 × 0.500) / 0.375 = 1.33. 2. Closer to the normal, when the speed decreases. Farther from the normal, when the speed increases. 3. The law of reflection, θᵣ = θᵢ, is a different job, already taught, for light that bounces off a surface.

History · The third of November, “The reward, I see the land!”, from the second-voyage letter

Check. 1. “On the first Sunday after All Saints, namely, the third of November, about dawn.” A pilot cried out “The reward, I see the land!” 2. The ship Capitana, in the letter’s wording. 3. This extract gives the date, the dawn, the pilot, and the cry. A later official island name is not in this extract and is not added. 4. Model: the third of November, “The reward, I see the land!”. Own words should match this idea: In Major’s 1870 wording, confirmed from the page, the second-voyage letter dates the landfall: “On the first Sunday after All Saints, namely, the third of November, about dawn, a pilot of the ship Capitana cried out ‘The reward, I see the land!’” Those words are the letter’s. “The reward, I see the land!” is the letter’s wording of the cry, not a modern official reconstruction. The date “the third of November” is the letter’s, already prepared by Wednesday’s “leaving Ferro on the thirteenth of October, within twenty days.” Near-miss: Not Monday’s Cadiz date, already taught, and not Wednesday’s “thirteenth of October,” already taught as the departure from Ferro. Not a later official identification of the land beyond the letter’s cry in this extract. Not an official modern conversion of the letter’s “within twenty days.” The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Monday: departure from Cadiz “on the twenty-fifth of September, of the year,” with the year missing in the body. Wednesday: “leaving Ferro on the thirteenth of October.” Friday: landfall on “the third of November.” Those are the letter’s dates, confirmed from the pages. 2. The wording is the letter’s, confirmed from the 1870 page, and is kept as a quotation, not rewritten as a modern official reconstruction. 3. A later official identification of the land, and later pages of the same letter, remain reserved.
