Elective · Anatomy: the arm — humerus, radius, and ulna

Check. 1. Humerus with femur (the single upper bone); radius and ulna with tibia and fibula (two lower bones). The comparison is of place, not of identical function. 2. The sites are public, and the page must contain no identifying person. A simple unlabeled bone diagram is enough. 3. Model: humerus. Own words should match this idea: The bone of the upper arm, between the shoulder and the elbow. It is a single bone, not a pair. Near-miss: Not the radius or the ulna (those are the two bones of the forearm), and not a muscle. The name is the bone’s name, not a word for the whole arm. radius and ulna. Own words should match this idea: The two long bones of the forearm, between the elbow and the wrist. The radius lies on the thumb side when the palm faces forward; the ulna lies on the little-finger side and forms the point of the elbow with its upper end (the olecranon, named only as the ulna’s elbow end if a finer label is wanted). Near-miss: Not a second humerus, and not a claim that the two forearm bones are interchangeable in position. Crossing of the radius over the ulna when the hand is turned is a later, careful look; today’s first look is the palm-forward position. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. The crossing of the radius when the hand turns is a later job; today’s placement is the clearer palm-forward view.
  2. The hand is not this sitting’s work.

Vocabulary review

Check. 1. If a² + b² = c², the triangle is right-angled opposite c. 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13², so a 5-12-13 triangle is right-angled opposite 13. 2. The possessor is in the dative, the thing possessed in the nominative, with a form of esse: for example, Mārcō gladius est. 3. 72.74 mN/m, with an uncertainty of 0.36 mN/m. 4. Longfellow: “And lo! almost where the ascent began, / A panther light and swift exceedingly, / Which with a spotted skin was covered o’er!” Dewing: “In our own age there has been born the Emperor Justinian.” 5. comí; viví. They share the regular -er / -ir yo ending -í. The regular -ar yo form is hablé, with -é. 6. Darii is AII: all M are P; some S are M; therefore some S are P. The conclusion stays particular; “all S are P” would claim more than the particular minor gives. 7. I = P × r × t, on the original principal only; the amount is P + I. The rate in a classroom example is invented, not an official rate at a named bank. 8. Humerus: the single bone of the upper arm (shoulder to elbow). Radius: the forearm bone on the thumb side, in a palm-forward view. Ulna: the forearm bone on the little-finger side, which forms the point of the elbow.
