Latin · The perfect of ēgredior, a first look

Check. 1. Ēgressus sum, ēgressus es, ēgressus est, ēgressī sumus, ēgressī estis, ēgressī sunt. 2. Puer ēgressus est: he went out, or he has gone out. We have not gone out, or we did not go out. 3. Ēgressus eram is the pluperfect, I had gone out, already completed before another past moment, not the perfect. 4. Model: perfect of ēgredior. Own words should match this idea: The perfect of ēgredior uses the same deponent perfect pattern: the participle ēgressus plus the present of sum. The six forms are ēgressus sum, ēgressus es, ēgressus est, ēgressī sumus, ēgressī estis, ēgressī sunt: I went out, or I have gone out, and so on. It is not the present ēgredior, not the future ēgrediar, and not the pluperfect ēgressus eram. Near-miss: Not ēgredior, I go out. Not ēgrediar, I shall go out. Not ēgressus eram, I had gone out. Not secūtus sum, I followed. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. You (s.) went out, or have gone out; they went out, or have gone out. 2. Ēgressus sum; ēgressī sunt. 3. Ēgressa est, she went out, or has gone out; secūtus est, he followed, or has followed. The verbs are different, even though both are deponent perfects.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXXIV: arc length in radians, s = rθ

Check. 1. s = rθ, with θ in radians. 2. s = 6. The 3 and the 2 radians are invented classroom numbers. 3. s = 2π. 6 × 60 treats 60° as if it were already in radians.

Science · Electric current, a first look, from OpenStax 20.1

Check. 1. I = ΔQ / Δt. 1 A = 1 C/s. 2. 180 A. 3. 3.33 × 10^3 s. 4. Model: electric current. Own words should match this idea: Electric current, in OpenStax 20.1, is the rate at which charge flows: I = ΔQ / Δt. The SI unit is the ampere (A), named for André-Marie Ampère (1775–1836), and 1 A = 1 C/s. It is not a voltage, and it is not an official household current invented for a house not in the book. Near-miss: Not voltage, which has not yet been today’s first look as Ohm’s law. Not drift velocity, which remains reserved. Not an official current in a house or appliance not in Example 20.1. Not last week’s sound intensity. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. I = 10 / 2 = 5 A. The 10 C and 2 s are invented classroom numbers, not official Example 20.1. 2. Current is the rate at which charge flows, in amperes. Voltage has not yet been today’s first look, and is not the same quantity. 3. Ohm’s law, drift velocity, official household voltages, and a simple circuit until Thursday, remain reserved where not yet taught. Drift velocity and Ohm’s law and official household voltages remain reserved.

Modern language · Spanish: the present subjunctive of ir, a first look

Check. 1. Vaya, vayas, vaya, vayamos, vayáis, vayan. 2. He or she wants you (pl.) to go. Van is the present indicative, not the subjunctive vayan. 3. Sea is the present subjunctive of ser, “that she be,” not “that she go.” 4. Model: present subjunctive of ir. Own words should match this idea: The present subjunctive of ir is irregular: vaya, vayas, vaya, vayamos, vayáis, vayan. It is not the present indicative voy or va, not the future irá, not the conditional iría, and not sea, the present subjunctive of ser. Near-miss: Not va, the present indicative. Not irá, the future. Not iría, the conditional. Not sea, yesterday’s subjunctive of ser. Not the regular -ir subjunctive viva, which is a different verb. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. That you (s.) go; that we go. Fair invented sentences use “quiere que tú vayas” and “quiere que vayamos.” 2. I go; I will go; I would go; that I go (present subjunctive). 3. The present subjunctive of estar, esté, waits until tomorrow. Other irregulars not yet taught remain reserved.

Seminar · Music: minuet and trio, a first look, on an invented figure

Check. 1. A minuet is stated, a contrasting trio follows, and the minuet returns, giving A–B–A. Theme and variations change one theme rather than insert a contrasting middle. 2. Minuet: 1-3-5 | 5-4-3. Trio: 1-5-4 | 4-5-1. Then the minuet, 1-3-5 | 5-4-3, returns. 3. The lesson uses an invented classroom figure. A copyrighted score is not being copied or transcribed. A rondo is A–B–A–C–A, with more than one return of A around two or more episodes. 4. Model: minuet and trio. Own words should match this idea: Minuet and trio is a form in which a minuet is stated, a contrasting trio follows, and the minuet then returns, often marked da capo, giving an A–B–A shape. It is not last week’s theme and variations, which change one theme rather than insert a contrasting middle, and not a rondo, which returns more than once around two or more episodes. Near-miss: Not theme and variations, already taught, in which the theme is changed but remains recognizable. Not a rondo, already taught as A–B–A–C–A. Not a copyrighted minuet copied or transcribed. Not binary form used as if the trio never returned to the minuet. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. The return of the minuet after the trio is part of the form. Without it, the shape would be only A–B, closer to binary form, already taught, than to minuet and trio. 2. A fair further trio keeps a contrasting shape, still labeled invented, and is not a decorated copy of 1-3-5 | 5-4-3. 3. A longer dance suite, and any later form not yet taught, remain reserved.
