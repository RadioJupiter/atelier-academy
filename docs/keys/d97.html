Latin · The two times of the ablative absolute, contrasted

Check. 1. The present-participle kind is contemporaneous with the main verb. The perfect-participle kind is already complete when the main verb happens. 2. With the boys speaking, the schoolmaster is silent. The speaking is going on at the same time. 3. It is an ordinary ablative with a preposition, not a loosely attached noun-plus-participle phrase. 4. With the work finished, the boys play. 5. Model: two times of the ablative absolute. Own words should match this idea: A present-participle absolute, already taught last week, is going on at the same time as the main verb. A perfect-participle absolute, yesterday’s first look, is already complete when the main verb happens. Both are noun plus participle, both ablative, loosely attached. Near-miss: Not a claim that every ablative is an absolute. An ordinary prepositional ablative, such as ē lūdō, is not an absolute. Not a first look at urbe captā itself, which was yesterday. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A sound pair will keep both words ablative in each phrase and will not turn ē lūdō into an absolute. Sample: Magistrō recitante, puerī silent; Opere cōnfectō, puerī lūdunt.
  2. Captā is perfect: already done, not still going on.
  3. With the sun rising, the birds sing. The rising is still going on.
  4. With the sun having risen, the birds sing. The rising is already complete.

Mathematics · Geometry LII: the surface area of a right cone

Check. 1. It unrolls to a sector of radius l whose arc is the base circumference 2πr, so the sector area is πrl. 2. l = 5. Total surface 24π square units. 3. Those are volumes of a cylinder and of a cone, already taught, not the outside surface. 4. 90π square units. 5. Model: surface area of a right cone. Own words should match this idea: The lateral surface of a right cone unrolls to a sector of a circle whose radius is the slant height l and whose arc is the base circumference 2πr, already taught. The area of that sector is (1/2) × l × 2πr = πrl. Adding the base disk, the total surface area is πrl + πr². The slant height is l = √(r² + h²), by the Pythagorean theorem, if r and h are given. Near-miss: Not last week’s volume V = (1/3)πr²h, and not yesterday’s pyramid. Not a first look at a sector or at arc length, which were taught on Day 83. Classroom lengths are invented. Exact answers stay in terms of π. slant height of a right cone. Own words should match this idea: The slant height l of a right cone is the distance from the apex to a point on the base circle, along the side. It is not the perpendicular height h. l² = r² + h². Near-miss: Not the perpendicular height used in last week’s volume. Not yesterday’s face slant height, which was the altitude of a triangular face on a pyramid. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. πrl + πr² = 60π + 36π = 96π square units.
  2. l² = 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289, l = 17. Total 136π + 64π = 200π square units.
  3. The radius was not a measured official object, and the course leaves exact circle measures in terms of π, as in weeks 16–19.
  4. A cylinder’s surface uses two disks and a rectangle (or 2πrh + 2πr²), already available from last week’s cylinder, but it is not retold as a first look today. Today is the cone.

Science · The latent heat of vaporization of water, from OpenStax

Check. 1. 2256 kJ/kg, or 2256 J/g, at 100.0 °C, the normal boiling point. 2. 2256 kJ. 3. 22.56 kJ. The mass is invented; the 2256 kJ/kg is OpenStax’s table value. 4. 2834 − 333.4 was a difference of two 0 °C Harvey figures, not a fetched 100 °C row, and was refused last week. 334 kJ/kg is OpenStax’s Lf; 333.4 J/g is Harvey’s ΔfusH; they come from different tables and are not blended. 5. Model: latent heat of vaporization of water (OpenStax). Own words should match this idea: OpenStax, College Physics 2e, Table 14.2, lists the latent heat of vaporization of water as 2256 kJ/kg (540 kcal/kg) at the normal boiling point, 100.0 °C. The energy to vaporize a mass m, without a temperature change during the change of phase, is Q = mLv. In this table’s units, 2256 kJ/kg is 2256 J/g. This is not Harvey/NIST’s 0 °C table, and it is not last week’s difference 2834 − 333.4, which this course refused to treat as an official 100 °C figure. Near-miss: Not Harvey’s ΔfusH(0 °C) = 333.4 J/g, or ΔsublH(0 °C) = 2834 J/g, already taught from a different table. OpenStax also lists Lf = 334 kJ/kg for water; that is close to Harvey’s 333.4 J/g, but the two figures are from different tables and are not blended. Evaporation below the boiling point, which OpenStax also discusses, is reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. 56.4 kJ.
  2. Q = mLv, with Lv = 2256 kJ/kg = 2256 J/g.
  3. That 2834 J/g is Harvey’s enthalpy of sublimation at 0 °C, a different change of phase at a different temperature and from a different table.
  4. OpenStax discusses it, but it is not today’s first look, which is Lv at the normal boiling point from Table 14.2.

Literature · The first circle: sighs, and “without hope we live on in desire” (Inferno IV.1–42)

Baptism, the portal of the Faith, or, if they were before Christianity, they adored not God in the right manner. He places himself among them. “Without hope we live on in desire.” 5. Model: the first circle (Inferno IV). Not Thursday’s naming of Limbo, or of the Mighty One, or of the poets, which remain reserved until then. Near-miss: Not a later theological label imported as if it were Longfellow’s wording here. Not Thursday’s “Limbo,” which is named at line 45, after this stretch. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Modern language · Spanish: pedir, contrasted in present and preterite

Check. 1. Present: pido, pides, pide, pedimos, pedís, piden. Preterite: pedí, pediste, pidió, pedimos, pedisteis, pidieron. 2. Present: all forms except nosotros and vosotros. Preterite: only the two third-person forms. 3. The form is the same and keeps e in both tenses. A time word, or the rest of the sentence, tells the tense: ahora pedimos (present), ayer pedimos (preterite). 4. Sample: Ahora pido un libro. Ayer ella pidió ayuda. 5. Model: pedir in present and preterite, contrasted. Own words should match this idea: The present of pedir, already taught, changes e to i in all forms except nosotros and vosotros: pido, pides, pide, pedimos, pedís, piden. The preterite, yesterday’s first look, changes e to i only in the third persons: pedí, pediste, pidió, pedimos, pedisteis, pidieron. Pedimos appears in both tenses and keeps e in both; the ending and the time word tell them apart. Near-miss: Not a first look at either set, except that the preterite was new yesterday. Not dormir, which is tomorrow. Instruction remains in English. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Now we ask for (or order). Yesterday we asked for (or ordered).
  2. The preterite first-person form is pedí, with e and the preterite ending -í. Pido is present.
  3. The present was taught on Day 21. Today is a contrast with yesterday’s preterite.
  4. Dormir, which changes o to u in the same two preterite forms. Do not force e → i onto dormir.

Elective · Music: inversion of a short invented figure

Check. 1. A short figure turned upside down: each interval keeps its size and changes its direction, from a chosen starting note. 2. A sequence repeats the same figure, right-side up, at a different pitch. An inversion turns the intervals over. 3. Up a step, then down a skip. 4. This course does not copy a copyrighted score. The figures are invented classroom ones. 5. Model: inversion of a short figure. Own words should match this idea: An inversion, in this lesson’s school sense, takes a short figure and turns its intervals upside down: a step up becomes a step down of the same size, a skip down becomes a skip up, and so on, starting from a chosen note. It is not last week’s sequence, which repeats the same figure at a different pitch, still right-side up. Classroom figures are invented and are not copied from a copyrighted score. Near-miss: Not a first look at last week’s sequence, or at the already-taught period and cadences. Not a tonal or real inversion of a copyrighted theme, which this course does not copy. Not a first look at invertible counterpoint, which is reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A sound answer will label one repetition at a new pitch as the sequence, and one upside-down version as the inversion, both on an invented figure.
  2. So the inversion can be heard as the same shape turned over, not as an unrelated new figure.
  3. It is a later craft, not today’s first look at a single invented line turned upside down.
  4. They are classroom examples, not official themes and not copied scores.
