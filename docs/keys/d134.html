Latin · Sequor and ēgredior, a second mixed practice of present and pluperfect

Check. 1. I follow, or I am following. She had gone out. You (pl.) had followed him. 2. Ēgredimur: nunc asks for the present, we go out. Secūtus erās: the following was already completed. 3. A fair pair: Puella mē secūta erat. Puerī ēgressī erant, et tum tacēbant.

Exercises. 1. Secūtus erat, he had followed; secūta erat, she had followed. Ēgressus erat, he had gone out; ēgressa erat, she had gone out. 2. Ēgressus erās; secūtī erāmus. 3. The participle must agree. With Puella the form is secūta erat.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXVIII: the law of sines in a non-right triangle

Check. 1. 4√3, and the common ratio is 8. 2. 120° = 180° − 60°, so sin 120° = sin 60° = √3/2. The 4 is a classroom length, not an official measurement. 3. Taking sin 120° as −√3/2, or treating the triangle as right-angled. The ambiguous case, the law of cosines, and radians remain reserved.

Exercises. 1. Area = (1/2) × 4 × 4 × sin 120° = 8 × (√3/2) = 4√3. 2. x / sin 30° = 6 / sin 120°, so x / (1/2) = 6 / (√3/2), and x = 6 / √3 = 2√3. 3. The derivation from the area formula did not assume a right angle, and sin 120° is still a positive sine, already taught from the unit circle.

Writing · A tighter original chreia: one saying, one reason, still short

Check. 1. A fair tighter piece keeps one person, one brief occasion, one saying or deed, and one sentence on why it fits, with invented figures labeled invented. 2. The reason is tied to this occasion; a fable’s moral is a stated general lesson, often after an animal story. A narrative tells a complete event in order, without a required saying. 3. A maxim, and a full later elaboration. Do not invent official facts about a historical person, or name a living person.

Exercises. 1. Diogenes, found in the sun and asked whether he wanted anything, says, “I would have you stand from between me and the sun.” It fits because he asks for no gift, only that the sun not be blocked. 2. The chreia is the short attributed saying, not the rest of Alexander’s war. 3. A maxim is a general saying without the anecdote, and it remains reserved.

Science · Malachite, a green copper carbonate, a first look

Check. 1. A green copper carbonate mineral. The USGS record gives Cu2CO3(OH)2. 2. Azurite is the related blue copper carbonate, Cu3(CO3)2(OH)2. Ultramarine is the blue pigment from lapis lazuli, not a copper carbonate. 3. Invent no official copper percentage, and no official price. A detailed workshop recipe, a synthetic verditer, and a fifth atelier tube remain reserved. 4. Model: malachite. Own words should match this idea: Malachite is a green copper carbonate mineral, related to Tuesday’s azurite but not the same mineral. The USGS Spectral Library, Clark et al., 2003, Open-File Report 03-395, sample HS254, records malachite with the formula Cu2CO3(OH)2. Hunt and Salisbury, quoted in that record, call it a basic carbonate of copper, “more common than azurite.” Invent no official copper percentage of a classroom stone, and no official price. Near-miss: Not azurite, Tuesday’s blue copper carbonate, whose formula is Cu3(CO3)2(OH)2. Not last week’s ultramarine, and not a fifth tube in the atelier palette. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Azurite, Cu3(CO3)2(OH)2; malachite, Cu2CO3(OH)2. They call malachite “more common than azurite,” and still only a minor ore of copper. 2. They group the same copper, carbonate, and hydroxide in two written forms. The USGS heading formula is Cu2CO3(OH)2. 3. A modern synthetic pigment would be a later, sourced look, not an invented process, and it is not being added to the palette.

Literature · Pride and Prejudice, Chapter 3: the overheard remark, and the report at home

Check. 1. “She is tolerable; but not handsome enough to tempt me; … for you are wasting your mite with me.” Elizabeth “told the story however with great spirit among her friends.” 2. Bingley calls Jane “the most beautiful creature I ever beheld.” Darcy, after looking at Elizabeth, calls her “tolerable; but not handsome enough to tempt me.” 3. They are 1813 forms on the set page. Teaching prose may say ankle, humor, and, if the word is needed outside the quote, mite only as the book’s word. Chapter 4 remains reserved.

They are the novel’s household words, not a description of a living person. 3. Today finishes the set Chapter 3. The next chapter remains reserved.

Atelier · A small still life of an object and a cloth, in the limited palette

Check. 1. One simple object and a cloth, under one lamp, painted with yellow ochre, an earth red, black, and white, and with the mixed earth hue. 2. Value first, across both, then a lean toward a hue without losing the value. A miss would be hue first, a fifth tube, or a portrait. 3. Invent no official length of the object or of the cloth. A portrait, a fifth tube, and azurite, malachite, or ultramarine as a palette color remain reserved.

Exercises. 1. By matching lightness, even if one is ochre and one is the mixed hue. 2. The subject is an object and a cloth, not a likeness of a face. 3. The limited palette remains four paints, with mixtures from that set only.

Modern language · Spanish: regular and irregular conditionals, beside the future

Check. 1. Both use -ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -ían. Hablaría keeps the infinitive hablar; haría uses the changed stem har-. 2. A fair trio: Mañana hablaré con ella, invented. En tu lugar, lo haría. Mañana iríamos al parque, invented place. 3. They would treat hacer and tener as regular in this tense. The subjunctive remains reserved.

Exercises. 1. Tomorrow I will say it, or I will tell it. She would go out, or she would leave. We would live here, invented. You (tú) would know. 2. I will come, future; I would come, or he or she would come, conditional; we would eat, conditional. 3. Hablaré and hablaría; haré and haría; iré and iría, according to will or would.

Seminar · Economics: insurance on invented figures, distinguished from a tax

Check. 1. An invented arrangement in which a named premium is paid in advance, and a named payment would be made if a named invented event happens. An income tax is a percent taken from invented income, not a premium paid for a named event. 2. The year’s premium is $120. The $500 would be paid only if the named invented event happens. 3. The figures are invented classroom numbers, not an official product or a living person’s policy, and the lesson is not a game of stakes. 4. Model: insurance (invented figures). Own words should match this idea: Insurance, in this first look, is an invented arrangement in which a named premium is paid in advance, and a named payment would be made if a named invented event happens. The figures are invented classroom numbers, not an official policy or a living rate. An invented premium of $10 each month for twelve months is $120 paid in advance; an invented payment of $500 would be made only if the named event happens. Near-miss: Not last week’s income tax, which was a percent taken from invented income, and not the earlier sales tax, which was a percent added to a price. Not a gambling lesson, and not an official insurance product or rate. Not a claim about a living person’s policy. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. 12 × 15 = $180, invented. The $300, invented, would be paid only if the named event happens. 2. $84 was an invented purchase price plus a sales tax, not an amount paid in advance for a named event. 3. Official published rates, deductibles, and claims about a living person’s policy.
