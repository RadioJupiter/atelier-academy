Latin · Review of Capitula I–X: cūr and quia

Check. 1. Cūr puella rīdet? 2. Puella rīdet, quia rosam habet. 3. Model: cūr and quia. Own words should match this idea: A question-and-reason pair already taught in Capitula I–X (Day 7): cūr asks why; quia begins the because-clause. Reviewed today in original sentences, not as a new chapter. Near-miss: Not a licence to invent later-chapter conjunctions, and not the same job as sed (which contrasts, but does not give a reason). The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. cūr is the question; quia begins the reason.
  2. Both heard and used.
  3. sed contrasts two clauses; quia gives a reason. “The girl laughs, but the boy does not” is contrast, not a because.
  4. A copied question-and-reason pair.

Mathematics · Geometry XXIII: a tangent and a secant (Euclid III.36)

Check. 1. PT² = 36, so PT = 6. 2. PB = 20 (100 = 5 × PB). 3. Model: secant. Own words should match this idea: A line that cuts a circle at two points. In today’s figure the secant is the line through P, A, and B. Near-miss: Not a tangent (which meets the circle at exactly one point), and not a chord named without its line. The chord is the segment AB; the secant is the line that contains it. the tangent-secant theorem. Own words should match this idea: If from a point P outside a circle a tangent touches the circle at T and a secant cuts the circle at A (nearer) and B (farther), then PT² = PA × PB. This is Euclid III.36. Near-miss: Not the intersecting-chords theorem (Euclid III.35), which is for two chords that meet inside the circle, and not a licence to invent a length we have not found. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. The tangent meets the circle only at T, so the two lengths from P along that line, to the point of contact, are the same length PT. Their product is PT × PT = PT².
  2. III.36 is a product, not a sum. 4 × PB = 36, so PB = 9, not 10.
  3. III.35 is two chords meeting inside the circle; III.36 is a tangent and a secant from a point outside. Both compare products of lengths, in different pictures.
  4. AB = 9 − 4 = 5, if A is between P and B. The theorem multiplies PA by PB (P to A, and P to B), not PA by AB.

Near-miss: Not a paraphrase passed off as the extract, and not a licence to continue into unset parts of the letter as if they had been set.   3. A checked copy, with attribution.
  4. It keeps the words in a 1900 English translation, not in Pliny’s Latin and not in a modern unsourced retelling.

Science · Corrective lenses, as a tool (not a classroom diagnosis)

Check. 1. Eyeglasses and contact lenses; surgery is listed by NEI but is not taught here. 2. The unit of lens strength has not been taught, and inventing one would pretend to a precision this lesson does not have. 3. Model: corrective lenses, as a tool. Own words should match this idea: Spectacles or contact lenses can change how light is focused on the retina, so a refractive error already taught (myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism) may be compensated. This course names that job; it does not diagnose an eye, prescribe a lens, or invent a diopter. Near-miss: Not a diagnosis, not a prescription, and not a claim that a lens “cures” the eye. Presbyopia was already named from the same NEI page; we still do not invent a classroom test for it. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism, and presbyopia.
  2. It belongs to an eye-care professional, not to a classroom diagnosis.
  3. A lens can compensate a refractive error. This lesson does not claim that the eye’s own optics have been made different, or that a disease has been cured.
  4. A note that the sitting is not a clinic and not a reward.

Modern language · Spanish: también and tampoco (present)

Check. 1. Yo también. 2. Yo tampoco. 3. Model: también and tampoco. Own words should match this idea: Two present-tense agreement words: también agrees with a positive statement (also); tampoco agrees with a negative (neither / not either). Instruction stays in English. Near-miss: Not a past tense, and not a mix-up that uses también after a no-sentence as if it meant neither. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. tampoco, not también no, in this pattern.
  2. Both heard and used.
  3. tampoco is the agreement-word for a negative. también would agree with a positive.
  4. Two copied agreements.

Music · A half cadence, as a pause, not an end

Check. 1. A half cadence ends on V, a pause. An authentic cadence moves V to I and can close. 2. Day 27 taught one close first, and reserved the others. Today adds only the half cadence, still in a major key with I, IV, and V. A deceptive cadence can still wait. 3. Model: half cadence. Own words should match this idea: A common-practice pause that ends on V, the dominant, rather than closing to I. It sounds unfinished: a stop in the middle of a thought, not an ending of the piece. It is not the already-taught authentic cadence (V–I). Near-miss: Not an authentic cadence, which moves V to I and can close. Not a claim that every stop on a G chord in any style is a half cadence. This is a common-practice school pattern, taught as such. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. I–V is the pause (half); V–I can close (authentic). In C major, V is G–B–D and I is C–E–G.
  2. This is a common-practice school pattern. A stop in another style, or on another chord, is not automatically a half cadence.
  3. A sound sentence: it pauses because it ends on V, but it has not moved to I, so it is not an authentic close.
  4. A note that the seminar is not a reward.
