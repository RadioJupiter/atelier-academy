Latin · Friday mix: sequor and ēgredior, subjunctive and future

Check. 1. Sequar, sequāris, sequātur, sequāmur, sequāminī, sequantur. Sequātur, with ā, is the present subjunctive; sequētur, with ē, is the future. 2. Ēgrediar, ēgrediāris, ēgrediātur, ēgrediāmur, ēgrediāminī, ēgrediantur. Like sequar, ēgrediar is spelled the same as the future first-person singular. 3. A purpose clause with ut has not been taught this week; the job was the forms, contrasted with the future.

A purpose clause with ut remains reserved.

History · The letter’s date, and the Lisbon postscript, from Major 1870

Check. 1. The letter: “off the Canary Islands, on the fifteenth of February, fourteen hundred and ninety-three.” The postscript: “Done on the fourteenth day of March.” 2. After the letter was written, “there arose a south-west wind,” which compelled him “to lighten my vessels and run this day into this port of Lisbon.” “In all the Indies I have always found the weather like that in the month of May.” 3. It is the letter’s wording, but Major’s footnote disputes that figure, so it is not taught as a verified official duration. 4. Model: the date of the first-voyage letter, and the Lisbon postscript. Own words should match this idea: In Major’s 1870 wording, the letter closes: “This, thus briefly, in accordance with the events. Done on board the caravel, off the Canary Islands, on the fifteenth of February, fourteen hundred and ninety-three. At your orders. THE ADMIRAL.” A postscript then says that after the letter was written, “as I was in the sea of Castille, there arose a south-west wind, which compelled me to lighten my vessels and run this day into this port of Lisbon.” It also says, “In all the Indies I have always found the weather like that in the month of May. I reached them in thirty-three days,” and it is “Done on the fourteenth day of March.” Those are the letter’s and postscript’s own datings and claims, confirmed from the 1870 pages. “Castille” and “marvellous,” later in the same postscript, stay inside quotations. The postscript’s “returned in twenty-eight” is the letter’s wording, not taught as a verified official duration: Major’s footnote disputes that figure. Major also notes that Columbus arrived at Lisbon on the 4th of March; that is Major’s note, not a figure invented here. Near-miss: Not a verified official duration of the return from the postscript’s “twenty-eight,” which Major’s footnote disputes. Not an invented official modern conversion of those dates. Not a first look at a later voyage. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. It matches the already-taught opening of the letter, the claim of reaching the Indies in thirty-three days. 2. Major notes that Columbus arrived at Lisbon on the 4th of March. That is Major’s note, not a figure invented here, and it is not used to replace the postscript’s own dating. 3. A fortress, a sufficient number of men, arms, artillery, provisions for more than a year, a barge, and a sailing master; then the letter’s claim of “the greatest friendship” and “calling me his brother.”

Spanish · Friday mix: the present perfect, hay, and haya

Check. 1. He, has, ha, hemos, habéis, han, plus a regular participle. Ha is the helping form, he has, or she has; hay is there is, or there are; haya is that there be. 2. You (pl.) have lived; I hope that there be time. Han is a helping form before a participle; haya is the present subjunctive, “that there be,” not a taught helping form here. 3. They are irregular participles and remain reserved.

Exercises. 1. I have spoken; there is, or there are; that there be; I would speak. 2. A fair set: he hablado, I have spoken; has comido, you (s.) have eaten; ha vivido, he has lived, or she has lived; hemos hablado, we have spoken; habéis comido, you (pl.) have eaten; han vivido, they have lived. 3. Irregular participles, and a present-perfect subjunctive, remain reserved.

Seminar · Anatomy: the twelfth thoracic vertebra, from Gray, 1918

Not an official length. Not a diagnosis, and not a body described from life. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.
