Latin · Result clauses, reviewed

Check. 1. She comes so quickly that she follows him (positive result). She comes so slowly that she does not follow him (negative result). He rises so quickly that he goes out (positive result). 2. A negative result uses ut nōn, not nē. 3. A result often has tam, ita, or sic, and a negative result uses ut nōn. A purpose marks an intended purpose: ut, or nē when negative.

Exercises. 1. Model: Puella tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur, and Puella tam lentē venit ut eum nōn sequātur. 2. Model: Puella manet nē eum sequātur, a negative purpose, with no tam marking a result.

Mathematics · Sine, cosine, and tangent, reviewed

Check. 1. Sin A = opposite/hypotenuse, cos A = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan A = opposite/adjacent, and tan A = sin A / cos A. 2. Sin A = 3/5, cos A = 4/5, tan A = 3/4. 3. Sin A = 5/13, cos A = 12/13, tan A = 5/12.

Exercises. 1. Tan A = 8/15. Sin A = 8/17, cos A = 15/17, and (8/17)/(15/17) = 8/15. 2. They are classroom figures, not official measurements.

Writing · Invective, reviewed

Exercises. 1. Model: a short blame of the already-used invented type, with already-taught reasons. 2. It treats one type by blame, not two types side by side.

Science · Official household 120 V, official Example 20.9, and official Example 20.8, reviewed

Exercises. 1. V_rms. 2. 12.0 V was the official voltage of the headlight in a different official example.
