Latin · Agreement, and et or sed

Check. 1. Puella laeta rosam habet. 2. Puer et puella rīdent.

  1. Two subjects joined by et normally take a plural verb: rīdent, not rīdet.
  2. When the second clause contrasts with the first, not merely adds to it.
  3. Two copied model sentences.

Mathematics · A tangent and a secant (Euclid III.36)

Check. 1. PT² = 36, so PT = 6 (a length is positive). 2. PB = 20.

  1. PB = 25/4 = 6.25; AB = 6.25 − 4 = 2.25.
  2. The tangent meets the circle only at T, so the two lengths from P along that line are both PT, and their product is PT × PT = PT².
  3. Those 4 and 9 would be roots in a different picture, unless a question has actually given a tangent and a secant with those lengths.

Writing & Rhetoric · An attributed quotation

Check. 1. “…it was afterwards discovered to have been Mount Vesuvius…” (Firth, 1900, Attalus text). 2. Pompeii, and a death toll.

  1. For example: J. B. Firth, trans., letter of Pliny the Younger to Tacitus, 1900, as posted at Attalus (some wording modified).
  2. A paraphrase is in your own words and is still attributed; it is not the source’s wording in quotation marks.
  3. Stabiae is a later reserved part of the letter, not in the set extract.

Science · How we see

Check. 1. Astigmatism: far-away and nearby objects look blurry or distorted. 2. Presbyopia: hard for middle-aged and older adults to see things up close; the NEI names it as common in adults 45 and older.

  1. Eyeball length, corneal shape, or aging of the lens (any two). This course will not invent diopter numbers (or cell counts).
  2. The module teaches sourced wording and a tool, not a diagnosis of a person in the room.

Literature · The set Dryden extracts

Check. 1. “All were attentive to the God-like Man;” — Dryden, Aeneid Book II, 1697. 2. Any two of: Tenedos, the gates, Sinon, Priam, later night scenes, the departure, Creusa, later books.

  1. “Thus they pretend, but in the hollow Side,” — Dryden, 1697. Souldiers is Dryden’s spelling in the 1697 English, not a claim that Virgil wrote that English word.
  2. The steed passage gives the fabric and the pretended vow. The hollow-side lines, set later, give the hidden soldiers. Both are already set; neither opens Sinon or the gates.

Atelier · A value check of two forms

Check. 1. Half-close them, so the light and dark masses are easier to compare. 2. They are thrown by the same lamp on the same ground.

  1. Light, middle, and dark (and the cast shadow as a separate dark on the ground).
  2. The sphere, still a sphere, not a face.
  3. A corrected thumb-nail, if needed.

Modern language · Spanish: también and tampoco

Check. 1. Yo también. 2. Yo tampoco.

  1. tampoco, not también no, in this pattern.
  2. Both heard and used.
  3. tampoco is the agreement-word for a negative.

Music · A close and a pause: authentic and half cadences

Check. 1. A half cadence ends on V, a pause. An authentic cadence moves V to I and can close. 2. I–V is the pause (half); V–I can close (authentic). In C major, V is G–B–D and I is C–E–G.

  1. This is a common-practice school pattern. A stop in another style, or on another chord, is not automatically a half cadence.
  2. Only the authentic cadence and the half cadence have been taught so far. The third type is still later.
  3. A sound sentence: it pauses because it ends on V, but it has not moved to I, so it is not an authentic close.
