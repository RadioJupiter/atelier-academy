Latin · Agreement, and et or sed: a Term 1 close

Check. 1. Puella laeta rosam habet. 2. Puer et puella rīdent. 3. Model: agreement and et or sed, a Term 1 close. Own words should match this idea: A close of two already-taught habits: an adjective agrees with its noun in case, number, and gender, and et joins while sed contrasts. They are different jobs, still in Capitula I–X. Near-miss: Not a new chapter, and not a licence to use et where the sense is contrast, or to leave an adjective in a different case from its noun. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Two subjects joined by et normally take a plural verb: rīdent, not rīdet.
  2. When the second clause contrasts with the first, not merely adds to it.
  3. Two copied model sentences.
  4. A note that Capitula I–X still bound the work.

Mathematics · A tangent and a secant (Euclid III.36): a Term 1 close

Check. 1. PT² = 36, so PT = 6 (a length is positive). 2. PB = 20. 3. Model: a tangent and a secant, a Term 1 close. Own words should match this idea: A close of Euclid III.36, already taught: if from a point P outside a circle a tangent touches at T and a secant cuts at A (nearer) and B (farther), then PT² = PA × PB. Lengths are positive. This is not the intersecting-chords theorem (III.35), and not a quadratic inequality. Near-miss: Not a sum (4 + 6 = 10 is not the theorem), not a quadratic’s roots, and not III.35. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. PB = 25/4 = 6.25; AB = 6.25 − 4 = 2.25.
  2. The tangent meets the circle only at T, so the two lengths from P along that line are both PT, and their product is PT × PT = PT².
  3. Those 4 and 9 would be roots in a different picture, unless a question has actually given a tangent and a secant with those lengths.
  4. A note that intersecting chords stay in their own figure.

Writing & Rhetoric · An attributed quotation: a Term 1 close

Check. 1. “…it was afterwards discovered to have been Mount Vesuvius…” (Firth, 1900, Attalus text). 2. Pompeii, and a death toll. 3. Model: an attributed quotation, a Term 1 close. Own words should match this idea: A close of the already-taught habit of quoting only a set extract and attributing it: the words themselves, plus the source. A paraphrase is not a quotation. Firth’s English is not Pliny’s Latin, and Dryden’s English is not Virgil’s Latin. Near-miss: Not a later reserved part of the letter, not an unattributed block, and not a claim that Pliny wrote these English words. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. For example: J. B. Firth, trans., letter of Pliny the Younger to Tacitus, 1900, as posted at Attalus (some wording modified).
  2. A paraphrase is in your own words and is still attributed; it is not the source’s wording in quotation marks.
  3. Stabiae is a later reserved part of the letter, not in the set extract.
  4. A note that the extract does not grow today.

Science · How we see: a Term 1 close

Check. 1. Astigmatism: far-away and nearby objects look blurry or distorted. 2. Presbyopia: hard for middle-aged and older adults to see things up close; the NEI names it as common in adults 45 and older. 3. Model: How we see, a Term 1 close. Own words should match this idea: A close of the Term 1 seeing module already taught: the eye as a kind of camera, accommodation, refractive errors from the National Eye Institute (myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism, presbyopia), and corrective lenses as a tool. The habit is sourced claims, with no invented cell counts, no invented diopters, and no classroom diagnosis. Near-miss: Not a new disease name, not a prescription, not invented diopters, and not a classroom diagnosis. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Eyeball length, corneal shape, or aging of the lens (any two). This course will not invent diopter numbers (or cell counts).
  2. The module teaches sourced wording and a tool, not a diagnosis of a person in the room.
  3. The examination is Thursday.
  4. A note that the sitting is not a reward.

Literature · The set Dryden extracts: a Term 1 close

Check. 1. “All were attentive to the God-like Man;” — Dryden, Aeneid Book II, 1697. 2. Any two of: Tenedos, the gates, Sinon, Priam, later night scenes, the departure, Creusa, later books. 3. Model: the set Dryden extracts, a Term 1 close. Own words should match this idea: A close of the three short, already-set extracts from Dryden’s 1697 Aeneid Book II: the opening four lines, the five-line steed passage, and the four hollow-side lines. The English is Dryden’s, not Virgil’s Latin. Later of Book II (Tenedos, the gates, Sinon, Priam, later books) remains reserved. Near-miss: Not a new extract, not a claim that Virgil wrote these English words, and not a summary of reserved later scenes as if they had been set. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. “Thus they pretend, but in the hollow Side,” — Dryden, 1697. Souldiers is Dryden’s spelling in the 1697 English, not a claim that Virgil wrote that English word.
  2. The steed passage gives the fabric and the pretended vow. The hollow-side lines, already set later, give the hidden soldiers. Both are already set; neither opens Sinon or the gates.
  3. A note that no fourth extract is opened.
  4. The examination is Thursday.

Music · A close and a pause: authentic and half cadences, a Term 1 close

Check. 1. A half cadence ends on V, a pause. An authentic cadence moves V to I and can close. 2. I–V is the pause (half); V–I can close (authentic). In C major, V is G–B–D and I is C–E–G. 3. Model: a close and a pause, a Term 1 close. Own words should match this idea: A close of two already-taught common-practice cadence types in a major key, with I, IV, and V only: an authentic cadence (V–I) can close; a half cadence ends on V and is a pause, not an end. A deceptive cadence can still wait. Near-miss: Not a claim that every stop in any style is a half cadence, and not a first look at a deceptive cadence. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. This is a common-practice school pattern. A stop in another style, or on another chord, is not automatically a half cadence.
  2. Day 27 reserved it, and last week added only the half cadence. Today still does not begin that third type.
  3. A sound sentence: it pauses because it ends on V, but it has not moved to I, so it is not an authentic close.
  4. A note that the seminar is not a reward.
