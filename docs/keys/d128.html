Latin · Present sequor and ēgredior, mixed with already-taught deponent tenses

Check. 1. Sequor: nunc asks for the present, I follow, not I had spoken. 2. Ēgreditur: puer is third person, he goes out. Ēgredior is I go out. 3. Sequor, sequeris, sequitur, sequimur, sequiminī, sequuntur. Ēgredior, ēgrederis, ēgreditur, ēgredimur, ēgrediminī, ēgrediuntur.

Exercises. 1. You (s.) follow. We go out. I had spoken. 2. A fair pair: Nōs sequimur. Tū ēgrederis. 3. Only the present tables of sequor and ēgredior have been taught. A pluperfect of sequor would be a new first look, and it remains reserved.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXIII: 30°, 45°, and 60° on the unit circle, a first look

Check. 1. The legs 1, 1 have hypotenuse √2. Scaling by 1/√2 gives P = (√2/2, √2/2). Sin 45° = cos 45° = √2/2. 2. From a half equilateral of side 2, then divide by 2: at 30°, P is (√3/2, 1/2), so cos 30° = √3/2, sin 30° = 1/2. At 60°, P is (1/2, √3/2), so cos 60° = 1/2, sin 60° = √3/2. 3. The unit circle has radius 1, so the hypotenuse must be 1. A decimal is an approximation, not the exact value taught today. 4. Model: sine and cosine of 30°, 45°, and 60° on the unit circle. Own words should match this idea: The already-taught 45° isosceles right triangle, and the 30°-60° triangle from half an equilateral triangle, are scaled so that the hypotenuse is 1, and then placed on the unit circle. At 45°, P is (√2/2, √2/2). At 30°, P is (√3/2, 1/2). At 60°, P is (1/2, √3/2). These are exact values, not official measured lengths of a real object. Near-miss: Not a decimal approximation treated as an official replacement of the exact value. Not a first look at radians. Not a new triangle; the triangles were already taught, and today they are scaled to hypotenuse 1. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Sin 30° = 1/2, cos 30° = √3/2, sin 60° = √3/2, cos 60° = 1/2. 2. They are complementary angles, already taught: sin(90° − 60°) = cos 60°, and 30° = 90° − 60°. On the circle, the y at 30° is the x at 60°. 3. Radians remain reserved. Signs in other quadrants wait until tomorrow.

Writing · A tighter narrative: order, time, and place, with no moral

Check. 1. A fair tighter narrative is four or five short sentences of action, in order, with a time and a place, and with no moral. 2. A fair cut is any sentence of advice, or a moral such as “care is wise,” or an event told out of order that you put back in sequence. 3. It has no stated moral at the end. If you added one, it would be a fable.

Exercises. 1. Both are short complete actions, in order, with when and where, and without a moral. 2. So the action is situated, and the account stays a narrative rather than a floating list of events. 3. Chreia and maxim remain reserved.

Physics · Intensity under constructive interference, from OpenStax Example 16.10

Check. 1. 4.00 W/m². 2.00 W/m² would add the intensities as if intensity followed amplitude, not amplitude squared. 2. The area of 4.00 W/m² is smaller than the area the waves covered before, and there are places where the intensity is zero. 3. I′ = 8.00 W/m². 4.00 W/m² is Example 16.10’s official result from 1.00 W/m², or a mistaken doubling of 2.00. 4. Model: intensity under constructive interference. Own words should match this idea: In OpenStax Example 16.10, intensity is proportional to amplitude squared. If two identical waves interfere perfectly constructively, the amplitude doubles, so the intensity becomes four times as great, not two times. For two waves each of intensity 1.00 W/m², the resulting intensity at that location is 4.00 W/m². Energy is not created: the area of 4.00 W/m² is smaller than the area the two waves covered before, and there are other places where the intensity is zero. Near-miss: Not a simple sum of the two intensities, 2.00 W/m², which would ignore that intensity follows amplitude squared. Not a claim that energy appears from nowhere. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. E = 5.04 × 10^6 J, from I = 700 W/m², A = 0.500 m², t = 4.00 h. 2. The resulting amplitude is zero, so the intensity at that point is zero. 3. Decibels, and a first look at sound as Chapter 17, remain reserved.

History · Inter Caetera, May 4: the proviso, and the birthday from which 1493 begins

Check. 1. None of the lands beyond the line are to be in the actual possession of any Christian king or prince up to the birthday of our Lord Jesus Christ just past from which the present year 1493 begins. 2. No right acquired by any Christian prince in actual possession prior to that birthday is to be understood as withdrawn or taken away. 3. May 3 had a proviso about a Christian owner, but it did not draw the hundred-league line or name this birthday. The Treaty of Tordesillas, the first-voyage letter, and a conversion of a hundred leagues into an official modern measurement remain reserved.

Exercises. 1. To go, for trade or any other reason, to the islands or mainlands west and south of the line, without a special permit from the rulers of Castile or their heirs. 2. That is the wording in the grant. A later short name is a gloss; the lesson starts from the document. 3. An official island count, an official modern length of a league, and an official longitude of the line.

Atelier · A value match between the mixed hue and ochre

Check. 1. An ochre step and a mixed-earth-hue step of the same lightness, checked by half-closing the eyes or by comparing the two as values. 2. Changing the hue in order to make the colors look the same, rather than matching lightness. Last week the same value match was taught between ochre and the unmixed earth red. 3. A fifth tube, ultramarine as a palette color, and a portrait.

Exercises. 1. That would be a hue match, and it would undo the mixed hue. Today’s job is a value match across different hues. 2. Value is lightness, already taught in monochrome. Half-closing the eyes reduces the pull of hue. 3. The job is a match between two scales, not a likeness of a face.

Modern language · Spanish: irregular future of poder, saber, and querer

Check. 1. Podré, I will be able; sabré, I will know; querré, I will want. Querré is written with rr. 2. A fair pair: Mañana podremos terminar, invented task. Mañana lo sabré. 3. *Poderé would treat poder as regular in this tense. Conditional and subjunctive remain reserved.

Exercises. 1. She will not be able to go, invented. We will know it tomorrow. They will want to see it, invented. 2. You (tú) will be able; we will know; you (vosotros) will want. 3. Podré; querré.

Seminar · Art history: Dewing, Buildings I.3.6–9, the shrine at Pegê

Check. 1. Another shrine of the Virgin, in the place called Pegê. A dense grove of cypresses, a flowering meadow in soft glebe, a park of beautiful shrubs, and a spring bubbling silently forth with a gentle stream of sweet water. 2. The church is not easy to describe as it deserves; “it surpasses most shrines both in beauty and in size.” Invent no official length, and no official shrine count. 3. Both were erected outside the city-wall, as “invincible defences” to the circuit-wall. Blachernae is the one by the sea; Pegê is the one close to the Golden Gate. 4. Model: the shrine at Pegê. Own words should match this idea: In Dewing, Buildings I.3.6–9, the 1940 Loeb translation as posted on LacusCurtius, Justinian “dedicated to the Virgin another shrine in the place called Pegê.” The surroundings are a dense grove of cypresses, a flowering meadow, a park of shrubs, and “a spring bubbling silently forth with a gentle stream of sweet water.” Of the church itself Dewing says “it surpasses most shrines both in beauty and in size.” Both this church and last week’s church at Blachernae were erected outside the city-wall, “in order that both of them may serve as invincible defences to the circuit-wall of the city.” Invent no official length, and no official shrine count. Near-miss: Not last week’s church at Blachernae as if it were this second shrine. Not the Heraeum, which begins at I.3.10 and remains reserved. Not an official modern measurement of the church or of the spring. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. “A spring bubbling silently forth with a gentle stream of sweet water.” “It must suffice to say only this, that it surpasses most shrines both in beauty and in size.” 2. Dewing says they were placed outside the wall so that both may serve as “invincible defences” to the circuit-wall. “Defences” is Dewing’s spelling, and it stays inside the quotation; teaching prose may say defenses. 3. The Heraeum, from I.3.10. Official lengths, and an official shrine count, remain reserved.
