Latin · Friday mix: the present of sequor and ēgredior, with already-taught deponent tenses

Check. 1. Sequor, sequeris, sequitur, sequimur, sequiminī, sequuntur. Ēgredior, ēgrederis, ēgreditur, ēgredimur, ēgrediminī, ēgrediuntur. 2. Ēgredior: nunc, I go out, is present. Locūtus eram is I had spoken. 3. Ēgrediminī: you (pl.) go out. Sequiminī would be you follow.

Exercises. 1. You (s.) follow. He goes out (Puer ēgreditur). I had spoken, and then I was silent. 2. A fair pair: Nōs sequimur. Tū ēgrederis. 3. A pluperfect of sequor, and any tense of these two verbs beyond the present, remain reserved.

Mathematics · Friday mix: the unit circle, exact values, and signs

Check. 1. The circle of radius 1 centered at the origin. Cos θ = x, sin θ = y. 2. 0°: sin 0, cos 1. 30°: sin 1/2, cos √3/2. 45°: both √2/2. 60°: sin √3/2, cos 1/2. 90°: sin 1, cos 0. 3. 150°: sin 1/2, cos −√3/2. 270°: sin −1, cos 0. 330°: sin −1/2, cos √3/2. Second: sine +, cosine −. Fourth: sine −, cosine +.

Exercises. 1. An isosceles right triangle with legs 1 has hypotenuse √2. Scaling to hypotenuse 1 gives legs √2/2. 2. Sin 210° = −1/2, cos 210° = −√3/2, third quadrant, reference angle 30°. 3. Radians remain reserved. A decimal approximates √2/2; it does not replace the exact value.

Writing · Friday mix: a narrative, and a fable distinguished from it

Check. 1. A fair narrative is a short complete invented action, in order, with a time and a place, and with no moral. 2. A fable would keep a short action and add a stated moral at the end. 3. A mid-story lecture, a moral inside the narrative, or a change of order that hides the action. Chreia and maxim remain reserved.

Exercises. 1. Narrative: noon, the desk, the open jar, the sheet, the sheet pulled back, the jar closed, and no moral. Fable: the same action, then a stated moral such as “A lid is cheaper than a spoiled page.” 2. Otherwise the piece is no longer a narrative in this week’s sense; it has become a fable. 3. “Necessity is the mother of invention” is Townsend’s moral, and it belongs to that fable, not to a narrative.

Physics · Friday mix: intensity, Example 16.9, and intensity under constructive interference

Check. 1. Intensity is power per unit area. Energy is proportional to amplitude squared. E = 5.04 × 10^6 J; concentrated I′ = 1.40 × 10^5 W/m². 2. The amplitude doubles, so the intensity is four times, 4.00 W/m². The high-intensity area is smaller, and there are zeros elsewhere. 3. E = 7.20 × 10^5 J. I′ = 8.00 W/m². Both of those starting figures are invented.

Exercises. 1. 1300 W/m² is the book’s intensity just above the atmosphere. 700 W/m² is the book’s average on Earth’s surface in Example 16.9. 2. Zero, because the resulting amplitude is zero. 3. Decibels, and Chapter 17 on sound, remain reserved.

History · Friday mix: the May 4 line, the proviso, and last week’s May 3 grant

Check. 1. A pole-to-pole line, distant one hundred leagues towards the west and south from any of the islands commonly known as the Azores and Cape Verde. 2. The lands beyond the line must not be in the actual possession of any Christian king or prince up to the birthday of our Lord Jesus Christ just past from which the present year 1493 begins. A right acquired by a Christian prince in actual possession prior to that birthday is not withdrawn. 3. May 3 granted lands discovered and to be discovered, with a proviso about a Christian owner, but without this line. The Treaty of Tordesillas and the first-voyage letter remain reserved. Do not convert a hundred leagues into an official modern kilometer or longitude.

Exercises. 1. To the kings of Castile and León and their heirs, forever. 2. The lands are granted no matter whether they are found in the direction of India or towards any other quarter. 3. An official island count, an official modern length of a league, and an official longitude of the line.

Atelier · Friday mix: the mixed earth hue, a scale, a match, and two objects

Check. 1. Yellow ochre, an earth red, black, and white. The mixed hue is ochre and the earth red, then adjusted with white or black, in a short even scale. 2. A mixed-hue step and an ochre step of the same lightness, checked by half-closing the eyes. On the objects: value first, then a lean toward a hue without losing the value. 3. A portrait, a fifth tube, and ultramarine or lapis as a palette color. A mixture or a store tube is not an official mineral identification, and no official catalog name is being claimed for the mix.

Exercises. 1. The mixed hue is made from the four paints, not added as a fifth ready-made color. The limit still trains mixing. 2. Value families, a half-closed-eye check, and a value match across hues. 3. The week’s job is the mixed hue, a scale, a match, and a two-object study, not a likeness of a face.

Modern language · Spanish Friday mix: regular and irregular simple futures

Check. 1. The endings are still -é, -ás, -á, -emos, -éis, -án, on either the infinitive or a changed stem. Haré, I will do or make; tendré, I will have; podré, I will be able. 2. A fair trio: Mañana hablaré con ella, invented. Mañana se lo diré. Mañana vendremos a las dos, invented time. 3. *Teneré would treat tener as regular in this tense. Conditional and subjunctive remain reserved.

Exercises. 1. Tomorrow I will do it, or I will make it. She will not be able to go, invented. We will go out, or we will leave. They will want to see it, invented. 2. You (tú) will go; he or she will have; we will know. 3. Iré; diré; querré.

Seminar · Artistic anatomy: Gray 1918, the atlas, a first look

Check. 1. Because it supports the globe of the head. Its chief peculiarity is that it has no body; Gray says the body has fused with that of the next vertebra. 2. It has no spinous process, is ring-like, and consists of an anterior arch, a posterior arch, and two lateral masses. Gray: the anterior arch forms about one-fifth of the ring; the posterior arch about two-fifths. 3. Invent no official length of the ring, and no official diameter of a foramen. The axis and the seventh cervical vertebra remain reserved. 4. Model: the atlas (first cervical vertebra). Own words should match this idea: In Gray, 1918, as posted at Theodora, the first cervical vertebra is named the atlas “because it supports the globe of the head.” Its chief peculiarity is that it has no body, “due to the fact that the body of the atlas has fused with that of the next vertebra.” Its other peculiarities are that it has no spinous process, “is ring-like, and consists of an anterior and a posterior arch and two lateral masses.” Official lengths remain reserved. The axis and the seventh cervical vertebra remain reserved. Near-miss: Not last week’s typical cervical vertebra as if it were the atlas. Not the axis, or the vertebra prominens, which Gray still says must be separately described and which remain reserved. Not an official measured length invented for the ring or for the arches. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. It is the rudiment of a spinous process. Last week’s typical cervical vertebra, of the remaining four, has a body and a short bifid spinous process; the atlas is set apart as having neither. 2. “The lateral masses are the most bulky and solid parts of the atlas, in order to support the weight of the head.” 3. Gray still says the second and the seventh must be separately described. The lesson is artistic anatomy from a public-domain text, not a medical diagnosis, and no body is being described from life.

Vocabulary review

Check. 1. Cos θ is the x-coordinate, sin θ the y-coordinate, on the circle of radius 1 at the origin. 30° is (√3/2, 1/2); 150° is (−√3/2, 1/2); 330° is (√3/2, −1/2). 2. Sequor, sequeris, sequitur, sequimur, sequiminī, sequuntur. Sequor is the present, I follow, or I am following. Locūtus eram is the already-taught pluperfect of loquor, I had spoken, and is not a form of sequor. 3. Hablaré, haré, tendré, querré. Haré: mañana asks for the simple future, not the present hago. 4. I is power per unit area, I = P/A. E = 5.04 × 10^6 J. Intensity follows amplitude squared, so a doubled amplitude gives four times the intensity, not two; energy is not created, because that higher intensity covers a smaller area, and there are zeros elsewhere. 5. Lapis lazuli is a rock, not a single mineral; the blue comes from lazurite, and calcite and pyrite are commonly present. Ultramarine is the blue pigment prepared by grinding the rock and separating the lazurite, not the rock itself. Invent no official mineral percentage of a classroom stone, and no official yield or price. 6. A pole-to-pole line “distant one hundred leagues towards the west and south from any of the islands commonly known as the Azores and Cape Verde.” Do not convert the hundred leagues into an official modern length or longitude. 7. Mr. Bennet had already paid the visit to Netherfield, “though to the last always assuring his wife that he should not go.” The ball is named in the 1813 wording as “To-morrow fortnight,” which stays inside the quotation. 8. P(not 4) = 5/6. The tax is $50; $450 remains. $550 adds the tax to the income, as if it were last week’s sales tax.
