Mathematics · Geometry XIX: the equation of a circle

Check. 1. x² + y² = 9, invented. 2. Yes: 1 + 1 = 2. 3. Model: equation of a circle. Own words should match this idea: The coordinate form of the locus of points at a fixed distance r from a center (h, k): (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². When the center is the origin, this is x² + y² = r². Near-miss: Not the graph of a function y = f(x) in general (a full circle fails the “one y for each x” test). And not a parabola, whose equation is quadratic in one variable only, in the form you have used. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. (x − 2)² + (y − 0)² = 4, or (x − 2)² + y² = 4, invented.
  2. Yes: (2 − 2)² + (2 − 0)² = 4.
  3. The vertical line x = 0 meets it at (0, 2) and (0, −2), two outputs for one input.
  4. It is the set of points at distance r from a center, written with coordinates.
  5. It is an invented classroom locus, not a measured path of a spacecraft.

Science · Photoreceptors, and a first look at color vision

Check. 1. In the retina. 2. Day 34 already refused it, and today’s NEI pages, as used here, were not turned into a count we measured or invented. 3. Model: photoreceptor. Own words should match this idea: A cell in the retina that turns light into an electrical signal. The National Eye Institute describes these as special cells in the retina that turn light into electrical signals, which then travel by the optic nerve; the brain turns the signals into images. Near-miss: Not a count of cells, and not a claim that the retina “sees” by itself. We still invent no number of photoreceptors, rods, or cones. color vision deficiency. Own words should match this idea: A difference in how colors are seen, compared with most people. The National Eye Institute also uses the everyday name “color blindness,” and notes that most often it makes certain colors hard to tell apart; it often runs in families; men have a much higher risk than women; and many people adjust to it. Near-miss: Not “seeing only in gray” in the usual case (the NEI says complete color vision deficiency, seeing no color at all, is rare). And not a classroom diagnosis of anyone in the room. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. The NEI picture has photoreceptors sending signals, and the brain turning signals into images, including color as an experience of that process.
  2. Trouble telling certain colors apart (seeing colors differently than most people).
  3. The lesson is an account of a condition, not a test of a person in the room.
  4. “How the eyes work” and “Color blindness.”
  5. A note that the work is not a score.
