Latin · The present subjunctive of ēgredior, a first look

Check. 1. Ēgrediar, ēgrediāris, ēgrediātur, ēgrediāmur, ēgrediāminī, ēgrediantur. Ēgrediar is spelled the same as the future first-person singular. 2. He goes out, in the present subjunctive. Ēgrediētur is the future, he will go out, or she will go out. 3. Sequātur is the present subjunctive of sequor, a different verb, taught yesterday. 4. Model: present subjunctive of ēgredior. Own words should match this idea: The present subjunctive of ēgredior is ēgrediar, ēgrediāris, ēgrediātur, ēgrediāmur, ēgrediāminī, ēgrediantur. The first-person singular ēgrediar is spelled the same as the already-taught future ēgrediar; ēgrediātur, with ā, is not the future ēgrediētur, with ē. It is not sequātur, a different verb. Near-miss: Not ēgrediētur, she will go out, or he will go out. Not ēgressus sit, a later perfect subjunctive, reserved. Not sequātur, yesterday’s present subjunctive of sequor. A purpose clause with ut remains reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. You (s.) go out; we go out, both in the present subjunctive. Fair original sentences may use Puer or Puella, with he or she in the gloss. 2. He goes out, or she goes out, the present indicative; he will go out, or she will go out, the future; he goes out, or she goes out, the present subjunctive. 3. A purpose clause with ut remains reserved.

Mathematics · Geometry XCII: the full disk as a sector, and more invented sectors

Check. 1. A = πr², the area of the whole disk. The sector formula is consistent with the already-taught area of the circle. 2. 3π square units. The figure is invented. 3. A circular segment waits until Thursday. Today is still the sector, including the full disk as the sector with θ = 2π.

Exercises. 1. A = (1/2) × 25 × (π/5) = 5π/2 square units. The figure is invented. 2. A = (1/2) × 16 × π = 8π, which is half of the disk πr² = 16π. The figure is invented. 3. That degree formula expects θ in degrees. π/2 is a radian measure; the matching degree number is 90. Left unchanged, (π/2)/360 shrinks the area. Use A = (1/2)r²θ, or convert first.

Science · Resistivity, a first look, from OpenStax 20.3

Check. 1. Resistivity ρ is an intrinsic property of a material, independent of its shape or size. For a uniform cylinder, R = ρL / A. 2. 5.6 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m, official from Table 20.1. 3. Example 20.5 uses this tungsten value to find a filament diameter and waits until Thursday. 4. Model: resistivity. Own words should match this idea: In OpenStax, College Physics 2e, 20.3, resistivity ρ is “an intrinsic property of a material, independent of its shape or size.” For a uniform cylinder of length L and cross-sectional area A, made of a material with resistivity ρ, the resistance is R = ρL / A. Near-miss: Not Ohm’s law I = V/R, already taught, used as if it already gave ρ. Not an official household voltage, which remains reserved. Not the temperature coefficient of resistivity, which remains reserved. Not Example 20.5, which waits until Thursday. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. R = (5.6 × 10⁻⁸) × 2.00 / (1.00 × 10⁻⁶) = 0.112 Ω. The resistivity is official; L and A are invented and labeled invented. 2. Resistance R depends on shape and size as well as on the material. Resistivity ρ is a property of the material itself, independent of shape or size. 3. Example 20.5 waits until Thursday. The temperature coefficient of resistivity, and official household voltage, remain reserved.

Literature · Pride and Prejudice, Chapter 7: Longbourn, Meryton, and the militia

Check. 1. Longbourn is “only one mile from Meryton.” A militia regiment has recently arrived, to remain “the whole winter,” and Meryton is “the head quarters.” 2. “You must be two of the silliest girls in the country. I have suspected it some time, but I am now convinced.” 3. They are 1813 British spellings, and British spellings stay inside quotations. 4. Model: Longbourn, Meryton, and the militia, in Chapter 7. Own words should match this idea: Pride and Prejudice, Chapter 7, in the 1813 wording, opens a new stretch of the story: “The village of Longbourn was only one mile from Meryton.” A militia regiment has recently arrived, “to remain the whole winter, and Meryton was the head quarters.” Mr. Bennet later says to Catherine and Lydia, “you must be two of the silliest girls in the country. I have suspected it some time, but I am now convinced.” British spellings such as “neighbourhood” and “head quarters,” as printed, stay inside quotations. Near-miss: Not an official identification of Longbourn or Meryton with a modern town, which is not made here. Not Thursday’s rain and walk. Not Chapter 8, which remains reserved. Not a first look at an official fortune as a modern conversion; the chapter’s “two thousand a year” is the novel’s wording if it is quoted, not a converted official modern income. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. The regiment “was to remain the whole winter, and Meryton was the head quarters.” 2. The rain, Jane’s ride, and Elizabeth’s walk wait until Thursday. 3. The chapter’s money wording, if quoted, stays the novel’s wording. It is not converted into an official modern income.
