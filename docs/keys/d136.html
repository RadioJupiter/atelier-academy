Latin · The future of sequor, a first look

Check. 1. It is a third-conjugation deponent future: sequar, sequēris, sequētur, sequēmur, sequēminī, sequentur. 2. Puella mē sequētur: she will follow me. We shall follow him. 3. Loquar is the future of loquor, I shall speak. The future of ēgredior, ēgrediar, waits until tomorrow. 4. Model: future of sequor. Own words should match this idea: The future of sequor is a deponent future of the third conjugation: sequar, sequēris, sequētur, sequēmur, sequēminī, sequentur, I shall follow, you will follow, and so on. It is not the present sequor, and it is not secūtus eram. Near-miss: Not sequor, I follow. Not secūtus eram, I had followed. Not loquar, I shall speak, which is the future of loquor. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. You (s.) will follow; they will follow. 2. Sequar; sequentur. 3. The grammar is taught in original sentences. A textbook narrative is not being copied here.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXIX: the law of cosines, a first look

Check. 1. c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C. When C is 90°, cos 90° = 0, and c² = a² + b², the Pythagorean theorem. 2. c = 5, because c² = 9 + 16 − 0 = 25. 3. c = 5, because c² = 25 + 25 − 25 = 25. The side lengths and the 60° are invented classroom numbers. 4. Model: the law of cosines. Own words should match this idea: The law of cosines says that in any triangle, c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C, where c is the side opposite angle C, and a and b are the other two sides. When C is 90°, cos 90° = 0, and the formula becomes c² = a² + b², the already-taught Pythagorean theorem. It is not the law of sines. Near-miss: Not the law of sines, a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C. Not a claim that the formula holds only in a right triangle. Not a conversion into radians. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. c = 6√2, because c² = 72. 2. The law of sines uses sines of opposite angles. Today’s job is the cosine of the included angle. 3. Radians and the ambiguous case remain reserved.

Writing · A maxim, a first look, after last week’s chreia

Check. 1. A short general precept, not tied to one person on one occasion. The set example is “Eat to live, and not live to eat.” from Poor Richard, 1733. 2. A chreia needs a person, an occasion, and a saying or act. A narrative tells a complete event in order, without a required precept. A fable’s moral is a stated general lesson, often after an animal story. A maxim is the precept itself. 3. The saying is a general precept, not a medical or official dietary rule. A full later elaboration, and an original maxim, wait until later in the week. 4. Model: maxim (progymnasmata). Own words should match this idea: A maxim in this course is a short general precept, not attributed to one named person on one occasion. It is the next progymnasmata exercise after last week’s chreia. Near-miss: Not a chreia, which needs a person, an occasion, and a saying or act. Not a fable’s moral after an animal story. Not a narrative of a complete event in order. Not a medical rule. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. “Eat to live, and not live to eat.” The 1733 wording, including its capitals, stays inside the quotation; teaching prose uses US spelling. 2. A fair sentence keeps the idea that one eats in order to live, and does not invent a meeting or a speaker the 1733 page does not give. 3. A full later elaboration of a maxim remains reserved. So does any maxim not taken from a source that can be checked, if it is offered as historical wording.

Physics · Sound intensity level, and the decibel, a first look, from OpenStax 17.3

Check. 1. β = 10 log10(I/I0), with I0 = 10^−12 W/m², the threshold intensity at 1000 Hz. 2. β = 0 dB, the threshold of hearing. Table 17.2 lists 80 dB as 1×10^−4 W/m², loud radio, classroom lecture. 3. The table’s examples are official OpenStax pairings. A street not in the table has no official classroom dB invented for it. 4. Model: sound intensity level (the decibel). Own words should match this idea: The sound intensity level β, in decibels, is defined in OpenStax 17.3 by β = 10 log10(I/I0), where I is the sound intensity in watts per square meter and I0 = 10^−12 W/m² is the reference intensity, the threshold of hearing at 1000 Hz. It is not the intensity I itself, and it is not a sound pressure level in water. Near-miss: Not the intensity I in W/m². Not a sound pressure level, a different decibel scale used especially in water, which OpenStax 17.3 sets aside. Not an invented official decibel level of a real place not in Table 17.2. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. β = 10 dB. Table 17.2 lists that official pair as the rustle of leaves. The 10^−11 W/m² here is used as the book’s intensity, and may also be treated as an invented intensity that happens to match the table. 2. 30 dB is three factors of 10, so 10^3 = 1000 times as intense. 3. The sound pressure level, especially in water, remains reserved. So do official levels of places not in Table 17.2.

Do not add an official modern identification of the islands, and do not treat Major’s footnotes as the letter. 4. Model: the first-voyage letter (to Luis de Santangel). Not Major’s footnotes identifying islands with later names such as Watling’s Island, which are not taught as official identifications. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Modern language · Spanish: the present subjunctive of a regular -ar verb, a first look

Check. 1. Hable, hables, hable, hablemos, habléis, hablen. 2. He or she wants us to speak. Hablamos is the present indicative, we speak, not the subjunctive hablemos in a “quiere que” frame. 3. Hablaría is the conditional, already taught, “would speak,” not the present subjunctive. 4. Model: present subjunctive of a regular -ar verb. Own words should match this idea: The present subjunctive of a regular -ar verb is formed from the present first-person singular stem, with the endings -e, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en: hable, hables, hable, hablemos, habléis, hablen. It is not the present indicative habla, not the future hablará, and not the conditional hablaría. Near-miss: Not habla, the present indicative. Not hablará, the future. Not hablaría, the conditional. Not the present subjunctive of an -er or -ir verb, which waits until tomorrow. Not an irregular subjunctive. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. That you (s.) speak; that they speak. Fair invented sentences: “Quiere que tú hables.” “Quiere que ellos hablen.” 2. I speak; I will speak; I would speak; that I speak (present subjunctive). 3. Regular -er and -ir present subjunctives wait until tomorrow. Irregular subjunctives remain reserved.

Seminar · Logic: independent events, P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B), a first look

Check. 1. Two events are independent when the happening of one does not change the probability of the other. Then P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). 2. 1/4; 1/12. 3. 4 and 5 cannot both happen on one roll; they are mutually exclusive, and P(4 and 5) = 0. 4. Model: independent events. Own words should match this idea: Two events are independent when the happening of one does not change the probability of the other. For independent events, P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Independence is not the same as mutually exclusive events, which cannot both happen. Near-miss: Not mutually exclusive events, which cannot both happen, so P(A and B) = 0, not P(A)P(B). Not a claim that every pair of events is independent. Not an official probability of a real event. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. (1/2) × (1/2) = 1/4. 2. That addition rule was for “or” when events cannot both happen. Today’s product is for “and” when events are independent. 3. They are classroom figures, not official probabilities of a real event.
