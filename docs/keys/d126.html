Latin · The first- and second-person present of sequor, a first look

Check. 1. Sequor, I follow or I am following; sequeris, you (s.) follow; sequitur, he or she follows; sequimur, we follow; sequiminī, you (pl.) follow; sequuntur, they follow. 2. A fair pair: Ego sequor. Nōs sequimur. Do not copy the model word for word if you can make a new short sentence. 3. It is a deponent: it looks passive and is translated as active, I follow. Locūtus eram is the pluperfect of a different verb, I had spoken, not the present of sequor. 4. Model: first- and second-person present of sequor. Own words should match this idea: Sequor is an already-taught deponent, I follow, still looking passive and translated as active. The first- and second-person present forms, reserved until today, are sequor, I follow; sequeris, you (s.) follow; sequimur, we follow; and sequiminī, you (pl.) follow. The already-used third persons remain sequitur and sequuntur. Near-miss: Not the pluperfect locūtus eram, and not a new tense. Not a non-deponent present such as dūcō. The first persons of ēgredior wait until tomorrow. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. You (s.) follow. They follow (Puerī sequuntur). We follow. 2. Sequor; sequeris. 3. They are a second reserved present table, taken up tomorrow, not mixed into today’s first look at sequor.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXI: the unit circle, a first look

Check. 1. The circle of radius 1 centered at the origin. Cos θ = x and sin θ = y. 2. At 0°, P is (1, 0), cos 0° = 1, sin 0° = 0. At 90°, P is (0, 1), cos 90° = 0, sin 90° = 1. 3. The adjacent side over the hypotenuse is x/1 = x. Radians, and the coordinates at 30°, 45°, and 60°, wait until later this week or remain reserved as named in those lessons. 4. Model: unit circle. Own words should match this idea: The unit circle is the circle of radius 1 centered at the origin of the coordinate plane. An angle in standard position is measured from the positive x-axis. If the terminal ray meets the unit circle at the point P(x, y), then, in degrees, cos θ = x and sin θ = y. This agrees, in the first quadrant, with the already-taught ratios in a right triangle: the adjacent side over the hypotenuse is x/1 = x, and the opposite side over the hypotenuse is y/1 = y. Near-miss: Not a circle of some other official radius treated as if it were already 1 without scaling. Not a first look at radians, which remain reserved. Not a replacement of last term’s right-triangle sine and cosine; those still hold in the first quadrant, and the unit circle extends them. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Because the radius of the unit circle is 1, and that radius is the hypotenuse of the triangle. 2. Sin 0° = 0; cos 90° = 0. 1 is the other coordinate, or the cosine of 0° and the sine of 90°. 3. The unit circle is a geometric model of radius 1, not a measured official object.

Writing · A narrative, a first look, after last week’s fable

Check. 1. A short account of a complete action, in order, with who, what, when, and where, and no stated moral. A fable ends in a stated moral. 2. At noon, on a desk, a jar stands open; a draft slides a sheet toward the rim; the sheet is pulled back; the jar is closed. 3. A stated moral at the end. Chreia and maxim remain reserved. 4. Model: narrative (progymnasmata). Own words should match this idea: A narrative, as the next exercise after last week’s fable, is a short account of a complete action, told in order, that makes clear who did what, when, and where. It does not end in a stated moral. The same invented event can be told as a fable (with a moral) or as a narrative (without one). Near-miss: Not last week’s fable, which ends in a stated moral. Not a long short story, and not a précis of a history chapter. Not a mid-story lecture in place of the action. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. A fair version keeps noon, the desk, the open jar, the moving sheet, the sheet pulled back, and the jar closed, in that order, with no moral. 2. When and where, and who takes part in the action. 3. It stops the action and puts advice in the middle, where a narrative should still be telling what happened.

Physics · Intensity of a wave, a first look, from OpenStax 16.11

Check. 1. Intensity is power per unit area, I = P/A, in W/m². A wave’s energy is directly proportional to its amplitude squared. 2. E = 5.04 × 10^6 J. I′ = 1.40 × 10^5 W/m². 3. E = 400 × 0.250 × 7200 = 7.20 × 10^5 J. 400 W/m² is an invented classroom figure, not the book’s 700 W/m². 4. Model: intensity of a wave. Own words should match this idea: In OpenStax 16.11, intensity I is power per unit area, I = P/A, where P is the power carried by the wave through area A. The SI unit is watts per square meter, W/m². A wave’s energy is directly proportional to its amplitude squared. Because power is energy per unit time, E = IAt when I, A, and t are constant. Near-miss: Not last week’s amplitude itself, which is a displacement, not a power per unit area. Not an official living solar figure other than the book’s 700 W/m² and the book’s 1300 W/m² just above the atmosphere, if that second figure is named as the book’s. Decibels remain reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Watts are joules per second, so (J/s)/m² × m² × s = J. 2. 1300 W/m² is the book’s figure just above the atmosphere. 700 W/m² is the book’s average on Earth’s surface in Example 16.9. They are not the same place. 3. Decibels, and Example 16.10 on constructive interference and intensity, which waits until Wednesday. Sound as a first look remains for a later week if it is not opened this week.

History · Inter Caetera, May 4, 1493: the line of a hundred leagues, a first look

Check. 1. The bull Inter Caetera of May 4, 1493, in Frances Gardiner Davenport’s 1917 translation, Document 07. 2. From the Arctic pole to the Antarctic pole, distant one hundred leagues towards the west and south from any of the islands commonly known as the Azores and Cape Verde. 3. The May 3 bull granted lands discovered and to be discovered with a proviso about a Christian owner, but it did not draw this line. Do not convert a hundred leagues into an official modern kilometer or longitude. The Treaty of Tordesillas, the first-voyage letter, and those later calculations remain reserved. 4. Model: Inter Caetera (May 4, 1493). Own words should match this idea: Inter Caetera of May 4, 1493, is a second bull of Alexander VI, used here in Frances Gardiner Davenport’s 1917 English translation, Document 07. Unlike the May 3 bull, it draws a line from the Arctic pole to the Antarctic pole, “distant one hundred leagues towards the west and south from any of the islands commonly known as the Azores and Cape Verde,” and grants islands and mainlands found and to be found towards the west and south of that line. The line is the bull’s wording, not an official modern longitude or an official length in kilometers. Near-miss: Not the May 3 Inter Caetera, last week’s document, which granted lands discovered and to be discovered with a proviso about a Christian owner, but without this line. Not the Treaty of Tordesillas, which remains reserved. Not a conversion of a hundred leagues into an official modern measurement. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. “The said line to be distant one hundred leagues towards the west and south from any of the islands commonly known as the Azores and Cape Verde.” 2. The lands are granted “no matter whether the said mainlands and islands are found and to be found in the direction of India or towards any other quarter.” 3. It is not in the bull’s wording, and it would treat a later calculation as if it were the document itself.

Atelier · A mixed earth hue, from ochre and the earth red, a first look

Check. 1. A third color made by mixing yellow ochre and the earth red, then adjusted in value with white or black only. Mix the two earths, then lighten or darken the result. 2. Last week’s match kept the two hues unmixed and compared only their lightness. Today the hues are combined, then the value of the new hue is adjusted. 3. A fifth tube, or ultramarine as a palette color. The job is a mixed hue from the four paints, not a likeness of a face. 4. Model: a mixed earth hue. Own words should match this idea: A mixed earth hue, in this limited palette, is a third color made by mixing yellow ochre and the earth red, still adjusted in value with white and black only. It is not a fifth tube, and it is not ultramarine, which science may name this week but which is not added to the palette. Near-miss: Not a store tube of a new catalog color. Not last week’s value match, which kept the two hues unmixed and matched only their lightness. Not a portrait, and not lapis as a paint. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. The mix sets the hue; white and black still set the value, as in last week’s scales. 2. The limited palette remains four paints so that mixing is learned with that set. Ultramarine is a pigment named in science, not a tube added here this week. 3. No official catalog name of a store color is being claimed for a classroom mixture.

Modern language · Spanish: irregular simple future of hacer, decir, and ir, a first look

Check. 1. The endings are still -é, -ás, -á, -emos, -éis, -án, but the stems are not the unchanged infinitives. Haré, I will do or make; diré, I will say or tell; iré, I will go. 2. A fair trio: Mañana lo haré. Mañana se lo diré. Mañana iremos al parque, an invented place, not an official address. 3. *Haceré would keep the whole infinitive as if hacer were regular in this tense. Other irregulars such as tendré and pondré wait until tomorrow. 4. Model: irregular simple future (hacer, decir, ir). Own words should match this idea: Some Spanish verbs keep the same future endings already taught, -é, -ás, -á, -emos, -éis, -án, but not the whole unchanged infinitive. Today’s first set: hacer → haré, harás, hará, haremos, haréis, harán; decir → diré, dirás, dirá, diremos, diréis, dirán; ir → iré, irás, irá, iremos, iréis, irán. The endings are the same as in hablaré; the stems are not the full infinitives. Near-miss: Not the regular future of hablar, comer, or vivir, which keep the whole infinitive. Not the present hago, digo, or voy. Not an official timetable. Other irregular futures, such as tendré and pondré, wait until tomorrow. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. He or she will do or make; he or she will say or tell; they will go. 2. A fair pair: Mañana iré. Mañana lo haré. (Voy and hago are present.) 3. Times and places in the examples are invented classroom sentences, not official schedules.

Seminar · Logic: complementary events, P(not A) = 1 − P(A), a first look

Check. 1. Two events are complementary when one is exactly the cases in which the other does not happen, and one of the two must happen. Then P(not A) = 1 − P(A). 2. P(not 4) = 1 − 1/6 = 5/6. P(not even) = 1 − 1/2 = 1/2. 3. Those would be official claims about a real event, invented without evidence, or a use of chance as a game of stakes, which this lesson is not. 4. Model: complementary events. Own words should match this idea: Two events are complementary when one is exactly the cases in which the other does not happen, and one of the two must happen. If P(A) is the probability of A, then P(not A) = 1 − P(A), provided the outcomes are the same equally likely set already taught, and one of them must happen. Near-miss: Not an official probability of a real event, such as weather. Not a lesson in gambling. Not a claim that a real cube in the room has been tested as fair. Outcomes that are not equally likely remain reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. 7/8, and 3/4. 2. P(marked) = 3/6 = 1/2, so P(not marked) = 1/2. 1/3 is P(marked) if you forget to subtract, or if you treat three faces as if there were only three outcomes. 3. Outcomes that are not equally likely, and official probabilities of real events.
