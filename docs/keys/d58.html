Latin · is, ea, and quia

Check. 1. Puella est. Puer eam videt. 2. Puella rīdet, quia rosam habet.

  1. The boy is the object of videt, so the accusative eum is the form you need.
  2. quia gives a reason. sed contrasts two clauses.
  3. Two copied model sentences.

Mathematics · Composition, and a line with a parabola

Check. 1. f(f(2)) = f(6) = 18. 6 · 6 would be a product, which is a different job. 2. x² = 6, so x = √6 or x = −√6. Points (√6, 6) and (−√6, 6). Check: each square is 6, and the line is y = 6.

  1. x² = x + 6, x² − x − 6 = 0, (x − 3)(x + 2) = 0, x = 3 or x = −2. Points (3, 9) and (−2, 4). Check: 3² = 9 and 3 + 6 = 9; (−2)² = 4 and −2 + 6 = 4.
  2. A slip in the algebra might give a point that fits only one equation, or neither. The system requires both.
  3. The system asks for intersection points. The inequality asks where one graph is below the other (or below the axis, in the one-function case). They are different questions.

Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin’s hints

Check. 1. The uncle’s death is a later reserved part of the letter, not in the set extract. Franklin’s method reconstructs a set passage, not a later chapter. 2. J. B. Firth, 1900, Attalus text (some wording modified).

  1. A sound pair from the check, for example preserved “pine-tree” or “Vesuvius,” and a dropped “great length and height.”

Physics · Weight from g, for a labeled invented mass

Check. 1. W = 5 × 9.8 = 49 N. 2. 3.7 is g in m/s², not a mass. The invented mass stays in kilograms, and is the same in this model when the place changes.

  1. Earth: 4 × 9.8 = 39.2 N. Mars: 4 × 3.7 = 14.8 N.
  2. This course does not have a measured mass for these exercises. The numbers are classroom inventions, and they stay labeled.

History · The pine-tree cloud

Check. 1. “...in likeness and form it more closely resembled a pine-tree than anything else, for what corresponded to the trunk was of great length and height, and then spread out into a number of branches.” (Firth, 1900, Attalus text). 2. Pompeii, and a death toll.

  1. It is a later letter, in a 1900 translation. 79 CE is a conventional event-year, not a camera. The wording is Firth’s, and Attalus notes some modification.
  2. Monday’s sentence sets the pointing-out of a cloud at about the seventh hour. Today’s extract describes the cloud’s shape. Both are already set; neither opens the reserved later narrative.

Atelier · Two shadows, a few accents, and a stop

Check. 1. A dark plane is on the form itself, facing away from the lamp. A cast shadow is on another surface, where the form blocks the lamp. 2. Two or three, not a new layer over the whole group.

  1. It would invent a head, which is not today’s form and not a small value note.
  2. A dated stop-note, with the lamp marked.

Modern language · Spanish: antes de and después de

Check. 1. Antes de escribir, lee. 2. Después de escribir, lee.

  1. Antes de leer (infinitive, not lee).
  2. That pattern would pull in a new clause-type, and often a new mood, which has not been taught yet.
  3. Both heard and used with an infinitive.

Art history · Later Flavian buildings, from Platner

Check. 1. The stagnum of Nero’s domus Aurea (Platner). 2. The games are taught as Platner, citing Suetonius, Titus 7, among other authorities Platner lists. This course does not pretend to have read Suetonius independently.

  1. Each is a later, staged, or rebuilt fabric, taught from a 1929 dictionary, not a camera of the event-year.
  2. A capacity was not quoted from Platner in these lessons, so it is not invented.
  3. A sound sentence: Vespasian dedicated work before his death in 79, but Titus added stories and Platner places the enlarged dedication in 80, so a single year 79 does not describe the later building.
