Latin · is, ea, and quia: a Term 1 close

Check. 1. Puella est. Puer eam videt. 2. Puella rīdet, quia rosam habet. 3. Model: is, ea, and quia, a Term 1 close. Own words should match this idea: A close of already-taught pointing words and a reason-word: is / ea (and the accusative eum / eam) point back to a person already named, and quia gives a reason, often after cūr. They were reviewed last week; today gathers them for the term, still in Capitula I–X. Near-miss: Not a new chapter, not a relative clause of the kind Capitulum XI would begin, and not a mix-up that uses quia where sed is the contrast. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. The boy is the object of videt, so the accusative eum is the already-taught form.
  2. quia gives a reason. sed contrasts two clauses.
  3. Two copied model sentences.
  4. A relative clause as a new chapter is not begun. Today closes already-taught pointing words and quia.

Mathematics · Composition, and a line with a parabola: a Term 1 close

Check. 1. f(f(2)) = f(6) = 18. 6 · 6 would be a product, which is a different job. 2. x² = 6, so x = √6 or x = −√6 (this course’s positive square root, and its opposite). Points (√6, 6) and (−√6, 6). Check: each square is 6, and the line is y = 6. 3. Model: composition, and a line with a parabola, a Term 1 close. Own words should match this idea: A close of two already-taught algebra methods: composition (f ∘ g)(x) = f(g(x)), including iteration f(f(x)), which is not a product; and a line and a parabola as a system, solved by substitution, with every candidate point checked in both equations. Near-miss: Not f(x) · f(x) for f(f(x)), not a guess of a point without a check, and not Euclid III.36. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. x² = x + 6, x² − x − 6 = 0, (x − 3)(x + 2) = 0, x = 3 or x = −2. Points (3, 9) and (−2, 4). Check: 3² = 9 and 3 + 6 = 9; (−2)² = 4 and −2 + 6 = 4.
  2. A candidate from an algebra slip might satisfy only one equation, or neither. The system requires both.
  3. The system asks for intersection points. The inequality asks where one graph is below the other (or below the axis, in the already-taught one-function case). They are different questions.
  4. A note that a tangent-secant figure is a different picture.

Art history · Later Flavian buildings, from Platner: a Term 1 close

Check. 1. The stagnum of Nero’s domus Aurea (Platner). 2. The games are taught as Platner, citing Suetonius, Titus 7, among other authorities Platner lists. This course does not pretend to have read Suetonius independently. 3. Model: later Flavian buildings, a Term 1 close. Own words should match this idea: A close of two already-taught Platner and Ashby (1929) entries: the Arch of Titus, a later commemoration dedicated after Titus’s death, with an 1822 Valadier rebuild; and the Flavian amphitheater (the Colosseum), carried by Vespasian to the second arcade and dedicated before his death in 79, with Titus adding the third and fourth stories and an enlarged dedication in 80. Both are later buildings, not photographs of 70 or 79. Near-miss: Not a photograph of 70 or 79, not a seating number invented here, and not a claim that this course has independently read Suetonius. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Each is a later, staged, or rebuilt fabric, taught from a 1929 dictionary, not a camera of the event-year.
  2. A capacity was not quoted from Platner in these lessons, so it is not invented.
  3. A sound sentence: Vespasian dedicated work before his death in 79, but Titus added stories and Platner places the enlarged dedication in 80, so a single year 79 does not describe the later building.
  4. A note that the seminar is not a reward.
