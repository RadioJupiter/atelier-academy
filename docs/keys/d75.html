Latin · Friday mix: the year, the months, and Roman dates

Check. 1. Ablative for the named day itself (Kalendīs, Nonīs, Īdibus); accusative after prīdiē. 2. Quīntīlis was renamed Iūlius; Sextīlis was renamed Augustus.

  1. The Nones of March are the 7th; 7 + 1 − 4 = 4 March.
  2. A model: Annus duodecim mēnsēs habet. Īdibus Mārtiīs. Other correct original wording is accepted.

Mathematics · Friday mix: the area of a triangle

Check. 1. A = ½ × 10 × 9 = 45. 2. The rectangle has area 40, so each triangle has area 20.

  1. The base splits into 6 and 6. 6² + h² = 10², so 36 + h² = 100, h² = 64, h = 8. A = ½ × 12 × 8 = 48.
  2. The height must be perpendicular to the base. A slanted side does not meet the base at a right angle, so it is not h.

Writing & Rhetoric · Friday mix: informal fallacies

Check. 1. The reasons given may not be the ones that establish the conclusion. Truth of the conclusion and soundness of the route are different questions. 2. A straw man distorts the other person’s claim and answers the distortion. Ad hominem points at a trait of the person instead of testing the claim.

  1. Honest invented examples accepted if each pattern is used correctly and labeled invented.
  2. Those two options exhaust the property “is right-angled.” No third value of that property has been left out.

Physics · Friday mix: kinds of equilibrium, and a center of mass by hanging

Check. 1. Neutral equilibrium. 2. At the crossing of the two plumb lines.

  1. The kind depends on what a small displacement does, not only on the fact of rest.
  2. The same hanging-point would give the same line twice, and there would be no new crossing.

Modern language · Spanish: Friday mix of ser, ir, and hacer in the preterite

Check. 1. A model: Ayer fueron al parque (ir). Fueron is not the preterite of hacer; the ellos form of hacer is hicieron. 2. hablé, comí, viví. Fui is the irregular shared form of ser and ir, not a regular -ar, -er, or -ir yo ending.

  1. Each heard form used in a short sentence, with the verb labeled. Model: Fuiste (ir) a la escuela; hiciste (hacer) la tarea; fue (ser) un día largo.
  2. This week teaches that the forms are shared; the sentence tells whether the meaning is “was/were” or “went.” It does not invent a historical reason beyond that shared set.

Elective · Anatomy: the hand — carpus, metacarpals, and phalanges

Check. 1. The thumb sits on the radius side. 2. Proximal: the carpus (the wrist group). Distal: the phalanges of the digits. 3. Model: carpus. Own words should match this idea: The wrist, treated in this lesson as a group of short bones between the forearm and the metacarpals, not as a list of eight names to be memorized today. Near-miss: Not a metacarpal, and not a phalanx. The eight individual carpal names remain reserved; today’s job is the group. metacarpals. Own words should match this idea: The long bones of the palm, lying between the carpus and the phalanges; one metacarpal leads toward each digit. Near-miss: Not the carpus (the wrist group), and not the phalanges (the bones of the digits). “Meta-” here marks that they come after the carpus, not that they are a second wrist. phalanges of the hand. Own words should match this idea: The bones of the digits: each finger has three phalanges (proximal, middle, and distal); the thumb has two (proximal and distal). Near-miss: Not a metacarpal, and not a first look at a battle-array sense of the older word “phalanx.” Today’s job is the bones of the digits of the hand. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Five metacarpals, one toward each digit. The carpus is a group of short wrist bones whose eight names remain reserved; it is not counted as five.
  2. No official bone length is being measured, and official figures are not invented.

Vocabulary review

Check. 1. A = ½bh, with h perpendicular. The base 6 splits into 3 and 3; 3² + h² = 5², so h = 4; A = ½ × 6 × 4 = 12. 2. Kalendae are the first day of a Roman month. The Īdūs of March fall on 15 March. These facts follow Allen and Greenough, New Latin Grammar, §631. 3. Ferio is EIO: no M are P; some S are M; therefore some S are not P. The minor is only particular, so the conclusion stays “some S are not P,” not the universal “no S are P.” 4. Longfellow: “Not man; man once I was.” Dewing: “the most learned man in the skilled craft which is known as the art of building.” 5. ser/ir: fui, fue. hacer: hice, hizo. The z is part of the él/ella/usted form hizo, which is not *hico and not the yo form hice. 6. An authentic cadence arrives from V to I. A plagal cadence arrives from IV to I. Both arrive at the tonic; they differ in the leading chord. 7. I = 200 × 0.05 × 3 = 30; A = 200 + 30 = 230. The rate is not an official rate at a named bank. 8. Carpus: the wrist, as a group of short bones (individual names reserved). Metacarpals: the long bones of the palm, one toward each digit. Phalanges: the bones of the digits; the thumb has two, the other fingers three.
