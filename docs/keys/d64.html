Latin · Capitulum XI, part 4: AcI with esse

Check. 1. It is a predicate adjective describing corpus, the accusative subject of esse, so it is accusative (and neuter singular) as well. 2. Putō cor sānum esse. 3. Dīcō caput magnum esse. The adjective must be neuter accusative, matching caput, not masculine magnus. 4. The first is AcI after putō: accusative subject, infinitive esse, accusative predicate. The second is an ordinary sentence with a nominative subject and finite est. 5. Model: AcI with esse. Own words should match this idea: The same accusative-and-infinitive construction, with esse as the infinitive. A predicate word that describes the accusative subject is itself accusative: Putō corpus sānum esse, “I think that the body is healthy.” Near-miss: Not a nominative predicate as in Corpus sānum est, which has a finite form of esse and no AcI. After putō or dīcō, both the subject and the describing word are accusative, and esse is infinitive. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Puer ōs magnum esse dīcit.
  2. Putō crūra longa esse. (Neuter plural accusative, longa.)
  3. Puella crūs longum esse dīcit. Use esse, not est.

Science · Water: the triple point, and the ice point at one atmosphere

Check. 1. Solid, liquid, and vapor, in equilibrium. It is unique because that three-phase condition occurs at only one pressure and one temperature for a given substance (here, water). 2. 0.01 °C. It is the triple point of water on ITS-90 (273.16 K). 3. The ice point at 1 atm is about 0 °C, for solid and liquid at one atmosphere. The triple point is 0.01 °C, where solid, liquid, and vapor coexist at a unique pressure that is not 1 atm. 4. Approximately 99.974 °C (373.124 K). That is a boiling point at one atmosphere (liquid and vapor at 1 atm), not the three-phase triple point. 5. Model: triple point. Own words should match this idea: The unique pressure and temperature at which the solid, liquid, and vapor of a substance all coexist in equilibrium. For water, this point is exactly 273.16 K on ITS-90, which is 0.01 °C. It is not the same condition as the ice point at one atmosphere. Near-miss: Not the melting point at one atmosphere, and not the boiling point at one atmosphere. Those are two-phase points at a stated ordinary pressure. The triple point is the unique three-phase condition. Do not treat 0.01 °C as a typo for 0 °C, or treat them as two names for the same point. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Because 0.01 °C is the triple-point temperature (273.16 K − 273.15), and 0 °C is the familiar ice point at 1 atm. They are different conditions. Treating them as the same would blend two points into one.
  2. No. That is the two-phase ice point at one atmosphere (about 0 °C). The triple point also requires the vapor phase, at 0.01 °C and at its own unique pressure.
  3. Historically, 0 °C and 100 °C were the freezing and boiling points of pure water at one atmosphere. On ITS-90, the atmospheric boiling temperature is approximately 99.974 °C.

Modern language · Spanish: ayer, anoche, and el lunes pasado, with the preterite

Check. 1. yesterday; last night; last Monday. 2. Ayer tú cantaste. Anoche nosotros hablamos. 3. Because el lunes pasado places the action in the past, so compramos is read as preterite, not present. 4. Do not mix hoy and anoche as the time of one verb; pick one time. Ayer ellos compraron pan (ellos takes -aron). 5. Model: ayer, anoche, el lunes pasado. Own words should match this idea: Spanish time words that place a completed action in the past, and so often accompany the preterite: ayer, “yesterday”; anoche, “last night”; el lunes pasado, “last Monday” (and likewise el martes pasado, and so on). They do not themselves conjugate; the verb still carries the preterite ending. Near-miss: Not a new verb tense, and not a present-tense time word such as hoy (“today”) or ahora (“now”). The nosotros form still looks like the present; these time words are one clear way to mark it as past. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A model: Ayer canté. Anoche compramos pan. El lunes pasado ella cantó. Other accurate originals are accepted.
  2. El lunes pasado yo cociné. (or: El lunes pasado cociné.)
  3. hablo is present. Ayer needs a preterite: Ayer yo hablé.
