Mathematics · Functions and their graphs

Check. 1. y = (x + 2)² − 5. The vertex is (−2, −5). 2. 3 to the right and 2 up.

  1. A V with vertex at (0, 0); the same V moved to vertex (2, 0); moved to (0, −2); moved to (2, −1).
  2. 2(x + 2)² − 3: vertex (−2, −3), opening upward, narrower than y = x².
  3. (a) x ≥ 3 (b) all x except ±2 (c) −2 ≤ x ≤ 2.
  4. f(g(2)) = f(4) = 11; g(f(2)) = g(7) = 49. Not equal in general: composition is not commutative.
  5. The parabola y = x² − 4 with the part between x = −2 and x = 2 (which dips to −4) reflected up, peaking at (0, 4).
  6. |x − 1| + |x + 1| is the sum of distances from x to 1 and to −1. It equals 2 for every x in [−1, 1] (infinitely many solutions), and equals 3 at x = ±1.5.
  7. The graph is symmetric: for x ≥ 0 it is (x − 2)² − 1, with minimum −1 at x = 2 and value 3 at x = 0. A horizontal line y = a meets it four times exactly when −1 < a < 3. (At a = 3 there are three solutions: 0 and ±4.)

Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin's method, part 3: verse into prose

Check. 1. Compare with the original, and list what you missed; then rewrite once more. 2. No. A miss is a lost job (a cause, a contrast, a fact), not a different but faithful order.

  1. that TIME | of YEAR | thou MAYST | in ME | be-HOLD: five iambs (iambic pentameter).
  2. Model: When you look at me you can see the season of the year when only a few yellow leaves, or none at all, still hang on branches that shake in the cold, branches like the bare, ruined choirs of a church where, not long ago, sweet birds were singing.
  3. The bare branches are compared to the choirs (the part of a church where the choir sang, with carved wooden stalls or arches); they are "ruin'd" because they are stripped and empty, as many monastery churches in England stood ruined after the dissolution of the monasteries in the 1530s (a common, though not certain, reading). The birds are the former singers.
  4. Check: four lines, ABAB rhyme, nothing in the prose omitted.

Literature · Writing about the Iliad: the analytical essay

Rubric (each criterion 1–4): Thesis arguable and precise · Evidence: accurate, well-chosen quotations with line references · Analysis: explains how the evidence supports the claim · Counter-argument acknowledged · Sentence control (variety; no run-ons) · Accuracy of plot facts. A strong essay on (a) might argue that Hector is the human center of these books (Book 6) while Achilles is the poem's announced subject (Book 1, line 1), so the poem is built on the contrast.

Atelier · Cast drawing 3: halftones

Check. 1. The light family. 2. If it is as dark as the shadow, the form goes flat and the terminator disappears.

Common faults: halftones too dark (the light side starts to merge with the shadow family); too many small marks (the form breaks up); reflected light brightened until it competes with the light. The light side should read as one family, from paper-white down to the darkest halftone, all lighter than the shadow.
