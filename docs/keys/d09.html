Latin · Capitulum IV, part 1: calling and commanding

Check. 1. Mārce. 2. Goodbye (to one person). 3. Model: vocative. Own words should match this idea: The case used for calling or addressing someone: the form you use when you speak to a person, not about them. Near-miss: The vocative is not the accusative (the object), and it is not a command. Commands are a mood of the verb. imperative. Own words should match this idea: The verb form used to give a command: ‘do this’ or ‘do not do this’. Near-miss: The imperative is not the vocative (the vocative is a noun case), and it is not the same as ‘is’ or ‘are’. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. serve, domine, ancilla, puer, Iūlī.
  2. vocā, vidē, audī, venī, respondē.
  3. Check: vocative + imperative, separated by a comma, e.g. Ancilla, cantā! Domine, audī!

Mathematics · Functions and their graphs

Check. 1. y = (x + 2)² − 5. The vertex is (−2, −5). 2. 3 to the right and 2 up. 3. Model: function. Own words should match this idea: A rule that assigns to each allowed input exactly one output. The graph is the set of points (x, f(x)). Near-miss: A function cannot send one input to two different outputs. A graph that fails the vertical-line test is not a function. vertex. Own words should match this idea: The turning point of a parabola. For y = (x − h)² + k, the vertex is (h, k). Near-miss: The vertex is not necessarily on an axis, and it is not the same as the discriminant. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A V with vertex at (0, 0); the same V moved to vertex (2, 0); moved to (0, −2); moved to (2, −1).
  2. 2(x + 2)² − 3: vertex (−2, −3), opening upward, narrower than y = x².
  3. (a) x ≥ 3 (b) all x except ±2 (c) −2 ≤ x ≤ 2.
  4. f(g(2)) = f(4) = 11; g(f(2)) = g(7) = 49. Not equal in general: composition is not commutative.
  5. The parabola y = x² − 4 with the part between x = −2 and x = 2 (which dips to −4) reflected up, peaking at (0, 4).
  6. |x − 1| + |x + 1| is the sum of distances from x to 1 and to −1. It equals 2 for every x in [−1, 1] (infinitely many solutions), and equals 3 at x = ±1.5.
  7. The graph is symmetric: for x ≥ 0 it is (x − 2)² − 1, with minimum −1 at x = 2 and value 3 at x = 0. A horizontal line y = a meets it four times exactly when −1 < a < 3. (At a = 3 there are three solutions: 0 and ±4.)

Science · Light: polarization and the quantitative model

Check. 1. Sunlight contains all visible colors in a mixture that our eyes see as white. From the ground some blue is scattered out of the direct beam, which makes the Sun look slightly yellow; above the atmosphere there is no such loss. 2. Model: polarization. Own words should match this idea: The preferring of one direction of vibration in a light wave. Scattered sunlight is partly polarized, most strongly about 90° from the Sun. Near-miss: Polarization is not ‘the same as color’, and it is not a reason to look at the Sun. frequency. Own words should match this idea: The number of wave cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz). For light in vacuum, frequency times wavelength equals the speed of light c. Near-miss: Frequency is not the same as wavelength, though the two are tied together by c = fλ in vacuum. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. f = c/λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ / 5.00 × 10⁻⁷ = 6.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz.
  2. t = 1.496 × 10¹¹ / 2.998 × 10⁸ ≈ 499 s ≈ 8 min 19 s.
  3. Strongest about 90° from the Sun, in a band across the sky.
  4. (700/400)⁴ = 1.75⁴ ≈ 9.4.
  5. Sunlight contains all visible colors in a mixture that our eyes see as white. From the ground some blue is scattered out of the direct beam, which makes the Sun look slightly yellow; above the atmosphere there is no such loss.

Literature · Writing about the Iliad: the analytical essay

Check. 1. A quotation is evidence, not a claim. Without an introduction and an explanation, the reader is asked to guess how the lines support the paragraph. That is not analysis. 2. Model: thesis. Own words should match this idea: One sentence that can be argued against, stating the judgment the essay will defend. Near-miss: A thesis is not a topic (‘this essay is about Hector and Achilles’), and it is not a plot summary. analytical essay. Own words should match this idea: A short argued piece in which every paragraph begins with a claim, every quotation is introduced and explained, and the other side is acknowledged. Near-miss: It is not a retelling of Books 1–6, and a quotation left to ‘speak for itself’ is not analysis. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Rubric (each criterion 1–4): Thesis arguable and precise · Evidence: accurate, well-chosen quotations with line references · Analysis: explains how the evidence supports the claim · Counter-argument acknowledged · Sentence control (variety; no run-ons) · Accuracy of plot facts. A strong essay on (a) might argue that Hector is the human center of these books (Book 6) while Achilles is the poem's announced subject (Book 1, line 1), so the poem is built on the contrast.

Atelier · Cast drawing 3: halftones

Check. 1. The light family. 2. If it is as dark as the shadow, the form goes flat and the terminator disappears.

Common faults: halftones too dark (the light side starts to merge with the shadow family); too many small marks (the form breaks up); reflected light brightened until it competes with the light. The light side should read as one family, from paper-white down to the darkest halftone, all lighter than the shadow. 3. Model: halftone. Own words should match this idea: The middle values on the lit side of a form, between the lights and the terminator, that show the surface turning toward shadow. Near-miss: A halftone is not a shadow, and it is not the highlight. Putting a shadow value into a halftone flattens the turn. highlight. Own words should match this idea: The smallest, brightest spot where the light is most directly reflected to the eye; it sits inside the lights, not in the shadows. Near-miss: The highlight is not ‘the whole lit side’, and it is not added as a last decorative dot if the lights around it are already too pale. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Modern language · Spanish: ir and the near future

Check. 1. I go, or I am going. 2. Vamos a estudiar. 3. Model: ir. Own words should match this idea: The Spanish verb ‘to go’, irregular in the present: voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van. Near-miss: Ir is not ser (voy is not soy), and it is not the same as English ‘I will’ by itself. The near future needs ir + a + infinitive. near future. Own words should match this idea: A present-tense way of talking about a planned or soon-to-happen action: ir + a + infinitive. Near-miss: This is not a separate ‘future tense’ ending, and a + infinitive without ir is not the near future. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. (a) Vamos a la biblioteca. (b) Los viernes voy al museo. (c) ¿Qué vas a estudiar mañana? (d) Van a pintar el sábado.
  2. Check ir a + infinitive and lower-case day names with el.

Economics & personal finance · Compound interest

Check. 1. Each deposit grows by the factor (1 + r)^n, and n is the number of years it stays invested. Early deposits have the largest n, so they grow by far the most — more than the cash value of the extra deposits themselves. 2. Model: compound interest. Own words should match this idea: Interest paid on the principal and on the interest already earned. With principal P, annual rate r, and n years, compounded yearly: A = P(1 + r)^n. Near-miss: Compound interest is not simple interest (which pays only on the original principal), and the same mathematics that grows savings also grows debt. principal. Own words should match this idea: The original amount of money borrowed or invested, before interest. Near-miss: Principal is not the interest, and it is not the same word as principle (a rule). rule of 72. Own words should match this idea: A short estimate: money at r percent a year doubles in about 72/r years, a good approximation for rates between about 2% and 15%. Near-miss: It is not exact, and it is not reliable for every rate. Use the formula when you need a precise amount. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. 1000 × 1.07¹⁰ ≈ $1,967.15. Simple interest: 1000 + 10 × 70 = $1,700.
  2. About 12 years; about 8 years.
  3. (1.01)¹² − 1 ≈ 12.68%.
  4. Each deposit grows by the factor (1 + r)ⁿ, and n is the number of years it stays invested; early deposits have the largest n, so they grow by far the most.
