Latin · Friday mix: the future of sequor and ēgredior, with already-taught deponent tenses

Check. 1. Sequar, sequēris, sequētur, sequēmur, sequēminī, sequentur. Ēgrediar, ēgrediēris, ēgrediētur, ēgrediēmur, ēgrediēminī, ēgredientur. 2. Puer mē sequētur, et tum ēgrediētur: he will follow me, and then he will go out. 3. Loquar is I shall speak, from loquor. Secūtus eram is I had followed, the pluperfect, not the future sequar.

Exercises. 1. Sequēmur; ēgredientur. Secūtī erāmus; ēgressī erant (or the feminine forms if the subject requires them). 2. A fair mix keeps each verb’s time: for example a future sequētur beside a pluperfect ēgressus erat. 3. Any later deponent tense not yet taught, including a future perfect, remains reserved.

Mathematics · Friday mix: the law of cosines, with last week’s law of sines as a check

Check. 1. c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C. When C is 90°, c² = a² + b². 2. 4√3, because c² = 48. The law of sines gives the same 4√3, because 4 / sin 30° = (4√3) / sin 120°. 3. 120°, because the cosine is −1/2.

Exercises. 1. 5, because c² = 25. 2. 90°, because the cosine is 0. 3. Radians and the ambiguous case remain reserved.

Writing · Friday mix: a maxim distinguished from a chreia, a narrative, and a fable

Check. 1. “Eat to live, and not live to eat.” It is a short general precept, not tied to one person on one occasion. 2. A chreia needs a person, an occasion, and a saying or act. A narrative tells a complete event in order, without a required precept. A fable’s moral is a stated general lesson, often after an animal story. 3. A fair invented maxim is short, general, and labeled invented. It must not become a chreia, a narrative, a fable, or a claim about a living person.

Exercises. 1. A maxim. 2. They are the source wordings (1813, or 1733) and stay inside quotations; teaching prose uses US spelling. “Mite” and “ancle” belong to Chapter 3, already taught, not to today’s maxim, but the same quotation rule holds. 3. A full later elaboration of a maxim remains reserved.

Physics · Friday mix: sound intensity level, Table 17.2, and Examples 17.2 and 17.3

Check. 1. β = 10 log10(I/I0), I0 = 10^−12 W/m². Example 17.2: I = 5.04×10^−4 W/m², β = 87 dB. 2. 3.01 dB, about 3 dB, for a factor of two (Example 17.3 and Table 17.3). 10.0 dB for a factor of ten (Table 17.3). 3. β = 40 dB. Table 17.2 lists 40 dB, 1×10^−8 W/m², as average home. If the 10^−8 is used as an invented intensity, it is labeled invented, even though it matches the table’s official pair.

Exercises. 1. 20 dB is two factors of 10, so 100 times as intense. 2. 0 dB means I = I0, the threshold intensity at 1000 Hz. 3. The sound pressure level, and official dB values for places not in Table 17.2, remain reserved.

History · Friday mix: the first-voyage letter, with last week’s Tordesillas line as a contrast

Check. 1. A letter to Luis de Santangel, Chancellor of the Exchequer of Aragon, a voyage report. “In thirty-three days' time I reached the Indies.” 2. San Salvador (“Guanaham”), Santa-Maria de Concepcion, Fernandina, Isabella, Juana; then “La Española,” “eighteen leagues from the former.” 3. The treaty, June 7, 1494, later divides lands east and west of a 370-league line. Do not convert the letter’s days or leagues, or the treaty’s 370 leagues, and do not add official modern island identifications.

Exercises. 1. He thought Juana “must be the mainland,—the province of Cathay,” then learned “that this land was certainly an island.” 2. They are Major’s 1870 English, and they stay inside quotations; teaching prose uses US spelling. 3. Later parts of the letter, and any official modern identification or conversion, remain reserved.

Atelier · Friday mix: the rounded form, a value scale, the mixed hue, and a small still life

Check. 1. A color-value study of a simple rounded object in the four-paint set, with mixtures already taught. Value first means the light, the half, and the shadow are placed before a hue is chased. 2. Often on the half, checked against a short scale so the value matches the turn and does not become a stripe. 3. Not a portrait, and not a fifth tube. Cinnabar, azurite, malachite, and ultramarine stay out of the box.

Exercises. 1. A fair paragraph names one rounded object, a cloth, a short scale, and a half in the mixed earth hue, all under one light, still in the four paints. 2. The cloth is a support and a partner in the still life. The mix also includes the turn of the rounded form, the scale, and the half. 3. A portrait, a fifth tube, and a master copy in color remain reserved.

Modern language · Spanish Friday mix: regular present subjunctives, with the future and the conditional

Check. 1. Hablar: hable, hables, hable, hablemos, habléis, hablen. Comer: coma, comas, coma, comamos, comáis, coman. Vivir: viva, vivas, viva, vivamos, viváis, vivan. 2. Coma. 3. Vaya is an irregular present subjunctive of ir, not yet taught.

Exercises. 1. Fair invented examples: “Quiere que vivamos aquí.” “Mañana viviremos aquí.” “En tu lugar, yo viviría aquí.” 2. Habla is the present indicative; the subjunctive is hable. 3. Irregular subjunctives remain reserved.

Seminar · Artistic anatomy: Gray 1918, the seventh cervical vertebra, a first look

Check. 1. “A long and prominent spinous process, hence the name vertebra prominens.” 2. The process is “thick, nearly horizontal,” “not bifurcated,” ending in a tubercle for the ligamentum nuchæ. “The usual arrangement is for both artery and vein to pass in front of the transverse process, and not through the foramen.” 3. The lesson is artistic anatomy from a public-domain text, not a medical diagnosis, and no body is being described from life. Official lengths remain reserved. 4. Model: the seventh cervical vertebra (vertebra prominens). Own words should match this idea: In Gray, Anatomy of the Human Body, 1918, “The Cervical Vertebrae,” the seventh cervical vertebra is distinguished by “a long and prominent spinous process, hence the name vertebra prominens.” The process “is thick, nearly horizontal in direction, not bifurcated, but terminating in a tubercle to which the lower end of the ligamentum nuchæ is attached.” Official lengths remain reserved. Near-miss: Not the atlas, which has no body and no spinous process in the ordinary sense already taught. Not the axis, whose distinctive mark is the odontoid process. Not a medical diagnosis, and not a body described from life. Not an official length. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. The atlas has no body and no ordinary spinous process; the axis is marked by the odontoid process. The seventh is marked by the long, not bifurcated, spinous process. 2. It is Gray’s “sometimes,” part of the 1918 record, not a classroom diagnosis of a living person. 3. Official lengths remain reserved. Later osteology not yet taught remains reserved.

Check. 1. c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C. The third side is 4√3, because c² = 48. 120° = 180° − 60°, so cos 120° = −cos 60° = −1/2. 2. Sequar, sequēris, sequētur, sequēmur, sequēminī, sequentur.
