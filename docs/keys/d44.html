Mathematics · Geometry XX: a line and a circle, as a system

Check. 1. (1, −1) and (−1, 1), because x² + x² = 2, x = ±1, and y = −x. 2. No: x² + 9 = 4 gives x² = −5, no real x. 3. Model: intersection of a line and a circle. Own words should match this idea: The point or points that lie on both a line and a circle. Substituting the line into the circle’s equation gives a quadratic equation, which may have two solutions, one solution (a tangent line), or none. Near-miss: Not “a line always cuts a circle twice.” A line may miss, or may touch once. And not the intersection of two lines (that was a linear system). The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. (0, 3) and (0, −3), invented.
  2. One meeting, at (0, 1): x² + 1 = 1, so x = 0. Tangent at (0, 1).
  3. A line may miss, or may touch once, as y = 3 and y = 2 showed with x² + y² = 4.
  4. One real solution means the line and the circle share exactly one point, which is the tangent case already named.
  5. We are finding points that satisfy both equations at once, as in a system.

The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  4. A copied précis.
  5. A note that the work is practice, not a score.

Science · Afterimages: a careful observation, without an invented mechanism

Check. 1. A later color or shape after the gaze has moved from a bright color or form to a plain surface. 2. For example a time in seconds, a wavelength, a cell count, or a claimed full mechanism. 3. Model: afterimage. Own words should match this idea: A color or shape that may still appear after the gaze has moved from a bright color or a bright form to a plain surface. Today it is taught as a careful classroom observation, not as a counted receptor mechanism. Near-miss: Not a proof of a particular receptor theory, and not a demand to stare at the sun or at any other unsafe light. A colored paper on a table is enough, or the observation may be described without being forced. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. It is a further fact about experience, named carefully; we do not invent a cell-by-cell cause we have not sourced.
  2. We have not sourced a numbered law of that kind for this lesson, and we will not invent one.
  3. Stop. The try is optional; the name can still be used from the description.
  4. It is an observation about an experience, not a test of a person.
  5. A note that the work is not a score.

Modern language · Spanish: deber + infinitive

Check. 1. No debe quitar la regla. 2. Debemos estudiar. 3. Model: deber + infinitive. Own words should match this idea: A present-tense pattern: deber plus an infinitive, for “ought to / should / must” do something. It is not a past tense, and it is not the English noun “debt” used as a verb. Near-miss: Not tener que (Day 26), which was already taught as “have to.” The two can be close in everyday use; today we learn deber + infinitive as its own pattern, and we do not invent a fine legal contrast we have not taught. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. Debo estudiar (infinitive after debo).
  2. A heard sentence with deber + infinitive.
  3. For example ir a, acabar de, or tener que.
  4. The Spanish is a present-tense pattern; the Celsius figures stay in the English lessons.
  5. A copied model sentence.

Economics & personal finance · An incentive (an invented shop example)

Check. 1. A reason that makes a choice more or less attractive, often a gain or a loss that follows the choice. 2. A person may still refuse; an incentive tilts, it does not, by itself, force. 3. Model: incentive. Own words should match this idea: A reason that makes a choice more or less attractive, often a gain or a loss that follows the choice. It is not automatically money, and it is not automatically a good reason. Near-miss: Not a command, and not a full course in taxes or wages. Opportunity cost (Day 4) is still the thing given up; an incentive is something added that tilts a choice. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. A clearly invented second example, labeled invented.
  2. The lesson said an incentive is not automatically money, and not the only reason people act.
  3. The example would pretend to be a measured official rule.
  4. A cautious, labeled connection, or a note that the leftover was itself invented.
  5. A note that the work is not a score.
