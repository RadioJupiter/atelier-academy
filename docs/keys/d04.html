Mathematics · Geometry II: congruent triangles and the isosceles triangle

Check. 1. No. AAA shows the same shape, not necessarily the same size. 2. Angle F (corresponding vertices C and F).

  1. In triangles AOC and BOD: AO = OB, CO = OD (bisected), ∠AOC = ∠BOD (vertical). SAS ⇒ congruent ⇒ AC = BD. ∎
  2. Triangles OAD and OBC: OA = OB, OD = OC, angle O common. SAS ⇒ AD = BC. ∎
  3. Triangles ABM and ACM: AB = AC, BM = MC, AM common. SSS ⇒ congruent, so ∠BAM = ∠CAM (bisector) and ∠AMB = ∠AMC; these are adjacent and supplementary, so each is 90°. ∎
  4. Compare triangle ABC with ACB: ∠B = ∠C, BC = CB, ∠C = ∠B. ASA ⇒ congruent ⇒ AB = AC. ∎
  5. Not valid. With ∠A = 30°, AC = 10 and BC = 6 (opposite A): the height from C to the line of AB is 10 · sin 30° = 5 < 6 < 10, so a circle of radius 6 around C cuts that line at two points on the same side of A, giving two different triangles (∠B ≈ 56.4° or ≈ 123.6°).
  6. AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C; AB = BC ⇒ ∠A = ∠C. So all three are equal. ∎

(4) Wit and morality combined. (5) Refresh memories daily against folly. (6) Idle mind sprouts follies. (7) Socrates: heaven to men; me: clubs, coffee-houses.

Atelier · Value: the nine-step scale

Check. 1. How light or dark a surface is. 2. Uneven steps will throw off later judgements of mid-grays and shadows.

The steps should appear equally spaced when squinting; the most common fault is too many light steps and a sudden jump to dark. A good scale makes step 5 a convincing middle gray. In the gradation, no bands or blotches should be visible at arm's length.
