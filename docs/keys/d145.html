Latin · Friday mix: the perfect of sequor and ēgredior, with already-taught deponent tenses

Check. 1. Secūtus sum, secūtus es, secūtus est, secūtī sumus, secūtī estis, secūtī sunt. Ēgressus sum, ēgressus es, ēgressus est, ēgressī sumus, ēgressī estis, ēgressī sunt. 2. Puer mē secūtus est, et tum ēgressus est: he followed me, or has followed me, and then he went out, or has gone out. 3. Loquar is I shall speak, from loquor, a future. Secūtus eram is I had followed, the pluperfect, not the perfect secūtus sum.

Exercises. 1. Secūtī sumus; ēgressī sunt. Sequēmur; ēgredientur. 2. A fair mix keeps each verb’s time: for example a perfect secūtus est beside a future ēgrediētur. 3. Any later deponent tense not yet taught, including a future perfect, remains reserved.

Mathematics · Friday mix: the radian, arc length s = rθ, and exact values in radians

Check. 1. A radian is the central angle whose arc equals the radius. A full turn is 2π radians. 2. 60° = π/3; s = 6 × (π/3) = 2π. The radius 6 is invented. 3. sin(π/6) = 1/2; cos(π/3) = 1/2; sin(π − π/4) = sin(3π/4) = sin(π/4) = √2/2.

Exercises. 1. 270° = 3π/2. Sin(3π/2) = −1; cos(3π/2) = 0, already taught as 270° on the unit circle. 2. θ = (2π)/4 = π/2 radians, which is 90°. 3. The ambiguous case remains reserved. Official measured arcs on a real wheel are not invented.

Writing · Friday mix: a refutation distinguished from a maxim, a chreia, a narrative, and a fable

Check. 1. A refutation examines a claim and gives reasons why it should not be accepted. The set invented claim is “A story is true because it has been told many times.” 2. A maxim is a short general precept. A chreia needs a person, an occasion, and a saying or act. A narrative tells a complete event in order, without a required precept. A fable’s moral is a stated general lesson, often after an animal story. 3. A fair invented refutation is short, labeled invented, and aimed at a claim. It must not become a maxim, a chreia, a narrative, a fable, a confirmation, or a claim about a living person.

Exercises. 1. A maxim. 2. A refutation. 3. Confirmation remains reserved. A full later elaboration of a maxim also remains reserved.

Physics · Friday mix: the Doppler effect, Example 17.4, and a first look at a sonic boom

Check. 1. f_obs = f_s × v_w / (v_w ± v_s), minus for approach. 167 Hz and 136 Hz. 2. 150 Hz, because the relative velocity between engineer and horn is zero. 3. A constructive interference of sound created by an object moving faster than sound. The Example 17.4 train, at 35.0 m/s, is slower than the example’s 340 m/s, so the shift is an ordinary Doppler shift. 4. Model: a sonic boom. Own words should match this idea: A sonic boom, in OpenStax 17.4, is a constructive interference of sound created by an object moving faster than sound, a shock wave along a cone. It is not the ordinary Doppler shift of a source still slower than sound, and it is not an official measurement of a particular aircraft not in the book. Near-miss: Not the Doppler shift of Example 17.4, whose train is slower than sound. Not an official recorded boom of a particular flight. Not Cerenkov radiation, which remains reserved, and not an invented official value of the speed of light. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. 17.0 Hz toward and 14.0 Hz away. The denominators 340 − 35.0 and 340 + 35.0 do not produce equal shifts. 2. Two, one from its nose and one from its tail. 3. Bow wakes more generally, Cerenkov radiation, an official speed of light, and official frequencies of vehicles not in the example remain reserved.

History · Friday mix: the first-voyage letter’s 178 leagues, May and November, and rivers bearing gold

Check. 1. “One hundred and seventy-eight full leagues due east.” “188 leagues” is Major’s footnote, not the letter. 2. Trees “as green and luxuriant as in Spain in the month of May”; nightingales “in November.” Mountains “with which the island of Cetefrey cannot be compared,” without an official modern identification. 3. “The harbours on the coast,” and rivers “most of them bearing gold.” Do not invent official gold weights, official counts, official identifications, or conversions of the leagues.

Exercises. 1. “Española is a wonder.” “Most of them bearing gold,” of the rivers; or “extensive mines of gold and other metals.” 2. The letter is a voyage report, earlier than the June 7, 1494 treaty. 3. Later parts of the letter, official modern identifications, and official conversions and quantities remain reserved.

Modern language · Spanish Friday mix: sea, vaya, and esté, with regular subjunctives, the future, and the conditional

Check. 1. Ser: sea, seas, sea, seamos, seáis, sean. Ir: vaya, vayas, vaya, vayamos, vayáis, vayan. Estar: esté, estés, esté, estemos, estéis, estén. 2. Vaya. 3. Haya is the irregular present subjunctive of haber, not yet taught.

Exercises. 1. Fair invented examples: “Quiere que vayamos mañana.” “Mañana iremos.” “En tu lugar, yo iría.” 2. Está is the present indicative of estar; the subjunctive is esté. Sea is ser, not ir; “that he go” is vaya. 3. Haya, and other irregular subjunctives not yet taught, remain reserved.

Seminar · Anatomy: the thoracic vertebrae, from Gray 1918, a first look

Check. 1. They “are intermediate in size between those of the cervical and lumbar regions; they increase in size from above downward.” They have facets on the sides of the bodies for the heads of the ribs, and facets on the transverse processes of all except the eleventh and twelfth for the tubercles of the ribs. 2. The middle bodies “are heart-shaped.” The spinous process is “long,” “directed obliquely downward,” and “ends in a tuberculated extremity.” 3. The lesson is artistic anatomy from a public-domain text, not a medical diagnosis, and no body is being described from life. Official lengths are not invented. The peculiarities of the first, ninth, tenth, eleventh, and twelfth, and the lumbar vertebrae, remain reserved. 4. Model: the thoracic vertebrae (Gray, 1918). Own words should match this idea: In Gray’s 1918 Anatomy, via Theodora, the thoracic vertebrae “are intermediate in size between those of the cervical and lumbar regions; they increase in size from above downward.” They are “distinguished by the presence of facets on the sides of the bodies for articulation with the heads of the ribs, and facets on the transverse processes of all, except the eleventh and twelfth, for articulation with the tubercles of the ribs.” Official lengths are not invented, and the lesson is not a diagnosis. Near-miss: Not last week’s seventh cervical vertebra, vertebra prominens, which is cervical, not thoracic. Not a lumbar vertebra, not yet taught in full. Not an official length, and not a diagnosis of a living person. Not a body being described from life. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Vertebra prominens is the seventh cervical vertebra, marked by a long, not bifurcated, spinous process, already taught. Today’s first look is the thoracic series, marked by rib facets. 2. It is Gray’s note that those vertebrae have peculiarities. Those peculiarities are not today’s first look and remain reserved. 3. Official lengths, a diagnosis, a body from life, T1 and T9–T12 in detail, and the lumbar vertebrae remain reserved.

Vocabulary review

Check. 1. A radian is the central angle whose arc equals the radius; 2π rad = 360°, so π rad = 180°, and π/3 = 60°. Arc length s = rθ = 6 × (π/3) = 2π. 2. Secūtus sum, I followed, or I have followed; sequor, I follow; sequar, I shall follow; secūtus eram, I had followed. Ēgressus sum is I went out, or I have gone out, the perfect of ēgredior, a different verb. 3. Sea; vaya; esté. Vaya: “that she go” is ir, not ser, and not the indicative va. 4. For a moving source, f_obs = f_s × v_w / (v_w ± v_s), minus toward the observer, plus away. Official Example 17.4: approaching 167 Hz, receding 136 Hz, and the engineer hears 150 Hz. 340 m/s is that example’s official speed of sound, and is not to be replaced by 331 or 343. 5. Current is I = ΔQ/Δt, charge passing a point per unit time; 1 A = 1 C/s. Example 20.1(a): 720 C in 4.00 s is 180 A. 6. “One hundred and seventy-eight full leagues due east.” “188 leagues” is Major’s editorial footnote, not the letter’s body, and is not taught as an official correction. Do not convert the 178 leagues into official modern miles. 7. “I could easily forgive his pride, if he had not mortified mine.” Mary: “Vanity and pride are different things, though the words are often used synonimously.” “Pride relates more to our opinion of ourselves, vanity to what we would have others think of us.” “Synonimously” is the 1813 wording. 8. P = 3/6 + 3/6 − 2/6 = 2/3. The remainder is $10, because 40 + 20 + 10 = 70 and 80 − 70 = 10. A deductible is borne first only if a named event happens; the $10 is leftover income after invented expenses, not a deductible.
