Latin · Deponent tenses, reviewed

Check. 1. Loquor, I speak; loquēbar, I was speaking or used to speak; locūtus sum, I spoke or have spoken. Cōnor, I try; cōnābar, I was trying or used to try; cōnātus sum, I tried or have tried. 2. Nōs loquēbāmur: we were speaking or used to speak (imperfect). Vōs locūtī estis: you (plural) spoke or have spoken (perfect). Agricolae loquuntur: the farmers speak (present). 3. Puerō locūtō is an absolute, a loosely attached phrase with no sum. The imperfect “was speaking” is loquēbātur.

Exercises. 1. Model: Ego loquor / loquēbar / locūtus sum, with honest translations. 2. Cōnantur, they try; cōnātī sunt, they tried or have tried.

Mathematics · Similar triangles and a scale factor, reviewed

Check. 1. AA: two pairs of equal angles are enough, because the third angles then match from the already-taught angle sum of a triangle. 2. 10, 24, and 26. 3. Similar triangles have the same shape, not necessarily the same size. Same size would be congruent, a different already-taught idea.

Exercises. 1. k = 5/10 = 1/2; the legs are 3 and 4. 2. k = 9/3 = 3; the other leg is 12, the hypotenuse is 15.

Writing · Franklin’s method, reviewed on Inferno VI.1–57

Check. 1. First make hints, then reconstruct from those hints. The check is against the set text, Longfellow, not against a smoothed paraphrase. 2. Rain “Eternal, maledict, and cold, and heavy,” with hail, sombre-hued water, and snow. Cerberus, three gullets, quieted when earth is thrown into the rapacious gullets. “You citizens were wont to call me Ciacco”; “the pernicious sin of gluttony.” 3. “The rustic party” belongs to lines 58–115, already taught later in the canto, not to today’s already-set 1–57 hints.

Exercises. 1. A fair reconstruction includes the maledict rain, Cerberus quieted with earth, and Ciacco’s self-naming. A word to check is “maledict,” or “unctuous,” or “wont.” 2. Do not add an official historical identification beyond the name the poem gives.

Physics · Forced oscillations and resonance, reviewed

Check. 1. A forced oscillation is driven by a periodic driving force. The natural frequency is the frequency with no driving and no damping. Resonance is driving at that natural frequency, when energy is transferred most efficiently and the amplitude is greatest. 2. Less damping: higher and narrower peak. Heavy damping: lower amplitude and a broader response. 3. OpenStax 16.8 does not give an official wind speed for that bridge; a number would be invented.

Exercises. 1. A higher, narrower peak. Do not invent an official measured pitch. Waves, wavelength, and vw are Wednesday’s review, from 16.9, not today’s 16.8 job. 2. Waves, from OpenStax 16.9.

History · Dewing, Buildings I.2, reviewed

Check. 1. The column is “not a monolith.” The brass, “in its colour, is softer than pure gold.” The figure holds a globe with a cross, and has “neither sword nor spear nor any other weapon.” 2. Eirenê is “scarcely second to any of the churches in Byzantium, save that of Sophia.” The later houses are the House of Isidorus and the House of Arcadius, in “shewing honour to God.” 3. Invent no official column height, brass weight, income, room count, or shrine count. I.3 begins the churches of Mary, a new chapter, and remains reserved.

Exercises. 1. They are Dewing’s forms (British spelling as in the set text) and must not be silently changed inside a quotation. 2. He refuses a detailed catalogue of the other shrines, because they are “so numerous and so great in size.”

Modern language · Spanish: the preterite, reviewed

Check. 1. Hablé, I spoke; comí, I ate; fui, I went or I was; vi, I saw. Fui serves both ir and ser, as already taught. 2. They share the already-taught preterite fui, but not an imperfect. 3. Ayer pidió: yesterday he or she asked for (or ordered). Ayer durmió: yesterday he or she slept.

Exercises. 1. Model: Ayer comí temprano. Ayer vi el cielo. 2. Iba is imperfect, I was going or used to go, not a completed preterite.

Seminar · Logic: third-figure moods, reviewed

Check. 1. M is the subject of both premises. 2. All M are P; some M are S; therefore some S are P. Conclusion: some spades are metal. That is a conclusion from invented terms, not an official fact about tools. 3. Darapti has two universal premises (all M are S). Datisi’s second premise is particular (some M are S).

Exercises. 1. Ferison is EIO-3: No M are P; some M are S; therefore some S are not P. Felapton is EAO-3: its second premise is universal (all M are S). 2. Datisi’s conclusion is particular, some S are P, not universal.
