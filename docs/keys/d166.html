Latin · A result clause with ut, a first look

Check. 1. Puella tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur: she comes so quickly that she follows him (result). Puella venit ut eum sequātur: she comes in order that she follow him (purpose). 2. Sequētur is the future, not the present subjunctive sequātur required in both clauses. 3. Tam signals the result: so quickly that the following happens. 4. Model: a result clause with ut, a first look. Own words should match this idea: A result clause with ut, in this first look, marks what happens as a result, not a purpose. It is often signaled by tam, ita, or sic in the main clause, and the verb in the ut-clause is the already-taught present subjunctive. Puella tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur may be glossed she comes so quickly that she follows him, or he comes so quickly that he follows him, according to the subject. It is not last week’s purpose clause Puella venit ut eum sequātur, she comes in order that she follow him, and not the future sequētur. Near-miss: Not last week’s purpose clause with ut, which marks an intended purpose and need not have tam, ita, or sic. Not sequētur, the future. Not a negative result, which uses ut nōn and waits until tomorrow. Not nē, which still marks a negative purpose. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Model: Puella tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur, and Puella venit ut eum sequātur, with the translations above. 2. Puer tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur: he comes so quickly that he follows him.

Mathematics · Continuous at a point, a first look

Check. 1. f is defined at a, and lim x→a f(x) = f(a). 2. f(3) = 6, and lim x→3 2x = 6, so both conditions hold. 3. Yes. q(3) = 0, and lim x→3 (x − 3) = 0, so q is continuous at 3. 4. Model: continuous at a point, a first look. Own words should match this idea: A function f is continuous at a number a, in this first look, when f is defined at a and the limit of f(x) as x approaches a equals f(a). On the invented function f(x) = 2x, f is continuous at 3, because f(3) = 6 and, from the already-taught table, lim x→3 2x = 6. The English adjective continuous is from Latin continuus, from contineō, “hold together,” as given on Wiktionary. A formal epsilon argument remains reserved. Near-miss: Not a first look at a limit as if continuity had already been named in week 32. Not a claim that a function is continuous at a point where it is not defined. Not a formal epsilon argument, which remains reserved. Not ∞ treated as a number to substitute. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. u(5) = 5, and lim x→5 x = 5, so u is continuous at 5. 2. v(5) = 4, and lim x→5 4 = 4, so v is continuous at 5.

Modern language · Spanish: the irregular participle puesto, a first look

Check. 1. He puesto, has puesto, ha puesto, hemos puesto, habéis puesto, han puesto. I have put; he has put, or she has put. 2. Poner does not form a regular participle *ponido; the form is puesto. 3. Ha is the helping form of haber. Hay means there is, or there are. Haya is “that there be.” 4. Model: the irregular participle puesto, a first look. Own words should match this idea: Puesto is the irregular past participle of poner, to put or to place, used with the already-taught helping forms of haber: he puesto, has puesto, ha puesto, hemos puesto, habéis puesto, han puesto. It is not visto, last week’s participle of ver, and not dicho, hecho, or escrito. It is not a regular *ponido. The Spanish participle puesto is the past participle of poner, from Latin postus, from positus, as given in the Spanish section of Wiktionary. Other irregular participles, and a present-perfect subjunctive, remain reserved. Near-miss: Not last week’s visto as if it were today’s participle. Not dicho, hecho, or escrito as if they were new. Not *ponido, a regular form that poner does not use. Not a present-perfect subjunctive, which remains reserved. Not the other-language etymology of a different word puesto. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Model: He puesto el libro en la mesa. Hemos puesto los libros en la mesa. 2. Ella ha visto el libro: she has seen the book. Ella ha puesto el libro: she has put the book, or she has placed the book.
