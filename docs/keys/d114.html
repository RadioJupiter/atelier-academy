Latin · The deponent imperfect, a second mixed practice

Check. Model: deponent imperfect review, week 23. Own words should match this idea: A second mixed practice of this week’s deponent imperfect with last week’s perfect and with the present table, still in original sentences. The review names no new verb and no new tense. Near-miss: Not a first look, and not a new deponent. Sequor and ēgredior in the first person remain reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Cōnābar, cōnābāris, cōnābātur, cōnābāmur, cōnābāminī, cōnābantur. Cōnābar: I was trying, or I used to try. Cōnābātur: he or she was trying, or used to try. Heri ego loquēbar, sed tū locūtus es: yesterday I was speaking, but you spoke, or have spoken.

Exercises. Model: Ego loquēbar, I was speaking. Puer locūtus est, the boy spoke. Locūtī estis is the perfect, “you (plural) spoke,” so it is the form that is missing the imperfect meaning.

Mathematics · Geometry LXVI: sine, cosine, and tangent, mixed

Check. Model: sine, cosine, and tangent, mixed. Own words should match this idea: A mixed practice of the three already-taught right-triangle ratios, together with tan A = sin A / cos A and tan(90° − A) = 1 / tan A. Degrees only. No new ratio is named. Near-miss: Not a first look at any of those ratios. The radian, the unit circle, and the reciprocal names remain reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Sin A = 3/5, cos A = 4/5, tan A = 3/4, and (3/5)/(4/5) = 3/4. Tan A = 4/3 would swap opposite and adjacent; that is tan(90° − A), not tan A.

Exercises. Tan A = 9/12 = 3/4, and (9/15)/(12/15) = (3/5)/(4/5) = 3/4. Tan(90° − A) = 12/9 = 4/3. Sin D = 12/13, cos D = 5/13, tan D = 12/5, and (12/13)/(5/13) = 12/5.

Science · OpenStax Example 14.5: conduction through an ice box

Check. Model: OpenStax Example 14.5, the ice box. Own words should match this idea: OpenStax Example 14.5 applies Q/t = kA(T2 − T1)/d to a Styrofoam ice box, then uses the already-taught heat of fusion to find how much ice melts in one day. The official figures in the example are A = 0.950 m², d = 2.50 cm = 0.0250 m, T1 = 0 °C, T2 = 35.0 °C, t = 1 day = 86,400 s, k = 0.010 J/s·m·°C for Styrofoam from Table 14.3, and Lf of ice = 334 × 10³ J/kg. The recomputed rate is 13.3 J/s, Q = 1.15 × 10⁶ J, and m = 3.44 kg. Near-miss: Not a first look at the conduction formula, already taught yesterday. Not Example 14.6, the aluminum pan, which remains reserved. Not an invented k for Styrofoam. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Knowns: A = 0.950 m², d = 0.0250 m, T1 = 0 °C, T2 = 35.0 °C, t = 86,400 s, k = 0.010 J/s·m·°C, Lf = 334 × 10³ J/kg. Q/t = 13.3 J/s. Q = 1.15 × 10⁶ J, m = 3.44 kg. Example 14.6 remains reserved.

Exercises. Q/t would be half of 13.3, which is 6.65 J/s. A k of 0.020 is not the Table 14.3 value for Styrofoam, so it would be invented for this box.

Literature · Inferno VI.58–115: the divided city, and the close of the canto

Check. Model: the divided city, and the close of Inferno VI. Own words should match this idea: Longfellow’s Inferno VI, lines 58–115, continues Ciacco’s speech and then closes the canto. Asked what shall come of “the citizens of the divided city,” Ciacco says they “will come to bloodshed,” that “the rustic party / Will drive the other out with much offence,” that “behoves it this one fall / Within three suns, and rise again the other,” and that “The just are two, and are not understood there; / Envy and Arrogance and Avarice / Are the three sparks that have all hearts enkindled.” He names Farinata, Tegghiaio, Jacopo Rusticucci, Arrigo, and Mosca as among “the blacker souls,” under “a different sin,” to be seen if the traveller descends so far. He then falls “prone like the other blind.” The Guide says he “wakes no more / This side the sound of the angelic trumpet.” Asked whether these torments will increase after “the mighty sentence,” the Guide answers, from “thy science,” that “as the thing more perfect is, / The more it feels of pleasure and of pain,” so that hereafter they “look to be” more than now. They find “Plutus the great enemy.” The lines are taught as the poem states them. The two who are just are not officially identified here, because the poem does not name them. Canto VII remains reserved. Near-miss: Not a first look at lines 1–57. Not an official historical commentary added to the poem. Canto VII, which begins from Plutus, remains reserved. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. After long contention they will come to bloodshed; the rustic party will drive the other out; then “behoves it this one fall / Within three suns, and rise again the other.” The just are two, and are not understood; the three sparks are Envy, Arrogance, and Avarice. Ciacco falls prone like the other blind. He wakes no more this side the angelic trumpet. After the mighty sentence, because the more perfect feels more, they look to be more tormented than now. They find Plutus the great enemy.

Exercises. They are among the blacker souls; a different sin downweighs them to the bottom; they can be seen if the traveller descends so far. Plutus is named at the end of Canto VI, but Canto VII, which would take him up, remains reserved.

Modern language · Spanish: preterite and imperfect, mixed

Check. Model: preterite and imperfect, mixed. Own words should match this idea: A mixed practice of this week’s choice between already-taught preterite and imperfect forms, including backgrounds paired with completed events, and the contrasts fui / iba and fui / era. No new verb is added. Instruction remains in English. Near-miss: Not a first look at the choice, and not a new paradigm. Vía remains a miss for veía. Identifying invented characters are not used. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. Ayer, mientras iba, vi el mercado: Yesterday, while I was going, I saw the market. Era is the imperfect of ser, “I was” or “I used to be,” not the verb for going; ayer fui al mercado uses fui as ir.

Exercises. Model: Cada mañana comía temprano. Ayer, mientras comía, vi el cielo. Fui: I went (ir) or I was (ser, completed). Iba: I was going or used to go (ir). Era: I was or used to be (ser).

Seminar · Economics: a markup, then a discount, invented figures

Check. Model: a markup, then a discount. Own words should match this idea: A markup raises an invented price by a percent of that price; a discount then taken after the markup is a percent of the new, marked-up price, not of the original, and not a simple subtraction of the two percents. This is distinguished from last week’s successive discounts, which took two percents off, each from the remainder. Tax and compound interest remain reserved. All figures are invented and labeled invented. Near-miss: Not a first look at a single discount, or at successive discounts, already taught. Not a tax, and not compound interest. Not an official store price. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card. $100 becomes $110, then $99. $100 would treat the percents as cancelling. $81 was last week’s two successive discounts, with no markup. $200 becomes $220, then 25 percent of 220 is 55, and 220 − 55 = $165.

Exercises. Last week took 10 percent off $100 and then 10 percent off $90, leaving $81. Today adds 10 percent first, then takes 10 percent off the new amount, leaving $99. The discount is always a percent of the current price, so 10 percent of 110 is larger than 10 percent of 100, and the two steps do not cancel.
