Mathematics · Geometry XLIX: the surface area of a sphere, and mixed solids

Check. 1. A = 4π × 16 = 64π square units. 2. V = (1/3)π × 9 × 4 = 12π cubic units. 3. One is an area, from 4πr²; the other is a volume, from (4/3)πr³. The matching numerals are a coincidence of r = 3. 4. Model: surface area of a sphere. Own words should match this idea: The surface area of a sphere of radius r is A = 4πr², in square units. OpenStax, College Physics 2e, Appendix C, Table C9, lists this formula beside the volume (CC BY). Exact answers stay in terms of π. Near-miss: Not a first look at yesterday’s volume (4/3)πr³, and not last week’s circle area πr² taken as the sphere’s surface. 4πr² is four times the area of a great circle, but it is still an area, not a volume. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

  1. V = (1/3) × 36 × 9 = 108 cubic units.
  2. Cylinder 90π cubic units; cone 30π cubic units.
  3. 4πr² = 64π, so r² = 16, r = 4. V = (4/3)π × 64 = 256/3 π cubic units.
  4. V = (32/3)π; (32/3) × 3.14 = 32 × 1.0466… ≈ 33.49 cubic units, using π ≈ 3.14, a labeled school approximation.

Writing & Rhetoric · A précis of Dewing I.1.61–63

Both from Harvey/NIST at 101.325 kPa. 2. 15 × 2834 = 42,510 J, or 42.51 kJ. Invented mass. 3. Both figures are at 0 °C. Their difference was not opened as an official 100 °C enthalpy of vaporization, and this course does not invent one.

  1. The ratio compares subliming 1 g of ice at 0 °C with melting 1 g of ice at 0 °C, using the two Harvey figures at that temperature.
  2. Warming: 21 J, a temperature change using cp. Melting: 3,334 J, a change of state at 0 °C. Invented masses.
  3. 14,170 / 2834 = 5 g. Invented sample.
  4. 0.9167 g/cm³ is a density of ice Ih at 0 °C, from the same table’s density row. ΔsublH is a phase-transition energy, in J/g.
