Mathematics · Geometry I: definitions, axioms and the first proofs

Check. 1. The vertical angle is 70° (vertical angles are equal). An adjacent angle on the straight line is 110° (adjacent angles on a straight line are supplementary). 2. 3x + (x + 20°) = 180°, so 4x = 160°, x = 40. The angles are 120° and 60°.

  1. Two different angles cannot be vertical (vertical angles are equal), so they are adjacent: x + (x + 40°) = 180°, x = 70°. 70°, 110°, 70°, 110°.
  2. Let the angles be α and β with α + β = 180°. Each bisector makes α/2 and β/2 with the common side, so the angle between the bisectors is α/2 + β/2 = 90°. ∎
  3. Let ∠AOB and ∠COD be vertical, each equal to α (OA opposite OC, OB opposite OD). Let OM bisect ∠AOB and ON bisect ∠COD. Then ∠MOB = α/2, ∠BOC = 180° − α (supplementary to ∠AOB), ∠CON = α/2. Sum: α/2 + (180° − α) + α/2 = 180°, so OM and ON form a straight angle: M, O, N are collinear. ∎
  4. Six rays leave the point, forming C(6,2) = 15 pairs of rays; 3 pairs are opposite rays (straight angles), leaving 12 angles. Each has exactly one vertical partner, so there are 6 pairs.
  5. Converse: "Equal angles are vertical." False: e.g., two separate right angles in different places are equal but not vertical.
  6. If ∠A = ∠B, then 180° − ∠A = 180° − ∠B. ∎

Writing & Rhetoric · Imitatio: copywork and copia

Check. 1. Copy exactly first. 2. The skill of saying the same idea in many accurate ways.

  1. Target: zero errors. Common slips are modernising try'd and compleat, and dropping the commas around "and, making short hints … ".
  2. (a) The short sentences move fast, like rapid events; the long sentence slows down to explain a careful process. (b) took the papers → made hints → laid them by → tried to complete them without the book → (by) expressing each sentiment fully. Clarity comes from chronological order, parallel participles (making, looking, expressing) and commas that mark each step.
  3. Check that each slot is filled and the sentence stays grammatical. Model: With this view I took six of the master drawings, and, tracing only the main angles of each, put them away for a week, and then, without looking at the originals, tried to redraw them, by rebuilding every proportion from those few lines.
  4. Examples: Your letter delighted me. · I was greatly pleased by your letter. · Nothing could have pleased me more than your letter. · How your letter cheered me! · Your letter brought me real joy. · Reading your letter was a pleasure. · Your letter arrived and my day brightened. · What pleasure your letter gave me! · Your letter was a gift. · I cannot say how much your letter pleased me. Check that all ten keep the same meaning.

Science · Phenomenon: the candle under the jar

  1. Results vary with the jar and timing; rises of roughly 10–20% are typical. The value is not reliably 21%.
  2. (b) is supported. Typical evidence: the water rises mostly after the flame goes out, as the gas cools; bubbles escape while the jar is lowered over the hot flame; more candles usually give a larger rise even though the jar holds the same oxygen; the oxygen explanation also ignores the CO₂ produced. Careful studies (Vera, Rivera & Núñez, 2011; see SOURCES.md) reach the same conclusion: heating, escape of gas and cooling dominate, and oxygen consumption is not the main cause.
  3. V₂/V₁ = 293/350 ≈ 0.837, so the volume shrinks by about 16%: the same size as the observed rise, from cooling alone.

Literature · Homer, Iliad, Book 1

Model points (your answer should use your own words and line references): 1. The wrath (anger) of Achilles. The poem is about one man's anger and its cost, not about the whole war or its victory; the first lines stress the deaths his anger causes among his own side. 2. Agamemnon: he dishonours a priest, causes the plague, and takes Briseis to save face. Achilles: he nearly draws his sword on his commander, and asks for his own army to suffer. A good answer weighs honour (timē) on both sides. 3. Any accurate example; the explanation should say what the comparison makes vivid or what the epithet stresses. 4. Examples: Apollo answers Chryses' prayer with the plague; Athena stops Achilles from drawing his sword; Thetis petitions Zeus; the gods quarrel on Olympus, mirroring the human quarrel.

Atelier · Bargue plate: the block-in

Check. 1. To place the largest shapes and proportions in light line. 2. The greatest height and the greatest width.

The plate itself is the key: compare in sight-size from the marked spot, then in the mirror. Standard for a beginning block-in: overall height-to-width ratio within 3%, and every major alignment that is vertical on the plate is vertical on yours. Do not shade until the block-in passes this check.
