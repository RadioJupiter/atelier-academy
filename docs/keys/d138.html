Latin · Sequar and ēgrediar, mixed with already-taught deponent tenses

Check. 1. Sequor, I follow; sequar, I shall follow; secūtus eram, I had followed. 2. Puella ēgrediētur: she will go out. Puer eam secūtus erat: he had followed her. 3. Sequuntur is the present, they follow, not the future sequentur.

Exercises. 1. Ēgrediēmur; sequitur. 2. A fair pair would keep sequēmur, we shall follow, in the future, and ēgressī erant, they had gone out, in the pluperfect. 3. The lesson uses Puer and Puella as the Latin words already taught for that work, so an unquoted English “boy” or “girl” is not added as if it were the Latin.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXXI: the law of cosines, finding an angle from three sides

Check. 1. cos C = (a² + b² − c²)/(2ab). 2. 90°, because the cosine is 0. 3. 120°, because the cosine is −1/2.

Exercises. 1. Each angle is 60°, because cos C = (25 + 25 − 25)/50 = 1/2. 2. That would require cosine +1/2, but (9 + 25 − 49)/30 = −1/2, which is 120°. 3. Radians and the ambiguous case remain reserved.

Writing · A short original maxim, labeled invented

Check. 1. A fair answer is one short general precept, clearly labeled invented. 2. It has no person and occasion, and it is not the moral of an animal story. 3. The 1733 wording is sourced. An original line is invented classroom work, not a newly discovered Franklin saying.

Exercises. 1. Remove the living person and the occasion; keep only a general precept, labeled invented. 2. A second precept should still be general, short, and labeled invented. 3. A full later elaboration of a maxim remains reserved.

Physics · Official Examples 17.2 and 17.3, and a factor of two in intensity

Check. 1. I = 5.04×10^−4 W/m², β = 87 dB, from Δp = 0.656 Pa, vw = 331 m/s, and ρ = 1.29 kg/m³, all official in Example 17.2. 2. 3.01 dB, or about 3 dB. 3. 3.0 dB, 7.0 dB, and 10.0 dB.

Exercises. 1. Twice as intense, because a difference of 3.0 dB is a factor of two. 2. 97 dB: 87 dB plus 10 dB. The starting 87 dB is official Example 17.2; the new intensity is an invented multiple, labeled invented, not an official measurement of a new sound. 3. The sound pressure level, and official dB values for places not in Table 17.2, remain reserved.

History · The first-voyage letter: Juana, Cathay, and La Española, in Major’s wording

Check. 1. He thought it “must be the mainland,—the province of Cathay,” then learned “that this land was certainly an island.” 2. One hundred and seven leagues eastward to a cape; eighteen leagues to the next island, which he named “La Española.” 3. It is the editor’s identification, not the letter’s body, and official modern identifications are not being added.

Exercises. 1. “When I reached Juana, I followed its coast to the westward, and found it so large that I thought it must be the mainland,—the province of Cathay.” 2. The letter is a voyage report, earlier than the June 7, 1494 treaty that later divided lands east and west of a line. 3. Do not identify the islands with later official modern names, and do not convert the leagues.

Atelier · Turning the form with the mixed earth hue

Check. 1. Often on the half, because that is where the turn leaves the light and has not yet reached the core shadow, provided the value matches. 2. Match the value to the scale, and keep the half as a turn, not as a hard band. 3. It is mixed from paints already in the four. The object is a simple rounded form, not a head or figure from life.

Exercises. 1. A fair half is a middle value in the mixed earth hue or in ochre, sitting between a lighter plane and a darker core shadow. 2. The mineral would be a fifth tube, which this course does not add, and the limited palette would be broken. 3. A portrait and a fifth tube remain reserved.

Modern language · Spanish: the present subjunctive contrasted with the indicative, the future, and the conditional

Check. 1. Comamos. 2. Hablo, I speak; hablaré, I will speak; hablaría, I would speak; hable, that I speak. 3. Habla is the present indicative. The invented “quiere que” frame in this week takes hable.

Exercises. 1. Haría, last week’s conditional, “would,” after “en tu lugar.” Haga would be an irregular subjunctive of hacer, which has not yet been taught and remains reserved. 2. A fair pair: “Quiere que ellos vivan aquí.” “Ellos vivirán aquí.” The first is subjunctive after “quiere que”; the second is future. 3. Irregular subjunctives remain reserved.

Seminar · Art history: Dewing, Buildings I.3.16–18, the rebuilt shrine of Michael

Check. 1. He “transformed it into a marvellously beautiful building.” It “is in the form of a rectangle (tetrapleuron), and the length appears not much greater than the width.” 2. A thick east wall of “many fitted stones,” drawn back in the middle “so as to form a recess”; “columns of naturally variegated hues”; the west wall “pierced by the doors which lead into the church.” 3. Dewing does not give those official figures. Book I.4 remains reserved. 4. Model: the rebuilt shrine of Michael (Dewing, Buildings I.3.16–18). Own words should match this idea: In Dewing’s 1940 translation of Procopius, Buildings I.3.16–18, the small, badly lighted shrine of Michael, torn down last week “even to the lowest foundations,” is rebuilt: Justinian, “increasing its size to the proportions which it now displays, … transformed it into a marvellously beautiful building.” The church “is in the form of a rectangle (tetrapleuron), and the length appears not much greater than the width.” Official measurements are not invented. Near-miss: Not last week’s unseemly small shrine as if it still stood. Not Book I.4, the churches of Peter and Paul and of Saints Sergius and Bacchus, which remain reserved. Not an official length or an official count of columns. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Last week’s shrine was torn down “even to the lowest foundations.” Today’s extract is the rebuilding of that same shrine, not a first look at a different church. 2. It is Dewing’s wording, and British or older forms in the translation stay inside quotations; teaching prose uses US spelling. 3. Buildings I.4, including Peter and Paul and Saints Sergius and Bacchus, and later Buildings, remain reserved.
