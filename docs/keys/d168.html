Latin · A result clause with ēgredior, a first look

Check. 1. He rises so quickly that he goes out (positive result). He rises so slowly that he does not go out (negative result). He sits in order that he not go out (negative purpose). 2. Ēgrediētur is the future, not the present subjunctive. 3. The third is a purpose clause: it uses nē and has no tam, ita, or sic marking a result. 4. Model: a result clause with ēgredior, a first look. Own words should match this idea: A result clause may also use the already-taught present subjunctive of ēgredior. Puer tam celeriter surgit ut ēgrediātur may be glossed he rises so quickly that he goes out. The verb is ēgrediātur, with ā, not the future ēgrediētur. A negative result uses ut nōn ēgrediātur, not nē ēgrediātur, which would be a negative purpose. Near-miss: Not ēgrediētur, the future. Not nē ēgrediātur used as if it were a negative result. Not Monday’s sequor result as if ēgredior had not yet been shown in this job. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Model: Puer tam celeriter surgit ut ēgrediātur, and Puer tam lentē surgit ut nōn ēgrediātur, with the translations above. 2. Model: Puer sedet nē ēgrediātur, he sits in order that he not go out.

Physics · Official Example 25.4, the critical angle for polystyrene to air

Check. 1. θc = sin⁻¹(1.00/1.49) = sin⁻¹(0.671) = 42.2°, official. 2. Any ray inside the plastic that strikes the surface at an angle greater than 42.2° is totally reflected. 3. The formula θc = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁) is used when the first medium has the larger index; here 1.49 > 1.00. 4. Model: official Example 25.4, the critical angle for polystyrene to air. Own words should match this idea: OpenStax official Example 25.4 asks for the critical angle for light traveling in a polystyrene pipe surrounded by air. The book takes the index of polystyrene as 1.49 and the index of air as 1.00. Then θc = sin⁻¹(1.00/1.49) = sin⁻¹(0.671) = 42.2°, the official result. Any ray inside the plastic that strikes the surface at an angle greater than 42.2° is totally reflected, in the book’s wording. Those figures are official book figures, not invented classroom numbers. Near-miss: Not an invented official index for polystyrene. Not Monday’s definition as if the official 42.2° had already been given then. Not official discussion angles such as water-to-air 48.6°, which remain reserved. Not a claim of total reflection when the ray starts in air. The fresh case should use the term correctly and not be copied from the card.

Exercises. 1. Yes, because 50° is greater than official 42.2°. 2. Those official figures belong to a later sitting and are not to be replaced with invented official numbers.
