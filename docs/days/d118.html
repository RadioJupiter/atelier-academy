Latin · Deponents, the absolute, and AcI, mixed

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • deponent tenses, mixed — Day 113
  • accusative and infinitive (AcI) — Day 63
  • two times of the ablative absolute — Day 97

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

No new construction. Choose among the three deponent tenses, keep the absolute off the tense list, and keep AcI distinct from both. Original sentences only.

Word How to say it Meaning
loquor LOH-kwor I speak, or I am speaking (present)
loquēbar loh-KWAY-bar I was speaking, or I used to speak (imperfect)
locūtus sum lo-KOO-toos soom I spoke, or I have spoken (perfect)
cōnābar koh-NAH-bar I was trying, or I used to try (imperfect)
puerō locūtō PWEE-roh lo-KOO-toh with the boy having spoken (absolute, already taught)

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Remember

Present loquor, imperfect loquēbar, perfect locūtus sum. The absolute puerō locūtō is a loosely attached phrase. AcI has an accusative subject and an infinitive verb.

Watch me

Watch four short labels.

  1. Puer loquēbātur: imperfect deponent.
  2. Puer locūtus est: perfect deponent.
  3. Puerō locūtō: absolute. Magister puerum adesse dīcit: AcI.

Together

Label: locūtī estis; loquēbāmur; loquuntur; cōnātō puerō.

Hint 1: Finite tenses

Locūtī estis is perfect; loquēbāmur is imperfect; loquuntur is present.

Hint 2: The absolute

Cōnātō puerō is an absolute, not a main-clause perfect with sum.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. Translate and label: Ego cōnābar. Nōs locūtī sumus. Puer loquitur.
  2. Why is puerō locūtō not AcI, and not the imperfect loquēbātur?
  3. In AcI, what jobs do the accusative and the infinitive have?

Exercises

  1. Write one original sentence for a deponent imperfect, one for a perfect, and one for AcI with esse. Translate them.
  2. Change loquēbantur to the present and to the perfect, and translate all three.

Mathematics · Tangent, reviewed, with sine and cosine

40 min · Review of already-taught tan A, tan A = sin A / cos A, and tan(90° − A) = 1 / tan A · degrees only

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • tangent of an acute angle — Day 111
  • tangent as sine over cosine — Day 112
  • complementary tangents — Day 113

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This is review. Tan A is opposite over adjacent. Tan A = sin A / cos A, rewritten from the already-taught ratios. Tan(90° − A) = 1 / tan A, because the sides swap. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, if A is opposite 3, tan A = 3/4 and tan(90° − A) = 4/3. Degrees only. The radian, the unit circle, and the reciprocal names remain reserved. Classroom lengths are invented.

Remember

Tan A is opposite over adjacent, and tan A = sin A / cos A. Tan(90° − A) = 1 / tan A. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, if A is opposite 3, tan A = 3/4. Degrees only.

Watch me

Watch the definition, the identity, then the complement.

  1. If A is opposite 3 and adjacent to 4, tan A = 3/4.
  2. (3/5) / (4/5) = 3/4, so sin A / cos A = tan A.
  3. Tan(90° − A) = 4/3. A miss is 3/5, which is sine, or the length 3 by itself.

Together

In a classroom 5-12-13 triangle, B is opposite 5. Give tan B in two ways, and give tan(90° − B).

Hint 1: Opposite over adjacent

Tan B = 5/12.

Hint 2: The identity

(5/13)/(12/13) = 5/12. Tan(90° − B) = 12/5.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key. Classroom lengths are invented.

  1. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, A is opposite 3. Give sin A, cos A, tan A, and tan(90° − A).
  2. Why is tan A = 3/5 a miss, and which already-taught ratio is that?
  3. A classroom 6-8-10 triangle has the same angle A, opposite 6. Why is tan A still 3/4?

Exercises

  1. In a classroom 5-12-13 triangle, D is opposite 12. Give tan D in two ways.
  2. A similar 10-24-26 triangle has E opposite 10. Give tan E and tan(90° − E).

Physics · Waves, reviewed

40 min · Review of already-taught OpenStax 16.9: a wave, λ, vw = λ/T = fλ, Example 16.8, and the two kinds of wave · official speeds not invented

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • wave, wavelength, and wave velocity — Day 111
  • transverse and longitudinal waves — Day 113

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This is review. A wave is a disturbance that propagates from the place it was created. Wavelength λ is the distance between adjacent identical parts. Wave velocity vw = λ / T = fλ. Example 16.8, already recomputed: λ = 10.0 m, T = 5.00 s, vw = 2.00 m/s, the speed of the disturbance, not the gull’s changing vertical speed. A transverse wave has its disturbance perpendicular to propagation (a guitar string, or visible light). A longitudinal wave has its disturbance parallel to propagation (sound in air or water). Water waves are a combination. Official measured speeds of sound, light, or earthquake P- and S-waves are not invented. Superposition, section 16.10, remains reserved. Resonance was Monday’s review, not a first look today.

Remember

A wave is a disturbance that propagates. vw = λ / T = fλ. Example 16.8: vw = 2.00 m/s. Transverse: disturbance perpendicular to propagation. Longitudinal: disturbance parallel to propagation. Invent no official speeds.

Watch me

Watch the definition, the example, then the two kinds.

  1. The disturbance moves; far from shore, the water itself is not what travels forward.
  2. Example 16.8: 10.0 m / 5.00 s = 2.00 m/s.
  3. A guitar string is transverse; sound in air is longitudinal. Do not invent an official P-wave speed.

Together

Recompute Example 16.8, then say whether a guitar-string wave is transverse or longitudinal, and why.

Hint 1: The example

λ = 10.0 m, T = 5.00 s, vw = 2.00 m/s.

Hint 2: The string

The disturbance is perpendicular to the direction of travel, so the wave is transverse.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key. Classroom waves, other than Example 16.8, are invented.

  1. Define a wave, and write the two forms of vw.
  2. Recompute Example 16.8. Why is the gull’s up-and-down speed a miss?
  3. Give one transverse example and one longitudinal example from OpenStax 16.9.

Exercises

  1. An invented wave has λ = 6.0 m and T = 2.0 s. Find vw and f.
  2. Why must you not invent an official P-wave speed in this review?

Modern language · Spanish: preterite versus imperfect, reviewed

40 min · Review of the already-taught choice between preterite and imperfect · instruction in English · no new forms

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • preterite versus imperfect, a first look — Day 111
  • background and a completed event — Day 112
  • era and fui, contrasted in use — Day 113

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

The forms are already taught. The job today is the choice. The preterite presents a completed past: ayer comí, ayer fui, ayer vi. The imperfect presents an ongoing, habitual, or background past: cada mañana comía, cada mañana iba, era, veía. A fair pairing, already taught: Ayer, mientras comía, vi el cielo. Ir and ser share fui, but not an imperfect. Veía, not vía. Instruction remains in English. Do not invent identifying characters.

Word How to say it Meaning
hablé ah-BLEH I spoke (completed)
hablaba ah-BLAH-bah I was speaking, or I used to speak
comí koh-MEE I ate (completed)
comía koh-MEE-ah I was eating, or I used to eat
fui FWEE I went, or I was (completed; ir and ser)
iba EE-bah I was going, or I used to go
era EH-rah I was, or I used to be
vi bee I saw (completed)
veía beh-EE-ah I was seeing, or I used to see

Remember

Preterite: completed. Imperfect: habitual, ongoing, or background. Ayer fui al mercado; cada mañana iba al mercado. Ayer, mientras comía, vi el cielo.

Watch me

Watch a completed sentence, a habitual sentence, then a pairing.

  1. Ayer comí: completed, preterite.
  2. Cada mañana comía: habitual, imperfect.
  3. Ayer, mientras comía, vi el cielo: background comía, completed vi.

Together

Choose and translate: Ayer ___ el cielo (vi / veía). Cada mañana ___ el cielo (vi / veía).

Hint 1: Ayer

A completed seeing takes vi.

Hint 2: Cada mañana

A habit takes veía, not vía.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. State the job of the preterite and the job of the imperfect, and give one already-taught example of each.
  2. Fill: Ayer, mientras ___ (comí / comía), ___ el cielo (vi / veía). Translate.
  3. Why do ir and ser share fui, but not iba or era?

Exercises

  1. Write one original habitual sentence and one original paired sentence. Translate them.
  2. Correct the miss: Ayer, mientras comí, veía el cielo. Say why it is a miss.

Seminar · Economics: discounts and a markup, reviewed

40 min · Review of already-taught single discounts, successive discounts, and a markup then a discount, invented figures · tax reserved

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • successive discounts — Day 109
  • a markup, then a discount — Day 114

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This is review, on invented figures only. A single 10 percent discount on $100 leaves $90. Two successive 10 percent discounts on $100 leave $81, not $80, because the second percent is taken off the remainder. A 10 percent markup on $100 makes $110; a 10 percent discount then, off the new price, leaves $99, not $100, and not $81. Tax and compound interest remain reserved. All figures are invented and labeled invented.

Remember

A second percent is taken off the current price, not off the original again, and not as a simple subtraction of percents. Two 10 percent discounts on $100 leave $81. Ten percent up, then 10 percent down, on $100, leaves $99.

Watch me

Watch the successive discounts, then the markup-then-discount, then the near-misses.

  1. 10 percent of 100 is 10, leaving 90; 10 percent of 90 is 9, leaving $81.
  2. 10 percent of 100 added on is 110; 10 percent of 110 is 11, leaving $99.
  3. A miss is $80, or $100 as if the two 10 percents cancelled.

Together

An invented $80 is marked up 25 percent, then discounted 10 percent. What is the final price, and why is $68 a miss?

Hint 1: The markup

25 percent of 80 is 20, so the new price is $100.

Hint 2: The discount

10 percent of 100 is 10, so the final price is $90. $68 would be 15 percent off 80, as if the percents could be subtracted first.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key. All figures are invented.

  1. An invented $100 takes two successive 10 percent discounts. What is the final price, and why is $80 a miss?
  2. An invented $100 is marked up 10 percent, then discounted 10 percent. What is the final price, and why are $100 and $81 both misses?
  3. An invented $200 is marked up 10 percent, then discounted 25 percent. What is the final price?

Exercises

  1. How do the two $100 paths differ?
  2. Why must you not treat 10 percent up and 10 percent down as a net of zero?
