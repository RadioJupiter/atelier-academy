Latin · The present subjunctive of ēgredior, a first look

40 min · Method: ēgrediar, ēgrediāris, ēgrediātur, ēgrediāmur, ēgrediāminī, ēgrediantur, contrasted with ēgrediētur and with sequātur; purpose clauses reserved

Learn

Key terms

present subjunctive of ēgredior
Definition
The present subjunctive of ēgredior is ēgrediar, ēgrediāris, ēgrediātur, ēgrediāmur, ēgrediāminī, ēgrediantur. The first-person singular ēgrediar is spelled the same as the already-taught future ēgrediar; ēgrediātur, with ā, is not the future ēgrediētur, with ē. It is not sequātur, a different verb.
Example
In an original sentence used only to show the form, Puella ēgrediātur can be glossed she goes out, in the present subjunctive. The ending is -iātur, not the future -iētur.
Not this
Not ēgrediētur, she will go out, or he will go out. Not ēgressus sit, a later perfect subjunctive, reserved. Not sequātur, yesterday’s present subjunctive of sequor. A purpose clause with ut remains reserved.
Connects to
Yesterday’s present subjunctive of sequor, and the already-taught future ēgrediar, ēgrediēris, ēgrediētur.

Ēgredior already has a present indicative, a future ēgrediar, ēgrediēris, ēgrediētur, and the perfect tenses already taught. Today the present subjunctive: ēgrediar, ēgrediāris, ēgrediātur, ēgrediāmur, ēgrediāminī, ēgrediantur. As with sequor, the first-person singular is spelled the same as the future; the other persons split, ā against ē. It is not sequātur, yesterday’s present subjunctive of sequor; the two verbs must not be swapped. The sentences are original, not copied from Ørberg. Identity is handled with Puer and Puella, and with he and she in the gloss. A purpose clause with ut remains reserved.

Word How to say it Meaning

ēgrediāris eh-greh-dee-AH-ris you (s.) go out (present subjunctive)
ēgrediātur eh-greh-dee-AH-toor he goes out, or she goes out (present subjunctive)
ēgrediāmur eh-greh-dee-AH-moor we go out (present subjunctive)
ēgrediantur eh-greh-dee-AHN-toor they go out (present subjunctive)

Mathematics · Geometry XCII: the full disk as a sector, and more invented sectors

40 min · Method: Singapore, faithful; θ = 2π recovers A = πr²; more invented sector areas, labeled invented

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Yesterday’s formula was A = (1/2)r²θ, with θ in radians. Today that formula is checked on the whole disk: if the central angle is a full turn, θ = 2π, then A = (1/2)r²(2π) = πr², the already-taught area of the circle. The sector formula is consistent with the disk. On an invented figure, r = 4 and θ = π/2, A = (1/2) × 16 × (π/2) = 4π, already found yesterday as a together item and now kept as a worked case. Remember

A = (1/2)r²θ, with θ in radians. When θ = 2π, A = πr², the area of the disk. Do not put a radian measure into the degree formula as if it were in degrees.

Key terms

resistivity
Definition
In OpenStax, College Physics 2e, 20.3, resistivity ρ is “an intrinsic property of a material, independent of its shape or size.” For a uniform cylinder of length L and cross-sectional area A, made of a material with resistivity ρ, the resistance is R = ρL / A.

Not this

Connects to

OpenStax, College Physics 2e, 20.3, says that the resistance of an object depends on its shape and on the material of which it is composed. For a uniform cylinder, R is directly proportional to the length L and inversely proportional to the cross-sectional area A. Resistivity ρ is defined so that R is also proportional to ρ, and “Resistivity ρ is an intrinsic property of a material, independent of its shape or size.” The official formula is R = ρL / A. Table 20.1 gives representative official values; the official resistivity of tungsten in that table is 5.6 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m. That table value is official, not invented. Official Example 20.5, which uses this tungsten value to find a filament diameter, waits until Thursday. The temperature coefficient of resistivity, and official household voltage, remain reserved.
