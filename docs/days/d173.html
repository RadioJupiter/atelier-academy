Mathematics · The derivative of invented f(x) = x²

40 min · Method: the difference quotient at a general x, then f'(x) = 2x for invented f(x) = x²; agrees with f'(3) = 6; a formal epsilon argument reserved

Learn

Key terms

the derivative of invented f(x) = x²
Definition
On the invented function f(x) = x², the difference quotient at a general x is [ (x + h)² − x² ] / h = (x² + 2xh + h² − x²) / h = 2x + h, for h ≠ 0. The derivative function is the limit as h approaches 0: f'(x) = lim h→0 (2x + h) = 2x. This agrees with yesterday’s point values: f'(3) = 6, f'(2) = 4, f'(1) = 2, and f'(0) = 0. The function is invented. A formal epsilon argument remains reserved. Derivatives of other functions remain reserved.
Example
For invented f(x) = x², f'(x) = 2x. So f'(5) = 10, and f'(−3) = −6. Those are invented-classroom values, not official data.
Not this
Not yesterday’s two point values as if the general rule had already been proved. Not a formal epsilon argument. Not a derivative of a different function, which remains reserved. Not ∞ treated as a number to substitute.
Connects to
Yesterday’s derivative at a point, and Monday’s difference quotient, on invented f(x) = x². Derivatives of other functions remain reserved.

Yesterday found, on invented f(x) = x², that f'(3) = 6, f'(2) = 4, f'(1) = 2, and f'(0) = 0. Today does the same calculation at a general x, and names the result as a function. The difference quotient is [ (x + h)² − x² ] / h = (2xh + h²) / h = 2x + h, for h ≠ 0. Then f'(x) = lim h→0 (2x + h) = 2x. That general rule agrees with the point values already found: twice 3 is 6, twice 2 is 4, twice 1 is 2, and twice 0 is 0. The function is invented. A formal epsilon argument remains reserved. Derivatives of other functions remain reserved.

Remember

On invented f(x) = x², the difference quotient at a general x is 2x + h, so f'(x) = 2x. This agrees with f'(3) = 6 and f'(2) = 4.

Watch me

Simplify the difference quotient of invented f(x) = x² at a general x, then take the limit.

  1. (x + h)² − x² = 2xh + h².
  2. Divided by h, for h ≠ 0, this is 2x + h.
  3. The limit as h approaches 0 is 2x, so f'(x) = 2x.

Hint 1: At 5

f'(5) = 2 × 5 = 10.

Hint 2: At −3

Physics · Fiber optics, a first look from official OpenStax 25.4

40 min · Method: OpenStax 25.4 qualitative fiber optics by already-taught total internal reflection; no invented official fiber figures; official specifications reserved

Learn

Key terms

fiber optics, a first look from official OpenStax 25.4
Definition
OpenStax 25.4, after the official critical angles, describes fiber optics qualitatively: light can be guided along a transparent fiber by already-taught total internal reflection, remaining inside the fiber so long as it strikes the boundary at an angle greater than the critical angle. The lesson uses the book’s qualitative account and already-taught official angles. It does not invent official attenuation figures, official fiber indices, or official medical measurements. Endoscopes and other named devices in the section are not taken up as a first look today, and remain reserved if they would need figures the book is not using here as today’s job.
Example
A ray inside a fiber, striking the inner surface at an angle greater than the already-taught critical angle, is totally reflected and can continue along the fiber. That is the book’s qualitative account, not an invented official specification.
Not this
Not official Example 25.4 as if it had already named a fiber. Not invented official attenuation or official fiber indices. Not Monday’s 48.6° used as if it were a fiber specification. Not a first look at a reserved named device that would need official figures not taught today.
Connects to
Monday’s official discussion angles, and last week’s total internal reflection and official 42.2°. Official fiber specifications remain reserved.

Monday taught official discussion angles from OpenStax 25.4, including water to air 48.6° and diamond to air 24.4°. Last week’s official 42.2°, and Monday’s official discussion angles, remain the official numbers already taught; they are not reused as if they were official fiber specifications.
