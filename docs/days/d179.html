Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • a purpose clause with nē, a first look — Day 161
  • a result clause with ut, a first look — Day 166
  • a result clause with ēgredior, a first look — Day 168

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This is the last Latin review before Friday’s examination. No new construction. Keep purpose (ut, nē) distinct from result (ut, ut nōn). Keep sequātur distinct from sequētur, and ēgrediātur distinct from ēgrediētur. Original classroom sentences only.

Remember

Purpose marks an intended purpose. Result marks what happens as a result, often with tam, ita, or sic. Negative purpose uses nē; negative result uses ut nōn. The verb is the present subjunctive, not the future.

Watch me

Watch one Friday-style item, so tomorrow’s examination has the same shape.

  1. Puella venit ut eum sequātur is purpose.
  2. Puella tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur is result.
  3. Puella tam lentē venit ut eum nōn sequātur is a negative result, not a nē-clause.

Together

Translate and label Puer sedet nē ēgrediātur and Puer tam celeriter surgit ut ēgrediātur.

Hint 1: The first

Nē marks a negative purpose: he sits in order that he not go out.

Hint 2: The second

Tam and ut mark a positive result: he rises so quickly that he goes out.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. Translate and label: Puella venit ut eum sequātur. Puella tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur. Puella manet nē eum sequātur. Puella tam lentē venit ut eum nōn sequātur.
  2. Why is nē the wrong negative in the fourth sentence?
  3. Give the present subjunctive and the future, third person singular, of sequor and of ēgredior.

Mathematics · A short Term 3 mix: continuity and the derivative of invented f(x) = x²

40 min · Last Term 3 review: already-taught continuity and the derivative of invented f(x) = x² · no new rule · a formal epsilon argument reserved

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • continuous at a point, a first look — Day 166
  • a function not continuous at a point where it is not defined — Day 167
  • the derivative of invented f(x) = x² — Day 173

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This is the last mathematics review before Friday’s examination. No new rule. A function is continuous at a when it is defined at a and the limit equals the value. Invented f(x) = 2x is continuous at 3, because f(3) = 6 equals the limit. Invented (x² − 4)/(x − 2) is not continuous at 2, even though the limit is 4, because it is not defined there. The derivative of invented f(x) = x² is found from the difference quotient [ (x + h)² − x² ] / h = 2x + h, for h ≠ 0; the limit as h approaches 0 is f'(x) = 2x. So f'(3) = 6. A formal epsilon argument remains reserved. Derivatives of other functions remain reserved. The functions are invented.

Remember

Continuous at a means defined at a, with the limit equal to the value. For invented f(x) = x², f'(x) = 2x, and f'(3) = 6. Continuity and a derivative remain different jobs.

Watch me

Watch a short mixed set in the shape of a Friday item.

  1. Invented 2x is continuous at 3, because f(3) = 6 = the limit.
  2. Invented (x² − 4)/(x − 2) is not continuous at 2, because it is not defined there, even though the limit is 4.
  3. For invented f(x) = x², the difference quotient is 2x + h, so f'(x) = 2x, and f'(3) = 6.

Together

Find f'(5) from the already-taught rule, and say whether invented f(x) = x² is continuous at 5.

Hint 1: The derivative

f'(5) = 2 × 5 = 10.

Hint 2: Continuity

f(5) = 25, and the limit of x² as x approaches 5 is 25, so yes, it is continuous at 5. That is not the same job as f'(5) = 10.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key. The functions are invented.

  1. State the two conditions for continuity at a point, and say why invented (x² − 4)/(x − 2) is not continuous at 2.
  2. Write the difference quotient of invented f(x) = x² at a general x, and the rule f'(x).
  3. Find f'(3) and f'(0), and say why continuity at 3 is not the same statement as f'(3) = 6.

Exercises

  1. Find f'(4) and f'(1/2).
  2. On invented p(x) = 3x, is p continuous at 2? Give p(2) and the limit.
