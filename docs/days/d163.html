Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Last week’s ut-clauses and this week’s nē-clauses now stand side by side. Ut eum sequātur is a positive purpose; nē eum sequātur is a negative purpose. Ut ēgrediātur and nē ēgrediātur are the same pair for ēgredior. The verb in each clause is the present subjunctive, not the future sequētur or ēgrediētur. The sentences remain original. Remember

Ut marks a positive purpose; nē marks a negative purpose. Both take the present subjunctive already taught. The futures sequētur and ēgrediētur are a different job. A result clause remains reserved.

Mathematics · Algebra / analysis: the limit of a quotient, a first look

40 min · Method: if lim f = L and lim g = M, and M ≠ 0, then lim (f / g) = L / M; invented functions; the rule does not apply when M = 0; continuity reserved

Learn

Key terms

the limit of a quotient, a first look
Definition
If two quantities have limits, and if the limit of the denominator is not zero, the limit of their quotient is the quotient of those limits. If lim x→a f(x) = L and lim x→a g(x) = M, and M ≠ 0, then lim x→a (f(x) / g(x)) = L / M. If M = 0, this rule does not apply. Last week’s limit lim x→2 (x² − 4)/(x − 2) = 4 is not an instance of today’s rule, because the denominator’s limit is 0; that limit was found by an already-taught factorization, not by dividing the two limits. The figures used today are invented. Continuity remains reserved.
Example
On the invented functions f(x) = 2x and g(x) = x + 1, as x approaches 3, lim f = 6 and lim g = 4, and 4 ≠ 0, so lim (f / g) = 6 / 4 = 3/2.
Not this
Not yesterday’s product as if it were already this first look. Not last week’s (x² − 4)/(x − 2) treated as if today’s rule applied, because that denominator’s limit is 0. Not continuity, which remains reserved.
Connects to
Yesterday’s limit of a product, Monday’s limit of a sum, and last week’s limit where the function is not defined at the point.
