Mathematics · Geometry LXXIV: signs of sine and cosine in the four quadrants, a first look

40 min · Method: the quadrant gives the signs; a first-quadrant reference angle gives the sizes

Learn

Key terms

signs of sine and cosine in the four quadrants
Definition
On the unit circle, cosine is the x-coordinate and sine is the y-coordinate, so their signs follow the signs of x and y. In the first quadrant both are positive. In the second, sine is positive and cosine is negative. In the third, both are negative. In the fourth, sine is negative and cosine is positive. A reference angle in the first quadrant gives the size of the coordinates; the quadrant gives the signs.
Example
At 150°, the reference angle is 30°, so the coordinates have the size √3/2 and 1/2, and the point is in the second quadrant: P is (−√3/2, 1/2). Cos 150° = −√3/2, sin 150° = 1/2.
Not this
Not a new set of absolute values for 30°, 45°, and 60°. Not a first look at radians. Not a claim that sine is positive in every quadrant.
Connects to
The unit circle, the axis angles, and yesterday’s 30°, 45°, and 60° values.

The four quadrants now give signs. Cosine is x, sine is y. First quadrant: both positive. Second: y positive, x negative, so sine positive and cosine negative. Third: both negative. Fourth: y negative, x positive, so sine negative and cosine positive. For 150°, which is 180° − 30°, the reference angle is 30°. The sizes are yesterday’s: √3/2 and 1/2. The point is in the second quadrant, so P is (−√3/2, 1/2): cos 150° = −√3/2, sin 150° = 1/2. For 330°, which is 360° − 30°, the fourth quadrant gives P = (√3/2, −1/2). Radians remain reserved. Invent no official turning of a real wheel.

Remember

Cosine is x, sine is y. Second quadrant: sine positive, cosine negative. At 150°, P is (−√3/2, 1/2). At 330°, P is (√3/2, −1/2).

Key terms

ultramarine (from lapis lazuli)
Definition
Ultramarine, in this first look, is the blue pigment prepared from lapis lazuli by separating the blue lazurite from the other minerals in the rock, after the rock has been ground. It is not the rock itself, and it is not a new official catalog color added to this week’s atelier palette. Invent no official historical price, and no official extraction yield.

Seminar · Economics: an income tax on invented figures, distinguished from a sales tax

40 min · Method: 10 percent of an invented $500 is $50, remaining $450; not an official rate, and not a sales tax

Learn

Key terms

income tax (invented figures)
Definition
An income tax, in this first look, is a named percent taken from an invented amount of income, not added to a purchase price. The figures are invented classroom numbers, not an official living tax rate. If the invented income is $500 and the invented rate is 10 percent, the tax is $50, and $450 remains.
Example
10 percent of $500 is 0.10 × 500 = 50, so the tax is $50 and the remaining amount is $450. A second invented check: 10 percent of $200 is $20, remaining $180.
Not this
Not last week’s sales tax, which was a percent added to an invented purchase price (5 percent of $80 was $4, total $84). Not an official federal, state, or local rate. Not a claim about a living person’s tax.
Connects to
Last week’s sales tax on invented figures. Later work on withholding, deductions, or official published rates remains reserved.

Last week’s economics lesson treated a sales tax as a named percent added to an invented purchase price: 5 percent of $80 was $4, and the total was $84. Today a first look at an income tax, on invented classroom figures only, not as an official living rate. An income tax is a named percent taken from an invented amount of income, not added to a purchase. If the invented income is $500 and the invented rate is 10 percent, the tax is 0.10 × 500 = $50, and $450 remains. A second invented check: 10 percent of $200 is $20, and $180 remains. A miss would be to add the $50 to $500 and call $550 the result, which would treat the income tax as if it were last week’s sales tax. Another miss would be to treat the 10 percent as an official published rate. Withholding, deductions, and official rates remain reserved.

Remember

An income tax is a named percent taken from invented income, not added to a price. 10 percent of $500 is $50, remaining $450. The rate is invented, not official. $550 would treat the tax as a sales tax.
