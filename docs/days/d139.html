Latin · Sequor and ēgredior, a second mixed practice of future, present, and pluperfect

40 min · Method: a second mixed practice; original sentences, not Ørberg

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • future of sequor — Day 136
  • future of ēgredior — Day 137

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

A second pass through the same three tenses of both verbs. Keep the time clear: sequētur and ēgrediētur are still ahead; sequitur and ēgreditur are under way; secūtus erat and ēgressus erat were already completed before another past moment. A short original pair may put a future next to a pluperfect, provided each form keeps its own time. Do not copy a textbook narrative.

Remember

Future, present, and pluperfect of sequor and of ēgredior must not be swapped, and the two verbs must not be swapped either.

Watch me

Translate, then give the tense of each verb: Nōs ēgrediēmur. Tū mē secūtus erās.

  1. We shall go out (future of ēgredior). You had followed me (pluperfect of sequor).
  2. Ēgrediēmur is still ahead; secūtus erās was already completed before another past moment.
  3. Do not read ēgrediēmur as a present, or secūtus erās as a future.

Together

What form of ēgredior means they will go out, and what form of sequor means I follow?

Hint 1: Future, third plural

Ēgredientur.

Hint 2: Present, first singular

Sequor.

Check

  1. Give sequēminī and ēgressa erat, with meanings.
  2. Translate: Ego nōn ēgrediar. Amīcī mē sequentur.
  3. Why is ēgrediuntur a miss for they will go out?

Exercises

  1. Put sequeris, sequēris, and secūtus erās in three short original sentences, and name the tense of each.
  2. Write one original sentence that uses both ēgrediar and sequētur, with the times kept distinct.
  3. What still remains reserved, and is not today’s job?

Mathematics · Geometry LXXXII: the law of cosines, a second mixed practice

40 min · Method: included angle to a side, and three sides to an angle; invented figures

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • the law of cosines — Day 136

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Today mixes both uses of the law of cosines. From two sides and the included angle, the opposite side is found from c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C. From three sides, an angle is found from cos C = (a² + b² − c²)/(2ab). On an invented triangle with sides 6 and 6 and included angle 60°, c² = 36 + 36 − 2·6·6·(1/2) = 36, so c = 6. On an invented triangle with sides 6, 6, and 6, the angle is 60°, which checks the first result. The figures are invented. The law of sines remains available as a check when a side-angle pair is already known, but it is not a substitute for the included-angle job. Radians and the ambiguous case remain reserved.

Remember

Use c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C to find a side, and the rearranged form to find an angle. Label invented figures invented.

Watch me

Find the third side of an invented triangle with sides 6 and 6 and included angle 60°, then find an angle of an invented triangle with sides 6, 6, and 6.

  1. c² = 36 + 36 − 36 = 36, so c = 6.
  2. cos C = (36 + 36 − 36)/72 = 36/72 = 1/2, so C = 60°.
  3. The two results agree. The figures are invented classroom numbers.

Together

Hint 1: The rearranged formula

cos C = (16 + 16 − 48)/(2·4·4) = (−16)/32 = −1/2, so C = 120°.

Hint 2: A check

That was the included angle already used on Tuesday.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. On an invented triangle with sides 6 and 6 and included angle 60°, what is the third side?
  2. On an invented triangle with sides 4, 4, and 4√3, what is the angle opposite 4√3?
  3. Why is the law of sines not a miss as a check, but a miss as a substitute for the included-angle job?

Exercises

  1. Find the third side of an invented triangle with sides 3 and 4 and included angle 90°.
  2. Find the angle opposite 5 in an invented triangle with sides 5, 5, and 5.
  3. What remains reserved, and why must the figures stay labeled invented?

Seminar · Economics: a deductible on invented figures, a first look

40 min · Method: invented premium, payment, and deductible; distinguished from a tax; not an official policy

Learn

Key terms

a deductible (invented figures)
Definition
A deductible, in this invented classroom arrangement, is an amount the holder bears first when the named event happens, before the invented payment is made. It is not a premium, not a tax, and not an official policy.
Example
On invented figures already taught, twelve $10 premiums are $120. If a named event happens, an invented $500 would be paid, but with an invented $100 deductible the payment is $500 − $100 = $400. If the event does not happen, nothing is paid. The figures are invented, not an official product.
Not this
Not the premium, which is paid in advance, invented here as $10 a month. Not a tax. Not a game of stakes. Not an official deductible, rate, or policy. Not last week’s $500 paid in full, unless the invented arrangement has no deductible.
Connects to
Last week’s insurance on invented figures, and earlier sales tax and income tax, also invented. Official insurance rates remain reserved.

Last week’s invented insurance paid an invented premium in advance, $10 a month, so twelve premiums are $120, and paid an invented $500 only if a named event happened. Today a deductible is added to that invented arrangement. A deductible, here, is an amount the holder bears first when the named event happens, before the invented payment is made. If the invented deductible is $100, the payment after the event is $500 − $100 = $400. If the event does not happen, nothing is paid, and the deductible is not charged as a separate official fee in this arrangement. The $10, the $120, the $500, and the $100 are invented classroom numbers, not an official product, rate, or policy.

The deductible is not the premium: the premium is paid in advance, whether or not the event happens. It is not a tax: last week’s income tax was a percent taken from invented income, and a sales tax was a percent added to an invented price. The lesson is not a game of stakes. Official insurance rates and official deductibles remain reserved.

Remember

On invented figures, a $100 deductible means that if the named event happens, the invented $500 payment is reduced by $100, so $400 is paid. The premium is still paid in advance. The figures are not official.

Watch me

  1. The year’s premium is 12 × 10 = $120, paid in advance.
  2. If the event happens, $500 − $100 = $400 is paid.
  3. If the event does not happen, nothing is paid. The figures are invented.

Hint 2: The premium

The premium is paid in advance, here $10 a month, and is not the amount borne first after the event.
