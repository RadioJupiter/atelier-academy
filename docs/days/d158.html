Latin · Purpose clauses with sequor and ēgredior, mixed with the futures

40 min · Method: mix ut sequātur and ut ēgrediātur with sequētur and ēgrediētur; original sentences; nē reserved

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • a purpose clause with ut, a first look — Day 156
  • a purpose clause with ut and ēgredior — Day 157

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

The new purpose clauses now sit beside the already-taught futures, so the vowel of the ending, and the verb, both have to be read with care. Ut sequātur is a purpose of following; sequētur is the future, he will follow, or she will follow. Ut ēgrediātur is a purpose of going out; ēgrediētur is the future. The two verbs are not swapped. The sentences are original. A negative purpose with nē remains reserved.

Remember

Ut sequātur and ut ēgrediātur are purpose clauses, with ā. Sequētur and ēgrediētur are futures, with ē. Sequor is to follow; ēgredior is to go out.

Watch me

Gloss these four original forms: ut eam sequātur; sequētur; ut ēgrediātur; ēgrediētur.

  1. In order that he follow her, or in order that she follow her, a purpose clause.
  2. He will follow, or she will follow, the future.
  3. In order that he go out, or in order that she go out.
  4. He will go out, or she will go out.

Together

Why is ut sequētur a miss for a purpose of following, and why is ut ēgrediātur a miss for “she will go out”?

Hint 1: The purpose

A purpose clause uses the present subjunctive, sequātur, not the future sequētur.

Hint 2: The future

“She will go out” is ēgrediētur, not a purpose clause.

Check

  3. Why is ut sequātur a miss if the purpose is going out?

Key terms

a limit as a positive integer grows large
Definition
A limit may also describe what happens as a positive integer n grows large. In this first look, the values of 1/n approach 0 as n grows large, written lim n→∞ 1/n = 0. The symbol ∞ is read “infinity” here only as a way of saying that n grows large without bound; it is not a number to be substituted, and a formal treatment of infinity remains reserved.
Example
On an invented table, n = 10, 100, and 1000 give 1/n = 0.1, 0.01, and 0.001, which approach 0. The table is invented and labeled invented.
Not this
