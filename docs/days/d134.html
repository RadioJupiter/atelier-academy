Mathematics · Geometry LXXVIII: the law of sines in a non-right triangle

40 min · Method: an invented isosceles triangle 30°-30°-120°, using sin 120° = sin 60° from Monday’s related angles

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • the law of sines — Day 133
  • related angles on the unit circle — Day 131

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Yesterday the law of sines was checked on a right triangle. Today a non-right invented triangle, labeled invented: the angles are 30°, 30°, and 120°, so the triangle is isosceles, and the sides opposite the 30° angles are equal. Monday’s related-angle work gives sin 120° = sin(180° − 60°) = sin 60° = √3/2. Let each equal side be 4, invented. The side opposite 120° is found from 4 / sin 30° = c / sin 120°, so 4 / (1/2) = c / (√3/2), and c = 4 × 2 × (√3/2) = 4√3. The common ratio is 8. A miss would be to take sin 120° as −√3/2, which is cos 120°, or to treat 120° as if it were 60° in the triangle itself. The ambiguous case, and the law of cosines, remain reserved. Radians remain reserved. Invent no official length.

Remember

In an invented 30°-30°-120° triangle with equal sides 4, the side opposite 120° is 4√3, because sin 120° = √3/2. The common ratio is 8.

Watch me

In the invented 30°-30°-120° triangle with equal sides 4, find the side opposite 120°, and show the common ratio.

  1. 4 / sin 30° = c / sin 120°. Sin 30° = 1/2, and sin 120° = √3/2.
  2. 4 / (1/2) = c / (√3/2), so 8 = c × 2/√3, and c = 8 × (√3/2) = 4√3.
  3. The common ratio is 8. The 4 is invented, not an official length.

Together

Why is sin 120° equal to √3/2, and why would −√3/2 be a miss?

Hint 1: Related angles

120° = 180° − 60°, so sin 120° = sin 60° = √3/2.

Hint 2: The miss

−√3/2 is cos 120°, or sin 240°, not sin 120°. Sine is positive in the second quadrant.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. In an invented 30°-30°-120° triangle with equal sides 4, what is the side opposite 120°, and what is the common ratio?
  2. Why is sin 120° equal to √3/2, and why must the 4 be called invented?
  3. What would be a miss, and what remains reserved?

Exercises

  1. The same invented triangle has equal sides 4. Using the area formula, what is the area, with included angle 120° between those sides?
  2. If the invented side opposite 120° is given as 6, and the base angles are 30° and 30°, what is each equal side?
  3. Why does the law of sines still hold when one angle is obtuse?

Key terms

malachite
Definition

The USGS Spectral Library, Clark et al., 2003, Open-File Report 03-395, sample HS254, records malachite with the formula Cu2CO3(OH)2. Not this

Connects to

Tuesday’s azurite was a blue copper carbonate, Cu3(CO3)2(OH)2. Today the related green mineral, malachite. Seminar · Economics: insurance on invented figures, distinguished from a tax

40 min · Method: an invented $10 monthly premium, $120 in a year, and an invented $500 payment if a named event happens; not an official policy

Learn

Key terms

insurance (invented figures)
Definition
Insurance, in this first look, is an invented arrangement in which a named premium is paid in advance, and a named payment would be made if a named invented event happens. The figures are invented classroom numbers, not an official policy or a living rate. An invented premium of $10 each month for twelve months is $120 paid in advance; an invented payment of $500 would be made only if the named event happens.
Example
Twelve invented premiums of $10 are 12 × 10 = $120. That $120 is not a tax, and it is not a sales price. The $500 is an invented payment, not an official claim.
Not this
Not last week’s income tax, which was a percent taken from invented income, and not the earlier sales tax, which was a percent added to a price. Not a gambling lesson, and not an official insurance product or rate. Not a claim about a living person’s policy.
Connects to
Last week’s income tax, and the earlier sales tax, both on invented figures. Later work on official published rates, or on deductibles, remains reserved.

Last week’s economics lesson treated an income tax as a named percent taken from invented income. Earlier, a sales tax was a percent added to an invented price. Today a first look at insurance, on invented classroom figures only, not as an official policy and not as a gambling lesson. A premium is a named amount paid in advance. If a named invented event happens, a named invented payment is made; if it does not happen, that payment is not made, and the premiums have still been paid. An invented example: a premium of $10 each month for twelve months is 12 × 10 = $120 paid in advance. If the named invented event happens, an invented payment of $500 is made. A miss would be to treat the $120 as last week’s income tax, or as a sales tax added to a price, or to treat the arrangement as a game of stakes. Do not invent an official living rate, and do not claim a living person’s policy. Deductibles, and official published rates, remain reserved.

Remember

Insurance, here, is an invented premium paid in advance, and an invented payment if a named event happens. Twelve times $10 is $120. The $500 is invented, not official. This is not a tax, and not a gambling lesson.
