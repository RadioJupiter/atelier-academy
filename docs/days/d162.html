Latin · A purpose clause with nē and ēgredior, a first look

40 min · Method: nē + ēgrediātur, contrasted with ēgrediētur, with ut ēgrediātur, and with nē eum sequātur; original sentences; result clauses reserved

Learn

Key terms

a purpose clause with nē and ēgredior
Definition
A negative purpose may also use the already-taught present subjunctive of ēgredior: nē ēgrediātur, in order that he not go out, or in order that she not go out. It is not the future ēgrediētur, and not yesterday’s nē eum sequātur, a different verb. It is not ut ēgrediātur, last week’s positive purpose with the same verb.
Example
In an original sentence, Puer sedet nē ēgrediātur: he sits in order that he not go out. The verb in the nē-clause is ēgrediātur, not ēgrediētur, and not sequātur.
Not this
Not ēgrediētur, he will go out, or she will go out. Not ut ēgrediātur, last week’s positive purpose. Not nē eum sequātur, yesterday’s nē-clause with sequor. Not a result clause, which remains reserved.
Connects to
Yesterday’s purpose clause with nē and sequor, and last week’s ut ēgrediātur.

Yesterday’s nē-clause used sequātur. Today the same negative purpose is done with ēgredior: nē ēgrediātur, in order that he not go out, or in order that she not go out. Puer sedet nē ēgrediātur: he sits in order that he not go out. The ending is -iātur, not the future -iētur. Last week’s ut ēgrediātur remains the positive purpose with the same verb. The sentences are original. Identity is handled with Puer and Puella, and with he and she in the gloss. A result clause remains reserved.

Mathematics · Algebra / analysis: the limit of a product, a first look

40 min · Method: if lim f = L and lim g = M, then lim (f g) = L M; also lim (c f) = c L; invented functions; a quotient reserved until tomorrow

Learn

Key terms

the limit of a product, a first look
Definition
If two quantities have limits, the limit of their product is the product of those limits. If lim x→a f(x) = L and lim x→a g(x) = M, then lim x→a (f(x) g(x)) = L M. A constant may be taken out: lim x→a (c f(x)) = c L. The figures used today are invented and labeled invented. Continuity, and a formal epsilon argument, remain reserved.
Example
On the same invented functions as yesterday, f(x) = 2x and g(x) = x + 1, as x approaches 3, lim f = 6 and lim g = 4, so lim (f g) = 6 × 4 = 24. The invented product is 2x(x + 1) = 2x² + 2x, whose values approach 24.
Not this

Key terms

alternating current and direct current, a first look
Definition
In OpenStax, College Physics 2e, 20.5, direct current, DC, “is the flow of electric charge in only one direction. It is the steady state of a constant-voltage circuit.” Alternating current, AC, is used when “the voltage source varies periodically, particularly sinusoidally.” The book says, “most household electricity is 120 V AC, which means that V_rms is 120 V.” That official 120 V is the book’s official household figure, not invented, and it is a first look at official household 120 V, reserved until today.
