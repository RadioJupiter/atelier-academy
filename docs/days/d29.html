Latin · Capitulum VIII, part 4: the ablative of price, and tantus

30 min · Text: Ørberg, Familia Romana, Cap. VIII · Method: two frames for price; original sentences; invented sums

Learn

Key terms

ablative of price
Definition
With verbs of buying, selling, and costing (emere, vēndere, cōnstāre), the price is put in the ablative, with no preposition: you pay with that money.
Example
Hic ānulus decem sēstertiīs cōnstat. This ring costs ten sesterces.
Not this
When the price is the predicate of pretium est, it is nominative: Pretium est decem sēstertiī. Do not mix the two frames. And this is not the dative (‘to/for someone’).
Connects to
Numbers such as decem (10) and vīgintī (20) are indeclinable in this lesson. Invented prices only; do not copy a textbook shop scene word for word.
tantus, -a, -um
Definition
An adjective meaning ‘so great, of such a size (or price).’ It often pairs with quantus (‘how great, as great as’).
Example
Tantum pretium Iūlia nōn dat. Julia does not give so great a price. Tantum pretium magnum est.
Not this
Not a demonstrative like hic or ille, though it points to a degree. It agrees like a first-and-second-declension adjective.
Connects to
A first working use is enough: so great a price, such a ring. Fine distinctions of quantī as a genitive of price can wait if they are not in today’s reading.
Word How to say it Meaning

vēndit WEN-dit sells

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Two frames. Hic ānulus decem sēstertiīs cōnstat. (ablative of price.) Pretium huius ānulī est decem sēstertiī. (nominative predicate — and huius is the genitive of hic, ‘of this,’ which you may now meet in a price sentence; if huius is new, you may also write Pretium est decem sēstertiī.)

Original sentences: Hic ānulus decem sēstertiīs cōnstat. Iūlia illum ānulum vīgintī sēstertiīs emit. Mārcus hortum nōn vēndit. Tantum pretium Iūlia nōn dat. Quantus est ānulus? Tantum pretium magnum est.

Keep prices invented and small. The skill is the case, not Roman shopping history.

Remember

With cōnstāre, emere, vēndere, the price is ablative (decem sēstertiīs). With pretium est, the price is nominative. tantus = so great. decem and vīgintī are indeclinable here.

Key terms: ablative of price, tantus, -a, -um.

Mathematics · Geometry XV: the circumcircle, revisited with this fortnight’s theorems

60 min · Method: week-4 perpendicular bisectors, plus the semicircle and cyclic-quad facts as tests

Learn

Key terms

circle through three points (review)
Definition
A unique circle passes through three points that are not on a single line (the circumcircle of the triangle they form). Its center is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of the sides — a construction you already have from week 4.
Example
The circumcircle of a right triangle has the hypotenuse as diameter (the converse of the angle in a semicircle, used honestly as such).
Not this
Not a circle through four points in general: four points have to satisfy an extra condition (for example, a cyclic quadrilateral) to lie on one circle.
Connects to
Today reviews that construction and ties it to this fortnight’s theorems, without a new construction script.

What you already can do. To find the center of the circle through A, B, and C (not collinear), construct the perpendicular bisectors of AB and of AC; they meet at the center O, and OA = OB = OC is the radius. That was Day 12’s perpendicular bisector as a locus, used as a circumcenter.

What this fortnight adds. If angle C is right, O is the midpoint of AB, because AB is a diameter. You can still construct bisectors, but you can also know where O must be before you draw.

A check on four points. If you want a circle through A, B, C, and D, first put a circle through A, B, and C, then test whether D lies on it — or test a cyclic-quadrilateral condition (opposite angles supplementary) when ABCD is already a quadrilateral.

No new compass step is taught today. The sitting is a synthesis: construct or reason, then name a theorem.

Remember

Three non-collinear points determine a circle; the center is where perpendicular bisectors meet. In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is a diameter, so the center is the midpoint of that side. Four points need an extra condition.

Key terms: circle through three points (review).

Watch me

ABC is right-angled at C, with AC = 8 and BC = 6. Where is the circumcenter, and what is the circumradius?

  1. The right angle is at C, so AB is a diameter of the circumcircle.
  2. AB = 10 (a 6-8-10 triangle), so the circumcenter is the midpoint of AB, and the radius is 5.
  3. You could still find it by perpendicular bisectors; the semicircle fact tells you the same point more quickly.

Together

Why do four vertices of a general parallelogram that is not a rectangle fail to lie on one circle, in this course’s terms?

Hint 1: Opposite angles

A non-rectangular parallelogram has opposite angles equal but not 90°, and a pair of equal acute angles is not supplementary.

Hint 2: III.22

A cyclic quadrilateral needs opposite angles to sum to 180°. This one does not.

Writing & Rhetoric · Concession and return in a three-paragraph close

45 min · Method: a real limit in the third paragraph, then a return in new words; Friday’s essay is next

Learn

Key terms

concession (three-paragraph close)
Definition
A real limit or opposing point, granted in the closing paragraph, after which you return to the thesis in new words. The concession must change what a careful reader would have to admit; it is not a decoration.
Example
Thesis: the school photosynthesis equation is a useful net summary. Concession: it does not show intermediate steps, and C6H12O6 is a representative sugar. Return: as a net bookkeeping of atoms and of the overall change, the equation still earns its place, if it is labeled as a summary.
Not this
Not “some people might disagree, but they are wrong,” and not a second support paragraph that never grants a limit.
Connects to
Days 19–20 already required a concession in two paragraphs. Today the close of a three-paragraph essay is the home of that job, unless a better place is earned.

Grant something real. A late source is still late. A net equation is still a summary. A copy is still a copy. A converse is still a different implication. Then say, in new words, what remains of the thesis after that limit.

Order in the close. One or two sentences of concession; one or two sentences of return. Do not end on the concession without a return, and do not pretend the concession was never granted.

A test. If you can delete the concession and the essay is unchanged, it was not a concession.

Remember

The close grants a real limit, then returns to the thesis in new words. If deleting the concession changes nothing, it was decoration. Do not end on the limit with no return.

Key terms: concession (three-paragraph close).

Watch me

Write a two-sentence close for the thesis “Inscribed angles are half the central angle on the same arc.”

  1. A model concession: The half-or-double relation does not apply when the angles stand on different arcs, and it is easy to misread a figure.
  2. A model return: When the same-arc condition is checked, the inscribed angle is still half the central angle, which is the fact to use.
  3. The return uses new words, not a paste of the opening thesis.

Together

Why is “Some students find circles hard, but they should try harder” not a concession to that same thesis?

Hint 1: About the claim

A concession limits the claim, not the reader’s character.

Hint 2: A real limit

A real concession would be about a different arc, or about a hypothesis that was not met.

Science · Autumn color: unmasking, and a caution about new reds

45 min · Method: two mechanisms, stated carefully; no invented percentages; chromatography as a hint, not a proof of every leaf

Learn

Key terms

autumn color (careful account)
Definition
A first account of why many deciduous leaves change color: chlorophyll often breaks down in autumn, so yellow and orange carotenoids already present can become visible. In some species, red or purple anthocyanins are made in the leaf in autumn; those colors are not always a mere unmasking of a pigment that was waiting in midsummer.
Example
A yellow maple or aspen leaf can often be told as an unmasking story at this lesson’s level; a deep red leaf may involve new anthocyanin, not only a leftover carotenoid.
Not this
Not “all red leaves were secretly red all summer,” and not invented percentages of trees, pigments, or days of frost. Weather, species, and the leaf’s own chemistry all matter, and this course will not reduce them to a single number.
Connects to
Chromatography already showed that a green leaf can hide a yellow pigment. Today that fact is used, with the anthocyanin caution attached.
anthocyanin
Definition
A class of red and purple pigments that, in many autumn leaves, are produced in the season itself, rather than merely unmasked. This course names them and keeps the caution; it does not invent a biosynthetic pathway or a percentage of species.
Example
A bright red autumn leaf is often an anthocyanin story in part, not only a carotenoid story.
Not this
Not another name for carotenoids, and not a required ingredient in every autumn leaf (many yellow leaves can be told without them).
Connects to
The etymology in the dump we checked was not full enough to quote beyond the obvious antho- (‘flower’) element, so a fuller origin is omitted here.

Two mechanisms, not one slogan.

  1. Unmasking. Chlorophyll, which dominated the midsummer leaf, often breaks down in autumn. Carotenoids already present can then be seen as yellow or orange.
  2. New reds. In some species, anthocyanins are made in the leaf in autumn, giving reds and purples that were not simply waiting as the leaf’s midsummer color.

What we will not do. No invented fraction of “how much color is unmasking.” No claim that a frost is the one cause in every climate. No studio or kitchen experiment that asks you to cook leaves in a way this page has not made safe.

A connection. A chromatogram of a summer green leaf can already show a yellow band: the unmasking story has a laboratory hint before autumn. A chromatogram of a red autumn leaf is a different sample and is not required today.

Remember

Yellow and orange often come from carotenoids unmasked as chlorophyll breaks down. Reds and purples, in many cases, involve anthocyanins made in autumn, not only unmasked. No invented percentages. Species differ.

Key terms: autumn color (careful account), anthocyanin.

Watch me

A student says, “All autumn colors were already in the leaf, and we just could not see them.” Repair the claim.

  1. The claim is a fair first approximation for many yellows and oranges (carotenoids already present, chlorophyll breaking down).
  2. It is not a safe account of all reds and purples, which may involve anthocyanins made in the season.
  3. A repaired claim names both mechanisms and refuses a single slogan.

Together

How does a summer chromatogram that shows a yellow band support, without completing, the unmasking story?

Hint 1: Already present

A yellow pigment can already be in a green leaf.

Hint 2: Not a complete proof

A summer paper does not, by itself, prove what happens in autumn, or explain a red leaf.

Key terms

written response (Odyssey so far)
Definition
A short, sourced piece of writing — here, in the spirit of this week’s three-paragraph essay — on a design question in Odyssey 1–12 (and, if you have been assigned them, no further). Example

Atelier · Accents, and a likeness check against the plate or plaster

90 min · Method: a measurement likeness pass, then a few late accents; the subject still wins

Learn

Key terms

accent (head)
Definition
A small, dark or sharp mark — or a small true highlight — that is placed late, on a found edge or at a tight plane, after the large values of the head are true. Accents are few.
Example
A thin dark at the inner opening of the lids, or a small highlight on a lit brow that is already in the light family, can be an accent.
Not this
Not a sprinkle of highlights on every feature, and not an accent used to “fix” a wrong terminator.
Connects to
Week 4 introduced accents. Today they are applied to this head, with likeness to the plate or plaster judged by proportion and light, not by added sparkle.
likeness to the plate
Definition
The match of the drawing to this Bargue head or plaster: tilt, silhouette, feature triangle, and light. It is likeness to the object in front of you, not to a general idea of a person, and not to a living, named sitter.
Example
If the mouth is high, the likeness fails, however refined the lid.
Not this
Not flattery, and not a “more beautiful” correction of the plate. The plate or plaster wins, as in week 5.
Connects to
A short likeness check is a measurement pass, not a new drawing.

A late sitting, not a new start. Stand back. Is the tilt true? Is the feature triangle true? Is the large terminator true? Only then, two or three accents.

Likeness check (measure). Compare: height of the head to width; brow to chin; placement of the ear if it shows; the mouth’s distance from the nose. Correct the largest error. Do not “pretty” the form.

Highlights. One small, true highlight is worth more than six invented ones. If you cannot see it with half-closed eyes as a distinct light, you probably do not need it yet.

Remember

Check tilt, triangle, and terminator first. Likeness is to this plate or plaster, by measurement, not a prettier invention. Then two or three accents, late and true. No named living sitter.

Key terms: accent (head), likeness to the plate.

Watch me

  1. The width of the head and the placement of the mouth are the larger errors.
  2. Refined lids will not save a wrong feature triangle or a wrong envelope.
  3. Correct the large measures, then return to any accent that was spent too early.

Modern language · Spanish: por and para, a core contrast

40 min · Method: jobs, not a single English gloss; a core set only; present tense

Learn

Key terms

para (core uses)
Definition
A preposition whose core uses in this lesson are purpose (in order to, for a goal), a deadline or a point in time toward which one works, a destination, and a recipient: para estudiar, para el viernes, para Madrid, para Ana.
Example
Este libro es para Ana. Estudio para aprender. La tarea es para el viernes.
Not this
Not a complete map of every use of para, and not interchangeable with por. If you mean duration, exchange, or a general cause, you often need por — tomorrow’s contrast is already today’s caution, and both are practiced now.
Connects to
A first working set is enough. Unusual or idiomatic uses will be taught later, not invented today.
por (core uses)
Definition
A preposition whose core uses in this lesson are duration (por dos horas), exchange or a price (por diez euros — a classroom phrase, not a Latin sesterce), motion through or along (por el parque), and a general reason or cause (por eso, por la lluvia, at this lesson’s level).
Example
Estudio por dos horas. Voy por la calle. Gracias por el libro.
Not this
Not purpose-as-a-goal (that is typically para), and not a deadline (para el viernes). Do not treat por and para as “both mean for” and guess.

Economics & personal finance · Money: medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value

40 min · Method: three jobs, tested with near-misses; no invented first-coin year; a link to last week’s trade table

Learn

Key terms

money (three jobs)
Definition
In this course’s working definition, money is whatever, in a given setting, does three jobs: a medium of exchange (you can pay with it), a unit of account (prices and debts can be written in it), and a store of value (it can be kept and later spent, with the usual cautions about inflation and risk, which are named, not computed today).
Origin
From Latin monēta, from the temple of Juno Moneta in Rome, where a mint stood (Wiktionary).
Example
A widely accepted coin or a modern currency typically does all three jobs in its own setting. A ticket to a play does a limited medium-of-exchange job at the door and is a poor general store of value or unit of account for a whole economy.
Not this
Not “a piece of paper with a number on it” as a complete definition (a page of doodles is paper with marks). And not a history of the first coin with a fixed year; early coinage in Asia Minor is often discussed by scholars for the first millennium BCE, and this course will not assign an exact year.
Connects to
Specialization and trade (last week) already asked how people exchange. Money, when it works, lowers the need for a double coincidence of wants — you need not find a person who both has bread and wants your particular labor at the same moment.
medium of exchange
Definition
The job of money as a thing commonly accepted in payment, so that trade does not have to be a direct swap of two particular goods.
Example
If a baker will take a standard coin (or a standard currency) for bread, that coin is working as a medium of exchange in that setting.
Not this
Not a unit of account by itself (you can write prices in a unit you rarely hand over), and not automatically a good store of value in a time of rapid inflation.
Connects to
The three jobs can come apart, which is why they are taught as a list, not as one word.

Three jobs, tested with near-misses. A gift card for one shop is a limited medium of exchange. A unit on a blackboard (“class points”) might be a unit of account inside a game and still not be money in the town. A house can store value and still be a clumsy medium of exchange. Money, when it is working, does all three well enough in its setting.

A connection to last week. In the invented Ana-and-Beau table, they swapped pages and staves directly. If they had a classroom token that both trusted, they could sell and buy without finding an exact match of wants. The token would be a model of a medium of exchange — still invented, still labeled.

What we will not do. No invented inflation percentage, no lecture on a particular central bank, and no exact year for “the first coin.” The Latin sēstertius of this week’s language lesson is a named historical coin; it is not a complete history of money.

Remember

Money, in this working sense, is a medium of exchange, a unit of account, and a store of value. The jobs can come apart. A numbered paper is not automatically money. No invented first-coin year, and no invented inflation rate.

Key terms: money (three jobs), medium of exchange.
