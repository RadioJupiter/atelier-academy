Latin · Capitulum II, part 2: cuius? and the genitive in use

30 min · Ørberg, Familia Romana, Cap. II

Learn

Cuius? means 'whose?'. Possession in the early chapters is often shown by this question and by a genitive form (a possessive ending) that you will see in the book. Learn the question and a few family words, then read.

Word How to say it Meaning
cuius KOO-yuss whose?
pater PAH-ter father
māter MAH-ter mother
fīlius FEE-li-uss son
fīlia FEE-li-ah daughter
meus / mea MEH-uss / MEH-ah my

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Learn

Read aloud twice the rest of Capitulum II, then the whole chapter once for meaning.

Notice nouns in -er: puer, puerī (boy) keeps its e; vir, virī (man). They are second-declension masculine nouns, like servus, with the same endings after the nominative singular.

Roman numerals (useful from now on, since chapters and many dates use them): I 1, V 5, X 10, L 50, C 100, D 500, M 1000. A smaller numeral before a larger one is subtracted: IV = 4, IX = 9, XL = 40, XC = 90, CD = 400, CM = 900.

Remember

Cuius est? — whose is it? Meus / mea agree with the thing owned, not with the owner.

Watch me

Cuius est vīlla? Vīlla est mea. Whose is the house? The house is mine. Mea is feminine because vīlla is feminine.

Together

How do you ask 'whose is it?' in Latin?

Hint 1: Question

Cuius est?

Hint 2: Answer pattern

Name the owner in the form the book is teaching, or use meus / mea.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. What does cuius mean?
  2. Does mea agree with the owner or with the thing owned?

Exercises

  1. Give nominative plural, genitive singular and genitive plural: puer, vir, servus, fēmina, oppidum.
  2. Translate into Latin: (a) a town of Italy; (b) the rivers of Germania; (c) the islands of Graecia; (d) the number of the boys.
  3. Write in Roman numerals: 14, 49, 99, 444, 1066, 2026. Read: XXIV, LXXX, MCMXC.

Mathematics · Algebra II: quadratic equations

60 min · Gelfand & Shen, Algebra

Learn

A quadratic equation can be written ax² + bx + c = 0 with a ≠ 0. Some quadratics factor: x² − 5x + 6 = (x−2)(x−3) = 0, so x = 2 or x = 3. Others need completing the square, or the quadratic formula, which comes from completing the square on the general equation. Always check roots in the original equation.

Learn

Completing the square is the method; the formula is its result. For ax² + bx + c = 0 with a ≠ 0:

x² + (b/a)x = −c/a ⇒ (x + b/2a)² = (b² − 4ac)/4a² ⇒ x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a.

The discriminant D = b² − 4ac decides the number of real roots: two if D > 0, one (double) if D = 0, none if D < 0.

Vieta's formulas. If r and s are the roots of x² + px + q = 0, then x² + px + q = (x − r)(x − s), so r + s = −p and rs = q. Use them to check answers and to work with roots without finding them.

Worked example. Solve x² − 6x + 5 = 0 by completing the square: (x − 3)² = 4, so x − 3 = ±2, x = 5 or 1. Check with Vieta: 5 + 1 = 6 = −(−6) ✓; 5 · 1 = 5 ✓.

Remember

Bring to ax² + bx + c = 0. Factor if you can. Otherwise complete the square or use x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac)) / (2a). Check by substitution.

Watch me

Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0.

  1. Factor: (x−2)(x−3) = 0.
  2. So x−2 = 0 or x−3 = 0: x = 2 or x = 3.
  3. Check: 4 − 10 + 6 = 0; 9 − 15 + 6 = 0.

Together

Solve x² = 9. How many solutions?

Hint 1: Rewrite

x² − 9 = 0 = (x−3)(x+3).

Hint 2: Solutions

x = 3 or x = −3. Two solutions.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0.
  2. Why can a quadratic have two solutions?

Exercises

  1. Solve by completing the square: x² + 6x − 7 = 0.
  2. Solve with the formula: 2x² − 3x − 2 = 0. Check with Vieta (divide by 2 first).
  3. For which values of k does x² + kx + 9 = 0 have exactly one root?
  4. Let r and s be the roots of x² − 5x + 3 = 0. Without solving, find r² + s² and 1/r + 1/s.
  5. Write a quadratic with integer coefficients whose roots are 2 + √3 and 2 − √3.
  6. Prove: if a and c have opposite signs, then ax² + bx + c = 0 has two real roots.
  7. (Stretch.) Solve x⁴ − 5x² + 4 = 0.

Writing & Rhetoric · Trivium I, grammar: the anatomy of a sentence

45 min · Method: the Trivium (grammar → logic → rhetoric). Grammar here means knowing precisely how sentences are built.

Learn

Grammar, in the trivium, is the craft of accurate sentences: parts of speech, agreement, and punctuation that makes the structure visible. Today's work names what a sentence is doing, so later logic and rhetoric have something solid to stand on. Mark the subject and the verb before you polish anything else.

Learn

The three arts. In the medieval liberal arts, the trivium was grammar (the structure of language), logic or dialectic (valid reasoning) and rhetoric (effective persuasion). This course teaches them in that order and then keeps all three running together.

Sentence anatomy. - Subject (who or what) and predicate (what is said about it). - Direct object (receives the action: Franklin bought a volume); indirect object (gave me the book); subject complement after a linking verb (the writing was excellent). - Phrase: a group of words without its own subject–verb pair (in the morning). Clause: has a subject and a verb. Independent clauses can stand alone; dependent clauses cannot. - Simple sentence: one independent clause. Compound: two or more independent clauses. Complex: an independent clause plus at least one dependent clause.

Remember

Find the subject and the finite verb first. Check agreement. Fix punctuation so that clauses are visible. Do not start with 'style'.

Watch me

Sentence: The students who finished the plate left the studio. Subject of the main clause: The students. Verb: left. Who finished the plate is a relative clause describing the students, not a second main sentence.

Together

In When the lamp failed, the room went dark, which is the main clause?

Hint 1: Subordinate

When the lamp failed cannot stand alone.

Hint 2: Main

the room went dark.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. What should you mark first in a sentence?
  2. Is who finished the plate a main clause?

Exercises

  1. Label subject, verb, and any object or complement: (a) I bought it. (b) I thought the writing excellent. (c) The Mind that lies fallow but a single Day, sprouts up in Follies. (Addison's sentence: find the dependent clause and say what it modifies.)
  2. Simple, compound or complex? (a) Achilles withdrew, and the Greeks suffered. (b) Although the stele was taken to Susa, it survived. (c) The water rose after the flame went out. (d) The plates were published in Paris.
  3. Rewrite The candle went out. The gas cooled. The water rose. three ways: as a compound, a complex and a simple sentence.

Physics · Free fall

45 min · Peer Instruction protocol

Learn

Near Earth's surface, if air resistance is small enough to ignore, all bodies fall with the same downward acceleration, about g = 9.8 m/s² (often taken as 10 m/s² in a quick estimate). The motion is uniformly accelerated: v = u + at, and s = ut + ½at², with a = g downward. Mass does not appear in these equations, which is the point of the famous (if simplified) falling-bodies claim.

Learn

Pre-reading. Without air resistance, all objects near Earth's surface fall with the same acceleration, g ≈ 9.8 m/s² downward, regardless of mass. Galileo argued this from experiments with inclined planes and thought experiments; on the Moon in 1971, astronaut David Scott dropped a hammer and a falcon feather together, and they landed at the same moment. Falling from rest: d = ½gt², v = gt.

ConcepTests

  1. A heavy ball and a light ball of the same size are dropped together from the same height; air resistance is negligible. (A) The heavy one lands first (B) The light one lands first (C) They land together.
  2. One ball is dropped and another is thrown horizontally from the same height at the same moment. (A) The dropped ball lands first (B) The thrown ball lands first (C) They land together.
  3. A ball is thrown straight up at 12 m/s (no air resistance). When it returns to the thrower's hand its speed is: (A) less than 12 m/s (B) 12 m/s (C) more than 12 m/s.

Problems

  1. A stone is dropped from a 20 m bridge. How long does it fall, and how fast is it moving when it hits the water?
  2. A ball is thrown up at 15 m/s. Find its maximum height and the time to reach it.
  3. Experiment: reaction time. Have someone hold a ruler vertically with its zero end between your open fingers, then drop it without warning. Catch it, and read the distance d it fell. Do it five times. Your reaction time is t = √(2d/g). If d = 15 cm, what is t?

Remember

g ≈ 9.8 m/s² downward (≈ 10 m/s² for estimates). v = u + gt (downward taken positive if you choose that). Mass cancels: heavy and light bodies fall together in this model.

Watch me

A stone is dropped from rest (u = 0). How fast is it moving after 2 s, taking g = 10 m/s²? How far has it fallen?

  1. v = 0 + 10×2 = 20 m/s downward.
  2. s = 0 + ½×10×2² = 5×4 = 20 m.

Together

The same stone, still dropped from rest, after 3 s (g = 10 m/s²). Find v and s.

Hint 1: Speed

v = 10×3 = 30 m/s downward.

Hint 2: Distance

s = ½×10×9 = 45 m.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. A coin and a feather are dropped in a vacuum. In this model, which one has the larger acceleration?
  2. A body is dropped from rest. After 1 s, how fast is it going, with g = 10 m/s²?

History · Greece: Herodotus and the idea of "inquiry"

45 min · Primary source: Herodotus, Histories, opening sentence (G. C. Macaulay translation, 1890, public domain)

Context. Herodotus of Halicarnassus (born in the 480s BCE, died after about 430 BCE) wrote an account of the wars between the Greeks and the Persian Empire (490 BCE and 480–479 BCE) and of the peoples involved. The Greek word historiē means inquiry. Cicero later called him "the father of history". He travelled, interviewed people and reported what he was told, often saying when he did not believe it.

The source.

This is the Showing forth of the Inquiry of Herodotus of Halicarnassos, to the end that neither the deeds of men may be forgotten by lapse of time, nor the works great and marvellous, which have been produced some by Hellenes and some by Barbarians, may lose their renown; and especially that the causes may be remembered for which these waged war with one another.

Exercises

  1. Herodotus states three purposes. List them in your own words.
  2. "Barbarians" here translates barbaroi, the Greek word for all non-Greek speakers. Does Herodotus's sentence treat their works as inferior? Quote the evidence.
  3. Compare this purpose with that of Hammurabi's stele (Day 3). How is a history different from a royal inscription?
  4. Why is the last purpose (the causes of the war) the beginning of history as an analytical discipline rather than a chronicle?

Atelier · Bargue plate: modeling and completion

90 min

Learn

Modeling means turning the flat contour into a form that looks solid, by shading in values you have already trained on the scale. Start with the big light and the big shadow, then the terminator (the edge where light turns to shadow), then only a few darker accents. Do not polish a cheek before the large light is in the right place.

Learn

Modeling the form. With the shadow shapes in place as a flat tone, model in the light side: the halftones, which darken gradually as the surface turns away from the light, reaching their darkest just at the terminator. Keep the shadow side simple and unified: darks in the shadow are fewer and quieter than they look. Reserve the lightest lights (the paper) for the planes facing the light most directly.

Order of work: (1) darken the core of the shadow near the terminator; (2) lay the darkest halftones; (3) work outward toward the light; (4) soften edges where the form turns gradually, and keep them sharp where it turns abruptly.

Remember

Big light, big shadow, then the terminator. Match values to the scale by squinting. Leave the smallest darks until last.

Watch me

Demonstration:

  1. Squint at the subject (or plate) until you see only two big regions: light and shadow. Map those two on the drawing with a light gray for the shadow family.
  2. Find the terminator and soften or sharpen it as the form requires (a sudden turn is a sharper edge; a gentle turn is softer).
  3. Compare a mid-gray to step 5 on your scale. Adjust.
  4. Add a few darker accents last, never first.

Together

Why squint when you start the shading?

Hint 1: Squinting

It hides small patches and leaves the two big families of value.

Hint 2: Use

You can then place the large shadow without fussing over pores and nicks.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. What should you place first when modeling: accents or the big light and shadow?
  2. What is the terminator?

Exercises

  1. (75 min) Complete the plate, matching its values. Check against your nine-step scale: assign each region of the plate a value number before you shade it.
  2. (15 min) Sign and date the back. Photograph the plate and your drawing side by side, converted to greyscale, at the same size. Write three corrections for next time.

Modern language · Spanish: present tense of -ar verbs

25 min

Learn

Regular -ar verbs in the present follow one pattern. Learn hablar (to speak) as the model: hablo, hablas, habla, hablamos, habláis, hablan. The ending tells you who is speaking; you often do not need a pronoun.

Word How to say it Meaning
hablar ah-BLAR to speak
hablo AH-bloh I speak
hablas AH-blahs you speak (tú)
habla AH-blah he/she speaks
hablamos ah-BLAH-mohs we speak
hablan AH-blahn they speak
estudiar es-too-DYAR to study
dibujar dee-boo-HAR to draw

Learn

hablar (to speak): hablo, hablas, habla, hablamos, habláis, hablan. Drop -ar and add -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an. Subject pronouns are usually left out, because the ending shows the person: Hablo español. Negate with no before the verb: No hablo alemán.

Useful -ar verbs: estudiar, dibujar, pintar, trabajar, escuchar, mirar, practicar, necesitar.

Remember

Take off -ar and add -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an. Estudio, dibujas, hablan follow the same pattern.

Watch me

Yo hablo español. I speak Spanish. Ella dibuja en el estudio. She draws in the studio. The -a ending of dibuja means he or she (or usted).

Together

Conjugate estudiar for 'we' and for 'they'.

Hint 1: We

estudiamos

Hint 2: They

estudian

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. What does hablas mean?
  2. How do you say 'they draw' from dibujar?

Exercises

  1. Conjugate dibujar fully.
  2. Translate: (a) We study Latin. (b) Do you (tú) paint? (c) They don't work on Sundays (los domingos). (d) I listen to music. (e) She needs a pencil.
  3. Write a five-sentence paragraph about your school day using five different -ar verbs.

Elective · Artistic anatomy 1: the skeleton under the skin

30 min · Seminar rotation: Elective (Fridays). See the Electives page for the full list; this term's Friday sessions begin with artistic anatomy and poetry.

Why artists study bones. Where bone lies just under the skin, it fixes the form: these subcutaneous landmarks do not change shape with fat or muscle, so they are the reliable measuring points of figure drawing.

Key landmarks. The clavicles (collarbones) and the jugular notch between them; the acromion at the point of the shoulder; the spinous process of the seventh cervical vertebra (the prominent bump at the base of the neck); the sternum; the olecranon (point of the elbow); the iliac crest and the anterior superior iliac spine at the front of the hip; the patella; the malleoli (ankle bones, the inner one higher than the outer).

Proportion of the head. In an adult head seen from the front, the eyes lie at about half the height of the head (chin to top of the skull), lower than beginners usually place them.

Exercises

  1. On your own body, find and touch each landmark listed. On a sheet, draw a simple standing figure and mark each landmark with a dot and label.
  2. Look at a photograph of an adult face from the front. Measure the chin-to-crown height and the eye level. What fraction is it?
  3. Why are the malleoli useful when drawing a standing figure?
