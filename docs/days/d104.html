Mathematics · Geometry LVIII: frustums and similar solids, mixed

Learn

Key terms

frustums and similar solids, mixed
Definition
A short mixed practice of this week’s methods: a frustum as a large cone minus a similar small cone, V = (1/3)πh(R² + Rr + r²), lateral area π(R + r)l, total surface with both bases, and similar solids with areas scaling by k² and volumes by k³. No new solid is begun.
Example
The classroom frustum with R = 6, r = 3, h = 4, l = 5 has volume 84π and total surface 90π. Two cubes of edges 2 and 6 have k = 3, and volumes in the ratio 27.
Not this
Not a first look at any of these. Classroom lengths remain invented. Exact answers stay in terms of π.
Connects to
Monday through Wednesday of this week.

Today is a mix, not a new formula. Use the similar-cone difference or the frustum formulas when the solid is a frustum, and use k² and k³ when the question is about similar solids.

Remember

Frustum volume: large cone minus small cone, or (1/3)πh(R² + Rr + r²). Frustum surface: π(R + r)l plus both bases. Similar solids: lengths k, areas k², volumes k³. Classroom lengths are invented.

Key terms

précis of Dewing I.1.77–78
Definition
A précis of Dewing I.1.77–78 keeps his points, order, and proportion: the Emperor again met the situation with a remedy; he ordered them to remove at once the upper parts of the masonry that were strained, the portions in contact with the arches, and to put them back much later, “as soon as the dampness of the masonry should abate enough to bear them.” They carried out the instructions, “and thereafter the structure stood secure.” “And the Emperor, in this way, enjoys a kind of testimonial from the work.”

Key terms

Definition

40 min · Method: a written pause, already taught, after this week’s monochrome work on the eyes

Key terms

written pause after the eyes
Definition
A written pause, already taught, is a short note of what was seen and what was corrected, written before another pass. Today it follows the eye as a volume, the lids as planes, and the likeness check of the two eyes together. The note names only what was actually seen or changed; it does not invent a highlight, a stamped second eye, a color pass, or a bone name not used in the drawing. Full color remains reserved.
Example
A sound pause might say: the first eye had been a flat almond and is now a volume in the socket; the upper lids needed a little more thickness; the second eye had been stamped and was seen again. It would not say that a color pass was begun, or that a vertebra had been drawn, if neither was done.

three-conjugation imperfect, contrasted with the preterite
Definition
Regular verbs in the imperfect now have three models, already taught this week: hablaba (-ar), comía (-er), and vivía (-ir). The -er and -ir verbs share the -ía endings. In this first look, the imperfect is still for ongoing or habitual past action, and the already-taught preterite (hablé, comí, viví) is for a completed event. The irregular imperfects of ir, ser, and ver remain reserved.
Example
Cuando yo caminaba, ella comía. Todos los días hablábamos y leíamos. Ayer hablé, comí, y leí.
Not this
Not a first look at any of the three imperfect sets, or at the preterites, already taught. Instruction remains in English. Classroom sentences do not identify a living person.
Connects to

Seminar · Economics: a simple discount on invented figures, a first look

40 min · Method: a first look at a simple percent-off discount on invented figures. Not a real store sale. Percent is already taught; “percent off” is today’s new job

Learn

Key terms

simple discount, percent off
Definition
A simple discount, in this first look, takes a percent off an invented price. If 25 percent is taken off an invented $80, the discount is one fourth of 80, which is $20, and the amount paid is $60. If 10 percent is taken off an invented $50, the discount is $5, and the amount paid is $45. The figures are invented so that the method can be practiced; they are not a real store sale, and not last week’s three-year repayment table.
Example
The percent, already taught, is the discount as a part of the original invented price, not a part of the amount paid. 25 percent of 80 is 20, not 25 percent of 60. A later first look at tax, compound interest, or a real advertised sale remains reserved.
Not this
Not a first look at percent, already taught, or at last week’s simple interest on a declining principal. Not a real store’s price, and not an official inflation or tax figure.
Connects to
Percent, already taught, and today’s invented “percent off.”

Percent is already taught. Today that method is used for a simple discount: a percent taken off an invented price, not off a real store sale.

If 25 percent is taken off an invented $80, first find 25 percent of 80, which is one fourth of 80, or $20. That $20 is the discount. The amount paid is 80 − 20, which is $60. The 25 percent is a part of the original $80, not a part of the $60.

A second invented case: 10 percent off $50 is $5 off, and the amount paid is $45. Last week’s three-year repayment table, with interest on a declining principal, is a different job, already taught, and is not redone as a first look today. Tax, compound interest, and a real advertised sale remain reserved.

Remember
