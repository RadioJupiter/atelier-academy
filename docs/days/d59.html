Writing & Rhetoric · A précis of the set extract: a Term 1 close

40 min · Method: already-taught précis, of the set pine extract only, with attribution

Learn

Key terms

a précis, a Term 1 close
Definition
A close of the already-taught précis: shorter than the set extract, in your own words, still true to it, and still attributed. It is not a quotation, and not a later chapter in miniature.
Example
From some distance the cloud’s mountain was not at first known; it was later found to be Vesuvius, and the shape was like a pine-tree, a long trunk spreading into branches. (Firth, 1900.)
Not this
Not a quotation pretending to be a précis, not an unattributed summary, and not a précis of reserved later scenes.
Connects to
Week 11 already asked for a précis of this same pine extract. Today gathers the method for the term.

A précis is shorter than the passage and is in your own words. It still has to be true to the set extract, and it still needs the attribution. It is not a quotation.

Set extract, still the pine-tree passage:

Those who were looking at the cloud from some distance could not make out from which mountain it was rising — it was afterwards discovered to have been Mount Vesuvius — but in likeness and form it more closely resembled a pine-tree than anything else, for what corresponded to the trunk was of great length and height, and then spread out into a number of branches.

J. B. Firth’s 1900 translation (the text used here is the version posted at Attalus, which notes that a few words and phrases have been modified).

A model précis. From some distance, people could not tell which mountain the cloud rose from; it was later found to be Vesuvius, and the shape was more like a pine-tree than anything else, a long high trunk spreading into branches. (Firth, 1900.)

The model does not name Pompeii, a death toll, or the reserved voyage. Yours should not either.

Economics · Unit price beside profit: a Term 1 close

30 min · Method: already-taught unit price and profit, gathered, in a labeled invented shop; a budget remains already taught

Learn

Key terms

unit price beside profit, a Term 1 close
Definition
A close of two already-taught invented-shop jobs: unit price is the price of one unit (total ÷ number of same-kind units); profit, already taught, is a leftover after costs. They are different jobs. Invented numbers stay labeled. A budget, already taught, is a third job and is not reteached as new today.
Example
A labeled invented pack of 4 identical notebooks costs $6: unit price $1.50. If the same pack cost the shop $4 (invented), the leftover profit would be $2 — a different job.
Not this
Not a real ticket, not a historical price, not a NASA figure, and not a claim that unit price is the same job as profit.
Connects to
Week 7 taught a budget; week 10 taught profit; week 11 taught unit price. Today gathers unit price beside profit, still in a labeled invented shop. A budget can be named as already taught, not as a first look.

Two invented-shop jobs have already been taught. Today gathers them. A budget (week 7) remains already taught and is not a first look today.

Unit price, already taught: total price ÷ number of same-kind units. Profit, already taught: a leftover after costs. They are different jobs. All shop numbers in this lesson are labeled invented classroom numbers, not a real ticket and not a historical price.

A worked invented pair. A pack of 4 identical notebooks is marked $6 (invented). The unit price is $6 ÷ 4 = $1.50 per notebook. If the shop’s invented cost for that pack is $4, the leftover profit is $6 − $4 = $2. The unit price can be known even when a profit is not being asked for.

We will not invent a NASA or a Roman price.

Remember

Unit price = total ÷ count, same-kind units. Profit is a leftover after costs, a different job. Invented numbers stay labeled. A budget is already taught, not a first look today.

Key terms: unit price beside profit, a Term 1 close.

Watch me

A labeled invented pack of 5 identical pencils costs $2. What is the unit price? If the invented cost is $1.50, what is the leftover profit?

  1. Unit price = $2 ÷ 5 = $0.40 per pencil.
  2. Profit = $2 − $1.50 = $0.50, a leftover after costs.
  3. The two jobs are different. The $2, the 5, and the $1.50 are invented and labeled.

Together

A labeled invented pack of 4 costs $6, and another pack of 3 of the same kind costs $6. Compare the unit prices only.

Hint 1: Each pack

First: $6 ÷ 4 = $1.50. Second: $6 ÷ 3 = $2.

Hint 2: The comparison

The totals are the same; the second pack has the higher unit price. Profit is not asked for here.
