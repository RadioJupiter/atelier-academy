This Friday mixes last week’s purpose clauses with ut and this week’s purpose clauses with nē, so the two markers stay distinct, and so the futures are not pulled back into the subjunctive job. Ut eum sequātur is a positive purpose. Nē eum sequātur is a negative purpose. The same pair holds for ēgrediātur. A result clause remains reserved.

Remember

Ut marks a positive purpose; nē marks a negative purpose. Both take the present subjunctive. Sequētur and ēgrediētur remain futures. A result clause remains reserved.

  2. What is a purpose clause with nē? Give Puella manet nē eum sequātur, with a short English gloss, and say how it differs from ut eum sequātur and from sequētur. Why is a result clause still a miss this week?
  3. Give ha visto, ha dicho, and hemos escrito, with short English glosses. Choose: Ella ___ (ha / hay / haya) visto la carta. Why is a new irregular participle beyond visto a miss this week?
  4. In official Example 25.3, what official θ₂ does the book find for a ray from air into diamond, and from which official n₂? What official n₂ did official Example 25.2 find?
  5. What is direct current, and what is alternating current, in OpenStax 20.5? What official household voltage does the book give, and what does V_rms mean in that wording?
  6. In the second-voyage letter, in Major’s 1870 wording, how many islands are recorded on the aforesaid Sunday, and what leagues do the pilots reckon? How is formal possession taken, and how is the fall of water described?
  7. In Pride and Prejudice, Chapter 9, what does Mrs. Bennet say about moving Jane, and what does she tell Elizabeth? What does she claim about families, and why do “neighbourhood” and “Every body” stay inside quotations?
  8. On an invented fair cube, what is the expected value already taught? In Dewing I.5.7–13, what wording is used for the circuit of the bay? In Gray 1918, how is the sacrum described?
