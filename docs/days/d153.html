Latin · Sequor and ēgredior: present subjunctive against the future

40 min · Method: mix sequātur with sequētur, and ēgrediātur with ēgrediētur; original sentences; purpose clauses reserved

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • present subjunctive of sequor — Day 151
  • present subjunctive of ēgredior — Day 152

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Yesterday and Monday gave the present subjunctive of sequor and of ēgredior. Today they are mixed with the already-taught futures, so the vowel of the ending has to be read with care. Sequātur is not sequētur. Ēgrediātur is not ēgrediētur. The first-person forms sequar and ēgrediar stay ambiguous in spelling; a short original sentence can still show the person, but the tense of those first-person forms is not settled by the spelling alone. The two verbs are not swapped. The sentences are original. A purpose clause with ut remains reserved.

Remember

Sequātur (ā) is the present subjunctive of sequor; sequētur (ē) is the future. Ēgrediātur is the present subjunctive of ēgredior; ēgrediētur is the future. Sequar and ēgrediar are spelled the same in both tenses. The two verbs are not interchangeable.

Watch me

  1. A fair pair: Puer eam sequātur, he follows her, present subjunctive; Puella ēgrediētur, she will go out, future.
  2. Do not write sequētur in the first sentence, and do not write ēgrediātur in the second, if those are the jobs named.

Together

Hint 1: The vowel

Present subjunctive: ā. Future: ē. That split does not appear in the first-person singular.

Hint 2: The verb

Sequor is to follow; ēgredior is to go out.

Mathematics · Geometry XCIII: sector area from the arc, A = (1/2)r s

40 min · Method: Singapore, faithful; A = (1/2)r s from s = rθ; invented figures, labeled invented

Learn

Key terms

sector area from the arc, A = (1/2)r s
Definition
Because the already-taught arc length is s = rθ with θ in radians, the sector area A = (1/2)r²θ can be rewritten A = (1/2)r s. If r and the arc s are known, the area can be found without first naming θ.
Example
On an invented figure, r = 10 and s = 4. Then A = (1/2) × 10 × 4 = 20, in square units. The same figure has θ = s/r = 4/10 = 2/5, and A = (1/2) × 100 × (2/5) = 20, the same area. The figure is invented and labeled invented.
Not this
Not A = (1/2)r²s, which treats the arc as if it were an angle. Not s = rθ used as if it were already the area. Not a degree formula applied to s.
Connects to
Yesterday’s A = (1/2)r²θ, and the already-taught arc s = rθ from Day 142. A circular segment waits until tomorrow.

The sector area is A = (1/2)r²θ, with θ in radians. The already-taught arc length is s = rθ, so θ = s/r when r is not zero. Substituting gives A = (1/2)r²(s/r) = (1/2)r s. If the radius and the arc are known, the sector area can be found at once. On an invented figure, r = 10 and s = 4. Then A = (1/2) × 10 × 4 = 20, in square units. Checking through the angle: θ = 4/10 = 2/5, and A = (1/2) × 100 × (2/5) = 20, the same area. The figure is invented and labeled invented. A circular segment waits until tomorrow.

Remember

A = (1/2)r s, and also A = (1/2)r²θ, with θ = s/r in radians. Do not write A = (1/2)r²s. Do not treat s as if it were already the area.

Physics · The law of reflection, a first look, from OpenStax 25.2

40 min · Method: OpenStax, College Physics 2e, 25.2, official wording θᵣ = θᵢ, angles from the normal; invented 30° figure, labeled invented; refraction reserved

Learn

Key terms

law of reflection
Definition
In OpenStax, College Physics 2e, 25.2, “The law of reflection states that the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence—θᵣ = θᵢ.” The angles “are measured relative to the perpendicular to the surface at the point where the ray strikes the surface.” That perpendicular is the normal.
Example
On an invented figure, a ray meets a flat surface so that the angle of incidence is 30°. The law gives θᵣ = 30° as well. The 30° figure is invented and labeled invented; the law itself is the book’s official statement.
Not this
Not the law of refraction, which remains reserved. Not a first look at a focal length, a curved mirror, or an official instrument length. Not a claim that a rough surface has no reflection: the book says a rough surface reflects in many directions, or is diffused.
Connects to
Monday’s ray and geometric optics, from OpenStax 25.1. The angles of incidence and of reflection, named with the law in 25.2.
angle of incidence
Definition
The angle of incidence, θᵢ, is the angle between the incoming ray and the perpendicular (the normal) to the surface at the point where the ray strikes. It is not the angle between the ray and the surface itself. Wiktionary gives the word incidence from Latin incidere, “to fall upon,” from in + cadere, “to fall.”
Origin
Wiktionary: incidence is from Middle French incidence, from Medieval Latin incidentia, “a falling upon,” from Latin incidens, present participle of incidere, “to fall upon,” from in, “on,” + cadere, “to fall.”
Example
If the incoming ray, the normal, and the surface are drawn as in OpenStax Figure 25.4, θᵢ is the angle from the normal to the incident ray. On an invented figure, that angle is 30°, labeled invented.
Not this
Not the angle between the ray and the surface, which would be the complement of θᵢ on a flat surface, and is not the book’s angle. Not θᵣ, the angle of reflection, which is equal to θᵢ but is measured on the reflected side.
Connects to
The law of reflection in the same OpenStax section, θᵣ = θᵢ.

OpenStax, College Physics 2e, 25.2, states the law of reflection: “the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence—θᵣ = θᵢ.” The angles “are measured relative to the perpendicular to the surface at the point where the ray strikes the surface.” That perpendicular is the normal. The angle of incidence, θᵢ, is the angle between the incident ray and the normal, not the angle between the ray and the surface. The angle of reflection, θᵣ, is the angle between the reflected ray and the same normal. On an invented figure, θᵢ = 30°, so θᵣ = 30°. The 30° figure is invented and labeled invented; the law is the book’s official statement. The book also notes that a rough surface reflects light in many directions, or is diffused, which is why a sheet of paper can be seen from many angles, while a smooth mirror reflects “at specific angles.” The law of refraction remains reserved. No official instrument length is used.

Remember

The law of reflection is θᵣ = θᵢ, with both angles measured from the normal, the perpendicular to the surface at the point of striking, not from the surface itself. An invented 30° incidence gives a 30° reflection. Refraction remains reserved.

Spanish · Ha, hay, and haya, kept distinct

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

The new present perfect now sits beside already-taught hay and haya, so the three have to be kept distinct. Hay: there is, or there are. Haya: the present subjunctive, “that there be.” Ha: the helping form in ha hablado, he has spoken, or she has spoken. A sentence such as Espero que haya tiempo still takes haya, not ha, and not hay. A sentence such as Ella ha hablado takes ha, not hay, and not haya. Irregular participles remain reserved. The sentences are original classroom Spanish.

Remember

Hay means there is, or there are. Haya means that there be. Ha, before a regular participle, means he has, or she has. Do not swap them. Irregular participles remain reserved.

Hint 2: Near-miss
