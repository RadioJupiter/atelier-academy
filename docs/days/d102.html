Latin · The present deponent table, used in short sentences

Key terms

present deponent table in use
Definition
The whole present table of a deponent can now be used in a short sentence: loquor, loqueris, loquitur, loquimur, loquiminī, loquuntur, and the same persons of cōnor. The meaning stays active in every person. Sequitur and ēgreditur, already taught in the third person, may be recognized, but their first and second persons, if they have not been set out, are not required today.
Example

Mathematics · Geometry LVI: the surface area of a frustum of a right cone

45 min · Method: the lateral area of a frustum is the large cone’s lateral area minus the small cone’s, which is also π(R + r)l; the total surface adds the two bases

Learn

Key terms

surface area of a frustum of a right cone
Definition
The lateral surface of a frustum of a right cone is what remains when the small cone’s lateral surface is taken from the large cone’s, both already taught as πrL. That difference is also π(R + r)l, where l is the slant height of the frustum. The total surface adds the two bases, πR² + πr², both exposed.
Example
Yesterday’s classroom cones: large radius 6, height 8, slant 10; small radius 3, height 4, slant 5. The frustum has slant l = 5. Lateral area 60π − 15π = 45π, or π(6 + 3)×5 = 45π. Total surface 45π + 36π + 9π = 90π square units, left in terms of π.
Not this
Not a first look at last week’s cone surface, or at yesterday’s volume. Not a composite that hides a face: both bases of the frustum are outside. Classroom lengths are invented.
Connects to
Last week’s πrl + πr², yesterday’s similar cones, and the Pythagorean theorem if l is not given.

Yesterday’s frustum had volume 84π. Today is the outside.

The large cone of radius 6 and height 8 has slant height √(6² + 8²) = 10, already taught by Pythagoras. Its lateral area is 60π. The similar small cone of radius 3 and height 4 has slant 5, lateral area 15π. The frustum’s lateral area is the difference, 45π. Its own slant height is 10 − 5 = 5, and the same 45π is π(R + r)l = π(6 + 3)×5.

Both bases are exposed, so the total surface is 45π + π×36 + π×9 = 90π square units, left in terms of π.

Remember

Lateral area of a frustum: large cone minus small cone, or π(R + r)l, with l the slant height of the frustum. Total surface: that lateral area plus both bases, πR² + πr². Classroom lengths are invented. Exact answers stay in terms of π.

Key terms: surface area of a frustum of a right cone.

Key terms

Definition

Science · Evaporation below the boiling point, and Lv at 37.0 °C from OpenStax

40 min · Method: OpenStax 14.3 — water can evaporate below the boiling point, and the same table’s footnote gives Lv = 2430 kJ/kg at 37.0 °C, more than last week’s 2256 kJ/kg at 100.0 °C

Learn

Key terms

latent heat of vaporization of water at 37.0 °C (OpenStax)
Definition
OpenStax, College Physics 2e, section 14.3, notes that water can evaporate at temperatures below the boiling point, and that more energy is required than at the boiling point. Footnotes 4 and 5 to Table 14.2 give the sourced figure used today: at 37.0 °C (body temperature), Lv for water is 2430 kJ/kg, or 580 kcal/kg. That is more than last week’s Lv = 2256 kJ/kg at 100.0 °C, from the same table. Q = mLv still holds, with the Lv that matches the temperature named in the source.
Example
OpenStax’s 2430 kJ/kg means that 1 kg of water already at 37.0 °C would need 2430 kJ to vaporize, on this figure. An invented classroom mass of 10 g, or 0.010 kg, would need 24.30 kJ, and is labeled invented. The 37.0 °C is OpenStax’s named temperature, not a temperature invented for this course, and not a living person’s measured temperature in this lesson.

Key terms

the noble castle (Inferno IV)
Definition

Atelier · The lids as planes, still monochrome

40 min · Method: the upper and lower lids are small planes wrapping yesterday’s volume, still monochrome

Learn

Key terms

the lids as planes
Definition
The upper lid and the lower lid, on the monochrome head, are small planes that wrap the already-seen volume of the eye. The upper lid often has more thickness, and, if the light is from above, a narrow light plane on its upper surface and a small shade on its underside. The lower lid is usually thinner and may catch a little light on its upper edge. Neither lid is a wire, and a fold is not invented if it is not seen.
Example
A careful pass checks each lid against yesterday’s volume and against the already-placed brow, then against the other eye, which is still seen, not stamped.
Not this
Not a first look at yesterday’s volume in the socket, except as the form the lids wrap. Not a first look at the family check. Full color remains reserved. A likeness check of the two eyes together is Wednesday.
Connects to

If the light is from above, its upper surface may take a narrow light, still inside the light family or on its edge, and its underside may turn into a little shade on the volume, as yesterday already allowed. The lower lid is usually thinner. It may catch a little light on its upper edge, if that light is seen. A fold, or a heavy outline, is not invented.
