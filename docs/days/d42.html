Latin · Review of Capitula I–X: third-family nouns

40 min · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English. A review, not a new chapter.

Learn

Key terms

third-family noun
Definition
A noun whose nominative may look unlike hortus or puella (for example rēx, nōmen), and whose other endings follow the third pattern already used in Capitula IX–X.
Example
Rēx nōn in hortō est. Nōmen is neuter, so Nōmen longum est, not *longus.
Not this
Not every new-looking nominative is a new chapter. Today only reviews the third family already opened in Capitula IX–X, with original sentences, not a textbook story.
Connects to
Yesterday’s first and second families still work beside these. Adjectives still match (longum with nōmen).
Word How to say it Meaning

nōmen NOH-men a name (nominative, third family, neuter)

Nōmen longum est NOH-men LONG-um est The name is long

Mathematics · Geometry XIX: the equation of a circle

60 min · Method: the already-taught locus, then the coordinate equation (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²

Learn

Key terms

equation of a circle
Definition
The coordinate form of the locus of points at a fixed distance r from a center (h, k): (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². When the center is the origin, this is x² + y² = r².
Example
x² + y² = 4 is the circle of radius 2 centered at (0, 0). The point (0, 2) satisfies it: 0 + 4 = 4. The point (3, 0) does not: 9 ≠ 4.
Not this
Not the graph of a function y = f(x) in general (a full circle fails the “one y for each x” test). And not a parabola, whose equation is quadratic in one variable only, in the form you have used.
Connects to
The locus definition of a circle (Geometry XVI), and the distance idea already in the plane. Completing the square can move a general x² + y² + … form into (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r², but today’s checks stay with the centered form.

A circle was already defined as a locus: all points at one fixed distance, the radius, from a center (Geometry XVI). Today that same locus is written in coordinates.

The equation. If the center is (h, k) and the radius is r, a point (x, y) is on the circle exactly when (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². That is the Pythagorean statement of the distance from the center (the squared distance is r²). When the center is the origin, h = 0 and k = 0, so x² + y² = r².

A check. On x² + y² = 4, the radius is 2. (0, 2) works; (2, 0) works; (0, 0) does not, because it is the center, not a point on the circle.

A circle is not, in general, the graph of a single function y = f(x). A vertical line through the origin meets x² + y² = 4 at (0, 2) and (0, −2): two y-values. Function graphs from Algebra IX–X stay functions; this is a locus in the plane.

Classroom circles stay labeled invented (a radius-2 classroom circle is not a NASA orbit).

Remember

(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². At the origin, x² + y² = r². A full circle is a locus, not generally a function graph y = f(x). Invented classroom circles stay labeled.

Key terms: equation of a circle.

Watch me

Does (0, 2) lie on x² + y² = 4? Does (3, 0)?

  1. 0² + 2² = 4, which equals the right-hand side, so (0, 2) is on the circle.
  2. 3² + 0² = 9, and 9 is not 4, so (3, 0) is not on the circle.
  3. The radius is 2; (3, 0) is 3 units from the origin.

Science · Photoreceptors, and a first look at color vision

50 min · Method: the already-taught path to the retina, then the NEI account of photoreceptors and of color vision deficiency; no invented counts

Learn

Key terms

photoreceptor
Definition
A cell in the retina that turns light into an electrical signal. The National Eye Institute describes these as special cells in the retina that turn light into electrical signals, which then travel by the optic nerve; the brain turns the signals into images.
Example
Light focused on the retina reaches photoreceptors; a signal can then leave by the optic nerve (already taught).
Not this
Not a count of cells, and not a claim that the retina “sees” by itself. We still invent no number of photoreceptors, rods, or cones.
Connects to
Days 32 and 34: the eye as a kind of camera, the retina, the optic nerve, and a refused cell count. Today names the cells the NEI page names, still without a count.
color vision deficiency
Definition
A difference in how colors are seen, compared with most people. The National Eye Institute also uses the everyday name “color blindness,” and notes that most often it makes certain colors hard to tell apart; it often runs in families; men have a much higher risk than women; and many people adjust to it.
Example
The NEI describes, as a common case, trouble telling the difference between certain colors, and a test that uses a circle of colored dots with a shape inside.
Not this
Not “seeing only in gray” in the usual case (the NEI says complete color vision deficiency, seeing no color at all, is rare). And not a classroom diagnosis of anyone in the room.
Connects to
Today’s photoreceptors, and the already-taught path from the retina to a signal. We do not invent a percentage beyond what we are prepared to attribute; the qualitative NEI claims above are enough for this lesson.

The path from the cornea to the retina and out by the optic nerve is already taught. The National Eye Institute (NEI) page “How the eyes work” says that in the retina, special cells called photoreceptors turn the light into electrical signals, that those signals travel through the optic nerve to the brain, and that the brain turns the signals into the images you see.

We still invent no count of photoreceptors, and no count of rods or cones. Day 34 already refused those counts. Today takes the NEI’s word photoreceptor and the brain’s job in making the image, including color as an experience the brain constructs from signals, not as a paint already “in” the light as a little colored chip on the retina.

Color vision deficiency. The NEI’s page on color blindness (color vision deficiency) says that it means you see colors differently than most people; that most of the time it makes certain colors hard to tell apart; that complete color vision deficiency (seeing no color at all) is rare; that the most common types are genetic and often run in families; and that men have a much higher risk than women. The common test it describes is a circle of colored dots with a shape inside. This lesson is not a diagnosis of anyone, and it is not a demand to use a test chart as a toy.

Source. National Eye Institute, “How the eyes work,” and “Color blindness,” accessed for this lesson. We do not invent a receptor count, a wavelength in nanometers, or a classroom diagnosis.

Remember

Photoreceptors in the retina turn light into signals (NEI). The brain turns signals into images. Color vision deficiency is a difference in how colors are seen (NEI); complete lack of color is rare. No invented cell counts, and no classroom diagnosis.

Key terms: photoreceptor, color vision deficiency.

Learn

Key terms

Aeneid
Definition

The Term 1 literature list, after Homer and Antigone, is Virgil’s Aeneid (selected books). We begin with a short opening, not with the whole epic.

Arms, and the Man I sing, who forc'd by Fate, And haughty Juno's unrelenting Hate; Expell'd and exil'd, left the Trojan Shoar.

Modern language · Spanish: tan… como (as … as)

30 min · Method: equality with tan + adjective + como, contrasted with más… que. Instruction in English

Learn

Key terms

tan… como
Definition
The present-tense pattern for equality with an adjective or adverb: tan + adjective + como, “as … as.”
Example
La caja es tan grande como la mesa. El lápiz no es tan largo como la regla.
Not this
Not the comparative más… que (Day 19), and not the superlative (Day 36). Equality is a different job from “more” or “the most.”
Connects to
Comparatives and the superlative already taught. Possessives from yesterday can sit in the same sentence: mi caja es tan grande como tu caja.
Word How to say it Meaning
tan… como tahn … KOH-moh as … as (equality)
tanto / tanta / tantos / tantas … como TAHN-toh … KOH-moh as much / as many … as (agrees with the noun)
igual de ee-GWAHL deh just as, equally (a second, shorter pattern: igual de grande)

Day 19 taught más… que (more than). Day 36 taught the superlative. Today is equality: tan + adjective + como, “as … as,” still in the present.

The pattern. La caja es tan grande como la mesa. El lápiz no es tan largo como la regla. The adjective still agrees: tan larga with a feminine singular noun, if you describe la regla.

tanto… como is for as much / as many: Hay tantas cajas como reglas, with tantas matching cajas.

Instruction stays in English. Original sentences only. No decimal comparison in the Spanish (0.4 and 0.40 stay in the English mathematics).

Remember

tan + adjective + como = as … as. tanto… como for as much / as many. This is not más… que and not the superlative. Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words.

Key terms: tan… como.

Music theory · The minor triad, beside the major triad already taught

30 min · Method: the already-taught triad and relative minor, then a minor third inside the three-note chord

Learn

Key terms

minor triad
Definition
A three-note chord built from a root, a minor third above the root, and a perfect fifth above the root (or a major third above the third). In a major key’s scale, the triad on degree VI is one common minor triad (the submediant); the tonic triad of a relative minor is the same three pitch classes.
Example
In C major, the triad A–C–E is a minor triad (A minor). C–E–G, already taught as a major triad, is still major.
Not this
Not a major triad (the third is different), and not a full course in minor-key harmony. Day 17 taught the triad; Day 22 taught the natural minor and the relative minor. Today names the minor third inside the triad.
Connects to
The major triad (Day 17), I–IV–V (Day 27), intervals including the major third and the perfect fifth (Day 32), and the relative minor (Day 22).

The triad is already taught, and so is the relative minor. Today names the minor triad from the inside: a root, a minor third, and a perfect fifth.

A contrast you can hear. In C major, C–E–G is a major triad (major third, then a minor third on top, or a perfect fifth from C). A–C–E is a minor triad (minor third from A to C, perfect fifth from A to E). The difference is the third, not a new kind of fifth.

Relative minor. A minor is the relative minor of C major (Day 22). Its tonic triad is this same A–C–E. We will not invent a long harmonic-minor course today, and we will not invent a tempo or a repertoire list. A keyboard, or a sung arpeggio, is enough if the guide has one; a written staff of three notes is enough if not.

The examples are in C for clarity, as earlier interval and triad lessons often were. They are classroom examples, not a claim about a particular historical performance.

Remember

A minor triad is a root, a minor third, and a perfect fifth. In C major, A–C–E is a minor triad; C–E–G remains major. The difference is the third. No invented harmonic-minor course today.

Key terms: minor triad.

Watch me

Build a minor triad on A, and name how it differs from C–E–G.

  1. A to C is a minor third; A to E is a perfect fifth. The notes are A–C–E.
  2. C–E–G has a major third from C to E.
  3. The fifth in both is perfect; the third is the difference.
