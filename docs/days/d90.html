Today is a mix of this week’s Latin, not a new topic. Close the cards. Be ready to use prior, posterior, and īnferior; domī; the deponents loquitur, cōnātur, sequitur, and ēgreditur in the third person and the infinitive; and fit, fiunt, fierī. First- and second-person deponent and fiō forms remain reserved.

Remember

Prior, posterior, and īnferior order or locate. Domī is a locative, at home, with no preposition. Deponents look passive and are translated as active, third person and infinitive only. Fiō is a different verb, “become / happen,” today in fit, fiunt, and fierī.

Watch me

Watch one mixed original sentence, then name each tool in it.

  1. Sī magister domī nōn est, discipulus ē lūdō ēgredī cōnātur.
  2. Sī is last week’s conjunction. Domī is this week’s locative. Ēgredī and cōnātur are deponents: he tries to go out.
  3. No first-person deponent was needed.

Together

Build one mixed sentence that uses a locative or a comparative of order, and one deponent.

Hint 1: One of each

Pick domī or prior, then loquitur, cōnātur, sequitur, or ēgreditur.

Hint 2: Active English

Translate the deponent as active.

Check

This Check mirrors only this week’s Latin.

  1. What do prior and īnferior each do, as taught this week?
  2. Translate: Puer domī est. Magister loquitur.
  3. What is a deponent verb, and which persons are reserved?
  4. Translate: Discipulī recitāre cōnantur. Tempestās fit.
  5. Write one original sentence with ēgreditur or sequitur, and one with fiunt.

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Today is a mix of this week’s geometry. Close the cards. Use V = ℓwh for a box, V = B × h for a right prism, and V = πr²h for a right cylinder. A tube uses last week’s ring as the base. Exact cylinder answers stay in terms of π.

Remember

V = B × h. A box is the rectangular case. A cylinder uses B = πr². A tube uses B = π(R² − r²). Keep area and volume distinct.

Watch me

  1. A box 5 × 4 × 3 has V = 60 cubic units.
  2. A cylinder of radius 4 and height 3 has V = 48π cubic units.
  3. A tube with R = 4, r = 2, h = 3 has V = π(16 − 4) × 3 = 36π cubic units.

Together

Hint 1: The cylinder

V = π × 36 × 2 = 72π cubic units.

Hint 2: The box

V = 72 cubic units. The cylinder’s base is a circle of radius 6, not a 6-by-6 square; 72π is not 72.

Elective · Anatomy: the eight carpal bones, from Gray 1918

40 min · Method: Gray’s eight carpal names, after the carpus as a group already taught on Day 75. Later names are a gloss only

Learn

Key terms

the eight carpal bones
Definition
Gray, 1918, names eight carpal bones in two rows. “Those of the proximal row, from the radial to the ulnar side, are named the navicular, lunate, triangular, and pisiform; those of the distal row, in the same order, are named the greater multangular, lesser multangular, capitate, and hamate.”
Example
The proximal row, radial to ulnar: navicular, lunate, triangular, pisiform. The distal row, in the same order: greater multangular, lesser multangular, capitate, hamate.
Not this
Not a first look at the carpus as a group, already taught on Day 75 with the metacarpals and the phalanges. Not last week’s seven tarsal names as a first look, though the two lists may be contrasted. Not a later set of names (scaphoid, triquetrum, trapezium, trapezoid) required as today’s list; those may be heard as a gloss only.
Connects to
Henry Gray, Anatomy of the Human Body, 1918, “6b. The Hand. 1. The Carpus,” as in the source note.

Henry Gray, Anatomy of the Human Body, 20th ed., ed. Warren H. Lewis, 1918, “6b. The Hand. 1. The Carpus,” https://www.bartleby.com/lit-hub/anatomy-of-the-human-body/6b-the-hand-1-the-carpus/ (public domain). Later names (scaphoid, triquetrum, trapezium, trapezoid) are mentioned only as a gloss, as last week’s later names for some cuneiforms were, and are not required as today’s list.

Day 75 taught the carpus as a group, and reserved the eight names. Last week named the seven tarsals from Gray. Today is the reserved carpal list, in Gray’s 1918 wording:

“The carpal bones, eight in number, are arranged in two rows. Those of the proximal row, from the radial to the ulnar side, are named the navicular, lunate, triangular, and pisiform; those of the distal row, in the same order, are named the greater multangular, lesser multangular, capitate, and hamate.”

Later common names — scaphoid for Gray’s navicular, triquetrum for triangular, trapezium for greater multangular, trapezoid for lesser multangular — may be heard as a gloss, as last week allowed later names beside some of Gray’s cuneiforms. They are not required as today’s list, and Gray’s wording remains the set wording.

The radial side is the thumb side (toward the radius); the ulnar side is toward the ulna, already placed in the arm. This lesson does not invent a count beyond Gray’s eight, and it does not reteach the metacarpals or the phalanges as first looks.

Remember

Gray names eight carpals in two rows, radial to ulnar. Proximal: navicular, lunate, triangular, pisiform. Distal: greater multangular, lesser multangular, capitate, hamate. The carpus as a group was already taught. Later names are a gloss only.

Vocabulary review

15–20 min · Spaced review of weeks 1–18 terms · Use your own words; then check the key

Learn

This is not a new lesson. Retrieval — saying a term from memory, then checking — is how a word becomes usable, not merely familiar. Close the glossary and the day's cards first. For each item, explain or apply the term, or distinguish it from a near-miss.

Remember

A definition you can only recognize on the card is not yet yours. Give the meaning, a fresh example, and a near-miss.

Watch me

A model answer for a near-miss item, so the others can be written in the same shape.

  1. Question: What is the volume of a right cylinder, and why is “2πr, times the height” a miss for that volume?
  2. The volume is V = πr²h, which is V = B × h with last week’s circle area as the base. A classroom cylinder of radius 3 and height 10 has volume 90π cubic units.
  3. 2πr is the circumference, a length, not the base area. Volume uses the area πr², times h, and is not a first look at last week’s circumference.
  4. A near-miss is to treat 90π as 90, dropping the π, or to treat the base area 9π as if it were already a volume.
