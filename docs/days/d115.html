Latin · Friday mix: the deponent imperfect, with present and perfect contrasts

40 min · Method: a Friday mix of this week’s imperfect with the already-taught present and perfect; no new tense

Learn

This Friday mix uses only forms already taught this week and last. Name the tense, translate, and do not treat an absolute as a main-clause verb.

Word How to say it Meaning
loquēbar loh-KWAY-bar I was speaking, or I used to speak
loquēbātur loh-kway-BAH-toor he or she was speaking, or used to speak
loquēbantur loh-kway-BAHN-toor they were speaking, or used to speak
cōnābar koh-NAH-bar I was trying, or I used to try
loquor LOH-kwor I speak, or I am speaking (present, already taught)
locūtus sum lo-KOO-toos soom I spoke, or I have spoken (perfect, already taught)

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Remember

Imperfect: loquēbar, I was speaking or used to speak. Perfect: locūtus sum, I spoke or have spoken. Present: loquor, I speak. The absolute puerō locūtō remains a loosely attached phrase.

Watch me

Watch one Friday item all the way through, so the others can follow the same shape.

  1. The form loquēbāmur is imperfect, first person plural.
  2. It means we were speaking, or we used to speak.
  3. A near-miss is locūtī sumus, the perfect, we spoke or have spoken.

Together

Label and translate: cōnābantur; cōnātī sunt; cōnantur.

Hint 1: Tense

Cōnābantur is imperfect; cōnātī sunt is perfect; cōnantur is present.

Hint 2: Meaning

They were trying or used to try; they tried or have tried; they try or are trying.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. Give the six imperfect forms of loquor, and translate loquēbar and loquēbātur.
  2. Translate and label the tense: Ego cōnābar. Nōs locūtī sumus. Puer loquitur.
  3. Why is puerō locūtō not an answer to “the boy was speaking”?

Exercises

  2. Change cōnābāminī to the present and to the perfect, and translate all three.

Mathematics · Friday mix: tangent, with sine and cosine

40 min · Method: a Friday mix of this week’s tangent work with last week’s sine and cosine; degrees only; no new ratio

Learn

This Friday mix uses only ratios and identities already taught. Classroom lengths are invented. Degrees only. The radian and the unit circle remain reserved.

Remember

Tan A is opposite over adjacent, and tan A = sin A / cos A. Tan(90° − A) = 1 / tan A. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, if A is opposite 3, tan A = 3/4 and tan(90° − A) = 4/3.

Watch me

Watch one Friday item all the way through.

  1. If A is opposite 3 in the 3-4-5 triangle, tan A = 3/4.
  2. Sin A / cos A = (3/5)/(4/5) = 3/4, the same value.
  3. A miss is to leave the answer as 3, or to use 3/5, which is sine.

Together

In a classroom 5-12-13 triangle, B is opposite 5. Give tan B in two ways, and give tan(90° − B).

Hint 1: Opposite over adjacent

Tan B = 5/12.

Hint 2: The identity

Sin B / cos B = (5/13)/(12/13) = 5/12. Tan(90° − B) = 12/5.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key. Classroom lengths are invented.

  1. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, A is opposite 3. Give sin A, cos A, tan A, and tan(90° − A).
  2. A classroom 6-8-10 triangle has the same angle A, opposite 6. Why is tan A still 3/4?
  3. Why is tan A = 3/5 a miss, and which already-taught ratio is that?

Exercises

  1. In a classroom 5-12-13 triangle, D is opposite 12. Give tan D in two ways.
  2. A similar 10-24-26 triangle has E opposite 10. Give tan E and tan(90° − E).

Physics · Friday mix: waves, wave velocity, and the two kinds of wave

40 min · Method: a Friday mix of OpenStax 16.9, including Example 16.8 and the transverse–longitudinal distinction; no new section

Learn

This mix uses only Monday’s and Wednesday’s work from OpenStax 16.9. Superposition, section 16.10, remains reserved. Official measured speeds of sound, light, or earthquake waves are not invented.

Remember

A wave is a disturbance that propagates. vw = λ / T = fλ. Example 16.8: λ = 10.0 m, T = 5.00 s, vw = 2.00 m/s. Transverse: disturbance perpendicular to propagation. Longitudinal: disturbance parallel to propagation.

Watch me

Watch one Friday item all the way through.

  1. Example 16.8 is recomputed: 10.0 m / 5.00 s = 2.00 m/s.
  2. That 2.00 m/s is vw, the speed of the disturbance, not the gull’s changing vertical speed.
  3. A miss would be to invent an official speed of sound and call it today’s answer.

Together

Recompute Example 16.8, then say whether a guitar-string wave is transverse or longitudinal, and why.

Hint 1: The example

λ = 10.0 m, T = 5.00 s, vw = 2.00 m/s.

Hint 2: The string

The disturbance is perpendicular to the direction of travel, so the wave is transverse.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key. Classroom waves, other than Example 16.8, are invented.

  1. Define a wave, and write the two forms of vw.
  2. Recompute Example 16.8. Why is the gull’s up-and-down speed a miss?
  3. Give one transverse example and one longitudinal example from OpenStax 16.9.

Exercises

  1. An invented wave has λ = 6.0 m and T = 2.0 s. Find vw and f.
  2. Why must you not invent an official P-wave speed in this mix?

Modern language · Spanish: Friday mix of preterite versus imperfect

40 min · Method: a Friday mix of this week’s tense choice, with already-taught forms only; instruction in English

Learn

This mix uses only already-taught forms. Instruction remains in English. Cada mañana and ayer remain useful markers. Do not invent identifying characters. Veía, not vía.

Word How to say it Meaning
hablé ah-BLEH I spoke (one completed time)
hablaba ah-BLAH-bah I was speaking, or I used to speak
comí koh-MEE I ate (one completed time)
comía koh-MEE-ah I was eating, or I used to eat
fui FWEE I went, or I was (completed; already taught for ir and ser)
iba EE-bah I was going, or I used to go (already taught)
era EH-rah I was, or I used to be (already taught)
vi bee I saw (already taught)
veía beh-EE-ah I was seeing, or I used to see (already taught)

Remember

Preterite for a completed past; imperfect for a habitual, ongoing, or background past. Fui is shared by ir and ser; iba and era are not shared. Ayer, mientras comía, vi el cielo is a fair pairing.

Watch me

Watch one Friday item all the way through.

  1. Cada mañana iba al mercado: habitual, imperfect of ir.
  2. Ayer fui al mercado: completed, preterite of ir.
  3. A miss would be Cada mañana fui, if the marker is a habit, or Ayer iba as the only verb, if the sentence was meant as one completed trip.

Together

Choose and translate: Cada mañana el mercado ___ grande (era / fue). Ayer yo ___ al mercado (iba / fui).

Hint 1: Description

Cada mañana el mercado era grande uses era, a description in the imperfect.

Hint 2: The trip

Ayer fui al mercado is a completed going.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. State the job of the preterite and the job of the imperfect, and give one already-taught example of each.
  2. Fill: Ayer, mientras ___ (comí / comía), ___ el cielo (vi / veía). Translate.
  3. Why do ir and ser share fui, but not iba or era?

Exercises

  1. Write one original habitual sentence and one original paired sentence. Translate them.
  2. Correct the miss: Ayer, mientras comí, veía el cielo. Say why it is a miss.

Seminar · Artistic anatomy: a typical vertebra, from Gray 1918

40 min · Method: a first look at Gray’s typical vertebra, from Theodora’s public-domain text of Gray 1918; official lengths and regional special features reserved

Learn

Key terms

a typical vertebra, from Gray
Definition
Gray 1918, in the public-domain text used here from Theodora, describes a typical vertebra as consisting of two essential parts: an anterior segment, the body, and a posterior part, the vertebral or neural arch; these enclose a foramen, the vertebral foramen. The vertebral arch consists of a pair of pedicles and a pair of laminæ, and supports seven processes: four articular, two transverse, and one spinous. When the vertebræ are articulated, the bodies form a pillar for the support of the head and trunk, the vertebral foramina constitute a canal for the protection of the medulla spinalis (spinal cord), and between every pair are two intervertebral foramina, for the transmission of the spinal nerves and vessels. Keep Gray’s spellings vertebræ and laminæ. Official lengths remain reserved. Regional special features of cervical, thoracic, or lumbar vertebræ remain reserved.
Example
Today’s job is this typical plan — body, arch, foramen, pedicles, laminæ, and the seven processes — not a recitation of last week’s curves, and not the already-taught count of 33.
Not this
Not a first look at the count of 33 vertebræ, already taught, or at the four curves, already taught last week. Not official 71 cm or 61 cm lengths, which remain reserved. Not the special features of a cervical, thoracic, or lumbar vertebra, which remain reserved. Vocabulary review

15–20 min · Spaced review of weeks 1–23 terms · Use your own words; then check the key

Learn

This is not a new lesson. Retrieval — saying a term from memory, then checking — is how a word becomes usable, not merely familiar. Close the glossary and the day's cards first. For each item, explain or apply the term, or distinguish it from a near-miss.

Remember

A definition you can only recognize on the card is not yet yours. Give the meaning, a fresh example, and a near-miss.

Watch me

A model answer for a near-miss item, so the others can be written in the same shape.

  1. Question: What is tan A in a right triangle, and why is “the opposite side” a miss if it is given as a length only?
  2. Tan A is the opposite side over the adjacent side. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, if A is opposite 3 and adjacent to 4, tan A = 3/4.
  3. The opposite side by itself is a length, not a ratio. Tangent is a comparison of two lengths.
  4. A near-miss is to use the opposite side over the hypotenuse, which is sine, or to treat a similar 6-8-10 triangle as if it had a new tangent of A.

Together

In your own words, what is a wave, in OpenStax 16.9, and how is vw computed in Example 16.8?

Hint 1: A wave

A wave is a disturbance that propagates, or moves from the place it was created. Wavelength λ is the distance between adjacent identical parts, and vw = λ / T = fλ.

Hint 2: The example

In Example 16.8, λ = 10.0 m and T = 5.00 s, so vw = 2.00 m/s. That is the speed of the disturbance, not the gull’s changing vertical speed. Invent no official speed of sound or of light today.
