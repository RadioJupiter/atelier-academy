Latin · Friday mix: sequor and ēgredior, subjunctive and future

40 min · Method: Friday mix of this week’s present subjunctives with already-taught tenses; purpose clauses reserved

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • present subjunctive of sequor and ēgredior — Day 151

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This Friday mixes the present subjunctives of sequor and ēgredior with the already-taught tenses of both verbs. The first-person singular of each present subjunctive is spelled the same as the future; the other persons are read by the vowel of the ending. The two verbs are not swapped. The sentences are original classroom Latin, not copied from Ørberg. A purpose clause with ut remains reserved.

Remember

Present subjunctive: sequātur, ēgrediātur (ā). Future: sequētur, ēgrediētur (ē). Sequar and ēgrediar are spelled the same in both tenses. Sequor is to follow; ēgredior is to go out.

Watch me

Choose forms for these four glosses: she follows him (present subjunctive); he will go out; they will have followed; we go out (present subjunctive).

  1. Eum sequātur.
  2. Ēgrediētur.
  3. Secūtī erunt, or secūtae erunt.
  4. Ēgrediāmur.

Together

In a short original pair, contrast sequētur with ēgrediātur, and say why the verbs and the vowels both matter.

Hint 1: The verbs

Sequor is to follow; ēgredior is to go out.

Hint 2: The vowels

Future ē; present subjunctive ā, except in the first-person singular, where the spelling is shared with the future.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. Give the six present-subjunctive forms of sequor, and say how sequātur differs from sequētur.
  2. Give the six present-subjunctive forms of ēgredior, and say how ēgrediar is like sequar.
  3. Why is a purpose clause with ut still a miss this week?

Exercises

  1. Write short original sentences for sequātur, ēgrediētur, secūta erit, and ēgrediāmur, with glosses.
  2. Contrast sequitur, sequētur, sequātur, and secūtus est in four short glosses.
  3. What remains reserved?

Mathematics · Geometry XCV: Friday mix of sectors and segments

40 min · Method: Singapore, faithful; Friday mix of this week’s sector and segment areas; invented figures, labeled invented

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • area of a sector in radians — Day 151
  • area of a circular segment — Day 154

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This Friday mixes the sector area in radians, A = (1/2)r²θ or A = (1/2)r s, the check that θ = 2π recovers the disk, and the circular segment as sector minus triangle. On an invented figure, r = 6 and θ = π/3, the sector area is 6π, and the segment area is 6π − 9√3, already found this week. On another invented figure, r = 10 and s = 4, the sector area is 20. The figures are invented and labeled invented. Do not put a radian measure into the degree formula as if it were in degrees, and do not leave the triangular piece inside a segment.

Remember

A = (1/2)r²θ = (1/2)r s, with θ in radians. A circular segment is sector minus triangle. θ = 2π recovers the disk. Figures are invented.

Watch me

On an invented figure, r = 2 and θ = π/2, give the sector area and the segment area.

  1. Sector: π square units.
  2. Triangle: 2. Segment: π − 2 square units.
  3. The figure is invented and labeled invented.

Together

On an invented figure, r = 8 and s = 3, find the sector area. Why would 12 − (1/2) × 8 × 8 × sin(3) be a rash miss?

Hint 1: The arc form

A = (1/2)r s = 12. That is the sector, and that is the job named.

Hint 2: The miss

Subtracting a triangle would be a segment, and sin(3) would also treat 3 as if it were already the angle in radians, which it is not: θ = s/r = 3/8.

Check

  2. On an invented figure, r = 2 and θ = π/2, what is the segment area?
