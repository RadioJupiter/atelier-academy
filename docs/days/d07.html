Latin · Capitulum III, part 2: verbs and pronouns

30 min · Ørberg, Familia Romana, Cap. III

Read aloud twice the middle third of Capitulum III.

Notice: three verb types in the third person singular: -at (vocat, pulsat, cantat), -et (videt, rīdet, respondet), -it (audit, venit, dormit). Pronouns: is, ea (he, she) become eum, eam as objects; questions ask quis? (who?) and quem? (whom?). Cūr? (why?) is answered with quia (because).

Exercises

  1. Replace the object with a pronoun: (a) Dominus servum vocat. (b) Puer ancillam videt. (c) Puella puerum audit.
  2. Ask the question that the underlined word answers: (a) Servus dominum videt. (b) Puella cantat. (c) Puer plōrat quia ancilla eum pulsat.
  3. Answer: Cūr servus dormit? Write a reason with quia (invent a sensible one).

Mathematics · Algebra III: inequalities and the mean

60 min · Gelfand & Shen, Algebra; the classical inequality between means

Rules. You may add the same number to both sides of an inequality, or multiply both sides by a positive number. Multiplying by a negative number reverses the sign.

Quadratic inequalities are solved from the roots and the shape of the graph: x² − 5x + 6 = (x − 2)(x − 3) < 0 exactly when x lies between the roots.

The arithmetic mean–geometric mean inequality (two numbers). For a, b ≥ 0: (a + b)/2 ≥ √(ab), with equality only when a = b. Proof. (√a − √b)² ≥ 0, since a square is never negative. Expand: a − 2√(ab) + b ≥ 0. ∎

Use it to optimise. Among rectangles of perimeter 40, which has the largest area? Sides a and b with a + b = 20: √(ab) ≤ 10, so ab ≤ 100, with equality when a = b = 10. The square wins.

Exercises

  1. Solve: (a) 3x − 5 > 7 (b) −2x + 1 ≤ 9 (c) x² − 5x + 6 < 0 (d) x² ≥ 9.
  2. Prove a² + b² ≥ 2ab for all real a, b.
  3. Prove x + 1/x ≥ 2 for all x > 0. When does equality hold?
  4. A frame is made from 2 m of moulding. What are the dimensions of the largest-area rectangular frame?
  5. Which is larger, √7 + √10 or √3 + √19? Decide without a calculator.
  6. (Stretch.) Prove that (a + b)(b + c)(c + a) ≥ 8abc for positive a, b, c.

Science · Phenomenon: why is the sky blue and the sunset red?

45 min · Phenomenon-based investigation

The phenomenon. On a clear day the sky is blue overhead; near sunset the Sun turns orange-red. Clouds are white. On the Moon the daytime sky is black.

Model. Sunlight contains all visible wavelengths (about 400 nm violet to 700 nm red). Air molecules are much smaller than these wavelengths, and they scatter light with an intensity proportional to 1/λ⁴ (Rayleigh scattering): short wavelengths are scattered far more than long ones. Light scattered toward you from all over the sky is therefore rich in short wavelengths. At sunset, sunlight crosses far more atmosphere to reach you, so most of the blue is scattered out of the direct beam, leaving orange and red. Cloud droplets are much larger than the wavelength and scatter all colours about equally, so clouds look white.

Experiment (15 min). Fill a clear glass or jar with water and stir in a few drops of milk. In a dark room, shine a flashlight through the side. Look at the glass from the side, then look through it toward the flashlight. Add milk drop by drop and repeat.

Questions

  1. Describe the colours you saw from the side and looking through toward the light. Connect each to the sky and the sunset.
  2. Using the 1/λ⁴ law, how many times more strongly is 450 nm blue light scattered than 700 nm red light?
  3. If violet (400 nm) scatters even more than blue, why is the sky not violet? Give two reasons.
  4. Why is the daytime sky on the Moon black?

Literature · Iliad, Books 4–6

45 min · Read Books 4–6 before this lesson

Book 4. The gods decide to break the truce; Athena prompts the archer Pandarus to wound Menelaus, and the battle resumes. Book 5. Diomedes, inspired by Athena, dominates the battlefield (his aristeia, a hero's "best moment") and even wounds Aphrodite and Ares. Book 6. Hector returns to Troy. He asks his mother to pray to Athena, rebukes Paris, refuses to rest with Helen, and meets his wife Andromache and their infant son at the Scaean Gate. The child is frightened by Hector's helmet with its horsehair crest; Hector laughs, takes it off, and prays that his son may one day be greater than his father.

Questions

  1. Why place the scene of Hector and Andromache in the middle of so much killing? What does it change for the reader?
  2. Andromache asks Hector to stay on the wall and fight defensively. What is his answer and what does it reveal about honour (aidōs, shame before others)?
  3. Compare Hector and Achilles so far: what does each fight for?
  4. Copy (exactly, as copywork) the passage of the helmet and the child from your translation. Then explain one detail in it that makes the scene vivid.

Atelier · Cast drawing 1: set-up and block-in

90 min

Cast drawing is the bridge between copying plates and drawing from life: a white plaster cast holds still, shows form without the distraction of colour, and can be lit consistently for days. Traditionally the casts are of classical sculpture (heads, hands, feet, ornament).

What to use. A plaster cast of a simple subject (a plaster ear, nose, hand or ornament, which art suppliers sell) is ideal. If you do not have one, use a white matte object with clear planes and curves: a white ceramic jug, or a white-painted wooden block and sphere together.

Set-up. Single light from above and to one side; dark backdrop behind the cast; your board beside the cast for sight-size; your viewing spot marked on the floor. Light and position must not change for the whole study, so photograph the set-up.

Exercises

  1. (15 min) Set up and photograph the arrangement. Make two small two-value thumbnails to choose the view.
  2. (65 min) Sight-size block-in: overall height and width first, envelope, plumb alignments, then the shadow shapes as lines. No tone.
  3. (10 min) Mirror check and a measured check of three proportions.

Modern language · Spanish: tener

25 min

tener (to have): tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tenéis, tienen. Spanish uses tener for many states where English uses "to be": tener hambre (be hungry), tener sed (thirsty), tener frío / calor (cold / hot), tener sueño (sleepy), tener razón (be right), tener miedo (be afraid). Tener que + infinitive = to have to: Tengo que estudiar.

Exercises

  1. Translate: (a) We are hungry. (b) Are you (tú) cold? (c) She is right. (d) They have to paint today. (e) I have three brushes (pinceles).
  2. Complete with the correct form of tener: (a) Yo ___ un cuaderno nuevo. (b) Ustedes ___ que practicar. (c) ¿Tú ___ sed?

Music theory · The major scale and key signatures

30 min · Seminar rotation: Music theory

The major scale follows the step pattern W W H W W W H (whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half). From C on the white keys it needs no sharps or flats: C D E F G A B C. Starting on any other note, sharps (♯, raise a half step) or flats (♭, lower a half step) are needed to keep the pattern.

Key signatures. Sharps are added in the order F C G D A E B; flats in the order B E A D G C F. G major has one sharp (F♯); D major two (F♯, C♯). F major has one flat (B♭); B♭ major two (B♭, E♭). Each letter name appears exactly once in a major scale.

Exercises

  1. Write the scales of G major, E major and B♭ major, marking the half steps.
  2. How many sharps are in A major? Name them.
  3. A key signature has three flats. Which major key is it? (Hint: in flat keys, the second-to-last flat names the key.)
