Latin · The pluperfect of ēgredior, a first look

40 min · Method: ēgressus eram, the same deponent pluperfect pattern; original sentences

Learn

Key terms

pluperfect of ēgredior
Definition
The pluperfect of ēgredior is formed in the same way: the perfect participle ēgressus plus the imperfect of sum. The six forms are ēgressus eram, ēgressus erās, ēgressus erat, ēgressī erāmus, ēgressī erātis, ēgressī erant: I had gone out, you had gone out, and so on. It is not the present ēgredior, and it is not secūtus eram.
Example
Puella ēgressa erat, tum tacēbat: she had gone out, then she was silent.
Not this
Not ēgredior, I go out. Not ēgressus sum, I went out or I have gone out. Not secūtus eram, I had followed.

Yesterday’s secūtus eram, and last week’s present ēgredior.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXVI: the area of a triangle as (1/2)ab sin C, a first look

40 min · Method: area = (1/2)ab sin C for two sides and the included angle; invented lengths; degrees only

Learn

Key terms

area of a triangle as (1/2)ab sin C
Definition
The area of a triangle is (1/2)ab sin C, where C is the included angle between sides a and b. The same area may also be written (1/2)bc sin A or (1/2)ac sin B. The sine is the one already taught, in degrees, on the unit circle or in a right triangle. When C is 90°, sin 90° = 1, and the formula becomes the already-taught (1/2)ab.
Example
An invented triangle with sides 6 and 6 and included angle 60° has area (1/2) × 6 × 6 × sin 60° = 18 × (√3/2) = 9√3. The lengths are invented, not official measurements of a room.
Not this
Not the area of a rectangle, length times width, as if the included angle were irrelevant. Not a sine of a side. Not the law of sines, which is tomorrow’s first look. Radians remain reserved.

Last term’s area of a triangle with a perpendicular height, last week’s sine, and today’s related angles.

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • related angles on the unit circle — Day 131

Last term, the area of a triangle was (1/2) × base × height. Today that height is written with a sine, so the area can be found from two sides and the included angle. In triangle ABC, drop a perpendicular from C to side c, or, more directly, take the included angle C between sides a and b. The height to side b is a sin C when C is the included angle, so the area is (1/2)ab sin C. Likewise the area is (1/2)bc sin A, or (1/2)ac sin B. When C is 90°, sin 90° = 1, and the formula is the already-taught (1/2)ab. An invented check, labeled invented: sides 6 and 6, included angle 60°, area (1/2) × 6 × 6 × (√3/2) = 9√3. A second invented check: the already-taught 30°-60°-90° triangle with hypotenuse 2, so the legs are 1 and √3, and the included right angle is 90°. Area (1/2) × 1 × √3 × sin 90° = √3/2. Radians remain reserved. The law of sines waits until tomorrow. Invent no official length of a classroom wall.

Remember

Seminar · Music: rondo form, A–B–A–C–A, a first look

40 min · Method: A–B–A–C–A on a short invented figure in scale degrees, not a copyrighted score

Learn

Key terms

rondo form
Definition
Rondo form, in this first look, is a musical shape in which a first strain A returns after each contrasting strain: A–B–A–C–A. The returns of A are the same A, or a close written-out repeat of it, not a new strain. The idea is taught on a short invented figure in scale degrees, not on a copyrighted score.
Example
An invented twenty-bar figure, labeled invented: four bars of A, 1–3–5–3; four of B, 2–4–6–4; the same four of A; four of C, 5–4–3–1; then the same four of A again, is rondo form in this sense.
Not this
Not last week’s ternary form, which is A–B–A only, without a second contrasting strain C and a further return of A. Not binary form, A then B. Not a copyrighted tune quoted as the example. A longer rondo, or a varied return, remains reserved.
Connects to
Last week’s ternary form, and the week-before-last’s binary form. A period already taught may sit inside a strain, but that is not required today.

Last week’s music lesson treated ternary form, A–B–A, on a short invented twelve-bar figure. Today a first look at rondo form, A–B–A–C–A. After a first strain A, a contrasting B, and a return of A, a second contrasting strain C appears, and A returns again. An invented twenty-bar figure, labeled invented and written only in scale degrees, not as a copyrighted tune: four bars of A, 1–3–5–3; four of B, 2–4–6–4; the same four of A; four of C, 5–4–3–1; then the same four of A again. B still starts on a different degree, as last week; C starts higher still, and is not a copy of B. The returns of A are heard as a coming-back, not as new starts. Do not quote a copyrighted melody as the example. A longer rondo, or a varied return of A, remains reserved.

Remember

Rondo form is A–B–A–C–A: a first strain, a contrast, a return, a second contrast, and a further return of A. Today’s invented twenty-bar figure, in scale degrees only, is not a copyrighted score.

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. What is rondo form, in this first look, and how does it differ from last week’s ternary form?
  2. Describe the invented twenty-bar figure in scale degrees, and say why it is not a copyrighted score.
  3. What would be a miss, and what remains reserved?
  4. For each of today's key terms (rondo form), write one sentence in your own words and one near-miss you must not confuse it with. Then apply one of the terms to a fresh case of your own (not the example on the card).

Exercises

  1. Why is each return of A not a new strain?
  2. How can a musical period sit inside a strain, and is that required today?
  3. Why must you not quote a copyrighted melody as the classroom example?
