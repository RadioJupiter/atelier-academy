Mathematics · Geometry XLIX: the surface area of a sphere, and mixed solids

45 min · Method: A = 4πr² from the same OpenStax Appendix C table, then mixed volumes of the pyramid, cone, and sphere with last week’s prism and cylinder

Learn

Key terms

surface area of a sphere
Definition
The surface area of a sphere of radius r is A = 4πr², in square units. OpenStax, College Physics 2e, Appendix C, Table C9, lists this formula beside the volume (CC BY). Exact answers stay in terms of π.
Example
A classroom sphere of radius 3 has surface area A = 4π × 9 = 36π square units, and volume 36π cubic units. The numerals may match; the units and the formulas do not. The 3 is invented.
Not this
Not a first look at yesterday’s volume (4/3)πr³, and not last week’s circle area πr² taken as the sphere’s surface. 4πr² is four times the area of a great circle, but it is still an area, not a volume.
Connects to
The two OpenStax listings are kept labeled and are not blended. A classroom case where the numerals match is a caution, not a general rule.

Science · Fusion and sublimation at 0 °C, compared, from the same table

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • enthalpy of sublimation of ice — Day 92

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Tuesday’s ΔsublH(0 °C) = 2834 J/g and last week’s ΔfusH(0 °C) = 333.4 J/g are both from the same Harvey/NIST table, at 101.325 kPa. Today is mixed use, not a new official number.

Melting 1 g at 0 °C takes 333.4 J. Subliming 1 g at 0 °C takes 2834 J, about 8.50 times as much. Last week’s ice cp, 2.10 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹ at 0 °C, may be used only as a contrast for a 1 °C warming of ice, not as a substitute for either phase-transition figure.

Do not treat 2834 − 333.4 as a 100 °C boiling enthalpy. Invented masses stay labeled invented.

“My son,” in Inferno III
Definition
In Longfellow III.121–129 the guide says, “My son,” and explains that those who perish in the wrath of God meet here out of every land, ready to pass over because celestial Justice spurs them, so that fear is turned into desire. “This way there never passes a good soul; / And hence if Charon doth complain of thee, / Well mayst thou know now what his speech imports.” In this course, “My son” is the guide’s address to the speaker in the poem, not a personal identification of a living pupil.

Not this

Elective · Economics: simple interest on a declining principal (invented figures)

40 min · Method: invented simple interest computed each year on the principal still outstanding, after a labeled invented principal repayment. Last week’s equal installments are a contrast, not a first look

Learn

Key terms

simple interest on a declining principal
Definition
Simple interest on a declining principal, in this lesson, means that each year’s interest is computed with the already-taught simple-interest formula, but on the principal still outstanding after a labeled invented principal repayment. The rate and the money figures in this lesson are invented and labeled invented, not a bank’s official plan.
Example
An invented $300 principal at an invented 10% per year has $30 interest in year 1. A $100 principal repayment at year end leaves $200 outstanding, so year 2’s interest is $20. The interest itself is paid as well, in this school account, and is listed separately from the principal repayment.
Not this
Not a first look at simple interest, or at last week’s equal installment repayment of a known total (principal plus an already-computed add-on charge). Not a compound-interest schedule, and not a bank’s official APR. Not a monthly declining-balance card plan, which would need a method not taught here and is reserved.
Connects to
Last week split a known invented total into equal payments. Today the principal itself falls, and the next year’s simple interest is computed on what remains.

Simple interest is already taught. Last week took a known invented total and split it into equal installments. Today is a different school job, still on invented figures: each year’s interest is simple interest on the principal still outstanding, after a labeled invented principal repayment.

Invented example, labeled invented: a principal of $300, at 10% per year. Year 1 interest is 0.10 × 300 = $30. A principal repayment of $100 is then made, and the $30 interest is paid as well, in this account. The outstanding principal is $200. Year 2 interest is 0.10 × 200 = $20.

This is not last week’s equal split of a fixed add-on total, and it is not compound interest, which would add unpaid interest to the principal. A monthly bank plan and an official APR remain reserved. All classroom money figures here are invented.
