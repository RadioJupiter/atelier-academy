Latin · Secūtus eram and ēgressus eram, mixed with already-taught deponent tenses

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • pluperfect of ēgredior — Day 132

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Put this week’s pluperfects beside last week’s presents and the already-taught tenses of loquor. Sequor is I follow; secūtus sum, I followed or have followed; secūtus eram, I had followed. Ēgredior, ēgressus sum, ēgressus eram. Loquor, locūtus sum, locūtus eram, and loquēbar remain available. Choose the tense from the time: nunc asks for the present; a later past action in the same sentence often asks for the pluperfect. Sentences stay original, not copied from Ørberg.

Remember

Sequor, secūtus sum, and secūtus eram are present, perfect, and pluperfect. Ēgredior follows the same three-tense pattern. Locūtus eram is still I had spoken.

Watch me

  2. Ēgressus erat: the going out was already completed before he was silent.
  3. Do not put a pluperfect with nunc, or a present with anteā and a later past action.

Mathematics · Geometry LXXVII: the law of sines, a first look

40 min · Method: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C, from yesterday’s area formula; checked on an invented 30°-60°-90° triangle

Learn

Key terms

the law of sines
Definition
The law of sines says that in any triangle, a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C, where side a is opposite angle A, side b opposite B, and side c opposite C. It follows from yesterday’s area formula: (1/2)bc sin A = (1/2)ac sin B, so b sin A = a sin B, and therefore a/sin A = b/sin B. The same steps give the third ratio. The sines are still in degrees. Radians remain reserved.
Example
An invented 30°-60°-90° triangle with hypotenuse 2 has sides 1, √3, and 2. Then 1/sin 30° = 1/(1/2) = 2, and 2/sin 90° = 2/1 = 2, and √3/sin 60° = √3/(√3/2) = 2. The three ratios are equal, as the law says. The 2 is invented, not an official length.
Not this
Not a claim that the sides themselves are equal. Not the law of cosines, which remains reserved. Not a first look at the ambiguous case, when two sides and a non-included angle are given, which remains reserved. Radians remain reserved.
Connects to
Yesterday’s area (1/2)ab sin C, last week’s exact sines, and the already-taught 30°-60°-90° triangle.

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • area of a triangle as (1/2)ab sin C — Day 132

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Yesterday the area of a triangle was written three ways: (1/2)bc sin A = (1/2)ac sin B = (1/2)ab sin C. Cancel (1/2) from the first two, and bc sin A = ac sin B. Cancel the common side c, and b sin A = a sin B. Divide by sin A sin B, and a/sin A = b/sin B. The same comparison with the third area form gives a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C. That is the law of sines, in any triangle, still in degrees.

A first check on an already-taught right triangle, with invented hypotenuse 2, labeled invented: the sides opposite 30°, 60°, and 90° are 1, √3, and 2. Then 1/sin 30° = 2, √3/sin 60° = 2, and 2/sin 90° = 2. The three ratios are equal, so the law agrees with last term’s 30°-60°-90° relations. A non-right triangle waits until tomorrow. The ambiguous case, sometimes called SSA, remains reserved, as does the law of cosines. Radians remain reserved. Invent no official length.

Remember

In any triangle, a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C. It follows from (1/2)bc sin A = (1/2)ac sin B. On an invented 30°-60°-90° triangle with hypotenuse 2, each ratio equals 2.

Physics · The speed of sound, frequency, and wavelength, from OpenStax 17.2

40 min · Method: vw = fλ, the book’s air formula, Table 17.1, and official Example 17.1 (348.7 m/s, 17 m, 1.7 cm)

Learn

Key terms

speed of sound
Definition
In OpenStax 17.2, the speed of sound, frequency, and wavelength are related by the already-taught wave equation vw = fλ. Perception of frequency is called pitch. For air at sea level, the book gives vw = 331 m/s × √(T/273 K), with T in kelvin. At 0°C the official table value is 331 m/s; at 20.0°C the book gives 343 m/s. Official Example 17.1 uses 30.0°C air, taken as 303 K, and finds vw = 348.7 m/s, then λ = 17 m at 20 Hz and λ = 0.017 m, or 1.7 cm, at 20,000 Hz, the extremes of the audible range, with those frequencies accurate to two significant figures.
Example
The book’s official Example 17.1: in 30.0°C air, vw = 331 m/s × √(303/273) = 348.7 m/s; at 20 Hz, λmax = 17 m; at 20,000 Hz, λmin = 1.7 cm.
Not this
Not an official speed of light, which is not being invented. Not last week’s intensity. Not a claim that the speed of sound is the same in every medium; Table 17.1 gives official values that differ, such as 1480 m/s in fresh water at 20°C and 5960 m/s in steel. Decibels remain reserved.
Connects to
Monday’s sound as a longitudinal pressure wave, and the already-taught relation vw = fλ.

OpenStax 17.2, “Speed of Sound, Frequency, and Wavelength,” continues Monday’s first look. Access for free at the OpenStax 17.2 page. The flash of fireworks is seen before the sound is heard, “implying both that sound travels at a finite speed and that it is much slower than light.” Perception of frequency is called pitch. The wave equation is the one already taught: vw = fλ. The speed of sound depends on the medium: “the more rigid (or less compressible) the medium, the faster the speed of sound.” Table 17.1, official values, includes air at 0°C, 331 m/s; fresh water at 20°C, 1480 m/s; and steel, 5960 m/s. For air at sea level, the book gives vw = 331 m/s × √(T/273 K), with T in kelvin. “At 0°C, the speed of sound is 331 m/s, whereas at 20.0°C it is 343 m/s, less than a 4% increase.” The speed “is nearly independent of frequency” in open air for sounds in the audible range, 20 to 20,000 Hz.

Official Example 17.1: wavelengths at the extremes of the audible range, 20 and 20,000 Hz, in 30.0°C air, with those frequencies accurate to two significant figures. T = 303 K, and vw = 331 m/s × √(303/273) = 348.7 m/s. Then λmax = 348.7 m/s ÷ 20 Hz = 17 m, and λmin = 348.7 m/s ÷ 20,000 Hz = 0.017 m = 1.7 cm. An invented practice frequency, labeled invented, may use the book’s official 343 m/s at 20.0°C: if f = 686 Hz, invented, then λ = 343 / 686 = 0.500 m. Invent no official speed of light, and no official living air temperature other than the book’s. Decibels remain reserved.

Remember

Vw = fλ, as for all waves. In air at sea level, vw = 331 m/s × √(T/273 K). Example 17.1: in 30.0°C air, vw = 348.7 m/s, λ = 17 m at 20 Hz and 1.7 cm at 20,000 Hz. Decibels remain reserved.

Watch me

  2. λmax = 348.7 / 20 = 17 m; λmin = 348.7 / 20,000 = 0.017 m = 1.7 cm.
  3. The frequencies are given as accurate to two significant figures, which is why the wavelengths are written 17 m and 1.7 cm.

Hint 2: The medium

History · The Treaty of Tordesillas: lands east and west of the line

40 min · Method: east of the 370-league line to Portugal, west to Castile and León, forever, in Davenport’s wording; no official conversion

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • Treaty of Tordesillas (June 7, 1494) — Day 131

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Monday’s line is now followed to the grant of the two sides, still in Davenport’s 1917 translation. Lands “on the eastern side of the said bound,” found or to be found by the King of Portugal and his vessels, “shall belong to, and remain in the possession of, and pertain forever to, the said King of Portugal and his successors,” provided the bound is not crossed. Lands “on the western side of the said bound,” found or to be found by the King and Queen of Castile, Aragon, etc., and their vessels, “shall belong to, and remain in the possession of, and pertain forever to, the said King and Queen of Castile, Leon, etc., and to their successors.” A later clause says that if Castile’s ships, sailing on Portugal’s side of the bound, should discover mainlands or islands there, those lands “shall pertain to and belong forever to the said King of Portugal and his heirs.” Do not convert the 370 leagues. The first-voyage letter remains reserved.

Remember

East of the 370-league line, lands found or to be found by Portugal pertain forever to the King of Portugal and his successors. West of the line, lands found or to be found by Castile pertain forever to the King and Queen of Castile, Leon, etc., and their successors.

The "Not this" line on the card names a common mix-up. Explain why that mix-up would change an answer in this lesson.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. What church does Dewing I.3.10 name, and by what later name is the place called?
  2. What does the same passage say about St. Anna, about Zoê, and about the shrine of Michael?
  3. What official figure must you not invent, and what remains reserved?

Exercises
