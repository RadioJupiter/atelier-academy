Latin · Friday mix: the ablative absolute, deponents, and fiō

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • ablative absolute — Day 91
  • deponent present participle in the absolute — Day 93

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Today is a mix, not a new chapter topic. Close the cards. Be ready to write a present-participle ablative absolute, including a deponent participle, and to use loquitur, ēgreditur, sequitur, cōnātur, fit, and fiunt in a main clause. Perfect-participle absolutes and first-person deponents remain reserved.

Remember

An ablative absolute, in this first look, is a noun plus a present participle, both ablative. Deponent participles stay active in meaning. Fiō is a different verb. The perfect-participle absolute is reserved.

Watch me

Watch one mixed sentence and name its parts.

  1. Puerō loquente, discipulī ē lūdō ēgrediuntur et tacitī fiunt may be split into clear sentences if too heavy. A clean model: Puerō loquente, discipulī ēgrediuntur.
  2. Puerō loquente is the absolute, deponent participle, active.
  3. Ēgrediuntur is a deponent main verb. A reserved perfect-participle phrase was not needed.

Together

Build one sentence with an absolute and one deponent or fiō form in the main clause.

Hint 1: The phrase

Noun and present participle, both ablative.

Hint 2: Active English

Loquente is “speaking,” not “being spoken.”

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Today is a mix. Close the cards. Use V = (1/3)Bh for a right pyramid, V = (1/3)πr²h for a right cone, V = (4/3)πr³ and A = 4πr² for a sphere, and last week’s V = B × h or V = πr²h when a prism or cylinder is named. Exact answers with π stay in terms of π. The sphere formulas are from OpenStax, College Physics 2e, Appendix C.

Remember

Pyramid V = (1/3)Bh. Cone V = (1/3)πr²h. Sphere V = (4/3)πr³ and A = 4πr². A prism or cylinder of the same base and height is three times the pyramid or cone. Keep area and volume distinct.

  2. Cone r = 3, h = 9: V = 27π cubic units; matching cylinder 81π.
  3. Sphere r = 3: V = 36π cubic units; A = 36π square units — same numerals, different units.

Hint 1: The cone

V = (1/3)π × 36 × 2 = 24π cubic units.

Hint 2: The cylinder

72π cubic units, three times as much.

Elective · Anatomy: the skull, from Gray 1918 — cranium and face

40 min · Method: Gray’s division of the skull into a cranium of eight bones and a face of fourteen, with the eight cranial names required, and the mandible as the movable exception

Learn

Key terms

the skull, cranium and face (Gray)
Definition
Gray, 1918, writes: “The skull is supported on the summit of the vertebral column, and is of an oval shape, wider behind than in front. It is composed of a series of flattened or irregular bones which, with one exception (the mandible), are immovably jointed together. It is divisible into two parts: (1) the cranium, which lodges and protects the brain, consists of eight bones, and (2) the skeleton of the face, of fourteen.” The eight cranial bones he then names are the occipital, the two parietals, the frontal, the two temporals, the sphenoidal, and the ethmoidal.
Example
The twenty-two bones are eight plus fourteen. The mandible is the one exception that is not immovably jointed. The hyoid, which Gray mentions in this section, is not one of the twenty-two.
Not this
Not a first look at last week’s eight carpals, or at the earlier carpus as a group, though those lists may be contrasted as already-taught bone names in the hand. Not a Basle regrouping of some nasal bones as cranial, which Gray notes and which is a gloss only. Not a required recitation of every facial name on this first day, beyond the count of fourteen and the mandible as the movable exception; the fourteen as Gray lists them may be read from the table.
Connects to
Henry Gray, Anatomy of the Human Body, 1918, “5. The Skull,” as in the source note.

Gray, 1918, places the skull on the summit of the vertebral column, oval, wider behind than in front, of bones “which, with one exception (the mandible), are immovably jointed together,” and divisible into a cranium of eight bones and a face of fourteen.

The eight cranial bones, in Gray’s list: occipital; two parietals; frontal; two temporals; sphenoidal; ethmoidal.

The fourteen facial bones, in Gray’s table, may be read: two nasals, two maxillae, two lacrimals, two zygomatics, two palatines, two inferior nasal conchae, vomer, and mandible. Today’s required work is the division 8 + 14 = 22, the eight cranial names, and the mandible as the movable exception. The full fourteen need not be recited from memory on this first day, but they should be recognized from Gray’s list. The hyoid is not a twenty-third skull bone in that count. A Basle regrouping that Gray mentions is a gloss only.

Gray’s skull has twenty-two bones: a cranium of eight and a face of fourteen. The mandible is the movable exception. The eight cranial names are required today. The hyoid is not one of the twenty-two. Last week’s carpals are a contrast, not a first look.
