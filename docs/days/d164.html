Latin · A tighter mix of purpose clauses with ut and nē

40 min · Method: tighter mix of ut and nē with already-taught tenses; result clauses reserved

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • a purpose clause with nē, a first look — Day 161

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

The same purpose clauses can now be kept distinct from already-taught tenses in a tighter mix. Nē eum sequātur is still a negative purpose, not the future sequētur, and not a present indicative already taught. Ut ēgrediātur is still a positive purpose, not ēgrediētur. A result clause remains reserved. The sentences remain original.

Remember

Keep ut and nē, the present subjunctive, and the futures distinct. Do not let an already-taught present indicative take the place of the subjunctive in a purpose clause. A result clause remains reserved.

Watch me

Contrast nē ēgrediātur with ēgrediētur, and contrast ut eum sequātur with a present already taught, in short original sentences with glosses.

  1. Puer sedet nē ēgrediātur, he sits in order that he not go out; Puer ēgrediētur, he will go out.
  2. Puella venit ut eum sequātur is a purpose. A present such as Puella sequitur, she follows, is not a purpose clause.

Together

Why is “Puer sedet nē ēgrediētur” a miss, and why is a result clause still reserved?

Hint 1: The ending

The nē-clause takes ēgrediātur, the present subjunctive, not the future ēgrediētur.

Hint 2: The job

A result clause is a different job and remains reserved.

Check

  1. Give one original ut-clause and one original nē-clause, each with a gloss, and name the mood of the verb in each.
  2. Why is ēgrediētur a miss in “Puer sedet nē ___”?
  3. Why is a result clause still a miss?

Mathematics · Algebra / analysis: a tighter mix of the limit of a sum, a product, and a quotient

40 min · Method: mix this week’s three limit rules on invented functions; the quotient rule requires M ≠ 0; continuity reserved

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • the limit of a sum, a first look — Day 161
  • the limit of a product, a first look — Day 162
  • the limit of a quotient, a first look — Day 163

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This week’s three rules now stand together. If lim f = L and lim g = M, then lim (f + g) = L + M, lim (f g) = L M, and, if M ≠ 0, lim (f / g) = L / M. On the invented functions f(x) = 2x and g(x) = x + 1, as x approaches 3, those limits are 10, 24, and 3/2. If the denominator’s limit is 0, the quotient rule does not apply. Continuity remains reserved.

Remember

lim (f + g) = L + M, lim (f g) = L M, and, if M ≠ 0, lim (f / g) = L / M. On the invented functions as x approaches 3, those results are 10, 24, and 3/2. If M = 0, the quotient rule does not apply.

Watch me

  1. lim (f + g) = 10, lim (f g) = 24, lim (f / g) = 3/2.
  2. The quotient rule requires that lim g = 4 ≠ 0.

Spanish · The present perfect with visto, beside already-taught tenses

40 min · Method: keep the present perfect with visto distinct from already-taught tenses; hay and haya still distinct

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • the irregular participle visto, a first look — Day 161

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

The present perfect with visto still has to be kept distinct from already-taught tenses. He visto is I have seen, not the present veo, I see, and not a preterite already taught. Ha visto is not hay. Hemos visto is not vemos alone, without the helping verb, if the job is the present perfect. Haya remains “that there be.” Other irregular participles remain reserved.

Remember

He visto, ha visto, and hemos visto are present-perfect forms. They are not the already-taught present, not a preterite used in place of the participle, not hay, and not haya.

Watch me

Contrast ha visto with ve, and contrast ha visto with hay, in short original sentences with glosses.

  1. A fair contrast: Ella ve la carta, she sees the letter, or she is looking at it; Ella ha visto la carta, she has seen the letter.
  2. Hay una carta, there is a letter, is not Ella ha visto la carta, she has seen the letter.
  3. Do not use hay as a helping form with visto.

Together

Why is “Yo veo” a miss if the job is he visto, and why is haya a miss if the job is ha visto?

Hint 1: The present

Veo is the already-taught present, I see. He visto is the present perfect, I have seen.

Hint 2: Haya

Haya is the present subjunctive, “that there be,” not the helping form ha.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. Contrast he visto with the already-taught present veo, with glosses.
  2. Why is “Ella hay visto la carta” a miss?
  3. Why is a new irregular participle beyond visto still a miss this week?

Exercises

  1. Write three short original sentences: one present, one present perfect with visto, and one present perfect with dicho, hecho, or escrito, each with a gloss.
  2. Choose: Yo ___ (he / hay / haya) visto la carta.
  3. What remains reserved?
