Latin · Result clauses, reviewed

40 min · Term 3 review of already-taught result clauses with ut and with ut nōn · original sentences · no new construction

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • a result clause with ut, a first look — Day 166
  • a negative result with ut nōn, a first look — Day 167

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Yesterday reviewed purpose clauses. Today reviews already-taught result clauses. A result clause marks what happens as a result, often with tam, ita, or sic: ut for a positive result, ut nōn for a negative result, not nē. Puella tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur: she comes so quickly that she follows him. Puella tam lentē venit ut eum nōn sequātur: she comes so slowly that she does not follow him. The verb is still the present subjunctive, not the future. No new construction. Original classroom sentences only.

Word How to say it Meaning
ut eum sequātur oot EH-oom seh-KWAH-toor so that she follows him, in a result clause
ut eum nōn sequātur oot EH-oom nohn seh-KWAH-toor so that she does not follow him
nē eum sequātur nay EH-oom seh-KWAH-toor in order that she not follow him, a purpose, not a result

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Remember

A result clause uses ut, or ut nōn when negative, often with tam, ita, or sic. Nē still marks a negative purpose, not a result.

Watch me

Give one original positive result and one original negative result with sequātur, and translate them.

  1. Puella tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur: she comes so quickly that she follows him.
  2. Puella tam lentē venit ut eum nōn sequātur: she comes so slowly that she does not follow him.
  3. The negative is ut nōn, not nē.

Together

Why is nē the wrong negative in a result clause?

Hint 1: The jobs

Nē marks a negative purpose.

Hint 2: The result

A negative result uses ut nōn.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. Translate and label: Puella tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur. Puella tam lentē venit ut eum nōn sequātur. Puer tam celeriter surgit ut ēgrediātur.
  2. Why is nē a miss in the second sentence?
  3. How can you tell a result clause from a purpose clause?

Exercises

  1. Write two original result sentences, one positive and one negative, and translate them.
  2. Write one original negative purpose with nē, and say why it is not a result.

Mathematics · Sine, cosine, and tangent, reviewed

40 min · Term 3 review of already-taught right-triangle ratios · invented lengths · no new theorem

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • sine and cosine at the axis angles — Day 127
  • the law of sines — Day 133

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Today reviews already-taught sine, cosine, and tangent in a right triangle. No new theorem. Sin A is opposite over hypotenuse, cos A is adjacent over hypotenuse, and tan A is opposite over adjacent. Also tan A = sin A / cos A, already taught. Classroom lengths are invented. In an invented 3-4-5 triangle, if A is opposite 3, sin A = 3/5, cos A = 4/5, and tan A = 3/4. Limits and the derivative wait until later this week.

Remember

Sin A = opposite/hypotenuse, cos A = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan A = opposite/adjacent, and tan A = sin A / cos A. In an invented 3-4-5 triangle, if A is opposite 3, tan A = 3/4.

Watch me

In an invented 3-4-5 triangle, with A opposite 3, give sin A, cos A, and tan A, and check that tan A = sin A / cos A.

  1. Sin A = 3/5, cos A = 4/5, tan A = 3/4.
  2. (3/5) / (4/5) = 3/4, so tan A = sin A / cos A.
  3. The lengths are invented.

Together

In an invented 5-12-13 triangle, with A opposite 5, what is tan A?

Hint 1: Opposite and adjacent

Opposite 5, adjacent 12.

Hint 2: The ratio

Tan A = 5/12.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key. The lengths are invented.

  1. Write the already-taught definitions of sin A, cos A, and tan A, and the already-taught relation tan A = sin A / cos A.
  2. In an invented 3-4-5 triangle, with A opposite 3, give the three ratios.
  3. In an invented 5-12-13 triangle, with A opposite 5, give sin A, cos A, and tan A.

Exercises

Official Example 20.8 uses a book example rate of 12 cents per kWh, not a named utility rate: 60 W for 1000 hours is 60.0 kWh, electricity $7.20, total $7.45 including a 25-cent bulb; a compact fluorescent light, official total $1.95 for 1000 hours ($1.80 + $0.15). Remember
