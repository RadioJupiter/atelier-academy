Mathematics · A short Term 2 mix: similarity and right-triangle ratios

40 min · Last Term 2 review: already-taught similar triangles together with sine, cosine, and tangent · degrees only · keep the pictures straight · no new theorem

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • similar triangles — Day 61
  • sine of an acute angle — Day 106
  • tangent of an acute angle — Day 111

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This is the last mathematics review before Friday’s examination. Keep a similarity-and-scale-factor picture separate from a right-triangle-ratio picture, even though the second was justified by the first. Degrees only. Classroom lengths are invented. The radian and the unit circle remain reserved.

Remember

Similar triangles keep corresponding sides in the ratio k. Sin A is opposite over hypotenuse, cos A is adjacent over hypotenuse, tan A is opposite over adjacent, and tan A = sin A / cos A. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, if A is opposite 3, tan A = 3/4. A similar 6-8-10 triangle has the same tan A.

Watch me

Watch a scale-factor item, then a ratio item, then a warning to keep the jobs separate.

  1. A 3-4-5 triangle scaled by k = 3 is 9-12-15.
  2. In that 9-12-15 triangle, if A is opposite 9, tan A = 9/12 = 3/4, the same tangent, not a new ratio.
  3. A miss is to treat the new sides as a new sine or tangent, or to invent an official degree measure.

Together

A classroom 5-12-13 triangle is enlarged by k = 2. What are the new sides, and what is tan of the angle opposite the side that grew from 5?

Hint 1: The sides

10, 24, and 26.

Hint 2: The tangent

The angle opposite 10, which corresponds to 5, still has tan = 10/24 = 5/12.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key. Classroom lengths are invented.

  1. In a classroom 3-4-5 triangle, A is opposite 3. Give sin A, cos A, tan A, and tan(90° − A).
  2. A classroom right triangle similar to that 3-4-5 triangle has hypotenuse 15. Give the other two sides and tan A.
  3. Why is tan A = 3/5 a miss in the 3-4-5 triangle?

Exercises

  1. In a classroom 5-12-13 triangle, D is opposite 12. Give sin D, cos D, and tan D, and check tan D against sin D / cos D.
  2. Why must the radian and the unit circle stay reserved in this mix?

Word How to say it Meaning

Remember

Seminar · A typical vertebra, and the column, reviewed

40 min · Review of already-taught Gray 1918: the count of 33, the four curves, and a typical vertebra · official lengths reserved

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • vertebral column, from Gray — Day 105
  • curves of the vertebral column, from Gray — Day 110
  • a typical vertebra, from Gray — Day 115

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This is review, from the already-set public-domain Gray via Theodora, not a first look. The column is formed of 33 vertebræ: 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, and 4 coccygeal. The four curves: cervical and lumbar convex forward, thoracic concave forward, pelvic concavity downward and forward; thoracic and pelvic primary, cervical and lumbar secondary or compensatory. A typical vertebra has a body and a vertebral or neural arch, enclosing the vertebral foramen; the arch has pedicles and laminæ, and seven processes: four articular, two transverse, and one spinous. Stacked bodies form a pillar; stacked foramina form a canal for the medulla spinalis (spinal cord); intervertebral foramina transmit nerves and vessels. Keep vertebræ and laminæ. Official 71 cm and 61 cm lengths, a slight lateral curvature, and regional special features remain reserved.

Remember

33 vertebræ, in the already-taught regional counts. Four curves, with official lengths reserved. A typical vertebra: body, arch, foramen, pedicles, laminæ, seven processes. Keep Gray’s spellings.

Watch me

  1. 33: 7, 12, 5, 5, and 4, as already taught.
  2. Four curves, primary and secondary as already taught. Official lengths reserved.
  3. Body and arch enclose the foramen; seven processes. Regional special features remain reserved.
