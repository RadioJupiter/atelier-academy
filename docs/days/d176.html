Latin · Purpose clauses, reviewed

40 min · Term 3 review of already-taught purpose clauses with ut and with nē · original sentences · no new construction

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • a purpose clause with ut, a first look — Day 156
  • a purpose clause with nē, a first look — Day 161

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This week reviews already-taught Term 3 Latin. No new construction. Today reviews purpose clauses. A purpose clause marks an intended purpose: ut for a positive purpose, nē for a negative purpose. The verb is the already-taught present subjunctive, not the future. Puella venit ut eum sequātur: she comes in order that she follow him. Puella manet nē eum sequātur: she stays in order that she not follow him. A result clause is tomorrow’s review, not today’s job. Original classroom sentences only. Identity is handled with Puer and Puella, and with he and she in the English gloss.

Word How to say it Meaning
ut eum sequātur oot EH-oom seh-KWAH-toor in order that she follow him, or in order that he follow him
nē eum sequātur nay EH-oom seh-KWAH-toor in order that she not follow him, or in order that he not follow him
sequētur seh-KWAY-toor she will follow, or he will follow

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Remember

A purpose clause uses ut, or nē when negative, and the present subjunctive, not the future. A result clause waits until tomorrow’s review.

Watch me

Give one original ut-clause and one original nē-clause with sequātur, and translate them.

  1. Puella venit ut eum sequātur: she comes in order that she follow him.
  2. Puella manet nē eum sequātur: she stays in order that she not follow him.
  3. Both use sequātur, not sequētur.

Together

Why is sequētur a miss in a purpose clause?

Hint 1: The form

Sequētur is the future, with ē.

Hint 2: The job

A purpose clause takes the present subjunctive sequātur, with ā.

Check

  1. Translate and label: Puella venit ut eum sequātur. Puella manet nē eum sequātur. Puer surgit ut ēgrediātur.
  2. Why is sequētur a miss?
  3. What job does a purpose clause mark, and what review waits until tomorrow?

Exercises

  2. Change Puer sedet nē ēgrediātur to a positive purpose, and translate both.

Mathematics · A line and a plane in space, reviewed

40 min · Term 3 review of already-taught space geometry · no new theorem · a formal epsilon argument reserved

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • a line and a plane in space, a first look — Day 121
  • a line perpendicular to a plane, a first look — Day 122

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Term 3 opened with space geometry. Today reviews already-taught relations of a line and a plane, and of a line perpendicular to a plane. No new theorem. In space, a line and a plane have three possible relations: the line may lie in the plane, meet the plane at exactly one point, or be parallel to the plane and never meet it. A line is perpendicular to a plane if it meets the plane at a point P, the foot, and is perpendicular to every line in the plane that passes through P. Later Term 3 work on trig, limits, continuity, and the derivative waits until later sittings this week.

Remember

A line and a plane may lie together, meet at one point, or be parallel. A line perpendicular to a plane is perpendicular to every line in the plane through the foot.

Watch me

State the three relations of a line and a plane, then the already-taught test for a perpendicular.

  1. The line may lie in the plane, meet it at exactly one point, or be parallel to it and never meet it.
  2. A perpendicular meets the plane at a foot P, and is perpendicular to every line in the plane through P.
  3. No new relation is added today.

Together

If a line meets a plane at one point and is not perpendicular to a line in the plane through that point, is it perpendicular to the plane?

Hint 1: The test

Perpendicular to the plane means perpendicular to every line in the plane through the foot.

Hint 2: Today

If it fails even one such line, it is not perpendicular to the plane. It may still meet the plane at exactly one point.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. State the three already-taught relations of a line and a plane in space.
  2. When is a line perpendicular to a plane?
  3. Why is a new space theorem a miss this week?

Exercises

  1. Give one example, in words, of a line that lies in a plane, and one of a line that is parallel to a plane.
  2. Why is “meets the plane at one point” not yet enough to say the line is perpendicular to the plane?

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Today reviews already-taught refraction. The index of refraction is n = c/v. Snell’s law is n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂. Official Example 25.3: air n₁ = 1.00, θ₁ = 30.0°, diamond n₂ = 2.419, sin θ₂ = (0.413)(0.500) = 0.207, θ₂ = 11.9°, official. Official Example 25.2 found n₂ = 1.33. Total internal reflection and official Example 25.4 wait until Wednesday’s review. Official figures are not invented.

Remember

Official Example 25.3 gives θ₂ = 11.9° for a ray from air into diamond, from official n₂ = 2.419. Official Example 25.2 found n₂ = 1.33.

Watch me

  3. θ₂ = 11.9°, official.

Already-taught present-perfect forms include he/has/ha/hemos/habéis/han visto, and he/has/ha/hemos/habéis/han puesto. They remain distinct from already-taught dicho, hecho, and escrito, and puesto remains distinct from hay and haya. Vuelto waits until tomorrow. The present-perfect subjunctive remains reserved. No new participle.

Word How to say it Meaning
visto VEES-toh seen, the already-taught irregular participle of ver
puesto PWEH-stoh put, or placed, the already-taught irregular participle of poner
he puesto eh PWEH-stoh I have put, or I have placed

If the six faces are equally likely, and the already-taught invented payoffs are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6, the already-taught expected value is (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6) / 6 = 21/6 = 7/2. No new term.
