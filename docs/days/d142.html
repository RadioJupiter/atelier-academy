Latin · The perfect of ēgredior, a first look

40 min · Method: ēgressus sum; original sentences, not Ørberg

Learn

Key terms

perfect of ēgredior
Definition
The perfect of ēgredior uses the same deponent perfect pattern: the participle ēgressus plus the present of sum. The six forms are ēgressus sum, ēgressus es, ēgressus est, ēgressī sumus, ēgressī estis, ēgressī sunt: I went out, or I have gone out, and so on. It is not the present ēgredior, not the future ēgrediar, and not the pluperfect ēgressus eram.
Example
Puella ēgressa est, tum tacēbat: she went out, or has gone out, then she was silent. The going out is completed, but is not marked as already completed before another past moment.
Not this
Not ēgredior, I go out. Not ēgrediar, I shall go out. Not ēgressus eram, I had gone out. Not secūtus sum, I followed.
Connects to
Yesterday’s perfect of sequor, and the already-taught present, future, and pluperfect of ēgredior.

Ēgredior already has a present, a future ēgrediar, and a pluperfect ēgressus eram. Today the perfect: ēgressus sum, I went out, or I have gone out; ēgressus es; ēgressus est; ēgressī sumus; ēgressī estis; ēgressī sunt. Puer ēgressus est is he went out, or he has gone out, completed, not still ahead, and not already completed before another past moment. Secūtus sum remains I followed. Sentences are original, not copied from Ørberg. Identity in a sentence is given with Puer or Puella, not with an unquoted English “boy” or “girl.”

Mathematics · Geometry LXXXIV: arc length in radians, s = rθ

40 min · Method: s = rθ when θ is in radians; invented figures labeled invented

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • the radian — Day 141

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

From the definition of a radian, the arc length s cut off by a central angle θ, when θ is measured in radians, is s = rθ. That is the definition rearranged: θ = s/r, so s = rθ. The already-taught degree form of arc length used a different conversion; today the radian form is used, and the angle must be in radians, not in degrees, before the product is taken.

On an invented circle of radius 3 with a central angle of 2 radians, s = 3 × 2 = 6. On an invented circle of radius 6 with a central angle of π/3 radians (that is, 60°), s = 6 × (π/3) = 2π. If an invented arc is π and the radius is 2, then θ = s/r = π/2 radians, which is 90°. The radii and the 2 radians are invented classroom numbers. Do not multiply a degree measure by the radius as if it were already in radians, and do not invent an official arc on a real wheel.

Remember

When θ is in radians, s = rθ, and θ = s/r. Convert a degree measure into radians before using the product. Label invented figures invented.

Watch me

  2. The radius 6 is an invented classroom number; 2π is exact.
  3. Do not compute 6 × 60, as if the angle were already in radians.

Hint 2: Degrees

2 radians is 2 × 180/π = 360/π degrees, which is not a special angle already on the unit circle, and may be left as 360/π degrees, or simply as 2 radians. Do not invent a rounded official degree measure.

Science · Electric current, a first look, from OpenStax 20.1

40 min · Method: I = ΔQ / Δt, 1 A = 1 C/s; official Example 20.1; Ohm’s law reserved

Learn

Key terms

electric current
Definition
Electric current, in OpenStax 20.1, is the rate at which charge flows: I = ΔQ / Δt. The SI unit is the ampere (A), named for André-Marie Ampère (1775–1836), and 1 A = 1 C/s. It is not a voltage, and it is not an official household current invented for a house not in the book.
Example
Official Example 20.1(a): a truck battery sets in motion 720 C of charge in 4.00 s, so I = 720 C / 4.00 s = 180 A. Official Example 20.1(b): 1.00 C through a handheld calculator at 0.300 mA takes Δt = 3.33 × 10^3 s.
Not this

Key terms

present subjunctive of ir
Definition
The present subjunctive of ir is irregular: vaya, vayas, vaya, vayamos, vayáis, vayan. It is not the present indicative voy or va, not the future irá, not the conditional iría, and not sea, the present subjunctive of ser.
Example
In an invented sentence, “Quiere que yo vaya.” Vaya is the present subjunctive of ir, “that I go,” not the indicative voy or va.
Not this
Not va, the present indicative. Not irá, the future. Not iría, the conditional. Not sea, yesterday’s subjunctive of ser. Not the regular -ir subjunctive viva, which is a different verb.
Connects to

Ir is irregular in the present subjunctive: vaya, vayas, vaya, vayamos, vayáis, vayan. It is not va, not irá, and not iría. Seminar · Music: minuet and trio, a first look, on an invented figure

40 min · Method: A–B–A, minuet, contrasting trio, minuet da capo; invented scale-degree figure; no copyrighted score

Learn

Key terms

minuet and trio
Definition
Minuet and trio is a form in which a minuet is stated, a contrasting trio follows, and the minuet then returns, often marked da capo, giving an A–B–A shape. It is not last week’s theme and variations, which change one theme rather than insert a contrasting middle, and not a rondo, which returns more than once around two or more episodes.
Example
An invented classroom figure in scale degrees, not a copyrighted score: minuet 1-3-5 | 5-4-3, trio 1-5-4 | 4-5-1, then the minuet 1-3-5 | 5-4-3 again. The figure is invented, and the return of A after B is the form.
Not this
Not theme and variations, already taught, in which the theme is changed but remains recognizable. Not a rondo, already taught as A–B–A–C–A. Not a copyrighted minuet copied or transcribed. Not binary form used as if the trio never returned to the minuet.
Connects to
Last week’s theme and variations, and earlier rondo, ternary, and binary forms. A longer dance suite remains reserved.

Last week’s form was theme and variations: one theme, then changed repetitions that remain recognizable. Today a different form: minuet and trio. A minuet is stated, a contrasting trio follows, and the minuet then returns, often marked da capo, so the large shape is A–B–A. The trio is a contrasting middle, not a variation of the minuet, and the return of the minuet is part of the form, not an optional extra.

The classroom figure is invented in scale degrees, not taken from a copyrighted score. Minuet (A): 1-3-5 | 5-4-3. Trio (B): 1-5-4 | 4-5-1. Then the minuet again: 1-3-5 | 5-4-3. The figure is labeled invented. A rondo, already taught as A–B–A–C–A, has more than one return of A around two or more episodes, and is not today’s first look. Binary form, already taught, is a two-part A–B shape and would be a miss if the minuet never returned. A longer dance suite remains reserved.

Remember

Minuet and trio is A–B–A: a minuet, a contrasting trio, and the minuet again. The classroom figure is invented. It is not theme and variations, and not a rondo.

Watch me

State the invented minuet and the invented trio in scale degrees, and say what happens after the trio.

  1. Minuet: 1-3-5 | 5-4-3. Trio: 1-5-4 | 4-5-1.
  2. The minuet returns, 1-3-5 | 5-4-3, often marked da capo.
  3. The figure is invented, not a copyrighted tune.

Together

Why is rewriting the minuet as a variation, and never inserting a contrasting trio, a miss for this form?

Hint 1: Last week

Theme and variations change one theme. Today’s trio is a contrasting middle.

Hint 2: The return

After the trio, the minuet comes back. A set of variations has no required contrasting B of that kind.
