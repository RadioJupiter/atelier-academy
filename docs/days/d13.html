Latin · Capitulum V, part 3 and review

30 min · Text: Ørberg, Familia Romana, Cap. V · Method: the natural method

Learn

Key terms

place where
Definition
The construction in + ablative that answers ubi? (where?): in hortō, in vīllā, in ātriō. It names the place at which someone or something is, not the place toward which someone is going.
Example
Ubi est Mārcus? Mārcus est in hortō.
Not this
Place where is not ‘place to which’. English ‘in’ and ‘into’ are easy to mix; this chapter’s in + ablative is ‘in’, not ‘into’.
Connects to
Tomorrow, Capitulum VI will add prepositions that take the accusative, including ad (‘to, toward’). Keep today’s in + ablative clearly on the ‘where?’ side.
ubi
Definition
The Latin question-word meaning ‘where?’, used to ask for a place-where answer in the ablative after in.
Example
Ubi est Iūlia? Iūlia est in cubiculō.
Not this
ubi is not ‘who’ (quis) and not ‘when’. The answer is a place, not a person.
Connects to
Yes–no questions still use −ne on the first word (Day 1): Estne Mārcus in hortō? Ubi? asks an open place question.

Mathematics · Algebra IV: systems of linear equations

60 min · Method: Singapore model method first, then substitution and elimination (Gelfand, Algebra)

Learn

Key terms

system of equations
Definition
Two or more equations that must be true at the same time, about the same unknowns. A solution is a pair (or triple, …) that fits every equation.
Origin
From late Middle French, from Latin systēma, from Ancient Greek σύστημα (‘a whole made of parts’), from σύν (‘together’) + ἵστημι (‘I set up’).
Example
x + y = 10 and x − y = 2, together, have the unique solution x = 6, y = 4.
Not this
A system is not ‘two problems side by side that may use different unknowns’. And a pair that fits only one of the equations is not a solution of the system.
Connects to
The bar model makes the same relationship visible before any letters are written, as in Day 1.
substitution
Definition
A method for solving a system: use one equation to express one unknown in terms of the other, and put that expression into the second equation.
Origin
From Middle French substitution, from Late Latin substitutio.
Example
From x = y + 6 and x + y = 48, replace x in the second equation: (y + 6) + y = 48.
Not this
Substitution is not guessing a number and checking, and it is not changing the unknown in only one place and leaving it in another.
Connects to
Elimination (adding or subtracting equations to cancel an unknown) is a second method for the same systems. Both must give the same pair.

A system is two (or more) equations that must both be true. The pair you want has to satisfy every equation at once.

Concrete and pictorial. “Two numbers add to 48, and one is 6 more than the other.” Draw a bar for the smaller number and a bar 6 units longer for the larger. Together they make 48. The two equal shorter pieces plus 6 make 48, so the two pieces make 42, and each is 21. The numbers are 21 and 27.

Abstract: substitution. Let the smaller be y and the larger be x. Then x = y + 6 and x + y = 48. Replace x in the second equation: (y + 6) + y = 48, so 2y = 42 and y = 21, x = 27.

Elimination. Sometimes it is cleaner to add or subtract the equations so that one unknown disappears. If x + y = 15 and 2x − y = 6, adding gives 3x = 21, so x = 7 and y = 8. Check in both originals: 7 + 8 = 15 and 14 − 8 = 6.

A system of two different linear equations in two unknowns has one solution (two lines meet at one point). If the two equations say the same thing, there are infinitely many solutions; if they contradict, there are none.

Remember

Draw a bar first when the story is part–whole or comparison. Substitution: express one unknown and replace it. Elimination: add or subtract to cancel an unknown. Always check the pair in both equations.

Key terms: system of equations, substitution.

Watch me

Writing & Rhetoric · The semicolon and the conjunctive adverb

45 min · Method: The Writing Revolution; grammar of the sentence (joining two independent clauses)

Learn

Key terms

semicolon
Definition
The mark (;) that can join two closely related complete sentences without a coordinating conjunction. Each side must be able to stand as a sentence on its own.
Origin
From semi- (‘half’) + colon, because the mark is a colon with the lower half replaced by a comma.
Example
The radius meets the tangent at one point; it is perpendicular there.
Not this
A semicolon is not a fancy comma, and it cannot join a sentence to a fragment. It is also not a colon: a colon often introduces a list, an explanation, or a quotation after a complete clause.
Connects to
Day 11’s sentence combining needed a legal join. The semicolon is one such join; *and/but/so* with a comma is another.
conjunctive adverb
Definition
A word such as however, therefore, moreover, meanwhile, or otherwise that shows the relation between two complete sentences. When it joins them, a semicolon stands before it and a comma after it.
Example
The locative looks like a genitive; however, it does not show possession in Rōmae habitat.
Not this
A conjunctive adverb is not a coordinating conjunction (and, but, so). You cannot legally write ‘The locative looks like a genitive, however it does not show possession’ — that is a comma splice.
Connects to
The transition’s meaning (contrast, result, addition) must still be real, as on Day 11.

Two complete sentences that belong together can be joined in more than one legal way.

Three legal joins. (1) A period, then a new sentence. (2) A comma plus and, but, or, so, yet. (3) A semicolon, with or without a conjunctive adverb.

The pattern with a conjunctive adverb. Sentence; however, sentence. The semicolon comes before however/therefore/moreover/meanwhile; a comma comes after it.

A colon is different. A colon, in the use taught here, follows a complete sentence and introduces an explanation, a list, or a quotation: The rule is simple: the preposition chooses the case.

Remember

Two complete sentences may be joined by a period, by a comma plus and/but/so, or by a semicolon (often with a conjunctive adverb: ; however, ). A comma alone is a splice. A colon introduces an explanation, a list, or a quotation.

Key terms: semicolon, conjunctive adverb.

Watch me

  2. With a conjunctive adverb: The radius meets the tangent at one point; therefore, it is perpendicular there.
  3. A comma splice — ‘…one point, it is perpendicular there’ — is not accepted.

Physics · Vectors and components

45 min · Method: Peer Instruction (Mazur) adapted for one student

Learn

Key terms

vector v
Definition
A quantity that has both a size (magnitude) and a direction, such as displacement, velocity, acceleration, or force. It can be drawn as an arrow, and it can be added by placing arrows tip to tail.
Origin
Learned borrowing from Latin vector (‘carrier’), from vehere (‘to carry’). The mathematical sense was coined in English in 1846 (Wiktionary, after W. R. Hamilton).
Example
A walk 3 m east and then 4 m north has a displacement of 5 m, at an angle whose tangent is 4/3, north of east.
Not this
A vector is not just an arrow on the page, and it is not the same as its magnitude. Distance and speed are not vectors; displacement and velocity are (Day 1).
Connects to
Day 1 already used signed one-dimensional quantities. Today the same idea is used in a plane, by components.
component vx, vy
Definition
The projection of a vector along a chosen axis. In a plane, vx and vy are the horizontal and vertical components, and the magnitude is √(vx² + vy²) when the axes are perpendicular.
Origin
Borrowed from Latin compōnēns, present participle of compōnere (‘to put together’).
Example
A velocity of 10 m/s at 30° above the horizontal has vx = 10 cos 30° = 5√3 m/s and vy = 10 sin 30° = 5 m/s.
Not this
A component is not a second, independent object riding along with the vector. It is the same vector, described along an axis. You may not add magnitudes of perpendicular components as if they were parallel.
Connects to
Pythagoras (Day 15’s rectangle diagonal) is the magnitude formula when the components are perpendicular.

A vector has size and direction. To add two vectors, place them tip to tail; the sum is the arrow from the first tail to the last tip.

Components. Choose perpendicular axes. Then each vector has a pair (vx, vy). Addition becomes ordinary addition of the corresponding components. The magnitude is √(vx² + vy²). If the vector makes an angle θ with the x-axis, vx = v cos θ and vy = v sin θ.

A 3-4-5 that you have met. 3 m east, then 4 m north: Δx = 3, Δy = 4, magnitude 5 m. The direction satisfies tan θ = 4/3, so θ is about 53° north of east.

You do not need a calculator for the 3-4-5 and 5-12-13 triples, or for 30-60-90 (sides 1 : √3 : 2) and 45-45-90 (1 : 1 : √2).

Remember

A vector has magnitude and direction. Perpendicular components add separately; the magnitude is √(v_x² + v_y²). v_x = v cos θ, v_y = v sin θ, with θ measured from the x-axis.

Key terms: vector, component.

  2. The magnitude is √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5 m.
  3. The direction is an angle θ north of east with tan θ = 4/3 (about 53°).

History · Kingship and the afterlife: the Pyramid Texts of Unas

45 min · Method: a primary text from the end of the Old Kingdom, described and used, not treated as a modern essay

Learn

Key terms

Pyramid Texts
Definition
Religious texts, in the Egyptian language, carved on the walls of royal pyramids beginning with the pyramid of Unas (Wenis), last king of the 5th Dynasty, at Saqqara. They are among the oldest substantial religious texts from Egypt, and they concern the king’s afterlife, offerings, and ascent.
Example
In Unas’s pyramid the writings are placed in the interior rooms, not on the outer faces. They speak of the king living among the gods and the stars, and of offerings so that he can live.
Not this
The Pyramid Texts are not a novel, not a single ‘book of the dead’ in the later New Kingdom sense, and not a text you can date to Khufu’s reign. They begin, in the royal pyramids, at the end of the 5th Dynasty, later than the Great Pyramid.
Connects to
Yesterday’s stone monument now has words from the same culture of kingship, a few generations later. The exact years remain uncertain; the sequence of dynasties is the useful frame.
kingship (Egyptian, this period)
Definition
The office of the Egyptian king (often called pharaoh in later usage) as both a political ruler and a figure in religion: the king’s tomb, titles, and afterlife texts present him as the one who maintains order and who will join the gods.
Example
A pyramid complex, a royal name in a cartouche, and the Pyramid Texts are all evidence of kingship, not only of engineering.
Not this
Kingship is not ‘tyranny’ as a slogan, and it is not the same in every century of Egyptian history. The Old Kingdom is not Ramesses II’s Egypt (Day 15).
Connects to
Hammurabi’s stele (Day 3) was also royal self-presentation. Comparison is allowed, but the two courts, languages, and centuries are different.

The first royal Pyramid Texts appear in the pyramid of Unas (also written Wenis), last king of the 5th Dynasty, at Saqqara. That is later than Khufu’s Great Pyramid. The texts are in the Egyptian language, carved inside the tomb, and they concern the king’s afterlife, offerings, and ascent to the sky.

How to use them in this course. You will not be asked to read Egyptian script. Use a short public-domain description or a carefully chosen published translation that names the spell or utterance, and then work as a historian: who is speaking, who is the audience (gods, the king’s afterlife, later priests), what is being asked for, and what you cannot yet know.

Do not invent the wording of a spell. If you do not have a translation in front of you, describe the kind of claim these texts make (the king lives, rises, is fed, joins the gods or the stars) and keep that description labeled as a summary of the genre, not as a quotation.

Kingship. The tomb, the complex, and the texts together present a king who must be maintained after death. That is evidence of a religious and political office, not only of a building method.

Remember

The Pyramid Texts begin, in royal pyramids, with Unas (late 5th Dynasty) at Saqqara, later than Khufu. They concern the king’s afterlife and offerings. Do not invent the wording of a spell. Use them as evidence of kingship, with the usual sourcing questions.

Key terms: Pyramid Texts, kingship (Egyptian, this period).

Atelier · The sphere, and hatching that follows the form

45 min · Method: a sphere or a ball; one main light; hatching as a first value tool · After the egg (Day 6) and the cylinder (Day 12)

Learn

Key terms

hatching
Definition
A way of building value with rows of strokes, rather than with a smudge alone. In this course the strokes follow the surface: they run as if they were lying on the form, changing direction as the surface turns.
Example
On a sphere, a hatch in the halftone may curve slightly around the ball, growing closer or heavier as the form turns toward the terminator, and lighter toward the highlight.
Not this
Hatching is not a scribble, and it is not a set of straight, parallel rain-lines that ignore the form (unless you are deliberately making a flat diagram). Cross-hatching — a second layer at an angle to the first — is tomorrow’s refinement, not a way to hide a careless first layer.
Connects to
The egg already taught the families of light (highlight, light, halftone, core shadow, reflected light, cast shadow). Hatching is a tool for laying those families down with control.
the sphere as a form
Definition
A solid whose surface is everywhere curved, with no plane edges. Under one light it shows the same families as the egg, but it is fully round: the terminator is a closed curve, and the cast shadow is typically an ellipse on the table.
Example
The highlight sits where the surface most directly returns the light toward you; the core shadow is a belt, not a flat half-moon; reflected light lives in the form shadow, not as a second highlight.
Not this
A sphere is not a shaded disc (a flat circle filled in), and a hard outline all the way around will flatten it. The contour is a turning edge: often sharper toward the light, softer or lost in the shadow.
Connects to
The cube was planes; the cylinder had an axis; the sphere has a center and a contour that must be a calm, even curve, checked from the viewing point.

A sphere is the fully curved solid. You already know the families of light from the egg. Today you also begin hatching as a way to put those families down.

Construction. A calm circle (or a very even ellipse, if the ball is slightly foreshortened as a volume in space). Find the center, the contour, and the direction of the light. The terminator is a curve, often an ellipse on the surface, not a straight line across a disc.

Hatching that follows the form. Make rows of strokes that could lie on the surface. On the sphere they will curve, slightly, with the ball. Change the value by the closeness and the weight of the strokes, not by smearing first and hoping. Leave the highlight; hatch the halftone more heavily as it approaches the core shadow; keep reflected light lighter than the core; treat the cast shadow as its own shape (often an ellipse).

Do not cover a bad contour with hatch. Correct the shape first, as you did with the cube and the cylinder.

A common error. Straight, parallel lines that cut across the ball like a curtain, or a shaded disc with a white cartoon highlight pasted on the wrong place.

Block in the contour and the terminator (10 minutes), then hatch the families, slowly.

Remember

A sphere shows the egg’s light families on a fully round form; the terminator is a curve, the cast shadow often an ellipse. Hatching builds value with strokes that follow the surface. Correct the contour before you hatch. Cross-hatching waits until Day 14.

Key terms: hatching, the sphere as a form.

Watch me

  1. The hatch does not follow the form: straight verticals treat the sphere as a flat disc.

Modern language · Spanish: ser and estar contrasted

30 min · Method: contrast of two verbs already met, now taught as a choice · Instruction in English

Learn

Key terms

ser versus estar
Definition
Two verbs that both translate as ‘to be’, used for different kinds of being. Ser is for identity, origin, characteristics, and time. Estar is for place and for a condition or state.
Example
La ciudad es antigua (characteristic). El libro está en la mesa (place). Estamos cansados (condition). Hoy es martes (time).
Not this
Ser is not ‘permanent’ as a magic rule, and estar is not ‘temporary’ as a magic rule. A place can be permanent (*Madrid está en España*) and still take estar, because it is a location.
Connects to
You met ser on Day 3 and estar on Day 4. Today you choose between them, including cases where the same adjective changes sense.
adjective with ser or estar
Definition
Some adjectives change their sense according to the verb: ser + adjective names a characteristic; estar + adjective names a condition.
Example
Es aburrido can mean ‘he/it is boring’; está aburrido means ‘he is bored’. Es listo ‘he is clever’; está listo ‘he is ready’.
Not this
This is not permission to swap the verbs at random, and it does not apply to every adjective in the same way. When in doubt, go back to the two jobs: characteristic versus condition or place.
Connects to
Agreement still holds: the adjective matches the subject in gender and number (*estamos listos*, *está lista*).

You already conjugate ser (soy, eres, es, somos, sois, son) and estar (estoy, estás, está, estamos, estáis, están). Today the work is choosing.

Ser for: - identity: Ella es estudiante. - origin: Somos de la ciudad. - a characteristic: El museo es grande. - time: Es la una. Hoy es viernes.

Estar for: - place: El libro está en la mesa. Madrid está en España. - a condition or state: Estoy cansado. La puerta está abierta.

The same adjective, two senses. Es aburrido (boring, a characteristic) versus está aburrido (bored, a condition). Es listo (clever) versus está listo (ready). La manzana es verde can describe its color; la manzana está verde often means it is unripe. Do not treat this as a license to swap the verbs everywhere; use it as a reason to ask what you mean.

A common error is es en la mesa for a location. Location takes estar.

Remember

Ser: identity, origin, characteristic, time. Estar: place and condition. Some adjectives shift sense (aburrido, listo, verde). Location always takes estar, even if the place does not move.

Art history · The Aegean: Cycladic, Minoan, Mycenaean

30 min · Method: close looking at named objects, with a caution about restoration · After Day 3 (how to look) and Day 8 (Egypt)

Learn

Key terms

Aegean Bronze Age
Definition
The Bronze Age cultures of the Aegean Sea and its islands and shores, treated here in three parts: Cycladic (the Cyclades islands), Minoan (Crete), and Mycenaean (the mainland palatial centers, such as Mycenae). The name Aegean is geographic, not the name those people used for themselves.
Origin
Aegean is from Latin Aegaeus, from Ancient Greek Αἰγαῖος; the further link to the mythical king Aegeus, or to a word for waves, is suggested rather than settled, so it is left aside here.
Example
A Cycladic folded-arm marble figure, a fresco from the palace at Knossos, and the Lion Gate at Mycenae belong to this world, in different places and centuries.
Not this
‘Aegean’ is not a single kingdom, and it is not ‘Greece’ in the later Archaic or Classical sense (Day 18). It is also not Egyptian art, though contact existed.
Connects to
Day 8 asked how a culture pictures the human figure. These objects give three other answers, earlier than the kouros.
the name ‘Minoan’
Definition
A modern name, coined by Arthur Evans in the early twentieth century from Minos, the mythical king of Crete, for the Bronze Age culture he was excavating at Knossos. It is not a name recovered from those people’s own writing in a form we can read with certainty.
Origin
Coined by Arthur Evans in the early twentieth century, from Ancient Greek Μίνως (Minos), the mythical king of Crete. (Wiktionary, ‘Minoan’.)
Example
Evans’s reconstructions and the fresco restorations (often by the Gilliéron workshop) are part of what we see in photographs of Knossos. Looking includes asking what is ancient plaster and pigment, and what is modern reconstruction.
Not this
‘Minoan’ is not a word we should treat as an ancient self-name, and a heavily restored fresco is not a photograph of the second millennium BCE.
Connects to
The caution is the same skill as Day 3: look at the object, and at how it has been presented.

Three neighboring Bronze Age cultures, not one style.

Cycladic. On the Cyclades, in the third millennium BCE (a conventional range for the well-known folded-arm marble figures is the Early Cycladic period, often placed in that millennium). The figures are usually highly simplified: a long head, a folded-arm pose, a flat or slightly modeled body, often originally painted. We do not have a contemporary written explanation of their use; be careful of words such as ‘idol’ if they go beyond the evidence.

Minoan Crete. Palaces such as Knossos, excavated and imaginatively reconstructed by Arthur Evans from 1900 onward. Wall paintings in fresco (pigment in plaster) survive in fragments; many textbook images include later restoration. A famous bull-leaping scene is to be looked at with that caution: describe what is actually preserved, and what is reconstructed, as far as a good modern photograph or museum note allows. The name Minoan is Evans’s.

Mycenaean mainland. Citadels such as Mycenae. The Lion Gate is a monumental entrance: a relieving triangle above the lintel holds a relief of two lionesses (the heads, probably of a different material, are lost) flanking a central column. This is Late Bronze Age palatial architecture on the mainland, later than the early Cycladic figures, and not the same culture as Knossos, though the worlds were in contact.

Linear A and Linear B may be named in passing: Linear B, used on the mainland and at some Cretan sites in the later period, was deciphered as an early form of Greek; Linear A is not deciphered in the same way. That is a fact about writing, not a license to invent translations.

Look, compare, and do not collapse the three into one ‘ancient Greek’ style.

Remember

Cycladic folded-arm marble figures (third millennium BCE, use unknown with certainty). Minoan: Evans’s name, Knossos, frescoes often restored. Mycenaean: Lion Gate at Mycenae, two lionesses and a column, heads lost. These are not yet Classical Greece.

Key terms: Aegean Bronze Age, the name ‘Minoan’.
