Latin · Capitulum IX, part 3: numerals — ūnus, duo, trēs, and the rest

30 min · Text: Ørberg, Familia Romana, Cap. IX · Method: the natural method · Original practice sentences

Learn

Key terms

declining numerals
Definition
In Latin, ūnus, duo, and trēs change endings (they decline). Many other numerals, including quattuor, quinque, and centum, do not change.
Example
Ūnus rēx in hortō est. Duae puellae in hortō sunt. Triāngulum tria latera habet. Centum is invariable: centum canēs.
Not this
Not “every number word declines.” *centum canēs* does not become *centī canēs. And ūnus takes familiar first-and-second-declension-like endings (ūnus, ūna, ūnum).
Connects to
Yesterday’s third-declension nouns can now be counted, still in original sentences. latera are sides (latus, “side,” not lātus, “wide”).
Word How to say it Meaning
ūnus, ūna, ūnum OO-nus, OO-nah, OO-num one (declines)
duo, duae, duo DOO-oh, DOO-eye, DOO-oh two (declines)
trēs, tria trace, TREE-ah three (trēs for masculine/feminine; tria for neuter)
quattuor KWAT-too-or four (invariable)
centum KEN-tum one hundred (invariable)

ūnus, duo, and trēs decline (ūnus/ūna/ūnum; duo/duae/duo; trēs/tria). quattuor, quinque, and centum are invariable. Count with yesterday’s nouns in original sentences.

Mathematics · Algebra X: graphs of quadratic functions

60 min · Method: a table of values, then the curve; the vertex from completing the square, already taught

Learn

Key terms

parabola
Definition
The graph of a quadratic function, y = ax² + bx + c with a ≠ 0. It is a curve with a single turning point, the vertex, not a straight line.
Example
y = x² − 4 has vertex (0, −4), opens upward (a = 1 > 0), and crosses the x-axis at (−2, 0) and (2, 0), because x² − 4 = (x − 2)(x + 2).
Not this
Not a linear graph, and not a circle. A parabola is not “any curved graph.”
Connects to
Day 5 already solved quadratics and completed the square. Today those solutions become points and a vertex on a graph. The etymology of the name is left aside if a short, clearly sourced origin is not being repeated here.
vertex of a parabola
Definition
The turning point of the graph of a quadratic. For y = x² + px + q, completing the square (Day 5) shows the x-coordinate of the vertex; the y-coordinate is the value of the function there. For y = ax² + bx + c, a first formula, once the square is completed or the axis of symmetry is named, is x = −b/(2a).
Example
For y = x² − 4, b = 0, so the vertex is at x = 0, and y = −4. For y = x² − 2x − 3, completing the square or x = −b/(2a) = 1 gives the vertex (1, −4).
Not this
Not an x-intercept automatically (the vertex lies on the x-axis only when the root is repeated). And not the slope of a line.
Connects to
The axis of symmetry is the vertical line through the vertex. That is a line of symmetry of the graph, not a claim about a crater or a drawing of a head.

A quadratic function has a curved graph, a parabola, not a straight line. Plot a table for y = x² − 4:

x y
−2 0
−1 −3
0 −4
1 −3
2 0

The points rise the same way on both sides of (0, −4). That turning point is the vertex. The curve crosses the x-axis at ±2, which matches the factors (x − 2)(x + 2) = 0 already available from Day 5.

The vertex from completing the square, or from x = −b/(2a). For y = x² − 2x − 3, x = −(−2)/2 = 1, and y(1) = 1 − 2 − 3 = −4, so the vertex is (1, −4). As a check, y = (x − 1)² − 4 after completing the square.

Do not force a line through a parabola. If the points are not collinear, that is the point of today’s graph.

Remember

A quadratic graphs as a parabola, with a vertex. Tables give points. x-intercepts, when they exist, are the roots already solved. x = −b/(2a) locates the vertex’s x-coordinate. Completing the square (Day 5) is the reason, not a new trick.

Key terms: parabola, vertex of a parabola.

Writing & Rhetoric · A parenthetical interruption that does not break the series

45 min · Method: explicit repair of a series or a sentence broken by an insertion

Learn

Key terms

parenthetical interruption
Definition
A word, phrase, or clause inserted into a sentence, often set off by commas or dashes, that adds a comment without taking over the grammatical job of the sentence.
Example
“Studies serve for delight, for ornament — and, when they are used, for ability.” The series is still three for-phrases; the interruption is a comment.
Not this
Not a new main clause that strands the series, and not a dash that begins a new subject and never returns. If the insertion changes the job, it belongs in a new sentence.
Connects to
Yesterday’s Bacon series is the test case. Parallelism (week 5) still binds the three parts; the interruption must not un-match them.

A comment may sit inside a sentence if the sentence can still be read without it and if a series remains parallel. Use commas or dashes honestly; do not let the interruption become a second essay.

Test: remove the interruption. If the sentence still works, and if a three-part series is still three matching parts, the insertion is doing its job.

Remember

An interruption is a comment, set off, that the sentence can survive without. A series must stay parallel around it.

Key terms: parenthetical interruption.

Watch me

  2. A repair: “A graph serves for a table, for a check, and for a picture of the rule.” (No interruption, clean series.)
  3. Or, with a comment: “A graph serves for a table, for a check, and — if the rule is quadratic — for a picture of a parabola.” The three for-phrases remain.

Physics · A first look at torque

50 min · Method: concrete lever, then the product of force and perpendicular distance

Learn

Key terms

torque (first look)
Definition
A measure of how effectively a force tends to turn an object about a point or an axis: the product of the force and the perpendicular distance from the point (or axis) to the line of the force. In this first look, τ = F × (perpendicular distance).
Example
A 4 N force whose line is 0.5 m perpendicular from a pivot gives a torque of 4 N × 0.5 m = 2 N·m about that pivot.
Not this
Not the same as work (also a force times a distance, but along the displacement), and not a new unit called a joule. The unit here is the newton-meter (N·m), named as a torque, not as an energy.
Connects to
A door handle far from the hinge is a picture of a larger perpendicular distance, and so a larger torque for the same force. A force through the pivot gives no torque.

A force can turn a body about a pivot. Torque, in this first look, is

τ = F × d⊥

where d⊥ is the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of the force. The unit is the newton-meter (N·m). That product has the same dimensions as a joule, but we do not call a torque a joule. The joule remains a unit of work or energy.

If the line of the force goes through the pivot, d⊥ = 0, and the torque is zero. If the force is not perpendicular to a given radius, you must use the perpendicular distance to the force’s line (or the perpendicular component of the force); today’s numbers will be given so that the perpendicular distance is clear.

Remember

Torque, in this first look, is force times perpendicular distance to the line of the force, in N·m, not in joules. A force through the pivot gives no torque.

Key terms: torque (first look).

Watch me

A 6 N force has a perpendicular distance 0.25 m from a pivot. Find the torque and name the unit.

  1. τ = 6 N × 0.25 m = 1.5 N·m.
  2. The unit is the newton-meter, not the joule.
  3. The 0.25 m must be the perpendicular distance to the line of the force.

History · How we know: later writers, and a first-person record

50 min · Method: source criticism — date the writer, name the kind of text, and do not treat advocacy as a neutral chronicle

Learn

Key terms

Res Gestae as advocacy
Definition
The Res Gestae Divi Augusti is a first-person record of deeds attributed to Augustus, known from later copies (notably an inscription at Ankara). It is a primary statement of how he wished to be seen, and therefore advocacy as well as a record.
Example
It can tell you what Augustus claimed about offices, money, and honors. It cannot, by itself, tell you how opponents would have told the same years.
Not this
Not a day-by-day chronicle written by a neutral observer, and not a modern constitution. A first-person monumental record is a kind of evidence, not the whole story.
Connects to
Use it with later writers, not instead of them, and not as a simple confirmation of them.

Two later writers, often used for this period, are Tacitus (early second century CE) and Suetonius (early second century CE). They wrote about a century after Augustus’s death (14 CE). They are evidence; they are not eyewitness diaries of 27 BCE.

The Res Gestae is different in kind: a first-person record of deeds, copied in inscriptions. Read it as advocacy as well as a list: it tells you what was claimed, in a public voice, about those deeds.

A careful habit: date the source, name its kind, and ask what it can show. Do not flatten all three into “what happened.”

Remember

Tacitus and Suetonius are later (early second century CE), not eyewitnesses of 27 BCE. The Res Gestae is a first-person record and advocacy. Date the source and name its kind.

Key terms: Res Gestae as advocacy.

Atelier · The neck, and how the head sits on it

50 min · Method: the neck as a form that carries the head, placed from the plate or plaster

Learn

Key terms

head on the neck
Definition
The head does not float: it sits on the neck, which has width, a slight forward or backward lean, and a connection into the shoulders. All of this is judged from the plate or plaster.
Example
A common check: the neck is thick enough to carry the head you have drawn, and its axis follows the tilt of this head, not a vertical default.
Not this
Not a stick under a finished mask, and not a neck copied from a front-view diagram onto a turned head. If the plate shows a short, thick neck, or a long one, that sight wins.
Connects to
Hair masses and the ear already placed will meet the neck at the back and side. Keep those placements; add the neck as a form, not as a new drawing beside the head.

Look at how this head is carried. Mark the neck’s width at the jaw and at the shoulder line, and the axis (the tilt). Then block the neck as a simple cylinder or column that can lean, and only then add a little of the sternomastoid or a shadow, if the plate shows it.

A head that is already tilted with a vertical neck will look unscrewed. Correct the axis first.

Remember

The neck carries the head: width and axis from the plate, then a simple form. A vertical default is a near-miss if the head is tilted. The plate wins.

Key terms: head on the neck.

Modern language · Spanish: the present of decir and venir

40 min · Method: two irregular presents, compared with hacer and tener already taught; English remains the language of instruction

Learn

Key terms

present of decir and venir
Definition
Two more irregular presents: decir (digo, dices, dice, decimos, decís, dicen) ‘to say / to tell’; venir (vengo, vienes, viene, venimos, venís, vienen) ‘to come.’ Both have a first-person -go, and venir has an e → ie change in several forms, like tener.
Example
Digo la verdad. ¿Qué dices? Vengo a la clase. Viene Ana.
Not this
Not *yo dico, *yo veno, or *yo veno. And still not a past tense (dije, vine are not today’s forms).
Connects to
Compare hacer (hago) and the new digo, vengo. Compare tener (tengo, tienes) with venir (vengo, vienes).
Word How to say it Meaning
digo, dices, dice, decimos, decís, dicen DEE-go, DEE-ses, DEE-seh, deh-SEE-mohs, deh-SEESS, DEE-sen I say, you say, he/she says, we say, you (pl.) say, they say
vengo, vienes, viene, venimos, venís, vienen VENG-go, VYEH-nes, VYEH-neh, beh-NEE-mohs, beh-NEESS, VYEH-nen I come, you come, he/she comes, we come, you (pl.) come, they come

Two more -go presents. decir: digo, dices, dice, decimos, decís, dicen. venir: vengo, vienes, viene, venimos, venís, vienen (e → ie in vienes, viene, vienen, like tienes, tiene, tienen).

Original sentences: Digo la frase. ¿Qué dice ella? Vengo a la mesa. ¿Cuándo vienes? (cuándo ‘when,’ if not already firm). No vienen hoy.

Remember

decir: digo, dices, dice, decimos, decís, dicen. venir: vengo, vienes, viene, venimos, venís, vienen (e → ie in several forms). Still the present.

Art history · Roman art: the Augustus of Prima Porta, and the Ara Pacis

40 min · Method: two often-cited works, with copy and dating cautions, not a museum-souvenir summary

Learn

Key terms

Augustus of Prima Porta (copy type)
Definition
A marble statue of Augustus, now in the Vatican Museums, generally treated as a copy of a (lost) bronze type rather than as the unique original bronze in our hands. This lesson does not fix a single year for the marble copy.
Example
The figure is a portrait of Augustus in a commander’s pose, with a decorated breastplate. It is evidence of how he could be shown; it is not, by itself, a photograph of a single day’s event.
Not this
Not a Greek original of the fifth century BCE, and not a work we should date to 27 BCE just because that year was this week’s conventional start of the principate. A portrait type can be later than the political moment it celebrates.

Ara Pacis (conventional dates)
Definition
The Ara Pacis Augustae, an altar in Rome associated with Augustan peace, conventionally voted in 13 BCE and dedicated in 9 BCE. Those years are a conventional scholarly range, not a classroom measurement.
Example
Processional reliefs on the altar show members of the imperial household and others, in a public, ceremonial style. This lesson does not treat a reconstruction or a museum setting as the original site unchanged.
Not this
Not a Republican work of the Punic Wars, and not a building dated to Actium (31 BCE) or to 27 BCE simply because those dates were taught this week. Use the conventional 13–9 BCE pair, labeled as conventional.
Connects to
As with Prima Porta, a public monument can be advocacy in stone as well as a source for style and ceremony.

Two Roman works, taught with their cautions:

  • The Augustus of Prima Porta: a marble statue in the Vatican, generally treated as a copy of a lost bronze type. We do not assign the marble a single year in this lesson. Read it as a public portrait and a claim, not as a snapshot.
  • The Ara Pacis: conventional dates 13 BCE (voted) and 9 BCE (dedicated). Use that pair, and call it conventional. A later museum setting is not the original context unchanged.

Do not drag 31 or 27 BCE onto these works as if those were their making-dates. History’s dates this week and art history’s dates can be related without being the same fact.

Remember

Prima Porta: a marble copy type of Augustus, no single year assigned to the copy here. Ara Pacis: conventional 13–9 BCE. Public images can be claims. Do not reuse 31 or 27 BCE as these works’ dates.
