Mathematics · Geometry XX: a line and a circle, as a system

60 min · Method: substitute the line into the circle’s equation, then count the real solutions

Learn

Key terms

intersection of a line and a circle
Definition
The point or points that lie on both a line and a circle. Substituting the line into the circle’s equation gives a quadratic equation, which may have two solutions, one solution (a tangent line), or none.
Example
The line y = x and the circle x² + y² = 2: substitute to get 2x² = 2, so x = ±1, and the points are (1, 1) and (−1, −1).
Not this
Not “a line always cuts a circle twice.” A line may miss, or may touch once. And not the intersection of two lines (that was a linear system).
Connects to
The equation of a circle (Day 42) and systems of linear equations (Algebra IV). The quadratic may be solved by factoring, by a square root, or by completing the square — tools already taught.

A line and a circle in the same plane may meet twice, once, or not at all. Today we find those meetings by substitution, not by guessing from a sketch alone (a sketch still helps).

The method. Write the line so one variable is easy (for example y = mx + c, or a vertical line x = a). Substitute into (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². The result is a quadratic in one variable (or a simple one-variable equation for a vertical line). Solve it, then find the other coordinate.

Two meetings. y = x and x² + y² = 2: x² + x² = 2, so 2x² = 2, x² = 1, x = ±1. The points are (1, 1) and (−1, −1).

One meeting (tangent). y = 2 and x² + y² = 4: x² + 4 = 4, so x² = 0, x = 0. The only point is (0, 2). The line is tangent to the circle there (it meets the circle once). This matches the already-taught picture of a tangent, now in coordinates.

No meeting. y = 3 and x² + y² = 4: x² + 9 = 4, so x² = −5, which has no real number x. The line misses the circle.

Classroom lines and circles stay invented.

Remember

Substitute the line into (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². A quadratic may give two meetings, one (a tangent), or none. Invented figures stay labeled.

Key terms: intersection of a line and a circle.

Science · Afterimages: a careful observation, without an invented mechanism

50 min · Method: a safe, optional observation after a bright color, then a name for the later appearance; no invented counts or times

Learn

Key terms

afterimage
Definition
A color or shape that may still appear after the gaze has moved from a bright color or a bright form to a plain surface. Today it is taught as a careful classroom observation, not as a counted receptor mechanism.
Example
If you look steadily at a bright patch and then look at a white page, a later tint or a later shape may appear for a short time. We invent no number of seconds and no cell count for that later appearance.
Not this
Not a proof of a particular receptor theory, and not a demand to stare at the sun or at any other unsafe light. A colored paper on a table is enough, or the observation may be described without being forced.
Connects to
Tuesday’s photoreceptors and the brain’s job in making an image. An afterimage is a further fact about experience; we do not invent a mechanism we have not sourced.

Tuesday’s lesson, from the National Eye Institute, placed photoreceptors in the retina and the making of an image in the brain’s use of signals. Today we add a careful observation, not a new official count.

Afterimage. After the gaze has rested on a bright color or a bright shape, a later color or shape may appear on a plain surface. That later appearance is an afterimage. We will not invent a number of seconds, a wavelength, or a receptor count to “explain” it today, and we will not treat the observation as a proof of a full theory of color.

A safe try (optional). A piece of colored paper on a table, looked at steadily, then a look at a white page, is enough if the guide chooses to try it. Do not stare at the sun, a lamp, or any other unsafe light. If the try is skipped, the idea can still be described, and the refused inventions still stand.

What we do not claim. We do not invent a complementary-color law as a numbered fact, and we do not diagnose anyone. The afterimage is named; the mechanism is left honestly open, or reserved for a later, sourced lesson.

Remember

An afterimage is a later color or shape after the gaze has moved. Optional, safe classroom try only (no unsafe lights). No invented times, wavelengths, or cell counts, and no claimed full mechanism today.

Literature · What the opening does not yet give: later books reserved

50 min · Method: reread the set opening, then name what is reserved, without a new extract

Learn

Key terms

a reserved telling
Definition
A later part of the Aeneid that this course has not yet released as a quotation or as a full plot, and that must not be smuggled into a précis of the opening.
Example
The wooden horse, Dido at Carthage, and the descent in Book VI are reserved until a sitting sets a short, attributed passage for one of them.
Not this
Not a ban on knowing that the epic continues, and not a spoiler-game. The point is not to invent a second extract, and not to treat the opening as if it already contained those later books.
Connects to

Arms, and the Man I sing, who forc'd by Fate, And haughty Juno's unrelenting Hate; Expell'd and exil'd, left the Trojan Shoar.

Today is not a new extract. It is a careful look at what the opening does not yet give, so that a précis and a discussion stay honest.

The epic continues. This course will take selected books, in short, attributed public-domain passages, in later sittings. Modern language · Spanish: deber + infinitive

30 min · Method: deber + infinitive in the present, beside already-taught + infinitive patterns. Instruction in English

Learn

Key terms

deber + infinitive
Definition
A present-tense pattern: deber plus an infinitive, for “ought to / should / must” do something. It is not a past tense, and it is not the English noun “debt” used as a verb.
Example
Debo estudiar. Debe poner la caja aquí. No debe quitar la regla.
Not this
Not tener que (Day 26), which was already taught as “have to.” The two can be close in everyday use; today we learn deber + infinitive as its own pattern, and we do not invent a fine legal contrast we have not taught.
Connects to
Day 26’s tener que, hay, and hay que; Day 38’s acabar de + infinitive; Monday’s and Wednesday’s object words can sit in the same sentence.
Word How to say it Meaning
debe DEH-beh he/she/it ought to, should, must (present of deber)
debo DEH-boh I ought to, I should, I must
deber + infinitive deh-BEHR + infinitive ought to / should / must + a verb: debo estudiar

Several + infinitive patterns are already in this course (ir a, acabar de, tener que). Today adds deber + infinitive, “ought to / should / must,” still in the present.

Original sentences: Debo estudiar. Debe poner la caja aquí. No debe quitar la regla. Debemos medir con la regla. (Medir is an infinitive you can hear as a pair with mide.)

Instruction stays in English. No NASA figures in the Spanish. Tener que remains available; today is not a test of splitting the two as a law court would.

Remember

deber + infinitive: ought to / should / must. Present tense only. Close to tener que in everyday use; today is the new pattern, not a legal split. Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words.

Economics & personal finance · An incentive (an invented shop example)

30 min · Method: a labeled invented shop, then the word incentive beside opportunity cost already taught

Learn

Key terms

incentive
Definition
A reason that makes a choice more or less attractive, often a gain or a loss that follows the choice. It is not automatically money, and it is not automatically a good reason.
Example
An invented classroom shop: a small extra amount for bringing a reusable bag may make that choice more attractive. The extra amount is an incentive, labeled invented.
Not this
Not a command, and not a full course in taxes or wages. Opportunity cost (Day 4) is still the thing given up; an incentive is something added that tilts a choice.
Connects to
Scarcity and opportunity cost, markets and prices, and last week’s leftover (an invented example). Today’s shop numbers stay invented and labeled.

Economics in this course has already taught scarcity, opportunity cost, markets, prices, money, a labeled budget, and a leftover. Today names an incentive: a reason that tilts a choice, often by adding a gain or a loss.

An invented shop. A shop offers a small extra amount, or a small discount, if a person brings a reusable bag. That extra amount is an incentive. The shop, the bag, and the amount are invented classroom numbers, labeled as such. They are not a government program we measured, and they are not a moral demand.

What it is not. An incentive is not the same as a command (you may still refuse). It is not automatically the same as opportunity cost (the cost is still what you give up; the incentive is something added that may change which choice looks better). It is not automatically “money is the only reason people act.”

We invent no inflation rate and no wage series today.

Remember

An incentive tilts a choice, often by a gain or a loss. It is not a command, and it is not automatically money. Invented shop numbers stay labeled. Opportunity cost is still the thing given up.

Key terms: incentive.
