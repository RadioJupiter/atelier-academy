Remember

Right square pyramid: B + 2bl, with l the face slant height, and l² = h² + (b/2)² if needed. Right cone: πrl + πr², with l = √(r² + h²). A composite volume adds the pieces; a composite surface counts only exposed faces.

Elective · Anatomy: the mandible, from Gray 1918

40 min · Method: a first closer look at the mandible in Gray 1918, already named last week as the movable exception among the twenty-two bones of the skull

Learn

Key terms

the mandible (Gray)
Definition
In Henry Gray, Anatomy of the Human Body, 20th ed., 1918, “The Mandible (Lower Jaw),” the mandible is “the largest and strongest bone of the face,” and “serves for the reception of the lower teeth.” “It consists of a curved, horizontal portion, the body, and two perpendicular portions, the rami, which unite with the ends of the body nearly at right angles.” It “articulates with the two temporal bones.” Last week already named it as the movable exception among the twenty-two bones of the skull.
Example
Gray also names, on a first closer day, the mental foramen, “for the passage of the mental vessels and nerve,” and, on the upper border of the ramus, two processes, “the coronoid in front and the condyloid behind, separated by a deep concavity, the mandibular notch.” A full recitation of every fossa, line, and ossification center is not required today.
Not this
Not a first look at last week’s cranium of eight and face of fourteen, or at the mandible as the movable exception, already taught. Not a required recitation of Gray’s age-changes, or of Meckel’s cartilage, which remain reserved. The hyoid, already noted last week as not one of the twenty-two, remains outside this count.
Connects to
Last week’s skull from Gray, and Theodora’s public-domain HTML of this section, https://www.theodora.com/anatomy/the_mandible_lower_jaw.html (the earlier Bartleby lit-hub posting used in weeks 17–18 is no longer serving the page).

Last week, Gray’s skull was a cranium of eight and a face of fourteen, twenty-two bones in all, with the mandible the movable exception. Today that bone is read more closely, still from Gray 1918.

“The mandible, the largest and strongest bone of the face, serves for the reception of the lower teeth. It consists of a curved, horizontal portion, the body, and two perpendicular portions, the rami, which unite with the ends of the body nearly at right angles.”

“The mandible articulates with the two temporal bones.” That is the movable joint already implied by last week’s exception.

On the body, “below the second premolar tooth, on either side,” is “the mental foramen, for the passage of the mental vessels and nerve.” On the upper border of the ramus, Gray names “two processes, the coronoid in front and the condyloid behind, separated by a deep concavity, the mandibular notch.”

A first closer day requires the largest-and-strongest claim, the body and two rami, the articulation with the two temporals, and a recognition of the mental foramen, the coronoid and condyloid processes, and the mandibular notch. It does not require a memorized recitation of every fossa, muscle attachment, or ossification center, or of the changes Gray describes at birth, in childhood, in the adult, and in old age, which remain reserved.
