Key terms (from earlier lessons)

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

A second pass through the four tenses of both verbs. Keep the time clear: secūtus est and ēgressus est are completed; sequētur and ēgrediētur are still ahead; sequitur and ēgreditur are under way; secūtus erat and ēgressus erat were already completed before another past moment. Remember

Perfect, future, present, and pluperfect of sequor and of ēgredior must not be swapped, and the two verbs must not be swapped either.

Watch me

  3. Do not read ēgressī sumus as a pluperfect, or sequēris as a present (sequeris would be the present).

Today mixes conversion between degrees and radians, arc length s = rθ with θ in radians, and the exact sines and cosines at the familiar angles. On an invented circle of radius 2 with a central angle of 90°, first convert: 90° = π/2, then s = 2 × (π/2) = π. Sin(π/2) remains 1. On an invented circle of radius 5 with a central angle of 2π/3 (that is, 120°), s = 5 × (2π/3) = 10π/3, and cos(2π/3) = −1/2. The radii are invented classroom numbers. Convert before multiplying, and keep exact values in π.

Remember

Science · A simple circuit, a first look, from OpenStax 20.1

40 min · Method: a closed path from a voltage source through a load and back; conventional current; Ohm’s law reserved

Learn

Key terms

a simple circuit
Definition
A simple circuit, in OpenStax 20.1, is a closed path for current, supplied by conducting wires connecting a load to the terminals of a voltage source, such as a battery. Conventional current is the direction that positive charge would flow, from positive to negative in the book’s schematic. It is not Ohm’s law, and it is not an official household wiring diagram.
Example
OpenStax 20.1, Figure 20.3, shows a battery, conducting wires, and a load: “a closed path for current to flow through is supplied by conducting wires connecting a load to the terminals of a battery.” The same schematic can represent many similar circuits. Official household wiring remains reserved.
Not this
Not Ohm’s law, which remains reserved. Not an official plan of a house. Not a current without a closed path. Not last Tuesday’s official 180 A treated as if it were a new kind of circuit.
Connects to
Tuesday’s electric current, I = ΔQ / Δt. Ohm’s law, drift velocity, and official household voltages remain reserved.

OpenStax 20.1, still at https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/20-1-current, turns from the definition of current to a simple circuit. “A closed path for current to flow through is supplied by conducting wires connecting a load to the terminals of a battery.” In the book’s schematic, a battery is represented by two parallel lines, conducting wires as straight lines, and the load as a zigzag. “The schematic represents a wide variety of similar circuits,” from a truck battery and a headlight to a small battery and a penlight; the analysis is the same. “The direction of conventional current is the direction that positive charge would flow,” from positive to negative in that schematic. If the path is not closed, the book’s simple circuit is not complete, and this lesson does not invent a current through an official open gap.

Tuesday’s official Example 20.1 currents still illustrate I = ΔQ / Δt; they do not yet apply Ohm’s law, which remains reserved. Official household voltages and official household wiring diagrams remain reserved. Drift velocity remains reserved. Invented charges and times, if used, stay labeled invented.

Seminar · Economics: a simple budget on invented figures, a first look

40 min · Method: invented income, expenses, and remainder; distinguished from a tax, a premium, and a deductible; not official

Learn

Key terms

a simple budget (invented figures)
Definition
A simple budget, in this invented classroom arrangement, is a plan that places an invented income beside invented expenses and names the remainder, income minus expenses. It is not a tax, not a premium, and not a deductible, and it is not an official household account.
Example
On invented figures, income is $80, and expenses are $40, $20, and $10, which total $70. The remainder is $80 − $70 = $10. The figures are invented classroom numbers, not an official income, rent, or account.
Not this
Not a tax, which last weeks took as a percent of invented income or a percent added to an invented price. Not a premium, paid in advance on invented insurance. Not a deductible, borne first only if a named event happens. Not an official budget, wage, or cost of living.
Connects to
Earlier sales tax, income tax, insurance, and a deductible, all on invented figures. Official rates and official household accounts remain reserved.

A simple budget, in this invented classroom arrangement, is a plan that places an invented income beside invented expenses and names the remainder: income minus the total of the expenses. On invented figures, income is $80. Expenses are $40, $20, and $10, which total $70. The remainder is $80 − $70 = $10. If the expenses were $90, the remainder would be negative, $80 − $90 = −$10, and the invented plan would not yet balance. The $80, the expenses, and the $10 are invented classroom numbers, not an official wage, rent, or household account.

The remainder is not a tax: an income tax was a percent taken from invented income, and a sales tax was a percent added to an invented price. It is not a premium: last weeks’ invented premium was paid in advance, $10 a month, whether or not a named event happened. It is not a deductible: a deductible was borne first only if the named event happened. Official budgets, official wages, and official costs of living remain reserved.

Remember

On invented figures, a simple budget places $80 of income beside $70 of expenses, so the remainder is $10. The remainder is not a tax, not a premium, and not a deductible. The figures are not official.
