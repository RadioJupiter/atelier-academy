Latin · A tighter mix of sequor and ēgredior

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

The present subjunctives of sequor and ēgredior now sit beside the already-taught present, future, perfect, pluperfect, and future perfect. Today’s job is to choose the form that matches the time relation, and not to swap the verbs. Sequātur is not sequitur, not sequētur, and not secūtus est. Remember

Mathematics · Geometry XCIV: the area of a circular segment, a first look

40 min · Method: Singapore, faithful; segment = sector minus the triangular piece; invented figures, labeled invented

Learn

Key terms

area of a circular segment
Definition
A circular segment, in this lesson, is the region between an arc and the chord that joins the arc’s ends. Its area is the area of the corresponding sector minus the area of the triangle formed by the two radii and that chord.
Example
On an invented figure, r = 2 and θ = π/2. The sector area is (1/2) × 4 × (π/2) = π. The triangle formed by the two radii is right-angled at the center, with legs 2 and 2, so its area is (1/2) × 2 × 2 = 2. The segment area is π − 2, in square units. The figure is invented and labeled invented.
Not this
Not the sector itself, which still includes the triangular piece. Not Dewing’s already-taught “segment of a circle” under a dome, an architectural use of the word, not this area. Not a first look at a new degree formula.
Connects to
This week’s sector area A = (1/2)r²θ, and the already-taught area of a triangle, including (1/2)ab sin C when the included angle is not a right angle.

A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc. If the ends of the arc are joined by a chord, the region between the arc and the chord is a circular segment. Its area is the sector area minus the area of the triangle formed by the two radii and that chord. On an invented figure, r = 2 and θ = π/2. The sector area is (1/2) × 4 × (π/2) = π. The triangle is right-angled at the center, with legs 2 and 2, so its area is (1/2) × 2 × 2 = 2. The segment area is π − 2, in square units. On a second invented figure, r = 6 and θ = π/3, the sector area is the already-checked 6π, and the triangle has two sides 6 with included angle π/3, so its area is (1/2) × 6 × 6 × sin(π/3) = 18 × (√3/2) = 9√3. The segment area is 6π − 9√3. The figures are invented and labeled invented. This is not Dewing’s already-taught architectural “segment of a circle.”

Remember

A circular segment is the region between an arc and its chord. Its area is the sector area minus the triangular piece. For a sector with angle θ at the center, the triangle’s area is (1/2)r² sin θ, and the segment area is (1/2)r²θ − (1/2)r² sin θ.

Science · Official Example 20.5, a headlight filament, from OpenStax 20.3

40 min · Method: OpenStax official Example 20.5, book arithmetic with ρ = 5.6 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m and the book’s 3.14; official, not invented

Learn

Key terms

official Example 20.5, a headlight filament
Definition
OpenStax, College Physics 2e, official Example 20.5, takes a tungsten car-headlight filament with a cold resistance of 0.350 Ω and a length of 4.00 cm, and asks for the diameter. The official resistivity from Table 20.1 is 5.6 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m. The book finds A = ρL / R = 6.40 × 10⁻⁹ m², and then, using 3.14 for π, D = 9.0 × 10⁻⁵ m.
Example
The official arithmetic, following the book, is A = (5.6 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m)(4.00 × 10⁻² m) / 0.350 Ω = 6.40 × 10⁻⁹ m², then D = 2√(A/π) with the book’s 3.14, which gives 9.0 × 10⁻⁵ m. Those figures are official, not invented. The book quotes the diameter to two digits because ρ is known to only two digits.
Not this
Not an invented filament, and not a replacement of the book’s 3.14 by a different approximation in this official example. Not official household voltage, which remains reserved. Not the temperature coefficient, which remains reserved.
Connects to
Tuesday’s resistivity, R = ρL / A, and Table 20.1’s official tungsten value, which this example uses.

OpenStax, College Physics 2e, official Example 20.5, is titled “Calculating Resistor Diameter: A Headlight Filament.” A car headlight filament is made of tungsten and has a cold resistance of 0.350 Ω. The filament is a cylinder 4.00 cm long. The official resistivity of tungsten from Table 20.1 is 5.6 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m. From R = ρL / A, the book rearranges to A = ρL / R and substitutes: A = (5.6 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m)(4.00 × 10⁻² m) / 0.350 Ω = 6.40 × 10⁻⁹ m². The area of a circle is A = πD² / 4, so D = 2√(A/π).
