Latin · Review of Capitula I–X: place phrases with in, ad, and ē

40 min · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English. A review, not a new chapter.

Learn

Key terms

place phrases (in, ad, ē)
Definition
Short preposition phrases you already have: in + the place-where form (in hortō, in the garden); ad + the toward form (ad hortum, to the garden); ē or ex + the place-from form (ē hortō, out of the garden).
Example
Puer in hortō est (he is in the garden). Puer ad hortum it (he goes to the garden). Puer ē hortō it (he goes out of the garden).
Not this
Not a new case name from a later chapter, and not a mixed-up pair (in hortō is not “to the garden”). Today reviews three place jobs already used in Capitula I–X.
Connects to
Day 41’s in hortō, and the present verbs already reviewed. Original sentences only.
Word How to say it Meaning

ad hortum ad HOR-tum to the garden (toward: ad + this form)

Today reviews three place phrases you have already met: in for the place where someone is, ad for the place toward which someone goes, and ē (or ex) for the place from which someone comes. Model sentences: Puer in hortō est. Puer ad hortum it. Puella ē hortō it. Puella nōn in hortō est.

The ending after the preposition is part of the phrase you already have; today’s job is to keep the three jobs distinct.

Mathematics · Geometry XXI: the incircle, from equal tangents

60 min · Method: the already-taught equal-tangents theorem, applied inside a triangle. Invented side lengths stay labeled.

Learn

Key terms

incircle
Definition
The unique circle that sits inside a triangle and is tangent to all three sides. The two tangent segments from any one vertex to the points of contact are equal, by the already-taught equal-tangents theorem.
Origin
From in- + circle (English Wiktionary).
Example
In a triangle with sides 6, 8, and 10 (an invented classroom triangle, labeled as invented), the three pairs of equal tangent segments are 4 and 4 along the sides from one vertex, 2 and 2 from another, and 6 and 6 from the third: 4 + 2 = 6, 2 + 6 = 8, 6 + 4 = 10.
Not this
Not the circumcircle (already taught), which passes through the three vertices. The incircle touches the sides; it does not have to pass through the vertices.
Connects to
Day 19: the two tangent segments from an outside point are equal. Today that fact is used at each vertex of a triangle.

A circle around a triangle, through the three vertices, is the circumcircle, already taught. Today is the circle inside the triangle that touches all three sides: the incircle.

At each vertex the two sides are tangents to that same circle. Day 19 already proved that the two tangent segments from an outside point are equal. So if the tangent lengths from the three vertices are called x, y, and z, the three sides are x + y, y + z, and z + x.

A worked triangle. An invented classroom triangle, labeled as invented, has sides 6, 8, and 10.

  • Let the side of length 6 be x + y, the side of length 8 be y + z, and the side of length 10 be z + x.
  • Adding all three: 2(x + y + z) = 24, so x + y + z = 12.
  • Then z = 12 − 6 = 6, x = 12 − 8 = 4, and y = 12 − 10 = 2.
  • Check: 4 + 2 = 6, 2 + 6 = 8, 6 + 4 = 10.

The incenter (the center of the incircle) and a formula for the inradius are tomorrow’s job. Today only uses equal tangents.

Remember

The incircle is tangent to all three sides. From each vertex the two tangent segments are equal (Day 19). It is not the circumcircle. Invented side lengths stay labeled.

Key terms: incircle.

Watch me

An invented triangle has sides 5, 5, and 6. Find the three tangent lengths from the vertices.

  1. Let the side 6 be z + x, and let each side 5 be x + y and y + z (the two equal sides).
  2. x + y + z = (5 + 5 + 6)/2 = 8.
  3. The tangent opposite the side 6, namely y, is 8 − 6 = 2. Then x = 8 − 5 = 3 and z = 8 − 5 = 3. Check: 3 + 2 = 5, 2 + 3 = 5, 3 + 3 = 6.

Together

An invented triangle has sides 7, 8, and 9. Find the three tangent lengths.

Hint 1: The sum

x + y + z is half the perimeter: (7 + 8 + 9)/2 = 12.

Hint 2: Each length

The tangent opposite a side equals (x + y + z) minus that side: 12 − 9 = 3, 12 − 8 = 4, 12 − 7 = 5.

Science · Refractive errors: myopia and hyperopia

50 min · Method: the already-taught path to the retina, then the NEI account of refractive errors; no invented diopters or diagnoses

Learn

Key terms

refractive error
Definition
A vision problem in which the shape of the eye keeps light from focusing correctly on the retina, so it is hard to see clearly. The National Eye Institute (NEI) uses this definition, and names eyeglasses or contact lenses as a treatment a doctor can prescribe. Surgery is listed by the NEI but is not taught in this lesson.
Example
Nearsightedness (myopia), in the NEI's wording, makes far-away objects look blurry; farsightedness (hyperopia) makes nearby objects look blurry.
Not this
Not a classroom diagnosis of anyone in the room, and not an invented diopter number or a surgery lesson. The word refraction is used here as the NEI uses it (the bending of light as it is focused on the retina), not as a new unsourced experiment.
Connects to
The eye as a kind of camera, accommodation of the lens, and the retina already taught. Today uses the NEI page “Refractive Errors.”
myopia
Definition
Nearsightedness: a refractive error that, in the NEI's wording, makes far-away objects look blurry.
Origin
Borrowed from Ancient Greek muopia (“shortsightedness”), from muein (“to shut, close”) + ops (“eye”) (English Wiktionary, entry “myopia”).
Example
A person who can read a page held close, but who finds a distant sign blurry, is the kind of case the NEI name describes. This is an illustration, not a diagnosis.
Not this
Not farsightedness, which the NEI describes as making nearby objects look blurry, and not a classroom test with invented distances.
Connects to
Today's refractive error, after last week's path to the retina.
hyperopia
Definition
Farsightedness: a refractive error that, in the NEI's wording, makes nearby objects look blurry.
Origin
From hyper- + -opia, the same eye-related second element as in myopia (English Wiktionary).
Example
A person who finds a nearby page blurry is the kind of case the NEI name describes. This is an illustration, not a diagnosis.
Not this
Not myopia, and not today's later types (astigmatism and presbyopia), which Thursday will take from the same page.
Connects to
Today's refractive error, beside myopia.

The eye as a kind of camera, the lens that can change shape (accommodation), and the retina are already taught. The National Eye Institute page "Refractive Errors" says that refractive errors are a type of vision problem that makes it hard to see clearly, and that they happen when the shape of your eye keeps light from focusing correctly on your retina.

The same page names four common types. Today takes two: nearsightedness (myopia), which "makes far-away objects look blurry," and farsightedness (hyperopia), which "makes nearby objects look blurry." Thursday will take the other two types from the same page.

The NEI says a doctor can prescribe eyeglasses or contact lenses to help a person see clearly. Surgery is listed on the page as a treatment; this lesson does not teach surgery, and it invents no diopter number, no focal length, and no classroom diagnosis.

The same page notes that refraction is the bending of light, and that the cornea and the lens bend light to focus it on the retina. That is enough for today; we do not add an unsourced experiment.

Remember

A refractive error is a focusing problem, in the NEI's account, when the eye's shape keeps light from focusing correctly on the retina. Myopia: distant blur. Hyperopia: near blur. Eyeglasses or contact lenses may be prescribed. No invented numbers, and no classroom diagnosis.

Key terms: refractive error, myopia, hyperopia.

Modern language · Spanish: me duele and me duelen

40 min · Method: present tense; a gustar-like pattern with doler. Instruction in English.

Learn

Key terms

me duele and me duelen
Definition
A present-tense pattern like *gustar*: the thing that hurts is the subject, and *me* marks the person affected. *Me duele* is used with a singular subject; *me duelen* with a plural subject.
Example
Me duele la cabeza. Me duelen los pies. *Le duele la cabeza* would be “his or her head hurts,” with the same pattern.
Not this
Not *Yo duele la cabeza,* which copies English and makes *yo* the subject of the wrong verb. The body part is the subject; the verb agrees with it.
Connects to
The *gustar* pattern already taught, now used with *doler* in the present. Instruction stays in English.
Word How to say it Meaning
me duele meh DWEH-leh it hurts me (one thing hurts)
me duelen meh DWEH-len they hurt me (more than one thing hurts)
la cabeza lah kah-BEH-sah the head
los pies lohs PYEHS the feet

Instruction stays in English. Today's pattern is like gustar, which you already have: the thing that affects you is the subject, and me marks the person affected.

Doler in the present, for this first look, is used as me duele (singular subject) and me duelen (plural subject). Model sentences: Me duele la cabeza. Me duelen los pies. ¿Te duele la cabeza? No me duele la cabeza.

The verb agrees with la cabeza or los pies, not with me. Yo duele la cabeza is the usual English-shaped miss.

Remember

Me duele + singular (Me duele la cabeza). Me duelen + plural (Me duelen los pies). The body part is the subject. Not Yo duele la cabeza.

Key terms: me duele and me duelen.

Music theory · A leading tone in a minor key, and a major V

50 min · Method: the already-taught minor triad and natural minor, then a raised seventh as a common practice for V, not a law.

Learn

Key terms

the leading tone in a minor key, a first look
Definition
The seventh scale degree raised by a half step so that it lies a half step below the tonic, often used to build a major V chord in a minor key. This is a common practice, not a law that every minor melody must follow, and not a claim that natural minor (already taught) is being cancelled.
Example
In A minor, the natural seventh is G. Raised, it is G♯, a half step below A. The triad E–G♯–B is then a major V, beside the already-taught minor triad on the tonic (A–C–E).
Not this
Not a new minor scale taught as the only minor, and not a claim about every folk or later melody. Natural minor remains as taught; today is one common way of making a V chord in minor.
Connects to
The minor triad (week 9), the natural minor scale and relative minor (week 5), and V in a major key (already taught).

You already have the minor triad, and the natural minor scale. In a major key, V was already taught as a major triad. In a minor key, a common practice is to raise the seventh degree so that V can be a major triad as well.

A first look, in A minor. The tonic minor triad is A–C–E (already taught as a minor triad). The natural seventh of A minor is G. Raised by a half step, G becomes G♯, a half step below A: a leading tone. The triad E–G♯–B is then a major V.

This is taught as a common practice, not as a law, and not as a replacement for natural minor. Some minor music keeps the natural seventh. Today only names the raised seventh as a way of making V major, beside a minor tonic.

No invented historical rule about every century, and no new key signature beyond what you already have.

Remember

In a minor key, the seventh may be raised to make a leading tone and a major V (in A minor: G♯, and E–G♯–B). This is a common practice, not a law, and it does not cancel natural minor.
