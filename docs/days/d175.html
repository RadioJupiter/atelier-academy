Latin · Purpose and result, a short weekly mix

40 min · Method: already-taught purpose and result; original sentences; no new construction

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • a result clause with ut, a first look — Day 166
  • a negative result with ut nōn, a first look — Day 167

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This sitting is a short mix of already-taught purpose and result. No new construction. Keep the four jobs distinct, and keep the present subjunctive distinct from the future. Original classroom sentences only.

Remember

Purpose: ut or nē. Result: ut or ut nōn, often with tam, ita, or sic. The verb is the present subjunctive, not the future.

Watch me

Watch one short mixed set.

  1. Puella venit ut eum sequātur is purpose.
  2. Puella tam celeriter venit ut eum sequātur is result.
  3. Puella tam lentē venit ut eum nōn sequātur is a negative result, not a nē-clause.

Together

Translate and label Puella manet nē eum sequātur and Puer tam celeriter surgit ut ēgrediātur.

Hint 1: The first

Nē marks a negative purpose.

Hint 2: The second

Tam and ut mark a positive result.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key.

  1. Translate and label four sentences, one for each already-taught job, using sequor or ēgredior.
  2. Why is nē the wrong negative in a result clause?
  3. Give the present subjunctive and the future, third person singular, of sequor and of ēgredior.

Exercises

  1. Write four original sentences, one for each job, and translate them.
  2. In one sentence, replace the present subjunctive with the future and say why that form is a miss.

Mathematics · The derivative of invented f(x) = x², a short weekly mix

40 min · Method: a short mix of this week’s difference quotient and derivative, on invented f(x) = x²; no new rule; a formal epsilon argument reserved

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • the difference quotient, a first look — Day 171
  • the derivative at a point, a first look — Day 172
  • the derivative of invented f(x) = x² — Day 173

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

This Friday mixes the week’s already-taught difference quotient and derivative, still on invented f(x) = x², with f'(x) = 2x. No new rule. A formal epsilon argument, and derivatives of other functions, remain reserved.

Remember

On invented f(x) = x², the difference quotient is 2x + h, for h ≠ 0, and f'(x) = 2x.

Watch me

Watch a short Friday set.

  1. f'(a) is the limit of the difference quotient as h approaches 0, when the limit exists.
  2. For invented f(x) = x², that limit is 2x.
  3. So f'(3) = 6, f'(2) = 4, and f'(5) = 10.

Together

Simplify the difference quotient of invented f(x) = x² at 4, and find f'(4).

Hint 1: The quotient

[ (4 + h)² − 16 ] / h = 8 + h, for h ≠ 0.

Hint 2: The limit

f'(4) = 8, which also follows from 2 × 4 = 8.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key. The function is invented.

  1. Write the definition of f'(a), and the rule f'(x) for invented f(x) = x².
  2. Find f'(3), f'(0), and f'(6).
  3. Why may h not be 0 in the difference quotient, and why may ∞ not be substituted for h?

Exercises

  1. Find f'(9) and f'(1/4).
  2. On invented f(x) = x², why is f continuous at 3, and why is that not the same statement as f'(3) = 6?

Seminar · Anatomy: the sacrum and the coccyx, mixed

40 min · Method: mix last week’s coccyx with the already-taught sacrum, from Gray 1918; official lengths reserved; not a diagnosis; no new padded term

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • the coccyx, Gray 1918 — Day 170
  • the sacrum, Gray 1918 — Day 165

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

Last week’s first look at the coccyx, from Gray 1918, said that it is usually formed of four rudimentary “vertebræ,” sometimes five or three, the first the largest, the last a nodule of bone, articulating with the sacrum at the base. The sacrum itself was already taught: a large, triangular bone, inserted like a wedge between the two hip bones, its base articulating with the last lumbar vertebra, its apex with the coccyx. Today those already-taught descriptions are kept distinct, without a new first-look name. “Vertebræ” stays inside the quotation. Official lengths remain reserved. The lesson is not a diagnosis, and no body is described from life.

Remember

The sacrum is a large triangular bone, a wedge between the two hip bones, its apex meeting the coccyx. The coccyx is usually four rudimentary “vertebræ,” the first largest, the last a nodule. Official lengths remain reserved, and the lesson is not a diagnosis.

Watch me

Together

Hint 2: The range

The number may be increased to five or diminished to three.

Vocabulary review

15–20 min · Spaced review of weeks 1–35 terms, with Term 3 emphasis · Use your own words; then check the key

Learn

This is not a new lesson, and it is not a new set of glossary cards. Retrieval — saying a term from memory, then checking — is how a word becomes usable, not merely familiar. Close the glossary and the day's cards first. For each item, explain or apply a term already taught, or distinguish it from a near-miss. The review mixes weeks 1–35, with emphasis on Term 3, including this week’s first looks.

Remember

A definition you can only recognize on the card is not yet yours. Give the meaning, a fresh example, and a near-miss.

Watch me

A model answer for a near-miss item, so the others can be written in the same shape.

  1. Question: What is the derivative of invented f(x) = x², and why is the difference quotient 2x + h not yet that derivative?
  2. For invented f(x) = x², f'(x) = 2x.
  3. The difference quotient, [ (x + h)² − x² ] / h = 2x + h, for h ≠ 0, is the increment. The derivative is the limit of that increment as h approaches 0.
  4. A second miss is to treat a formal epsilon argument, or the derivative of a different function, as if it had already been taught. Both remain reserved.

Together

In your own words, what is a comparison in this course, and which two invented types were compared? How does a comparison differ from an encomium and from an invective?

Hint 1: The job

A comparison sets two subjects side by side: likeness, difference, and what the difference comes to. The invented types are the careful geometer and the careless geometer.

Hint 2: The other jobs

An encomium praises one subject. An invective blames one subject. A comparison does not name a living person.

Check

Write in your own words, then look at the key. Do not copy a card. These items use terms already taught in weeks 1–35.

  1. Write the definition of f'(a), and the rule f'(x) for invented f(x) = x². Find f'(3) and f'(0). Why may h not be 0 in the difference quotient?
  2. What is a result clause with ut, and what is a negative result with ut nōn? Give one original sentence for each, and say how they differ from purpose clauses with ut and with nē.
  3. Give he vuelto and ha puesto, with short English glosses. Why is *volvido a miss, and how is vuelto distinct from puesto and from visto?
  4. Give official 42.2°, official 48.6°, and official 24.4°, each with its pair of materials. How, qualitatively, can light be guided along a fiber?
  5. In official Example 20.8, what official totals does the book find for 1000 hours, at the book’s example 12 cents per kWh, for the 60-W bulb and for the compact fluorescent light? Why is 12 cents not a named utility rate?
  6. In the second-voyage letter, what three islands does the letter name, and which was the first that they saw? What apparent length, and what later port, were already taught, without converting the leagues?
  7. In Pride and Prejudice, Chapter 11, what does Miss Bingley declare about reading? What is “quite a settled thing,” and what does Darcy say of vanity and of pride? Why do “sophas” and “chuses” stay inside quotations?
  8. On an invented fair cube, what is the expected value already taught? In Dewing I.6.9–14, in what form does the church of Anthimus rise? In Gray 1918, of how many rudimentary “vertebræ” is the coccyx usually formed?
