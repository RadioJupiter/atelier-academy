Latin · The future of ēgredior, a first look

40 min · Method: ēgrediar, ēgrediētur; original sentences, not Ørberg

Learn

Key terms

future of ēgredior
Definition
The future of ēgredior is a deponent future of the third conjugation in -iō: ēgrediar, ēgrediēris, ēgrediētur, ēgrediēmur, ēgrediēminī, ēgredientur, I shall go out, you will go out, and so on. It is not the present ēgredior, and it is not ēgressus eram.
Example
Puella ēgrediētur, tum tacēbit: she will go out, then she will be silent. The going out is still ahead.
Not this
Not ēgredior, I go out. Not ēgressus eram, I had gone out. Not sequar, I shall follow.
Connects to
Yesterday’s future of sequor, and last week’s present and pluperfect of ēgredior.

Ēgredior is a deponent of the third conjugation in -iō. Its present, taught two weeks ago, is ēgredior, ēgrederis, ēgreditur; its pluperfect, taught last week, is ēgressus eram. Today the future: ēgrediar, I shall go out; ēgrediēris; ēgrediētur; ēgrediēmur; ēgrediēminī; ēgredientur. The -iē- in the middle forms is the -iō future marker, not the ē of sequētur. Puer ēgrediētur is he will go out, still ahead. Sequar remains I shall follow. Sentences are original, not copied from Ørberg. Identity in a sentence is given with Puer or Puella, not with an unquoted English “boy” or “girl.”

Mathematics · Geometry LXXX: the law of cosines with an obtuse included angle

40 min · Method: cos 120° = −1/2; invented figures labeled invented

Learn

Key terms (from earlier lessons)

  • the law of cosines — Day 136

Open a card if you need a reminder; then close it and use the term in your own words.

When the included angle is obtuse, its cosine is negative, so the term −2ab cos C becomes an addition, and the opposite side is longer than it would be in an acute or right triangle with the same two sides. Cos 120° = −1/2, from last week’s related angles, because 120° = 180° − 60°, and cos(180° − θ) = −cos θ.

On an invented triangle with sides 4 and 4 and included angle 120°, c² = 16 + 16 − 2·4·4·(−1/2) = 32 + 16 = 48, so c = √48 = 4√3. That is the same opposite side last week’s 30°-30°-120° triangle gave by the law of sines, which is a check, not a new official figure. The 4, 4, and 120° are invented classroom numbers. Do not take cos 120° as +1/2, and do not switch to radians.

Remember

When C is 120°, cos C = −1/2, so −2ab cos C is positive, and the opposite side is longer than it would be for an acute included angle on the same two sides.

Watch me

Science · Cinnabar, a red mercury sulfide, a first look

40 min · Method: USGS Spectral Library HS133, Clark et al. 2003 OFR 03-395; not a studio recipe

Learn

Key terms

cinnabar
Definition
Cinnabar, in USGS Spectral Library sample HS133, Clark et al. 2003, Open File Report 03-395, is a sulfide mineral whose formula is given as HgS. The collection locality is Manhattan, Nevada. The microscopic description calls the color “blood red” and “red translucent.” It is not azurite, not malachite, and not a fifth atelier tube.
Example
USGS HS133 is recorded as cinnabar, HgS, from Manhattan, Nevada, in the Hunt and Salisbury Collection. The lesson uses that record, not an official price or a studio recipe.
Not this
Not azurite or malachite, which are copper carbonates. Not lapis lazuli or ultramarine. Not a classroom recipe, and not a tube added to the limited palette. Not an official mercury percentage of a stone not in the record.
Connects to
Last week’s azurite and malachite, and earlier ochre, hematite, lapis, and ultramarine, all from named sources. Cinnabar is mercury sulfide and is not handled as a studio material in this course.

The USGS Spectral Library record Cinnabar HS133, in Clark et al. 2003, Open File Report 03-395, at https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2003/ofr-03-395/DESCRIPT/M/cinnabar_hs133.html, identifies the mineral as cinnabar, a sulfide, with formula HgS. The collection locality is Manhattan, Nevada. The original donor is the Hunt and Salisbury Collection. The microscopic examination by G. Swayze describes a “blood red color, red translucent,” with “multiple good cleavages,” “all consistent with cinnabar.” The same record says the sample is impure and “contains 0.6% iron by weight,” a figure for this sample, HS133, not a general official iron percentage of all cinnabar. Cinnabar is mercury sulfide. It is not a studio material in this course, not a recipe, and not a fifth tube in the limited palette. Azurite and malachite, taught last week, are copper carbonates, not HgS. Invent no official price, and no official mercury percentage of a classroom stone.

Definition

When Jane and Elizabeth are alone, Jane, “who had been cautious in her praise of Mr. Bingley before, expressed to her sister how very much she admired him.” “He is just what a young man ought to be,” said she, “sensible, good humoured, lively; and I never saw such happy manners!—so much ease, with such perfect good breeding!” Elizabeth replies, “He is also handsome,” “which a young man ought likewise to be, if he possibly can. Modern language · Spanish: the present subjunctive of regular -er and -ir verbs

40 min · Method: coma and viva; contrasted with the indicative and with hable

Learn

Key terms

present subjunctive of regular -er and -ir verbs
Definition
The present subjunctive of a regular -er or -ir verb uses the endings -a, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an: coma, comas, coma, comamos, comáis, coman; viva, vivas, viva, vivamos, viváis, vivan. It is not the present indicative, and it is not yesterday’s hable pattern with -e.
Example
In an invented sentence, “Quiere que ella coma.” Coma is the present subjunctive of comer, not the indicative come.
Not this
Not come or vive, the present indicative. Not the -ar subjunctive hable, which uses -e. Not an irregular subjunctive such as sea or vaya, which remain reserved.
Connects to
Yesterday’s present subjunctive of a regular -ar verb, and the already-taught present indicative of regular -er and -ir verbs.

Regular -er and -ir verbs form the present subjunctive with -a, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an, the opposite vowel from their indicative. Comer: coma, comas, coma, comamos, comáis, coman. Vivir: viva, vivas, viva, vivamos, viváis, vivan. Yesterday’s hablar used -e, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en. Do not put -a on an -ar verb, or -e on an -er or -ir verb, in this tense. Invented classroom frames may still use “quiere que”: “Quiere que vivamos aquí.” Irregular subjunctives remain reserved.

Word How to say it Meaning

comamos koh-MAH-mohs that we eat
viva BEE-bah that I live, or that he or she live
vivan BEE-bahn that they live

Remember

Regular -er and -ir present subjunctives use -a: coma, viva. Regular -ar present subjunctives use -e: hable. Neither is the indicative.

Seminar · Music: theme and variations, a first look

40 min · Method: an invented scale-degree theme and two short variations; not a copyrighted score

Learn

Key terms

theme and variations
Definition
Theme and variations is a form in which a short theme is stated and then repeated with changes, each repetition a variation, while the theme remains recognizable. It is not rondo form, and it is not a copyrighted score.
Example
An invented four-bar theme in scale degrees, 1-3-5-3 | 5-4-3-1, may be followed by a variation that repeats the last note, and by a second variation that uses neighbor tones, 1-2-3-5 | 5-4-2-1. The figure is invented classroom work, not a copyrighted tune.
Not this
Not rondo form, A–B–A–C–A, whose episodes contrast with the refrain rather than varying one theme. Not binary or ternary form as a first look already taught. Not a copyrighted score, and not a transcription of a recorded work.
Connects to
Last week’s rondo, and earlier binary and ternary form. Varied or longer rondos remain reserved; so does any later form not yet taught.

Last week’s rondo was A–B–A–C–A: a refrain returns, and the episodes contrast with it. Today a different form, theme and variations: a short theme is stated, then repeated with changes, each repetition a variation, while the theme remains recognizable. The episodes of a rondo are not, in this first look, the same job as variations of one theme.

The set figure is invented, in scale degrees, not a copyrighted score. Theme, four bars: 1-3-5-3 | 5-4-3-1. Variation 1 keeps the same degrees and lengthens or repeats the last note. Variation 2 uses neighbor tones: 1-2-3-5 | 5-4-2-1. The theme should still be heard in each variation. Do not transcribe a recorded work, and do not import a copyrighted tune. Longer variation sets remain reserved.

Remember

Theme and variations states a short theme and then repeats it with changes. The set figure is invented, in scale degrees, not a copyrighted score. It is not rondo form.

Watch me

  2. Variation 1: the same degrees, with the last note lengthened or repeated.
  3. Variation 2: 1-2-3-5 | 5-4-2-1, neighbor tones around the same shape.
