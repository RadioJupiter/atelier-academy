Latin · Capitulum VI, part 3: the locative

30 min · Text: Ørberg, Familia Romana, Cap. VI · Method: the natural method

Learn

Key terms

locative
Definition
A special case used with the names of towns, cities, and small islands (and a few other words) to answer ubi? (where?). It often looks like a genitive in the first and second declensions: Rōmae (at Rome), Tusculī (at Tusculum).
Origin
From Latin locātīvus, from locus (‘place’).
Example
Mārcus Rōmae habitat. Iūlia Tusculī est.
Not this
The locative is not the genitive of possession (even when it looks the same), and it is not in Rōmā (which would be a wrong blend of in + a town name). For a common noun, place where is still in + ablative: in vīllā.
Connects to
Together with Rōmam (to) and Rōmā (from), you now have all three place questions for a town name.
habitāre
Definition
The Latin verb meaning ‘to live, to dwell’. It often takes a locative with a town name, or in + ablative with a common noun.
Example
Mārcus Rōmae habitat, but Mārcus in vīllā habitat.
Not this
habitāre is not ‘to have’ (habēre). The two verbs look related in English memory, but they are different Latin words with different objects and complements.
Connects to
The locative answers ubi habitat…? just as in + ablative answers ubi est…? for a villa or a garden.

The third question, ubi?, has a special form for town names: the locative.

Word How to say it Meaning
Rōmae ROH-my at Rome, in Rome (locative)
Tusculī TUS-ku-lee at Tusculum (locative)
habitat HA-bi-tat lives, dwells
habitant HA-bi-tant they live

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

The three-form table for a town. For Rōma: quō? Rōmam it; unde? Rōmā venit; ubi? Rōmae habitat. For Tusculum: Tusculum, Tusculō, Tusculī.

For a common noun the old rule stands: ad vīllam, ex vīllā, in vīllā. There is no locative of vīlla in this lesson.

Original sentences: Mārcus Rōmae habitat. Iūlia nōn Rōmae, sed Tusculī habitat.

Remember

Locative of a town answers ubi?: Rōmae, Tusculī. Do not use in Rōmā. Common nouns still use in + ablative. habitāre = to live (not habēre = to have).

Key terms: locative, habitāre.

Mathematics · Algebra VI: radicals

60 min · Method: Gelfand, Algebra, square roots as the inverse of squaring

Learn

Key terms

square root √a
Definition
For a ≥ 0, the square root √a is the unique number ≥ 0 whose square is a. It undoes squaring on the non-negative numbers.
Example
√25 = 5, because 5² = 25 and 5 ≥ 0. √0 = 0. There is no real square root of a negative number.
Not this
√a is not allowed to be negative: √25 is 5, not −5, even though (−5)² = 25 too. The equation x² = 25 has two real solutions, ±5; the symbol √25 names only the non-negative one.
Connects to
Yesterday, a² meant two factors of a. The square root is the inverse operation when a ≥ 0: (√a)² = a and √(a²) = a for a ≥ 0.
radical
Definition
An expression that uses a root symbol, such as √a or ³√a. A square-root radical with a whole-number radicand is often rewritten by taking out a square factor: √(k² · n) = k√n when k ≥ 0.
Origin
From Middle English radical, from Latin rādīcālis (‘of the root, having roots’), from rādīx (‘root’).
Example
√72 = √(36 · 2) = 6√2, and √12 + √27 = 2√3 + 3√3 = 5√3.
Not this
You may not add radicands: √12 + √27 is not √39. You may multiply them: √a · √b = √(ab) for a, b ≥ 0.
Connects to
Simplified form (for this course): no perfect-square factor other than 1 remains under a square-root sign, and no radical sits in a denominator that you can clear.

The square root √a, for a ≥ 0, is the unique number ≥ 0 that squares to a. The two real solutions of x² = 25 are x = 5 and x = −5; the symbol √25 means only 5.

Product law. For a, b ≥ 0, √a · √b = √(ab). In the other direction, a square factor comes out: √(k² · n) = k√n when k ≥ 0.

Sum. Radicals add only when they are like terms: √12 + √27 = 2√3 + 3√3 = 5√3. You cannot write √12 + √27 as a single square root of a sum.

Check by squaring. If you claim √72 = 6√2, then (6√2)² = 36 · 2 = 72.

Remember

√a (a ≥ 0) is the non-negative number that squares to a. √(k²n) = k√n (k ≥ 0). Multiply radicands; add only like radicals. Check by squaring.

Key terms: square root, radical.

Writing & Rhetoric · Franklin's method: reconstruction from the hints

45 min · Method: Franklin's reconstruction; the Day 14 hints from Johnson, Rasselas, chapter 1 (public domain)

Learn

Key terms

reconstruction
Definition
The step in Franklin’s method in which you rebuild a model passage from your hints alone, in complete sentences, and only then compare what you wrote with the original to see which choices you missed.
Origin
From Late Latin re- + constructiō, the same family as Day 11’s construction, but here the thing rebuilt is a passage of prose, not a geometric figure.
Example
From the hints ‘prince — valley — every pleasure — not satisfied’, you write a complete period, then look back at Johnson to see where his verb arrived and which noun he chose.
Not this
Reconstruction is not reciting the original from memory, and it is not revising the original to improve it. The comparison is for noticing, not for scoring.
Connects to
Day 7 practiced this on Addison. Today the hints are the ones you made on Day 14. If those hints were copied clauses, the exercise will feel empty — which is itself information.
comparison step
Definition
The last step of a reconstruction: laying your version beside the original and naming, in writing, two or three specific choices the author made that you did not — a verb, a delay, an apposition, a transition — not a vague judgment that the original is ‘better’.
Example
‘Johnson’s main verb arrives after two conditions; mine arrived at once’ is a comparison. ‘His is more elegant’ is not.
Not this
The comparison step is not a hunt for errors in the original, and it is not a grade. It is a list of choices to try in the next imitation.
Connects to
Day 12’s variation already asked you not to borrow wording. The comparison step is how you steal a *move* without stealing a phrase.

Take the hint sheet from Day 14. Do not open Johnson yet.

Rebuild. Write the paragraph from the hints, in complete sentences, as a period-rich prose of your own. Do not stop to polish mid-way. The point is to have a whole version on the page.

Compare. Open Rasselas, chapter 1, and look only at the opening paragraph you studied. Under your reconstruction, write two or three specific differences: where the main verb falls, a noun or verb you missed, a delay you flattened, a connection you made too early.

Do not score yourself. Name the choices. Then, if you have time, rewrite one of your sentences using one of Johnson’s moves (not his words).

Remember

Rebuild from hints only, in complete sentences. Then compare with the original and name two or three specific choices (verb, delay, connection), not a vague judgment. Try one move, not the wording, in a rewrite.

Key terms: reconstruction, comparison step.

Watch me

A reconstruction of a model sentence reads ‘A prince lived in a nice valley and had everything but was sad.’ The original delayed the main verb and did not say ‘nice’ or ‘sad’. Name two specific misses.

  1. The reconstruction flattens the period: the main verb arrives at once, in ‘lived’.
  2. The adjectives ‘nice’ and ‘sad’ are vague substitutions for more exact nouns or verbs in the model (pleasure, restlessness, or whatever the original actually used).
  3. A good comparison names those two choices, not ‘Johnson is a better writer.’

Hint 2: Why

A peek turns the exercise into copying. A weak reconstruction plus an honest comparison teaches more than a patched recitation.

Science · Pigment and binder

45 min · Method: the paint as a mixture, named from what each part does

Learn

Key terms

pigment
Definition
A finely divided solid that gives a paint its color (and often its opacity) and that remains as particles in the film. It is not the liquid that hardens around it.
Origin
From Middle English pigment, from Latin pigmentum, from pingere (‘to paint’) + -mentum.
Example
Yellow ochre is an earth pigment colored by iron oxides; a carbon black is a different solid, and neither one is the linseed oil.
Not this
A pigment is not a dye dissolved as a true solution (though some colorants are dyes), and it is not the binder. Crushing a rock does not, by itself, make a paint until a binder is added.
Connects to
Term 3 will return to ‘from rock to pigment’. Today you only need the distinction: the solid particles that color the film, versus the oil that forms the film.
binder
Definition
The substance that holds the pigment particles in a film and attaches the film to the support. In oil paint, the binder is a drying oil; in watercolor, it is usually gum, and the water is mainly a vehicle that evaporates.
Origin
From Middle English, bind + the agent ending -er.
Example
The same ochre, mixed in linseed oil or in gum and water, makes two different paints, because the binder is different.
Not this
The binder is not the pigment, and it is not a solvent added only to thin the paint (that solvent, if used, is meant to leave).
Connects to
The last two lessons explained how a drying-oil binder forms a film. Today that film is what holds the pigment.

A paint is, at its simplest, pigment in a binder.

The pigment is a solid in small particles: earth ochres (colored by iron oxides), carbon blacks, and, in later history, many others, including lead white (toxic, and not a material to handle in this course). The pigment gives color and, often, hiding power. It does not have to polymerize.

The binder is what becomes the film and holds those particles. For oil paint, that is the drying oil you have been studying. For watercolor, a gum binder remains after the water evaporates.

A small, safe comparison. If you have a yellow ochre or another dry earth pigment, mix a little with water alone, and a little with a drop of linseed oil, on a labeled plate. The oil mixture should, over the days of this module, begin to hold as a film; the water mixture will either dust off or, if a gum is present, hold by a different binder. Do not use lead or other toxic historical pigments.

The solvent (turpentine or a modern substitute), if used, is there to change the handling and then to leave. It is not the binder.

Remember

Pigment: solid colored particles. Binder: the substance that forms the film and holds the particles (a drying oil in oil paint). A solvent may evaporate; the binder, in oil paint, stays and hardens. Do not handle toxic historical pigments such as lead white.

Key terms: pigment, binder.

Watch me

A student calls linseed oil ‘the pigment of oil paint’ because it is what you pour out of the bottle. Repair the use of the word.

  1. Linseed oil is the binder, not the pigment.
  2. The pigment is the solid colorant, such as an ochre, that is ground into the oil.
  3. Without a pigment, the oil can still form a film, but the film is not a colored paint in the usual sense.

Hint 2: The binder

The binder is different: a drying oil in one case, usually a gum (with water as a vehicle) in the other. The film-forming process is then different too.

Literature · Iliad, Books 17–22: the shield, the return, the death of Hector

45 min · Method: close reading · Read Books 17–22 before this lesson

Learn

Key terms

ekphrasis
Definition
A set-piece description of a work of art inside a poem. The great example here is the shield of Achilles in Book 18, made by Hephaestus, which holds not only war but also a city at peace, a lawsuit, harvest, and dance.
Origin
From Ancient Greek ἔκφρασις (‘description’), from ἐκφράζω (‘I describe’), from ἐκ (‘out’) + φράζω (‘I explain, I point out’).
Example
The shield’s scenes include two cities, one in festival and one besieged, and work in the fields. The poem spends a long time on a world that is not, in that moment, the battlefield at Troy.
Not this
An ekphrasis is not a stage direction, and it is not ‘Homer getting distracted’. The shield is a made object in the story and a picture of a wider world than this war.
Connects to
You have seen extended similes do some of this work (Day 4). The shield is a larger, designed picture, with a craftsman and a viewer inside the poem.
the death of Hector
Definition
The climax of Book 22: Achilles, now back in the war, chases Hector around the walls of Troy and kills him, then mistreats the body. The poem gives you Hector’s flight, his decision to stand, Andromache still unaware, and the parents on the wall.
Example
Hector is wearing Achilles’ old armor, taken from Patroclus. The chase, the deception (Athena’s disguise, in the poem’s telling), and the death are to be read, not reduced to a score.
Not this
This is not ‘the end of the Iliad’. Two books remain, and they will not be a victory lap. It is also not a simple moral (‘Hector cowardly, Achilles just’). The poem is more difficult than that.
Connects to
Book 6 already gave you Hector with Andromache and the child. Book 22 is written against that memory.

Read Books 17–22. Book 17: the long fight over Patroclus’s body. Book 18: Achilles learns, Thetis goes to Hephaestus, and the shield is made — a great ekphrasis. Book 19: Achilles and Agamemnon are formally reconciled; Achilles still will not eat, and the war becomes his revenge. Books 20–21: he returns to the field. Book 22: Hector dies, and the body is dragged.

The shield. Do not skip it as decoration. List four kinds of scene Hephaestus puts on the shield, and write why a poem of this war might need a city at peace, or a lawsuit, or a harvest, on the armor of the man who is about to go back and kill.

Book 22. Read the death slowly. Notice who is watching (Priam, Hecuba, and later Andromache). Notice that Hector runs, then stands. If you quote, quote a short attributed phrase and explain it.

The poem is not over.

Remember

Books 17–22: the body, the shield (ekphrasis of a wider world), the formal reconciliation, Achilles’ return, and the death of Hector. The Iliad does not end with that death. Book 6’s Hector is still in memory when Book 22 happens.

Key terms: ekphrasis, the death of Hector.

Atelier · A plaster feature: from plane to a more particular form

45 min · Method: a small plaster cast of a feature (nose, ear, mouth, or eye), one light, fixed viewing point · After the geometric solids and the Bargue feature

Learn

Key terms

transition of planes
Definition
The way one plane of a form gives way to the next: as a sharp fold, as a soft turn, or as a series of small planes that together read as a curve. A plaster nose has both: a sharper change at a cartilage ridge, a softer turn into the cheek (if the cast includes it), and small planes around a nostril.
Example
After the cube, you can block a nose as a wedge (front plane, two side planes, the underside). Then you break those large planes where the plaster actually breaks, not where a symbol of a nose would.
Not this
A transition is not a smear that hides the large planes, and it is not a cut as sharp as a cube if the plaster is soft there. You look, then you decide.
Connects to
The Bargue feature (Day 15) was a printed translation of form. The plaster is a form in the round, with a real light you can move only if you mean to — and today you will not move it.
plaster feature
Definition
A small white or off-white cast of a nose, ear, mouth, or eye, used so that color does not distract and the light can be read as value. It is a particular form, still built from large planes first.
Example
A plaster ear is a set of ridges and bowls around a hole; it is not a question-mark symbol. A plaster nose is a wedge first, then the smaller planes.
Not this
This is not a portrait of a person, and it is not a full head (unless you already have a whole plaster head and choose a feature-sized passage). Stay with one feature, large on the page.
Connects to
Week 2’s larger cast taught set-up, terminator, and halftones. Today that method is applied to a smaller, more particular form, with hatching available.

A plaster feature is the meeting of the solids and the Bargue feature: a real, pale form in the round, particular, and still constructed from large planes.

Set-up. One feature, one main light (a clear light plane and a clear shadow), a fixed viewing point, sight-size if you can. A simple background or a cloth that does not compete.

Order of work. 1. Enclosing shape and main angles (as if it were a small cube or wedge). 2. Large planes and the transition between them (sharp, soft, or stepped). 3. Alignments: a plumb through the bridge of a nose, a horizontal across the wings, and so on. 4. Families of light, then hatch or plane tone, with control. The terminator may change character: harder on a sharp plane change, softer on a turn.

White plaster is not a reason to leave the drawing pale. The shadow family on plaster can be quite dark relative to the light family; what plaster gives you is a chance to see that relation without local color. Compare, do not invent a timid mid-gray everywhere.

A common error. Skipping the wedge and drawing the nostrils first; or treating the plaster as if it were a Bargue plate (it will have slightly different shapes in your light).

Timed block-in (15 minutes), then planes and a first modeling.

Remember

A plaster feature is built from large planes first, then particular transitions (sharp, soft, or stepped). It is a form in the round under your light, not a symbol and not a copy of yesterday’s plate. White plaster still has a full value range in one light.

Key terms: transition of planes, plaster feature.

Watch me

A student begins a plaster nose with the nostrils, then adds a shaded triangle. What should have come first, and what is a ‘transition of planes’ here?

  1. The enclosing shape and the large wedge (front, sides, underside) should have come first, with measured angles.
  2. A transition of planes is how those large planes give way — a hard fold, a soft turn, or small stepped planes — looked at on the plaster, not smeared in.
  3. Nostrils are later, smaller forms on that construction.

Together

Modern language · Spanish: the personal a

30 min · Method: explicit rule, then contrast with things and with tener · Instruction in English

Learn

Key terms

personal a
Definition
The preposition a placed before a direct object that is a specific person (or a pet, treated as a person). It does not translate as a separate English word.
Example
Veo a María. Busco a mi amigo. ¿Conoces a los estudiantes? But: Veo la casa. Busco el libro.
Not this
The personal a is not the a of ir a + infinitive, and it is not needed before a thing. It is also not used with tener for ordinary possession: Tengo un hermano, not *Tengo a un hermano* in that sense.
Connects to
You have used a in voy a estudiar and voy al museo. Those are different jobs. Today’s a marks a person as the direct object.
a specific person as object
Definition
The personal a appears when the object is a particular person you have in mind, including names, family words used of real people, and pronouns for people. It is not used when the object is an indefinite, unspecific ‘anyone’ in a few set patterns, and it is not used for things.
Example
Visito a mi profesora. No veo a nadie is a related pattern you may meet later; today, stay with clear, specific people versus things.
Not this
Do not put a before la casa, el libro, or la lección. Do not drop a before María.
Connects to
Direct-object pronouns still replace the whole object: ¿Ves a María? Sí, la veo. The a is not kept in front of la.

When the direct object is a specific person, Spanish puts a before that object. English has no separate word for this a.

People, not things. Veo a Ana. Visito a mi familia. Busco a mi amigo. But Veo el museo. Visito la ciudad. Busco el libro.

Names and family words. A name is specific: ¿Llamas a Pedro? A family noun used of a real person is too: Ayudo a mi hermano.

Not with tener (possession). Tengo un hermano. Tengo una profesora. Do not write Tengo a un hermano for ordinary ‘I have’.

Not the same a as motion or the near future. Voy a la escuela (motion). Voy a estudiar (near future). Veo a la profesora (personal a).

With pronouns. The personal a is not stacked in front of lo/la: ¿Ves a Ana? Sí, la veo.

Word How to say it Meaning
a ah personal a (before a specific person as object)
Veo a Ana BEH-oh ah AH-nah I see Ana
Busco a mi amigo BOOS-koh ah mee ah-MEE-goh I am looking for my friend
Tengo un hermano TEN-goh oon er-MAH-noh I have a brother (no personal a with tener)

If you are not sure, ask: is the object a particular person? If yes, use a. If it is a thing, do not.

Remember

Use a before a specific person as a direct object (Veo a Ana). Do not use it before a thing, and do not use it with tener for possession. This a is not the a of ir a. After a question with a person, the pronoun answer is just la veo, not a la veo.

Music theory · The triad

30 min · Method: build from intervals already taught · After Day 12

Learn

Key terms

triad
Definition
A three-note chord built from a root, a third above the root, and a fifth above the root. In this lesson the fifth is perfect, and the third is either major or minor.
Origin
From Latin triad-, the stem of trias (‘three, triad’), from Ancient Greek τριάς.
Example
A C-major triad is C–E–G: C to E a major third, C to G a perfect fifth. An A-minor triad is A–C–E: A to C a minor third, A to E a perfect fifth.
Not this
A triad is not ‘any three notes’, and it is not a scale. Three neighboring scale steps (C–D–E) are not a triad in this sense.
Connects to
Day 12’s intervals are the measuring tools. The quality of the third decides whether the triad is major or minor, when the fifth is perfect.
major and minor triad
Definition
A major triad has a major third and a perfect fifth above the root (the upper interval, from the third to the fifth, is then a minor third). A minor triad has a minor third and a perfect fifth (the upper interval is then a major third).
Example
G–B–D is major (G–B is a major third). E–G–B is minor (E–G is a minor third). Both have a perfect fifth from the root (G–D, E–B).
Not this
‘Major’ and ‘minor’ here name the triad, not the key of a whole piece, and not a mood. A minor triad can appear in a major key, and a major triad in a minor key.
Connects to
You do not need diminished or augmented triads for the check, except to know that they are what you get if the fifth is no longer perfect.

A triad stacks a root, a third, and a fifth.

Major. Root, major third, perfect fifth. In C major, the triad on C is C–E–G. The triad on F is F–A–C; on G, G–B–D. Those three are the major triads in C major (built on scale degrees 1, 4, and 5).

Minor. Root, minor third, perfect fifth. In C major, the triad on D is D–F–A (D–F is a minor third); on E, E–G–B; on A, A–C–E.

A building check. From the root, count the third’s quality first, then check that the fifth is perfect (seven half steps, or the span you already named a perfect fifth on Day 12).

The same three notes in another order are still that triad (C–E–G, E–G–C, G–C–E are all a C-major triad). Today’s check will mostly use root position: the root at the bottom.

Do not invent extra chromatic notes unless a question asks for a triad from a named quality (for example, a major triad on D needs F♯, because D–F is a minor third).

Remember

A triad is root + third + fifth. Major: major third and perfect fifth. Minor: minor third and perfect fifth. In C major, triads on C, F, G are major; on D, E, A are minor. The same three notes remain that triad if the order changes.
